9. Mouse-walk 9. 老鼠步

The mouse vanished into the tall grass on the other side of the stream. 小老鼠消失在小溪对岸的高草里。

Jack and Annie stared at the tree branch. 杰克和安妮盯着那根树枝。

“We have to try to cross it,” said Annie. “We’re supposed to follow nature.” “我们得试着过，”安妮说。 “我们应该跟随自然。”

“Forget it,” said Jack. “It’s too little. It’ll crack in a second.” “算了吧，”杰克说。 “太细了。 一下子就会断。”

“Maybe if we pretend we’re mice, we can do it,” said Annie.

“Oh brother,” said Jack. “Not again.” “天哪，”杰克说。 “又来了。”

“If you could be a rock, you can be a mouse,” said Annie. “Just be teeny and light and fast.” “你能变成石头，就能变成老鼠，”安妮说。 “只要又小又轻又快就行。”

Jack took a deep breath. 杰克深吸一口气。

“We have to,” said Annie. “我们必须过，”安妮说。

“Okay,” Jack said. “好吧，”杰克说。

“Say ‘squeak,’ ” said Annie. “说‘吱吱’，”安妮说。

“You’re nuts!” said Jack. “你疯了！”杰克说。

“Just do it,” said Annie. “It’ll help you feel more like a mouse.” “快说，”安妮说。 “这样你会更像老鼠。”

Jack groaned. “Okay,” he said. “Squeak.” 杰克哼了一声。 “好吧，”他说。 “吱吱。”

“Squeak,” said Annie. “吱吱，”安妮说。

“Squeak, squeak, squeak,” they said together. “吱吱，吱吱，吱吱，”他们一起说。

“Let’s go! Hurry!” said Annie.

Jack stepped onto the branch. 杰克踏上树枝。

I’m teeny. I’m light. I’m fast, he thought. Then he darted across the branch. 我很小。我很轻。 我很快，他心想。 然后他飞快地冲过树枝。

Jack moved so quickly, he didn’t think about anything—except getting to the other side. 杰克跑得太快了，什么都没想——只想着到对岸。

He forgot the wild, freezing water. He forgot the smallness of the branch. 他忘了那又急又冰的水。 也忘了树枝有多细。

Suddenly Jack was on the other side. Suddenly Annie was right beside him. 突然，杰克到了对岸。 突然，安妮就在他旁边。

They laughed and fell together into the grass. 他们笑着一起倒在草里。

“See! See! The branch didn’t break!” said Annie. “树枝没断！”安妮说。

“I guess it was big enough,” said Jack. “I guess we just had to think the right way.” “看来够粗，”杰克说。 “看来我们只要想对了就行。”

“The Peanut way,” said Annie. “花生的办法，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack, smiling. He felt great. “对，”杰克笑着说。他感觉棒极了。

He was still wet from his fall into the stream. But he didn’t mind anymore. 他掉进小溪还是湿的。 可他已经不在意了。

Jack pushed his glasses into place and stood up. “Okay, now we just have to find the tree house,” he said. 杰克把眼镜推正，站了起来。 “好了，现在只要找到树屋就行，”他说。

“No, we don’t,” said Annie. She pointed up. “不用找了，”安妮说。她往上指。

The tree house was outlined against the moonlit sky. High in a tree. Surrounded by white flowers. 树屋的轮廓映在月光下的天空里。高高地在一棵树上。 四周都是白花。

In the distance came the sound of voices. Then Jack saw flames. 远处传来说话声。 接着杰克看见了火光。

“The samurai are coming back,” said Jack. “We have to go.” “武士回来了，”杰克说。 “我们得走了。”

“Where’s Peanut?” said Annie. “We can’t leave Peanut.” “花生呢？”安妮说。 “必须走，”杰克说。

“We have to,” said Jack.

The voices of the samurai were getting closer. So were their torches. 武士的声音越来越近。 他们的火把也越来越近。

“Come on,” Jack said. He grabbed Annie’s hand. He pulled her toward the rope ladder. “快点，”杰克说。 他抓住安妮的手。 他把她拉向绳梯。

“Oh Jack—” she said sadly. “杰克——”她难过地说。

“Go! Go!” “快上！快上！”

Annie started up the rope ladder. 安妮开始往绳梯上爬。

Jack followed. He felt sad, too. He liked that little mouse now. He liked it a lot. 杰克跟着。他也很难过。 他现在喜欢那只小老鼠了。 很喜欢。

They climbed up and up. 他们往上爬，再往上爬。

Just before they got to the top, Jack heard it. 快到顶的时候，杰克听见了。

Squeak.

“Oh wow!” cried Annie. “Peanut’s inside!” “哇！”安妮喊。 “花生在里面！”

Annie pulled herself into the tree house. Jack followed. 安妮爬进树屋。杰克跟着进去。

He gasped. 他倒吸一口气。

Someone else was in the tree house, too. 树屋里还有别人。

A dark figure was sitting in the corner. 一个黑影坐在角落里。

“You have done well,” the figure said. “你们做得很好，”那人影说。

It was the ninja master. 是忍者大师。

“You have followed the way of the ninja,” he said. “你们已经跟随了忍者之道，”他说。

“Oh man,” breathed Jack. “天哪，”杰克轻轻说。

Squeak.

The master was holding Peanut. 大师正抱着花生。

“Take good care of your little helper,” he said, handing the mouse to Annie. “好好照顾你们的小帮手，”他说着，把老鼠递给安妮。

Annie kissed the mouse’s tiny head. 安妮亲了亲小老鼠的小脑袋。

“And take this—” said the master. He held his hand out to Jack. “还有这个——”大师说。 他把手伸向杰克。

He gave Jack a small, round stone. 他给了杰克一块又小又圆的石头。

“This moonstone will help you find your missing friend,” the master said. “这块月光石会帮你们找到失踪的朋友，”大师说。

Jack stared at the stone. Was this one of the four things? 杰克盯着那块石头。 这是那四样东西里的一样吗？

“You must go home now,” said the master. He picked up the Pennsylvania book and handed it to Annie. “你们现在必须回家了，”大师说。 他拿起宾夕法尼亚的书，递给安妮。

“Where did you find it?” asked Jack. “你在哪儿找到的？”杰克问。

“Here,” said the master. “You did not see it before. Because your heart knew you had a mission to complete first.” “就在这儿，”大师说。 “你们刚才没看见。 因为你们心里知道，得先完成任务。”

“What about you?” said Annie. “Can you come with us?” “那你呢？”安妮说。 “你能跟我们一起走吗？”

“Yes,” said Jack. “We need help finding Morgan.” “对，”杰克说。 “我们找摩根需要帮忙。”

The master smiled. “No, my friends. I must stay here. There will be more help along the way. But you must find the way on your own.” 大师笑了。 “不行，我的朋友。我必须留在这儿。 路上还会有更多帮助。 可你们必须自己找到路。”

Annie opened the book. She found the picture of Frog Creek. 安妮打开书。 她找到了蛙溪镇的图画。

She pointed to it. “I wish we could go there,” she said. 她指着那幅画。 “我希望我们能去那儿，”她说。

The wind started to blow. 风开始刮起来。

The white flowers started to shake. Clouds covered the moon. 白花开始摇晃。 云遮住了月亮。

“Remember,” the master said, “keep a kind heart.” “记住，”大师说，“要有一颗善良的心。”

Then he swung silently down the rope ladder. He disappeared into the dark night. 然后他悄悄顺着绳梯滑下去。 他消失在黑夜中。

“Wait!” Jack called. There was so much he wanted to ask the master. About nature. About ninjas. About their mission. “等一下！”杰克喊。 他有好多问题想问大师。关于自然。 关于忍者。 关于他们的任务。

But the tree house started to spin. 可树屋开始转起来。

It spun faster and faster! 转得越来越快！

Jack gripped the stone in his hand. He squeezed his eyes shut. 杰克紧紧握住手里的石头。 他把眼睛紧紧闭上。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止。
