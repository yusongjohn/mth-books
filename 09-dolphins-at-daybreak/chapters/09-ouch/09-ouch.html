9. Ouch! 9. 哎哟！

The sun shone on the ocean. It sparkled like a diamond. 阳光照在海面上。 它像钻石一样闪闪发光。

Jack felt safe now. His dolphin was taking good care of him. 杰克现在觉得安全了。 他的海豚把他照顾得很好。

The dolphins slowed down as they neared the reef. 靠近珊瑚礁时，海豚们放慢了速度。

Jack lowered his feet. He felt the bumpy coral. He let go of the dolphin’s fin and stood up in the water. 杰克放下脚。 他感觉到凹凸不平的珊瑚。 他松开海豚的鳍，在水里站了起来。

Annie stood, too. 安妮也站了起来。

Then she threw her arms around her dolphin and gave her a big hug. 然后她张开双臂搂住她的海豚，给了它一个大大的拥抱。

“Thank you, Sukie!” she cried. And she kissed the dolphin’s nose. “谢谢你，苏琪！”她喊道。 她亲了亲海豚的鼻子。

Sukie tossed her head and clicked at Annie. 苏琪甩了甩头，对安妮发出咔嗒声。

“Kiss Sam now!” Annie said to Jack. “现在亲山姆一下！”安妮对杰克说。

“You’re nuts,” said Jack. “你疯了，”杰克说。

But Sam nuzzled Jack’s head. Then he put his flippers around Jack’s neck. 可萨姆用鼻子蹭了蹭杰克的头。 然后它把鳍足绕在杰克的脖子上。

Jack couldn’t resist. He threw his arms around the dolphin and gave him a quick kiss. 杰克忍不住了。 他张开双臂抱住海豚，飞快地亲了一下。

Sam nodded and made clicking sounds like laughter. Then he turned to Sukie. 山姆点点头，发出像笑声一样的咔嗒声。 然后它转向苏琪。

The two dolphins chattered to each other for a moment. They nodded at Jack and Annie and swam gracefully away. 两只海豚互相交谈了一会儿。 它们朝杰克和安妮点点头，优雅地游走了。

“Bye, Sukie! Bye, Sam!” Annie shouted. “再见，苏琪！ 再见，山姆！”安妮喊道。

“Thanks!” Jack shouted. “谢谢！”杰克喊道。

The dolphins leapt high into the air. Then they dove back into the water with a SPLASH! 海豚们高高跃入空中。 然后它们啪的一声潜入水中！

Jack and Annie laughed. “I wish we could swim like that,” said Jack. 杰克和安妮笑了。 “真希望我们也能游成那样，”杰克说。

Jack and Annie watched the dolphins until they disappeared. 杰克和安妮看着海豚，直到它们消失。

“I miss them already,” Annie said softly. “我已经开始想它们了，”安妮轻声说。

“Me too,” said Jack. “我也是，”杰克说。

He sat down in the shallow water. 他在浅水里坐下来。

“I’m really tired,” he said. “我真的好累，”他说。

Annie sat beside him. 安妮坐在他旁边。

“Me too,” she said. “我也是，”她说。

The warm water lapped around their shorts and T-shirts. 温暖的海水拍打着他们的短裤和 T 恤。

Jack pulled off his pack. He took out his glasses and put them on. They were blurry with water. 杰克取下背包。 他拿出眼镜戴上。 它们被水弄得模糊不清。

“Guess what,” said Annie. “你猜怎么着，”安妮说。

“What?” said Jack. “什么？”杰克说。

“I saw the shark when we were swimming,” Annie said. “But I didn’t tell you. I wanted you to stay calm.” “游泳的时候我看见鲨鱼了，”安妮说。 “可我没告诉你。 我想让你保持冷静。”

Jack stared at her. “I saw it, too. I just swam faster so you would swim faster.” 杰克盯着她。 “我也看见了。 我只是游得更快，好让你也游得更快。”

“And I swam faster so you would swim faster,” said Annie. “而我游得更快，好让你也游得更快，”安妮说。

“I guess we swam double-fast then,” Jack said. He shook his head with wonder. “那我猜我们游得是加倍地快，”杰克说。 他惊讶地摇了摇头。

“What now?” said Annie. “现在怎么办？”安妮说。

“We go home,” said Jack. “我们回家，”杰克说。

“But we haven’t solved Morgan’s riddle yet,” said Annie. “可我们还没解开摩根的谜语，”安妮说。

Jack sighed. 杰克叹了口气。

He pulled his notebook out of his pack. It was soaked. 他从背包里抽出笔记本。它已经湿透了。

He pulled out the ocean book. It was soaked, too. 他拿出海洋书。 它也湿透了。

“We’ve failed,” he said. “My research is all wet. We’ll never be Master Librarians now.” “我们失败了，”他说。 “我的研究全湿了。 我们永远成不了图书大师了。”

Jack put everything away. “Let’s go,” he said sadly. 杰克把所有东西收好。 “走吧，”他伤感地说。

He stood up. Then he started across the pink reef toward the tree house. Annie followed him. 他站起来。 然后他开始穿过粉色的珊瑚礁，朝树屋走去。 安妮跟着他。

“Ouch!” Annie said. “哎哟！”安妮说。

“What’s wrong?” Jack looked back. “怎么了？”杰克回头看。

“I stepped on something.” Annie bent down to rub her foot. “我踩到什么了。”安妮弯下腰揉脚。

“What?” said Jack. “A shell?” “什么？”杰克说。 “一个贝壳？”

“Yeah, this … ” She picked up a large gray shell. “ Boy, is it rough. Rough and gray as a rock—” “是啊，这个……”她捡起一个大大的灰色贝壳。 粗糙灰暗像块石头——”

“And plain as plain can be!” whispered Jack. They had found the answer. “而且普通得不能再普通！”杰克低声说。 他们找到了答案。

The shell looked like a clamshell—only bigger and with more ridges. 这贝壳看起来像蛤壳——只是更大，棱也更多。

“How could this ugly shell be the answer to the riddle?” said Annie. “What about the part that says, ‘There’s great beauty in me’?” “这么丑的贝壳怎么会是谜语的答案？”安妮说。 我的里面藏着美丽。

“Wait—research,” said Jack. He opened the soaked ocean book. “等等——研究一下，”杰克说。 他打开湿透的海洋书。

The pages were stuck together. But he was able to turn a few. 书页粘在一起了。 可他还是翻开了几页。

He found a picture of the gray shell. He read: 他找到那个灰色贝壳的图。他读道：

Divers search for oysters in deep water. But sometimes oysters wash up on reefs or beaches. Inside some oysters you can find a pearl. The pearl’s natural beauty makes it a treasure. 潜水员在深水中寻找牡蛎。但有时牡蛎会漂到珊瑚礁或海滩上。有些牡蛎里面可以找到珍珠。珍珠的天然美丽让它成为一件珍宝。

“It must have a pearl inside it!” said Jack. “它里面一定有一颗珍珠！”杰克说。

Annie peered into the crack between the two halves of the shell. “I can’t see anything,” she said. “How does a pearl get in there, anyway?” 安妮朝两半贝壳之间的缝里看。 “我什么也看不见，”她说。 “珍珠到底是怎么进去的？”

Jack read aloud from the wet page: 杰克从湿湿的书页上大声读道：

Sometimes a grain of sand will get between the oyster’s shell and its skin. This irritates the oyster. So it makes a pearly material to surround the grain of sand. In this way, over a few years, a pearl is formed. 有时候，一粒沙子会卡在牡蛎的壳和皮肤之间。这会让牡蛎感到不舒服。于是，它会分泌出一种珍珠质把沙子包裹起来。这样，经过几年时间，一颗珍珠就形成了。

“I can’t tell if there’s a pearl in there or not,” said Annie. “我看不出里面有没有珍珠，”安妮说。

“Maybe we should bang it against a rock,” said Jack. “也许我们应该把它往石头上砸，”杰克说。

“Now that would reallyirritate the oyster,” said Annie. “那样可真会惹恼牡蛎，”安妮说。

“Yeah.” “是啊。”

“Maybe we should just leave it alone,” said Annie. “也许我们该别动它，”安妮说。

She gently put the oyster back in the water. 她轻轻把牡蛎放回水里。

“But how will we know if oyster is the right answer to the riddle?” said Jack. “可我们怎么知道牡蛎是不是谜语的正确答案？”杰克说。

“Morgan said we’ll know,” said Annie. “Come on.” “摩根说过我们会知道的，”安妮说。 “来吧。”

Jack pushed his glasses into place. Then he and Annie picked up their shoes and socks. 杰克把眼镜往上推了推。 然后他和安妮拿起鞋子和袜子。

They climbed through the window of the tree house. 他们爬进树屋的窗户。

Morgan’s scroll was lying on the floor. 摩根的卷轴躺在地板上。

It was open. 它是打开的。

“Look!” said Annie. “看！”安妮说。

She and Jack stared at the scroll. The riddle had faded away. 她和杰克盯着卷轴。 谜语已经渐渐消失了。

In its place was one shimmering silver word: 原来的地方变成了一个闪闪发亮的银色单词：

OYSTER 牡蛎

“Morgan’s magic,” whispered Annie. “摩根的魔法，”安妮低声说。

Jack let out a huge sigh. “We got it right,” he said. 杰克大大地松了一口气。 “我们答对了，”他说。

“And here’s the Pennsylvania book,” said Annie. “Let’s go home.” “宾夕法尼亚那本书也在这儿，”安妮说。 “我们回家吧。”

She opened the book. She pointed to a picture of the Frog Creek woods. 她打开书。 她指着蛙溪树林的图画。

“I wish we could go there!” she said. “我希望我们能去那儿！”她说。

The wind started to blow. 风开始刮起来。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

The wind blew harder and harder. 风刮得越来越猛。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静了下来。

Absolutely still. 完全静止。
