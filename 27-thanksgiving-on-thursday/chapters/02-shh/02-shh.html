2. Shh! 2. 嘘！

Jack opened his eyes. Bright, golden sunlight poured into the tree house. The air felt crisp and cool. 杰克睁开眼睛。 明亮的金色阳光倾泻进树屋。 空气清爽而凉爽。

Annie was wearing a long dress, a white cap, and an apron. 安妮穿着长裙，戴着白帽子，还围着围裙。

Jack wore a jacket with a frilly collar. He wore short pants, long socks, leather shoes, and a hat. His backpack was now a leather bag. 杰克穿着一件有褶边衣领的夹克。 他穿着短裤、长袜、皮鞋，还戴着帽子。 他的背包变成了皮袋子。

“I like your hat,” said Annie. “It’s funny.” “我喜欢你的帽子，”安妮说。 “挺好笑的。”

“Yours, too,” said Jack. “你的也是，”杰克说。

“You look like a Pilgrim,” said Annie. “你看起来像个清教徒，”安妮说。

“So do you,” said Jack. “Oh, man. I bet we’re in the time of the Pilgrims!” “你也是，”杰克说。 “天啊。 我敢打赌，我们到了清教徒的时代！”

He and Annie scrambled to the window. 他和安妮赶紧爬到窗边。

The tree house had landed in a tall oak near the edge of a forest. Red and yellow leaves rattled in the cool breeze. Past the forest was a small village and past the village was the ocean. 树屋落在森林边缘一棵高大的橡树上。 红叶和黄叶在凉爽的微风中沙沙作响。 森林过去是一个小村庄，村庄过去是大海。

“It looks like where the Pilgrims lived,” said Jack. “We studied it in school.” “看起来像清教徒住过的地方，”杰克说。 “我们在学校学过。”

He opened the research book and found a picture of the village by the sea. He read aloud: 他打开研究书，找到一幅海边村庄的图画。他大声读道：

In 1620, a group of 102 passengers sailed from England to America on a ship called the Mayflower. Many of the people on board wanted freedom of religion. They wanted to worship God in their own way—not the way the king of England made them. Others wanted to find a new life in a new land. Today, we call all the people who sailed on the Mayflower Pilgrims. 1620年，有一群102名乘客乘坐一艘叫五月花号的船，从英国出发前往美洲。船上的许多人想要宗教自由，他们想用自己的方式来敬拜上帝，而不是按照英国国王规定的方式。还有些人是想在新的土地上寻找新的生活。如今，我们把所有乘坐五月花号船的人称为朝圣者。

“Yes!” said Annie. “对！”安妮说。

Jack read on: 杰克继续读：

The Pilgrims wanted to settle near New York. But a storm blew their ship north. They landed in a bay on the coast of what is now Massachusetts. Six years before, Captain John Smith had explored the coast. He had named the bay Plymouth. 朝圣者们想在纽约附近定居。但一场风暴把他们的船吹向了北方。他们在一个现在属于马萨诸塞州海岸的海湾登陆。六年前，约翰·史密斯船长曾探索过这片海岸。他给这个海湾取名叫普利茅斯。

“Plymouth?” said Annie. “That’s where the first Thanksgiving was!” “普利茅斯？”安妮说。 “那就是第一个感恩节的地方！”

“Oh, man … ” Jack smiled. “So that’s the feast.” “天啊……”杰克笑了。 “原来宴会就是这个。”

“Wow,” said Annie. “My class put on a play about the first Thanksgiving.” “哇，”安妮说。 “我们班还演过关于第一个感恩节的话剧。”

“Mine, too,” said Jack. “我们也演过，”杰克说。

“I played Priscilla,” said Annie. “我演的是普丽西拉，”安妮说。

“I played a turkey,” said Jack. “我演过一只火鸡，”杰克说。

“Now we’ll get to meet the real Priscilla!” said Annie. “And Squanto! And Governor Bradford and Miles Standish! Come on!” “现在我们能见到真正的普丽西拉了！”安妮说。 “还有斯匡托！ 还有总督布拉德福德和迈尔斯·斯坦迪什！来吧！”

She started down the ladder. 她开始顺着梯子往下爬。

“Wait. What will we say?” asked Jack. “等等。 我们说什么呢？”杰克问。

“We’ll just tell them hi and stuff,” said Annie. “我们只要跟他们打个招呼之类的就行，”安妮说。

“Are you nuts?” said Jack. He put the book into his bag. “They won’t understand who we are! We need a plan.” “你疯了吗？”杰克说。 他把书放进袋子。 “他们不会明白我们是谁的！我们需要一个计划。”

He slung the bag over his shoulder and hurried down the ladder after Annie. 他把袋子挎上肩，赶紧顺着梯子追下安妮。

“Listen, we need—” Jack started. “听着，我们需要——”杰克开口说。

“I know, a plan,” said Annie. “But first let’s get closer to the village and just watch.” “我知道，一个计划，”安妮说。 “不过先靠近村子，只是观察就好。”

“Okay,” said Jack, “but we can’t let anyone see us. We have to be careful and quiet.” “好，”杰克说，“可我们不能让人看见我们。 我们得小心，还得安静。”

He and Annie started walking carefully through the woods. But they did not walk quietly. The autumn leaves crunched and crackled under their leather shoes. 他和安妮小心翼翼地穿过树林。 可他们走得并不安静。 秋叶在他们的皮鞋底下嘎吱作响。

“Shh!” said Jack. “嘘！”杰克说。

“I can’t help it,” said Annie. “You’re doing it, too!” “我控制不住，”安妮说。 “你也在响啊！”

“Then we have to stop,” said Jack. “Let’s get behind that tree and watch from there.” “那我们就得停下来，”杰克说。 “躲到那棵树后面，从那儿观察。”

They crunched over to a tree at the edge of the woods. In the distance was a row of small log houses with steep thatched roofs. 他们踩着落叶走到树林边缘的一棵树旁。 远处是一排小木屋，屋顶铺着陡峭的茅草。

Jack pulled out the book. He found the part about the village. Then he pushed his glasses up and read to himself: 杰克抽出书。 他找到关于村子的那部分。 然后他把眼镜往上推了推，自己读了起来：

The Pilgrims brought chickens, geese, goats, and sheep from England. They brought seeds to plant, and they knew how to make traps to catch wild animals for food. But they could not have survived without the help of a Wampanoag (wom-puh-NO-ag) Indian named Squanto. Squanto taught them how to grow corn. 朝圣者从英格兰带来了鸡、鹅、山羊和绵羊。他们带来了种子用来种植，还知道如何制作陷阱来捕捉野生动物作为食物。但是如果没有一位名叫斯夸托的瓦姆帕诺亚格（wom-puh-NO-ag）印第安人的帮助，他们就无法生存下来。斯夸托教他们如何种植玉米。

“Hi, you,” Annie whispered. Jack looked up. “嗨，你呀，”安妮小声说。杰克抬起头。

Annie was talking to a skinny yellow dog. The dog was sniffing a tree near them. 安妮正在跟一只瘦瘦的黄狗说话。 那只狗正在嗅他们附近的一棵树。

“Don’t let him see us,” Jack whispered. “别让它看见我们，”杰克小声说。

“Why?” said Annie. “为什么？”安妮说。

The dog looked at them and barked. 狗看着他们，叫了起来。

“That’s why!” said Jack. “就是因为这个！”杰克说。

The skinny dog barked again and again. 那只瘦瘦的狗一遍又一遍地叫着。

Two Pilgrim men ran from the other side of the houses. Then more Pilgrims appeared. They all looked in the direction of the barking dog. 两个清教徒男人从房子另一边跑了过来。 接着又出现了更多清教徒。 他们都朝狗叫的方向看去。

“Oh, no!” said Jack. “Let’s go back! We don’t have a plan yet!” “糟了！”杰克说。 “我们回去吧！ 我们还没有计划呢！”

He packed up his book and started away from the tree. Suddenly something tightened around his ankle. A tree branch snapped. 他收起书，开始离开那棵树。 忽然，什么东西勒紧了他的脚踝。 一根树枝啪地断了。

“AHHH!” Jack shouted as he was jerked up into the air. “啊啊啊！”杰克被猛地拽到空中，大叫起来。
