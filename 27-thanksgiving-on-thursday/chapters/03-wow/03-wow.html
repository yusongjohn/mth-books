3. Wow? 3. 哇？

“Jack!” cried Annie. “杰克！”安妮喊道。

The skinny dog barked and jumped around happily. 那只瘦狗快活地叫着，绕着跳来跳去。

Jack was hanging a few feet off the ground, with a rope around his ankle. His glasses and hat and bag had fallen to the ground. Jack felt the blood rushing to his head. 杰克倒挂着，离地几英尺，脚踝上绕着绳子。 他的眼镜、帽子和袋子都掉到了地上。 杰克觉得血往脑子里涌。

“I must have stepped into a hunting trap,” he said in a strangled voice. “我一定是踩进了捕猎陷阱，”他用哽住的声音说。

“I’ll free you,” Annie said. She tried to reach the rope, but it was too high. “我来救你，”安妮说。 她想去够绳子，可太高了。

Jack heard voices over the wild barking of the dog. A blur of people gathered around him and Annie. 杰克在狗狂吠声中听到了人声。 一群人影模糊地围到了他和安妮周围。

“Oh, mercy!” a woman cried. “哦，天哪！”一个女人叫道。

“We have caught a boy!” a man said. “我们抓住了一个男孩！”一个男人说。

The dog licked Jack’s face. 那只狗舔了舔杰克的脸。

“Help,” said Jack. “救命，”杰克说。

A burly man shooed the dog away, then grabbed Jack. Another cut the rope with a knife. Then they gently lowered Jack to the ground. 一个高大强壮的男人把狗嘘走，然后抓住了杰克。 另一个人用刀割断了绳子。 然后他们轻轻把杰克放到地上。

Jack sat in the leaves, feeling dizzy. He took the rope off his foot and rubbed his ankle. 杰克坐在落叶里，觉得头晕。 他解下脚上的绳子，揉了揉脚踝。

“Here,” said Annie, handing Jack his glasses, hat, and bag. “给你，”安妮说着，把眼镜、帽子和袋子递给杰克。

He put them all on and stood up. Now he could see. About forty or fifty Pilgrims—men, women, and lots of children—stared at him and Annie. Some of the children were laughing. 他把它们都戴上、拿好，站了起来。现在他能看清了。 大约四五十个清教徒——男人、女人，还有很多孩子——盯着他和安妮。 有些孩子在笑。

The girls were dressed just like the women. The boys were dressed just like the men. 女孩们穿得跟女人们一模一样。 男孩们穿得跟男人们一模一样。

One person, though, looked different from everyone else in the crowd. His skin was brown. A deer skin hung over his shoulder. His black hair was braided and had a feather in it. 不过，有一个人看上去跟人群里的其他人都不一样。 他的皮肤是棕色的。 一张鹿皮搭在他肩上。 他的黑头发编成辫子，上面插着一根羽毛。

Is that Squanto? Jack wondered. The Wampanoag Indian who helped out the Pilgrims? 那是斯匡托吗？杰克想。 那个帮助清教徒的万帕诺亚格印第安人？

Two Pilgrim men stepped forward. One had a smile on his face. The other was frowning. 两个清教徒男人走上前来。 一个脸上带着笑。 另一个皱着眉。

“Good day!” the friendly-looking man said. “Who art thou?” “日安！”那个看起来很友善的人说。 “尔等何人？”

“I’m Annie,” said Annie. “This is my brother, Jack. We come in peace.” “我是安妮，”安妮说。 “这是我哥哥杰克。 我们是来讲和的。”

“Welcome to Plymouth Colony,” said the man. “I am Governor Bradford. This is Captain Standish.” “欢迎来到普利茅斯殖民地，”那人说。 “我是总督布拉德福德。 这位是斯坦迪什船长。”

Captain Standish kept frowning. He carried a long gun over his shoulder. 斯坦迪什船长仍皱着眉。 他肩上扛着一支长枪。

“Oh, wow!” said Annie. “哦，哇！”安妮说。

“Wow?” said Captain Standish. “哇？”斯坦迪什船长说。

“Wow?” whispered others, as if they didn’t understand. “哇？”其他人低声重复，好像听不懂。

“I’ve just heard a lot about you,” said Annie. She looked around. “Is Priscilla here?” “我刚听说过好多关于你们的事，”安妮说。 她环顾四周。 “普丽西拉在这儿吗？”

“Shhh!” whispered Jack. “嘘！”杰克小声说。

“I am Priscilla,” said a young woman. She looked about seventeen or eighteen. Her face looked weary, and her eyes were sad. “我就是普丽西拉，”一位年轻女子说。 她看上去大概十七八岁。 她的脸看上去很疲倦，眼睛里带着悲伤。

“Hi,” said Annie shyly. “I was you.” “嗨，”安妮羞怯地说。 “我演过你。”

“Annie,” warned Jack. “安妮，”杰克警告道。

“Thou was me?” Priscilla asked. She sounded puzzled. “你演过我？”普丽西拉问。 她听起来很困惑。

“Never mind my sister,” said Jack. “She’s nuts.” “别管我妹妹，”杰克说。 “她有点疯。”

“Nuts?” repeated Priscilla. “疯？”普丽西拉重复道。

“Nuts?” whispered others. “疯？”其他人低声说。

“Oh, brother,” said Jack, with a nervous laugh. “哦，天哪，”杰克紧张地笑了笑。

“Oh, brother?” repeated Priscilla. “哦，天哪？”普丽西拉重复道。

Annie giggled. 安妮咯咯地笑了。

“Um. Never mind,” said Jack. “That’s just how we say things at home.” 算了，”杰克说。 “我们在家就这么说话。”

“And where is thy home?” Captain Standish asked. He didn’t sound as friendly as Governor Bradford or Priscilla. “那你们家在何处？”斯坦迪什船长问。 他听起来不像总督布拉德福德或普丽西拉那么友好。

“Um, we live in a village up north,” said Jack. “Our parents sent us here to, uh”—he remembered something from their research book—“to learn how to grow corn.” “嗯，我们住在北边的一个村子里，”杰克说。 “我们父母送我们来这儿，呃”——他想起研究书里的内容——“来学种玉米。”

“But how and when did your family come to America?” the captain asked. “可你们一家是怎样、又是何时来到美洲的？”船长问。

Jack was worried. Now that he had started making up a story, he couldn’t back out. Luckily, he remembered something else from their book. 杰克担心起来。 既然他已经开始编故事了，就不能反悔了。 幸好，他还记得书上的另一件事。

“We sailed to America with Captain John Smith,” he said, “when he was exploring the coast. Annie and I were babies then.” “我们是跟着约翰·史密斯船长航行到美洲的，”他说，“那时他在沿海探险。 安妮和我那时还是婴儿。”

“Ah, indeed?” said Governor Bradford. “啊，当真？”总督布拉德福德说。

Jack nodded. “Indeed,” he said. 杰克点点头。 “当真，”他说。

“I believe Squanto knew Captain John Smith when he was in Plymouth,” said Captain Standish. “Perhaps he remembers thee.” “我相信斯匡托在普利茅斯时认识约翰·史密斯船长，”斯坦迪什船长说。 “或许他还记得你们。”

Everyone in the crowd turned to the man with the braid. 人群都转向那位留辫子的男人。

Oh, no! thought Jack. He knew Squanto wouldn’t remember them. 糟了！杰克想。 他知道斯匡托不会记得他们。

“These children say they sailed with Captain John Smith,” Governor Bradford said to Squanto. “Does thou remember two wee babes named Jack and Annie?” “这些孩子说他们曾与约翰·史密斯船长同行，”总督布拉德福德对斯匡托说。 “你可记得两个叫杰克和安妮的小孩子？”

Squanto moved closer to Jack and Annie. He looked carefully at their faces. Jack held his breath. His heart pounded. 斯匡托走近杰克和安妮。 他仔细看着他们的脸。 杰克屏住呼吸。 他的心怦怦直跳。

Squanto turned to the governor. 斯匡托转身朝向总督。

“Yes,” he said quietly. “I remember.” “是的，”他轻声说。 “我记得。”
