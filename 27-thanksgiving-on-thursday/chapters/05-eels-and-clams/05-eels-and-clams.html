5. Eels and Clams 5. 鳗鱼和蛤蜊

Jack looked at Annie. 杰克看着安妮。

“We can’t stay here,” he said. “我们不能待在这儿，”他说。

“What?” she said. “We can’t go home now. The Pilgrims need us to help them.” “什么？”她说。 “我们现在不能回家。 清教徒需要我们帮忙。”

“But we don’t know how to do anything!” Jack said. “And Squanto is going to figure out he doesn’t really know us. And—” “可我们什么都不会做！”杰克说。 “而且斯匡托马上就会弄明白，他其实并不认识我们。还有——”

“Don’t worry so much,” said Annie. “We help Mom and Dad make our Thanksgiving dinner every year, don’t we? We can help the Pilgrims. But we’d better hurry!” “别那么担心，”安妮说。 “我们每年都帮爸爸妈妈做感恩节晚餐，不是吗？ 我们也能帮清教徒。 不过我们得快点！”

Clutching the big basket, she started running toward the bay. Jack sighed, then ran after her. 她抱紧大篮子，朝海湾跑去。 杰克叹了口气，然后追了上去。

At the rocky shore, they stopped and looked around. Little waves rolled onto the short stretch of sand. The salty air felt clean and fresh. Seagulls swooped over the water. 在布满岩石的岸边，他们停下来四处张望。 小小的波浪卷上那一小段沙滩。 带咸味的空气清新又凉爽。 海鸥掠过水面。

“I wonder where the eels are?” said Annie. “And the clams?” “不知道鳗鱼在哪儿？”安妮说。 “还有蛤蜊呢？”

“I’ll look in the book,” said Jack. “我查查书，”杰克说。

He pulled out their book and looked up eels in the index. He turned to the right page and read aloud: 他抽出书，在索引里查找鳗鱼。 他翻到正确的那一页，大声读道：

Squanto showed the Pilgrims a way to catch eels. He showed them how to push the eels out of the wet sand with their bare feet, then grab them with their hands. 萨昆托教朝圣者如何抓鳗鱼。他教他们用光脚把鳗鱼从湿沙子里踩出来，然后用手抓住它们。

“That sounds fun!” said Annie. She put down her basket and pulled off her shoes and stockings. She held up her long skirt with one hand. Then she walked over the rocks to the edge of the water. “听起来好玩！”安妮说。 她放下篮子，脱掉鞋子和长袜。 她一只手提起长裙。 然后她踩着岩石走到水边。

Jack put the research book into his bag. He pulled off his shoes and stockings and joined Annie. 杰克把研究书放进袋子。 他脱掉鞋子和长袜，走到安妮身边。

They dug their bare feet into the wet sand. 他们把光脚插进湿沙里。

“I don’t feel anything,” said Jack. “我什么都感觉不到，”杰克说。

“Let’s wade into the water,” said Annie. “我们蹚进水里吧，”安妮说。

Together they stepped forward. 他们一起往前走。

“Brrr!” said Annie. “嘶——冷！”安妮说。

“No kidding!” said Jack with a shiver. “可不是嘛，”杰克说。

He kept squishing the muddy sand with his toes. He felt pebbles and shells. Then he felt something soft. 他继续用脚趾挤压泥沙。 他感到了小石子和贝壳。 然后他摸到了什么软软的东西。

“Hey, I think I found one,” he said. “嘿，我觉得我找到一条了，”他说。

Annie splashed over to him. “Where?” 安妮溅着水过来。 “哪儿？”

“Stand back,” he said. “Here.” “往后站，”他说。 “这儿。”

Jack squished harder with his feet. The soft thing moved! Jack squished more. An eel slithered through the water. 杰克用脚更用力地挤压。 那软软的东西动了！ 杰克又踩了踩。 一条鳗鱼在水里蠕动着游过。

Jack grabbed it with both hands! 杰克两只手一把抓住！

“AHH!” he yelled. “啊！”他大叫。

The eel was long and skinny like a snake. It felt slimy and icky! It twisted and squirmed. Annie laughed as Jack tried to hold on to it. 鳗鱼又长又细，像条蛇。 摸上去又黏又恶心！ 它扭来扭去，不停地动。 安妮笑着看杰克使劲抓住它。

The eel wiggled out of Jack’s hands and fell against Annie. 鳗鱼从杰克手里滑脱，砸到安妮身上。

“Yikes!” she yelled, jumping away and bumping into Jack. “哎呀！”她大叫着跳开，撞到了杰克。

With more screams, they both tumbled into the cold water. 又是一阵尖叫，他俩都摔进了冰冷的水里。

They scrambled up and splashed back to shore. Annie was still laughing. 他们手脚并用爬起来，溅着水跑回岸边。 安妮还在笑。

“Poor eel!” she said, trying to catch her breath. “We scared him half to death!” “可怜的鳗鱼！”她喘着气说。 “我们把他吓得半死！”

“Him?” said Jack. “它？”杰克说。

“F-forget eels,” said Annie, her teeth chattering. “What about c-clams?” “忘——忘掉鳗鱼吧，”安妮说，牙齿打着战。 “那蛤——蛤蜊呢？”

Jack was wet and cold. But he took out the book again and looked up clams. He turned to the right page and read aloud: 杰克又湿又冷。 可他还是又拿出书，查找蛤蜊。 他翻到正确的那一页，大声读道：

Squanto taught the Pilgrims how to dig for quahog (KO-hog) clams. Quahog clams are hard-shell clams. They can live for sixty years or more. The oldest have been known to live for almost 100 years. They— 萨昆托教朝圣者如何挖掘蛤蜊（KO-hog）。蛤蜊是硬壳蛤蜊。它们可以活六十岁或更久。最老的蛤蜊甚至能活到几乎一百岁。它们—

“Oh, forget it,” Annie broke in. “哦，算了吧，”安妮打断道。

“What?” said Jack. “什么？”杰克说。

“We can’t catch them,” she said. “They live to be so old. We can’t just end their lives.” “我们不能抓它们，”她说。 “它们能活那么久。 我们不能就这么结束它们的生命。”

Jack sighed. He sat down on a rock. Annie sat next to him. Their clothes were soaking wet. Their feet were caked with muddy sand. Their basket was empty. 杰克叹了口气。 他在一块岩石上坐下。 安妮坐到他旁边。 他们的衣服湿透了。 他们的脚沾满了泥沙。 他们的篮子空空的。

“What other things do Pilgrim kids do to help?” said Annie. “清教徒小孩还帮做什么别的事？”安妮说。

Jack opened the book again. He looked up Pilgrim children. He read aloud: 杰克又打开书。 他查找清教徒儿童。他大声读道：

Pilgrim children worked very hard. They built fences and cared for animals. They planted, harvested, and ground corn. They picked pumpkins, peas, and beans. They guarded the fields. They fished and hunted. They carried water. They collected nuts. They cooked and cleaned. They did everything they were told. They never complained about being tired. 朝圣者的孩子们非常努力工作。他们建围栏，照顾动物。他们种植、收割并磨玉米。他们摘南瓜、豌豆和豆子。他们守卫田地。他们钓鱼、打猎。他们挑水。他们收集坚果。他们做饭、打扫。他们被吩咐做什么就做什么。他们从不抱怨累。

“Oh, man, I feel tired just reading this,” said Jack, closing the book. “We make lousy Pilgrim children.” “天啊，光读这个我就觉得累了，”杰克说着合上书。 “我们真是糟糕的清教徒小孩。”

“Yeah, I know,” said Annie. “Maybe we could do something like … like keep an eye on the turkey and tell them when it’s ready. That’s how I help Mom every year.” “是啊，我知道，”安妮说。 “也许我们可以做点事，比如……照看一下火鸡，好了就告诉他们。 我每年就是这样帮妈妈的。”

“Annie, Thanksgiving in Frog Creek is a whole different story from Thanksgiving with the Pilgrims,” said Jack. “安妮，蛙溪的感恩节跟清教徒的感恩节完全是两回事，”杰克说。

“Annie! Jack!” a voice called. 杰克！”一个声音喊道。

Jack quickly put away their book. Then they turned around. 杰克赶紧收起书。 然后他们转过身。

Priscilla was standing on a rock. She held a pumpkin and carried a basket filled with yellow squash and red corn. 普丽西拉正站在一块岩石上。 她抱着一个南瓜，还提着一篮黄色的瓜和红色的玉米。

“I was looking for you,” she said. “我在找你们，”她说。
