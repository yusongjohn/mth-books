9. Good Day 9. 日安

Slowly the feast came to an end. The guests wiped their plates with their last bits of bread. Then they wiped their hands and faces with their napkins. 盛宴慢慢结束了。 宾客们用最后一点面包擦干净盘子。 然后他们用餐巾擦手擦脸。

Jack and Annie stood up. 杰克和安妮站了起来。

“We have to go home,” Annie said to Priscilla. “我们得回家了，”安妮对普丽西拉说。

“Ah, thou must go back to thine own community now,” said Priscilla. “啊，你们现在得回到自己的共同体了，”普丽西拉说。

Annie nodded. Then she kissed Priscilla on the cheek. 安妮点点头。 然后她亲了亲普丽西拉的脸颊。

“Thanks for everything,” Annie said. “谢谢你所做的一切，”安妮说。

Jack wanted to kiss Priscilla, too, but he was too shy. 杰克也想亲普丽西拉，可他太害羞了。

“Thanks, Priscilla,” he said. “谢谢你，普丽西拉，”他说。

“I thank thee, Jack,” she said. Then she leaned over and kissed his cheek. “我也谢谢你，杰克，”她说。 然后她探过身，吻了吻他的脸颊。

Jack felt his face grow red. 杰克觉得脸红了。

“Excuse me, sir,” Annie said to Governor Bradford. “But we must leave now.” “对不起，先生，”安妮对总督布拉德福德说。 “可我们现在必须走了。”

“Oh, but we have not yet taught thee how to grow corn!” said the little girl Mary. “哦，可我们还没教你们怎么种玉米呢！”小女孩玛丽说。

Squanto stood up. 斯匡托站了起来。

“Come,” he said. “I will walk Jack and Annie back to the forest. I will teach them.” “来，”他说。 “我送杰克和安妮回森林。 我会教他们。”

“Oh, thou does not have to do that,” Jack said quickly. He feared that once they were alone, Squanto would figure out they’d never met before. “哦，您不必那样做，”杰克赶紧说。 他担心一旦只剩他们，斯匡托就会发现他们以前从未见过面。

But Squanto only smiled and waited for them to follow. 可斯匡托只是微笑着，等他们跟上。

“Bye, everyone!” said Annie, waving. “再见，大家！”安妮挥着手说。

Jack waved, too. All the Pilgrims and Wampanoag waved back at them. The skinny dog barked. 杰克也挥了挥手。 所有清教徒和万帕诺亚格人都向他们挥手。 那只瘦瘦的狗一遍又一遍地叫着。

Squanto led Jack and Annie away from the village toward the autumn woods. As they passed the cornfield, the dried stalks swayed in the breeze. They made shushing sounds. 斯匡托领着杰克和安妮离开村子，走向秋天的树林。 他们走过玉米地时，干枯的茎秆在微风中摇晃。 他们发出沙沙的声音。

Squanto stopped walking. He pointed to the field. 斯匡托停下脚步。 他指向田地。

“You must plant corn in the spring,” he said. “Put the seed in the ground when the oak-tree bud is as small as a mouse’s ear.” “你们必须在春天种玉米，”他说。 “等橡树的芽长到像老鼠耳朵那么小时，就把种子埋进土里。”

“Oh, wait, please,” said Jack. He slipped his notebook and pencil out of his bag. It was the first time he’d had a chance to take notes all day. He wrote: “哦，请等一下，”杰克说。 他从袋子里掏出笔记本和铅笔。 这是他一整天第一次有机会做笔记。他写道：

图中文字：How to Plant Corn:

oak-tree bud = mouse's ear 图中文字：如何种植玉米：橡树芽等于老鼠耳朵

Then he looked up at Squanto and nodded. 然后他抬头看着斯匡托，点了点头。

“Dig holes and put two rotting fish in each hole,” said Squanto. “挖些坑，每个坑里放两条腐烂的鱼，”斯匡托说。

“Rotting fish?” said Annie, making a face. “腐烂的鱼？”安妮做了个鬼脸说。

“Yes, rotting fish is good food for the soil,” said Squanto. “On top of the fish, place four corn seeds. Then cover them with dirt.” “是的，腐烂的鱼对土壤是好养料，”斯匡托说。 “在鱼上面放四粒玉米种子。 然后用土盖住它们。”

Jack quickly wrote: 杰克赶紧写下：

图中文字：2 rotting fish, 4 corn seeds

Cover with dirt. 图中文字：2条腐烂的鱼，4粒玉米种子，用泥土盖上。

“Got it,” he said, looking up. “记下了，”他抬起头说。

“I give you these corn seeds to take home,” said Squanto. He held up a small pouch. “我给你们这些玉米种子带回家，”斯匡托说。 他举起一个小袋子。

“Thanks,” said Annie, taking the pouch. “谢谢，”安妮接过袋子说。

“Thanks a lot,” said Jack. “Well, good-bye.” Jack was eager to get going—before Squanto could ask them questions about the past. “太谢谢了，”杰克说。 “好吧，再见。”杰克急着想走——免得斯匡托问起他们过去的事。

“Wait, I have a question,” said Annie. “Squanto, why did you say you remembered us?” “等等，我有个问题，”安妮说。 “斯匡托，你为什么说你记得我们？”

Squanto’s dark eyes twinkled. “I did not say I remembered you,” he said. “I only said I remember.” 斯匡托深色的眼睛闪着光。 “我没有说我记得你们，”他说。 “我只说我记得。”

“What did you remember?” asked Annie. “你记得什么？”安妮问。

“I remembered what it was like to be from a different world,” said Squanto. “Long ago, I lived with my people on this shore. But one day, men came in ships. They took me to Europe as a slave. In that new land, I was a stranger. I felt different and afraid. I saw the same fear in your eyes today. So I tried to help you.” “我记得来自另一个世界是什么感觉，”斯匡托说。 “很久以前，我和我的族人住在这片海岸。 可有一天，人们乘船来了。 他们把我当奴隶带到了欧洲。 在那片新土地上，我是个陌生人。 我感到与众不同，也很害怕。 今天我在你眼里看见了同样的害怕。 所以我想帮助你们。”

Annie smiled. “We thank thee,” she said. 安妮笑了。 “我们谢谢您，”她说。

“And now you must always be kind to those who feel different and afraid,” said Squanto. “Remember what you felt today.” “现在你们必须永远善待那些感到与众不同和害怕的人，”斯匡托说。 “记住你们今天的感受。”

“Indeed,” said Jack. “好的，”杰克说。

Before closing his notebook, he added one last thing: 合上笔记本之前，他又添上了最后一件事：

图中文字：Be kind to those who feel

different and afraid. 图中文字：对那些感到不同和害怕的人要善良。

Squanto bowed. 斯匡托鞠了一躬。

“Good day, Jack and Annie,” he said. “日安，杰克和安妮，”他说。

“Good day!” they said. “日安！”他们说。

Squanto turned and headed back to the village. The sun was setting. All of Plymouth was lit with a fiery light. 斯匡托转身朝村子走去。 太阳正在落下。 整个普利茅斯都被火焰般的光亮照着。

“It really was a good day,” said Annie. “真的是美好的一天，”安妮说。

“Yeah, it was,” said Jack. “是啊，”杰克说。

Annie sighed. “Ready to go home?” she asked. 安妮叹了口气。 “准备好回家了吗？”她问。

“Indeed,” Jack said. “好的，”杰克说。

They started running through the woods. Their feet crunched through the red and yellow leaves. They scrambled up the rope ladder into the tree house. 他们开始跑过树林。 他们的脚踩过红叶和黄叶，发出嘎吱声。 他们爬上绳梯，进了树屋。

From the distance came the sounds of the Pilgrims singing a hymn and the Wampanoag beating their drums. Annie picked up the Pennsylvania book. She pointed at a picture of the Frog Creek woods. 远处传来清教徒唱赞美诗的声音，还有万帕诺亚格人敲鼓的声音。 安妮拿起宾夕法尼亚那本书。 她指着一幅蛙溪树林的画。

“I wish we could go home!” she said. “但愿我们能回家！”她说。

“Good-bye, Priscilla!” Jack called. “再见，普丽西拉！”杰克喊道。

“Good-bye, Squanto!” said Annie. “Good-bye, everyone!” “再见，斯匡托！”安妮说。 “再见，大家！”

The wind started to blow. 风开始刮起来。

The wind blew harder. 风刮得更猛了。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。
