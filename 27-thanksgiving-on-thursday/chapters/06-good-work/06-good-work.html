6. Good Work 6. 干得好

“Good day, Priscilla!” said Annie. “日安，普丽西拉！”安妮说。

“Good day,” said Priscilla. She walked to them. “Did thou fill the basket with eels and clams?” “日安，”普丽西拉说。 她向他们走来。 “你们可把篮子装满鳗鱼和蛤蜊了？”

“Not really,” said Jack. “其实没有，”杰克说。

“The eel didn’t want to get caught,” said Annie. “And the clams live to be so old! We didn’t think it was right to take their lives.” “鳗鱼不想被抓住，”安妮说。 “还有蛤蜊能活那么久！ 我们觉得不该夺走它们的生命。”

Priscilla laughed. Her sad eyes sparkled. 普丽西拉笑了。 她忧伤的眼睛亮了起来。

“What strange children,” she said. “But you both look wet and cold. Would you like to come to my house and warm up by the fire?” “真是奇怪的孩子，”她说。 “可你们俩又湿又冷。 想不想来我家，在火边暖暖身子？”

“Yes!” said Jack and Annie. “想！”杰克和安妮说。

They washed off their feet and pulled on their shoes and stockings. Jack picked up his bag. Annie picked up their empty basket. 他们洗干净脚，穿上鞋子和长袜。 杰克拿起袋子。 安妮拿起空篮子。

“Would thou like to put some of my corn and squash in thy basket?” said Priscilla. “你们可愿把我的一些玉米和南瓜放进篮子里？”普丽西拉说。

“Oh, thanks!” said Annie. She took some corn and squash from Priscilla’s basket. “哦，谢谢！”安妮说。 她从普丽西拉的篮子里拿了些玉米和南瓜。

“And perhaps thou would like to carry the pumpkin?” Priscilla said to Jack. “或许你愿帮我扛这个大南瓜？”普丽西拉对杰克说。

“Sure!” said Jack. “当然！”杰克说。

“Sure?” said Priscilla. “当然？”普丽西拉说。

“I mean, indeed,” said Jack. He felt relieved. Now they wouldn’t have to go back empty-handed. “我是说，确实如此，”杰克说。他松了一口气。 现在他们不用空手回去了。

Jack wrapped his arms around the heavy pumpkin. Annie carried the basket. They followed Priscilla back to the village. 杰克双臂抱住沉重的南瓜。 安妮提着篮子。 他们跟着普丽西拉回到村子。

The Pilgrims and Wampanoag were gathering in a wide dirt street. Women were baking bread in an outdoor oven. Some boys were setting wooden planks on barrels to make tables. Mary, the little girl, was carrying a bucket of water. 清教徒和万帕诺亚格人正聚集在一条宽阔的土街上。 女人们在户外烤箱里烤面包。 一些男孩正把木板架在桶上当桌子。 小女孩玛丽正提着一桶水。

Squanto sat smoking a pipe with Chief Massasoit, Governor Bradford, and Captain Standish. 斯匡托正和马萨索伊特酋长、总督布拉德福德、斯坦迪什船长一起抽烟斗。

Jack hoped Mary wouldn’t ask him about the clams and eels. He hoped Squanto wouldn’t ask him about Captain John Smith. He hoped the governor and the captain wouldn’t ask him about home. Jack hid his face behind the fat pumpkin. 杰克希望玛丽别问他蛤蜊和鳗鱼的事。 他希望斯匡托别问他约翰·史密斯船长的事。 他希望总督和船长别问他家的事。 杰克把脸藏在胖南瓜后面。

Priscilla opened the door to a small house. Then she led Jack and Annie into a dark, smoky room. The only light came from one window and a fire. 普丽西拉推开一间小屋的门。 然后她领着杰克和安妮走进一间又暗又烟的房间。 唯一的光来自一扇窗户和一堆火。

“Sit by the hearth,” said Priscilla, “so your clothes can dry.” “坐在炉边，”普丽西拉说，“好让衣服干干。”

“Where’s the hearth?” Annie asked, looking around. “壁炉在哪儿？”安妮四处看着问。

Priscilla laughed again, shaking her head. “There, where the fire lies,” she said. 普丽西拉又笑了，摇着头。 “那儿，火所在之处，”她说。

Jack put down the pumpkin and his bag. Annie put down her basket. The hearth was so large, Jack could have stood in it. He and Annie got as close as they could to the warm, crackling fire. 杰克放下南瓜和袋子。 安妮放下篮子。 炉膛很大，杰克都能站进去。 他和安妮尽量靠近温暖、噼啪作响的火。

Several pots hung over the fire. Near the pots, a turkey was roasting on an iron rod. 几口锅挂在火上。 罐子旁边，一只火鸡正穿在铁棍上烤着。

“The Thanksgiving turkey,” whispered Annie. “感恩节火鸡，”安妮小声说。

“Cool,” said Jack. The very first Thanksgiving turkey, he thought. “酷，”杰克说。 这是第一只感恩节火鸡，他想。

“Would thou please stir the corn pudding whilst thou art drying?” asked Priscilla. She pointed to one of the pots. “你烘干衣服时，可愿搅搅玉米布丁？”普丽西拉问。 她指着其中一口锅。

“Indeed,” said Jack. “当然，”杰克说。

Priscilla took a wooden spoon out of a jug of water near the hearth. She gave it to Jack. He put it into the thick, bubbly pudding and stirred. 普丽西拉从炉边一罐水里取出一把木勺。 她把它递给杰克。 他把勺子伸进浓稠、冒泡的布丁里搅动。

“I must gather nuts,” said Priscilla. “Whilst I am gone, move the roots close to the ashes and stir herbs into the seafood chowder.” “我得去采坚果，”普丽西拉说。 “我不在时，把根菜移近灰烬，再把香草搅进海鲜浓汤。”

“Indeed,” said Annie. “好的，”安妮说。

After Priscilla left, Annie looked at Jack. 普丽西拉离开后，安妮看着杰克。

“What are ‘roots’ and ‘herbs’?” she asked. “‘根菜’和‘香草’是什么？”她问。

“Look in the book,” said Jack. “查查书，”杰克说。

Annie took the research book from Jack’s bag. She looked up roots and read aloud: 安妮从杰克袋子里拿出研究书。 她查了查“根茎类蔬菜”，大声读出来：

The Pilgrims called certain vegetables roots. These vegetables, such as carrots and turnips, grow under the ground. 朝圣者把某些蔬菜叫做根茎类蔬菜。这些蔬菜，比如胡萝卜和萝卜，是在地下生长的。

“Ah!” said Jack. He picked up some carrots and turnips near the hearth and moved them close to the hot ashes. “啊！”杰克说。 他拿起炉边一些胡萝卜和芜菁，移近热灰烬。

Next, Annie looked up herbs. She read aloud: 接着，安妮查找香草。她大声读道：

The Pilgrims called leafy vegetables that grow above the ground herbs. They made salads with herbs. They used dried herbs to flavor soups and seafood chowders. 朝圣者把长在地上、叶子茂盛的蔬菜叫做香草。他们用香草做沙拉，还用干香草给汤和海鲜浓汤调味。

Jack saw some dried plants hanging from the rafters. 杰克看见一些干植物挂在椽子上。

“Those must be the herbs,” he said. “那些一定是香草，”他说。

Annie broke off a leaf and sniffed it. 安妮掰下一片叶子闻了闻。

“Mmm, that smells good,” she said. She leaned close to one of the pots. “And that must be the seafood chowder. It smells like the ocean.” “嗯，好闻，”她说。 她凑近其中一口锅。 “那一定是海鲜杂烩汤。 闻起来像大海。”

She crumbled the leaf into the chowder. She took another spoon from the jug of water. She and Jack both stirred pots. 她把叶子捏碎，撒进杂烩汤里。 她从水罐里又拿了一把勺子。 她和杰克都在搅锅。

“Good work!” Priscilla said as she stepped back into the room. “干得好！”普丽西拉走进房间时说。

Jack smiled. The fire had made him hot and sweaty. The smoke burned his eyes. But he didn’t mind. Finally he felt useful. 杰克笑了。 火烤得他又热又出汗。 烟熏得他眼睛发疼。 可他不在意。 他终于觉得自己有用了。

Priscilla put some walnuts close to the fire. 普丽西拉把一些核桃放近火边。

“Squanto taught us which nuts are good to eat,” she said. “斯匡托教我们哪些坚果能吃，”她说。

“Squanto taught thee a lot,” said Annie. “斯匡托教了你们很多，”安妮说。

“He saved our lives,” Priscilla said quietly. “Last winter we were cold and hungry. Half our people died.” “他救了我们的命，”普丽西拉轻声说。 “去年冬天我们又冷又饿。 我们一半的人都死了。”

Annie gasped. “How?” she said. 安妮倒抽一口气。 “怎么死的？”她说。

“Sickness,” said Priscilla. “Fever took my mother, my father, and my brother.” Her eyes were bright with tears. “疾病，”普丽西拉说。 “热病夺走了我的母亲、父亲和哥哥。”她眼里闪着泪光。

No one spoke. The sound of the crackling fire filled the room. Then Annie put her arm around Priscilla. 没有人说话。 噼啪的火声充满了房间。 然后安妮搂住普丽西拉。

“We’re so sorry,” said Annie. “我们真难过，”安妮说。

“Yes, we are,” said Jack. “是的，我们是，”杰克说。

“Thank you,” Priscilla said with a sad smile. “ ’ Twas a terrible winter. But we never gave up hope. And now, God be praised, we have had a good harvest, and we have peace with our neighbors.” “谢谢你们，”普丽西拉带着忧伤的笑容说。 “那是个可怕的冬天。 可我们从未放弃希望。 如今，赞美上帝，我们有了好收成，也和邻居们和平相处。”

In the glow of the firelight, Priscilla was beautiful, Jack thought. Not only was she kind, but she was incredibly brave as well. 在火光映照下，普丽西拉很美，杰克想。 她不仅善良，而且勇敢得令人难以置信。

“Come,” she said. She wiped her eyes and stood up. “Something special is about to take place. Would thou like to watch?” “来，”她说。 她擦干眼泪，站了起来。 “有件特别的事马上就要发生。 你们想看吗？”

“Sure! I mean, indeed!” said Annie. “当然！ 我是说，好的！”安妮说。

She and Jack jumped up and followed Priscilla outside. 她和杰克跳起来，跟着普丽西拉走到外面。
