7. The Forest-of-Walking-Trees 7. 走动的树林

Annie gasped, then burst out laughing. 安妮惊讶地张大了嘴，然后忍不住笑出声来。

Heart-of-the-Wind laughed, too. “Yes, it is wonderful news! I am glad it makes you happy,” she said. “When the sun rises tomorrow, we will leave the City of Dawn and start the long journey back to Palenque. It will take us many moons to get there.” 心之风也笑了。 “是的，这是个好消息！ 我很高兴这让你开心，”她说。 “明天太阳升起时，我们将离开黎明之城，开始漫长旅程返回帕伦克。 要花我们很多个月才能到达那里。”

“But—but why me?” asked Jack. “但——为什么是我？”杰克问。

“My father believes you are the answer to his prayers,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “He can see you have many things to teach our people.” “你父亲认为你就是他祈祷的答案，”心之风说。 “他看得出来，你有许多东西可以教我们的人民。”

“No, no I don’t,” said Jack. His words spilled over each other. “I don’t know how to farm, I’m not an astronomer or a pyramid builder, I don’t know anything about your Mayan calendar—” He stopped to catch his breath. “不，不，我不会，”杰克说。 他的话一个接一个地冒出来。 “我不知道怎么种田，我不是天文学家，也不是金字塔建造者，我对你们的玛雅历法一无所知——”他停下来喘了口气。

“My father will teach you these things,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “And you will teach us about the wonders in your—what did you call it?” “你父亲会教你这些的，”Heart-of-the-Wind说。 “而你将教我们关于你们——你刚才叫它什么来着？——的奇迹。”

“Travel guide,” said Annie. “旅行指南，”安妮说。

“Yes,” said Heart-of-the-Wind, “the wonders in your travel guide.” “对，”Heart-of-the-Wind说，“关于你们旅行指南里的奇迹。”

“No, I can’t,” said Jack. “I don’t know how to make any of those things myself, like planes, submarines, skyscrapers, or computers. We live in a world with lots and lots of things, but we have to go to school and study for many years to understand how they work.” “不，我不能，”杰克说。 “我自己不会做那些东西，比如飞机、潜水艇、摩天大楼或者电脑。 我们生活在一个有很多很多东西的世界里，但我们必须去上学，花很多年时间去学习它们是怎么运作的。”

“Besides, my brother and I really need to go back to our own parents in Frog Creek, Pennsylvania,” said Annie. “再说，我和我哥哥真的得回宾夕法尼亚州蛙溪镇的爸爸妈妈那儿，”安妮说。

Heart-of-the-Wind looked confused. “Would your mother and father not think it a wonderful honor for Jack to be the next Great Sun of Palenque?” Heart-of-the-Wind看起来很困惑。 “你的爸爸妈妈不会觉得让杰克成为帕伦克下一任大太阳是一件很荣耀的事吗？”

“They might,” said Annie, “but they would miss us too much if we came to live with you.” “也许会，”安妮说，“但如果我们要和你们一起住，他们一定会太想我们了。”

“That’s right,” said Jack. “We do lots of things with our parents. We have dinner and play games and read books.” “没错，”杰克说。 “我们经常和爸爸妈妈一起做很多事情。 我们一起吃晚饭、玩游戏、读书。”

“And talk and laugh,” said Annie. “还有聊天和笑，”安妮说。

Heart-of-the-Wind lowered her head. “I have lost my own mother,” she said. “We talked and laughed together, too. I miss her very much.” Heart-of-the-Wind低下了头。 “我失去了我的妈妈，”她说。 “我们还一起聊天、一起笑呢。” 我非常想她。”

“So you understand?” Annie said softly. “We would miss our parents terribly. And it would break their hearts if we left them alone. We have to go back home.” “你明白了吗？”安妮轻声说。 “我们会非常想念我们的父母。 如果我们独自离开，他们会伤心欲绝的。 “我们必须回家。”

The Mayan girl looked at Jack and Annie for a long moment. Then she nodded. “Yes. You must go back to your true mother and father,” she said. “But I fear my father will not understand.” 玛雅女孩长时间地看着杰克和安妮。然后她点了点头。 “是的。” “你们必须回到你们真正的父母那里，”她说。 “但我担心我的父亲不会理解。”

“So could you help us get back to the cove on the beach?” asked Jack. “So we can cross the sea back to Cozumel? And then go home?” “所以你能帮我们回到海滩的海湾吗？”杰克问。 “所以我们可以横跨大海回到科苏梅尔？ 然后再回家吗？”

“To our mother and father,” said Annie. “回到我们的爸爸妈妈那里，”安妮说。

Heart-of-the-Wind took a deep breath. “Yes, I will help you. I will take you to the shore,” she said. “Even though it will make my father sad.” Heart-of-the-Wind深吸了一口气。 “是的，我会帮你们。 我会带你们去到岸边，”她说。 “即使这会让我的父亲难过。”

“Thank you!” said Jack. “谢谢你！”杰克说。

“At this time, there are watchmen on the wall,” said the Mayan girl. “But I have discovered a hidden way to get to the sea. In the weeks we have stayed here, I have often slipped away at night to explore the forests or swim in the waves.” “这个时候，城墙上有守卫，”玛雅女孩说。 “但是我已经发现了一条通往大海的隐秘小路。” 在我们住在这里的这几周里，我经常在晚上偷偷溜出去探索森林，或者在海浪里游泳。”

“Wow, you’re brave,” said Annie. “哇，你真勇敢，”安妮说。

“I like the freedom of the dark,” the Mayan girl said. “I can lead you wherever you want to go.” “我喜欢黑暗中的自由，”玛雅女孩说。 “我可以带你去你想去的任何地方。”

“That’s great!” Jack said. “太棒了！”杰克说。

“There are still guards outside the door,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “I will hold their attention while you climb out the window. Wait for me at the wall where we first met.” “门口还有守卫，”Heart-of-the-Wind说。 “我会吸引他们的注意，让你从窗户爬出去。 在我们第一次见面的那面墙等我。”

“Got it,” said Annie. “明白了，”安妮说。

“Stay in the shadows,” the Mayan girl said. “Be as quiet as cats.” Then she slipped out the door, closing it behind her. “躲在阴影里，”玛雅女孩说。 “像猫一样安静。”然后她溜出门口，把门关上。

“Let’s go!” said Annie. “我们走吧！”安妮说。

“Wait,” said Jack. “Let’s take off our shoes. They make too much noise.” “等一下，”杰克说。 “我们把鞋子脱掉。 它们太吵了。”

Jack and Annie pulled off their flip-flops and stuffed them in the bag. Barefoot, they stepped to the window. Annie climbed out first. “Take this,” Jack whispered. He dropped the bag into Annie’s waiting arms. Then he jumped silently to the ground. He could hear voices coming from the other side of the stone building: Heart-of-the-Wind and the guards. 杰克和安妮脱下他们的拖鞋，把它们塞进袋子里。 光着脚，他们走到窗户前。 安妮先爬了出去。 “拿着这个，”杰克低声说。 他把袋子放进安妮等待着的 arms 里。 然后他悄无声息地跳到了地上。 他能听到从石头建筑另一边传来的声音：Heart-of-the-Wind 和守卫们。

Jack and Annie crept away from the House of Columns, moving through the shadows. The night was eerily still, as if everyone in the City of Dawn had disappeared until sunrise. 杰克和安妮从柱子房子旁边悄悄地溜走，穿过阴影。 夜晚异常安静，仿佛晨光之城的所有人都消失到日出之前。

When they reached the break in the stone wall, they crouched down and waited for Heart-of-the-Wind. Jack saw the silhouettes of the guards standing on top of the wall. He counted four watchmen with bows and arrows facing the sea. 当他们到达石头墙的缺口时，他们蹲下身，等待 Heart-of-the-Wind 的出现。 杰克看见守卫们的剪影站在墙的顶部。 他数了数，有四个拿着弓箭、面向大海的守卫。

The Mayan girl suddenly appeared before them. Jack hadn’t seen or heard her approach. “Follow me,” she whispered, and she led the way through the narrow opening in the wall. 玛雅女孩突然出现在他们面前。 杰克既没有看到她，也没有听到她靠近。 “跟我来，”她小声说，然后带他们穿过墙上的狭窄开口。

Once they had all squeezed through, Heart-of-the-Wind surprised Jack by turning away from the sea and heading down through thick vegetation growing on the northern slope of the bluff. Jack and Annie followed her down the hill. Prickly bushes and shrubs scratched Jack’s bare legs and feet, but he didn’t stop. He wanted to be as fleet as Heart-of-the-Wind. 当他们都挤过去后，Heart-of-the-Wind让杰克惊讶地转而离开大海，朝悬崖北坡上茂密的植被走去。 杰克和安妮跟着她下山。 带刺的灌木和灌木丛刮着杰克光着的腿和脚，但他没有停下。 他想变得和Heart-of-the-Wind一样快。

At the bottom of the hill, the Mayan girl waited for Jack and Annie to catch up. “To return to the beach, we must first travel through the Forest-of-Walking-Trees,” she said. 在山脚下，玛雅女孩等着杰克和安妮赶上她。 “如果我们想回到海滩，必须先穿过行走之林，”她说。

“Forest-of-Walking-Trees?” said Annie. “That sounds cool. But sort of scary.” “行走之林？”安妮说。 “那听起来很酷。 但有点吓人。”

“Do not be afraid,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “They are very kind trees.” “不要害怕，”Heart-of-the-Wind说。 “这些树非常友善。”

Jack laughed. “I like kind trees,” he said. 杰克笑了。 “我喜欢友善的树，”他说。

“Me too,” said Annie. “我也是，”安妮说。

Heart-of-the-Wind led Jack and Annie to a moonlit swamp. As they sloshed through shallow water, Jack heard the hum of insects and the rustling of night creatures. The air smelled of salt water and rotting wood. Heart-of-the-Wind带领杰克和安妮来到一个月光下的沼泽地。 当他们踩着浅水前进时，杰克听到了昆虫的嗡嗡声和夜间动物的窸窣声。 空气里弥漫着海水和腐烂木头的味道。

“Whoa, those trees do have legs!” said Annie, peering into the shadows. “哇，这些树真的有腿！”，安妮一边朝阴影里张望，一边说。

“Those are just roots,” said Jack. He’d seen pictures of tropical mangrove trees with their roots rising aboveground. “那只是树根，”杰克说。 他看过热带红树林的照片，那些树的根会从地面冒出来。

“No, those trees have legs,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “But they walk only at night. Hello!” she called in a whisper. “May we pass by?” “不，那些树有腿，”Heart-of-the-Wind说。 “但它们只在晚上走路。 “你好！”她小声地喊道。 “我们可以从这儿过去吗？”

The wind blew, and the trees waved their branches. Their leaves rustled, whispering, Yes, yes, yes. 风儿吹着，树儿摇晃着枝条。 树叶沙沙作响，低声说：是的，是的，是的。

“Come,” said the girl. “来，”女孩说。

Jack and Annie followed Heart-of-the-Wind deeper into the watery forest, careful not to trip over roots and bushes. Jack jumped when something prickly darted between his ankles. He shuddered when something with lots of tiny legs ran down his arm. But he tried not to make a sound. He wanted to be like Heart-of-the-Wind. Nothing seemed to bother her as she moved silently and smoothly through the swamp. 杰克和安妮跟着心之风更深入水边森林，小心不要被树根和灌木绊倒。 当有什么尖尖的东西从杰克的脚踝间窜过去时，杰克吓了一跳。 他感到一阵发抖，因为有某种长着许多小腿的东西顺着他的手臂爬下来。 但他努力不发出声音。 他想成为心之风。 她移动时悄无声息、流畅自如，似乎什么也打扰不了她。

Suddenly a raucous howling ripped through the darkness. 突然，一阵刺耳的嚎叫划破了黑暗。

“Yikes!” said Annie. “天哪！”安妮说道。

Heart-of-the-Wind laughed. “It is only the Ones-Who-Tell-the-History-of-the-Forest,” she said. 心之风笑了。 “那只是讲述森林历史的人们，”她说。

“Cool,” said Jack. He knew the sounds came from howler monkeys, the loudest monkeys in the world. He’d learned about them on a nature show, but he preferred the Mayan girl’s name for them. Maybe in their language, the monkeys really are telling the history of the forest, he thought. “太酷了，”杰克说道。 他知道这些声音来自吼猴，这是世界上叫声最大的猴子。 他在一档自然节目中听说过它们，但他更喜欢玛雅女孩给它们起的名字。 他想，也许在它们的语言里，这些猴子真的在讲述森林的历史。

“Come along,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. As they started through the darkness again, a different sound came from nearby—a deep, ominous growl. “快跟上，”Heart-of-the-Wind说。 当他们再次穿过黑暗时，附近传来了一种不同的声音——一声低沉而令人不安的咆哮。

“Whoa!” whispered Jack. “哇！”杰克小声说。

Heart-of-the-Wind held up her hand. “Silence,” she said, keeping her voice low. “He is watching us.” Heart-of-the-Wind举起手。 “安静，”她说，压低了声音。 “他在看着我们。”

“Who?” whispered Annie. “谁？”安妮小声问。

“He-Who-Kills-with-One-Leap,” the girl said. “一跃致命者，”女孩说。

“Oh, just him,” whispered Jack, trying to make a joke. “哦，只是他，”杰克小声说，试图开个玩笑。

Heart-of-the-Wind laughed softly. “Yes. Him. There,” she said, pointing. Heart-of-the-Wind轻声笑了。 “是的。他。 在那里，”她说着，指着。

In the moonlight, Jack could see yellow eyes and spotted fur. A jaguar! he thought. “Um…should we go another way? Maybe?” he said. 在月光下，杰克能看到黄色的眼睛和斑点的毛皮。是一只美洲豹！ 他想。 “呃……我们是不是该走另一条路？也许？”他说。

“No. I will send him peaceful thoughts,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “不。 我会让他感受到平静的想法，”Heart-of-the-Wind说。

“Good idea,” whispered Annie. “好主意，”安妮小声说。

She and Jack were silent as the Mayan girl stared for a long moment at the jaguar. Then she took a deep breath. “He says we may pass his way.” 她和杰克一言不发，因为那位玛雅女孩盯着那只美洲豹看了很久很久。 然后，她深吸了一口气。 “他说我们可以从他的路上走过去。”

“He does?” said Jack. “他说什么？”杰克问。

“Cool,” said Annie. “太酷了，”安妮说。

Heart-of-the-Wind led them past the tree where the jaguar crouched on a limb. Clutching his bag, Jack barely breathed as he slipped past the huge cat. 心之风带领他们走过那棵树，美洲豹正蹲在树枝上。 杰克紧紧抓着他的包，屏住呼吸，小心翼翼地从那只大猫身边溜过去。

“Good night!” Heart-of-the-Wind called back to the jaguar. “晚安！”心之风对美洲豹喊道。

“Thank you!” called Annie. “谢谢！”安妮喊道。

The cat growled again. 那只猫又低吼了一声。

“He said, Sure, no problem,” said Jack. “他说，当然，没问题，”杰克说。

Heart-of-the-Wind laughed at his joke, and Jack laughed, too. Despite the danger, he was having a good time. 心之风笑了他的玩笑，杰克也笑了。 尽管有危险，他玩得很开心。

Jack and Annie followed Heart-of-the-Wind as she forged on through the forest, climbing over tangles of roots and wading through knee-high water. 杰克和安妮跟着心之风，穿过森林继续前进，爬过盘根错节的树根，蹚过齐膝高的水。

“She’s like an Eagle Scout,” Annie said to Jack. “她就像一个鹰童队员，”安妮对杰克说。

“I was thinking more like a Navy SEAL,” said Jack. “我倒觉得更像一个海豹突击队队员，”杰克说。

The Mayan girl came to a halt. “Do not go near that log,” she whispered, pointing to a fallen tree trunk. 玛雅女孩停了下来。 “别靠近那根木头，”她指着一根倒下的树干，低声说。

“Why not?” asked Jack. “为什么不能靠近？”杰克问。

“It lives,” she said. “它活着，”她说。

“It lives?” said Jack. “它活了？”杰克说。

Heart-of-the-Wind broke off a twig and tossed it at the log. The log moved. It was the snout of a crocodile! Its huge mouth opened and closed. Heart-of-the-Wind折下一根树枝，朝那根木头扔了过去。木头动了。 这是鳄鱼的鼻子！ 它巨大的嘴巴张开又合上。

Jack and Annie jumped back. They laughed at themselves and then followed Heart-of-the-Wind as she led them away from the crocodile. “He is called Earth-Monster-of-the-Underworld,” she said. 杰克和安妮往后跳。 他们笑了一下，然后跟着Heart-of-the-Wind，她带他们离开了鳄鱼。 “他被称为地下世界的地之怪物，”她说。

“Good name,” said Annie. “好名字，”安妮说。

“To get to the sea, we must travel through the Underworld,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “要到达大海，我们必须穿过地下世界，”Heart-of-the-Wind说。

“Um…and what’s that exactly?” asked Jack. “嗯……那到底是什么？”杰克问。

“The home of the ancestor spirits,” said the girl. “My father’s warriors will not enter the Underworld. They are frightened by what they cannot see. But I am not afraid.” “祖先灵魂的家园，”女孩说道。 “我父亲的战士们不会进入冥界。 他们害怕看不见的东西。 但我一点也不害怕。”

“Me neither,” said Annie. “我也不怕，”安妮说。

“Nope. Not one bit,” said Jack, and he meant it. As long as they were with Heart-of-the-Wind, scary stuff didn’t seem all that scary. At that moment, he wasn’t afraid of anything. “不， 一点都不怕，”杰克说，他确实是这么想的。 只要他们和风之心在一起，可怕的事情似乎也不那么可怕了。 那一刻，他什么都不怕。
