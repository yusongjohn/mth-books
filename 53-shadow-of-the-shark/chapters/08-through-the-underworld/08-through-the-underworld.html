8. Through the Underworld 8. 穿越冥界

“Soon we will enter the Underworld,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. She led Jack and Annie through the mangrove forest until they came to a river hidden by trees and vines. As the leafy branches swayed in the breeze, moonlight danced on the silvery water. “很快我们就要进入冥界了，”Heart-of-the-Wind说。 她带领杰克和安妮穿过红树林，直到他们来到一条被树木和藤蔓遮掩的河边。 当树叶枝条在微风中摇曳时，月光在银色的水面上跳起了舞。

“This is the Sacred Well,” the Mayan girl said. “Our journey to the Underworld begins here.” She stepped over to a hollowed-out log canoe at the water’s edge. She picked up a wooden paddle lying beside the boat. “这是圣井，”那个玛雅女孩说。 “我们的冥界之旅就从这里开始。”她走到水边的一只挖空的木头独木舟旁。 她捡起躺在船边的一根木头桨。

As Heart-of-the-Wind pushed the canoe into the Sacred Well, Jack turned to Annie. “Remember how the travel guide mentioned sinkholes filled with water?” he whispered. “This must be one of them.” 当Heart-of-the-Wind把独木舟推进圣井时，杰克转向安妮。 “还记得旅行指南提到过那些装满水的塌陷坑吗？”他小声说。 “这一定就是其中之一。”

“Sacred Well sounds way better than sinkhole, doesn’t it?” whispered Annie. “圣井听起来比塌陷坑好多了，不是吗？”安妮小声说。

“Definitely,” said Jack. “肯定，”杰克说。

Heart-of-the-Wind slipped down into the dugout canoe and picked up the paddle. “Please, climb in,” she said. 心之风滑进独木舟里，拿起桨。 “请，进来，”她说。

Jack and Annie carefully lowered themselves down into the rough interior of the boat. Jack held their bag on his lap. 杰克和安妮小心地爬进船里粗糙的内部。 杰克把他们的包放在腿上。

Heart-of-the-Wind began paddling through the waters of the Sacred Well. As she shifted her paddle from side to side, the log canoe glided over the shimmering river. It curved down a shadowy passageway between rocks and then arrived at the yawning mouth of a cave. 心之风开始用桨划过圣井的水。 当她把桨从一边换到另一边时，木头独木舟在闪闪发光的河面上滑行。 它沿着岩石间阴暗的通道转弯，然后来到一个洞穴张开的入口前。

“Now we enter the Underworld,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “现在我们进入冥界，”心之风说。

Moonlight shimmered on the underground river as the canoe glided in and out of light-filled passageways and tunnels. Water dripped and trickled down the cave walls. Soon one of the tunnels opened into a huge chamber that had a giant hole in the ceiling, letting in the full light of the moon. 月光在地下的河面上闪烁，独木舟在充满光线的通道和隧道中滑进滑出。 水滴答滴答地从洞穴的墙壁上流下来。 很快，其中一个隧道通到了一个巨大的大厅，天花板上有个大洞，让满月的光全部照了进来。

“Oh, man,” breathed Jack. “天啊，”杰克喘着气说。

Huge columns of white rock stretched down from the ceiling of the cavern. The columns looked as if they were made of gleaming ice, and thousands of tiny icicle-like strands covered the craggy walls. 巨大的白色岩石柱子从洞穴的天花板上一直延伸下来。 这些柱子看起来像是由闪闪发光的冰制成的，而且成千上万根像冰凌一样的细丝覆盖在崎岖的墙壁上。

“What are those?” Annie asked. “那些是什么？”安妮问。

“Stone sculptures made by the Rain God,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “那是雨神雕刻的石像，”风之心说。

Jack knew from a geology book that the formations were called stalactites. They were made by centuries of dripping and trickling water in caves. But he liked Heart-of-the-Wind’s answer better. 杰克从一本地质学书上知道，这些结构叫做钟乳石。 它们是由几个世纪以来洞穴中滴落和流下的水形成的。 但他更喜欢风之心的回答。

The Mayan girl carefully steered the canoe around the gleaming columns. “Look there,” she whispered. She pointed to a ledge in the chamber. 玛雅女孩小心地把独木舟绕过闪闪发光的石柱。 “看那儿，”她小声说。 她指着洞穴里的一个平台。

On the ledge was a platform that held carved wooden statues. Some looked like snarling jaguars, others like coiled serpents. The largest was a statue of an angry king with a terrible scowl. “They guard the Underworld and protect the ancestor spirits,” Heart-of-the-Wind explained. 平台上放着一些雕刻的木头雕像。 有些看起来像咆哮的美洲豹，有些像盘绕的蛇。 最大的那个雕像是一位生气的国王，脸上满是怒容。 “他们守护着冥界，保护祖先的灵魂，”Heart-of-the-Wind解释道。

“Cool,” said Annie. “真酷，”安妮说。

Heart-of-the-Wind steered the canoe around a corner and into another cave tunnel. This tunnel was completely dark, without a trace of light. The air was cold and clammy. The canoe bumped against the rock walls. Jack reached out and felt the slippery, drippy stone. He pulled his hand back and shuddered. Heart-of-the-Wind把独木舟绕过一个拐角，驶入了另一个洞穴隧道。 这条隧道完全黑暗，连一丝光都没有。 空气又冷又潮湿。 独木舟撞上了岩石墙壁。 杰克伸出手，摸到了又滑又湿的石头。 他把手缩回来，打了个哆嗦。

As Heart-of-the-Wind paddled on through the black tunnel, Jack clutched his bag and tried to still his racing heart. The Underworld frightened him a little now: the tireless drip- drip of water, the strange statues of serpents and jaguars, and now the dank utter darkness of the tunnel. Heart-of-the-Wind继续划着船穿过黑暗的隧道，杰克紧紧抓着他的包，努力让自己的心跳平静下来。 现在，地下世界让他有点害怕：水滴不断的声音，奇怪的蛇和美洲豹雕像，还有隧道里令人窒息的黑暗。

“Where are the ancestor spirits?” whispered Annie. “祖先的灵魂在哪里？”安妮小声问。

“They are all around us,” Heart-of-the-Wind whispered back. “They are watching everything we do.” “他们就在我们周围，”Heart-of-the-Wind低声回答。 “他们一直在看着我们的一举一动。”

Oh, man, Jack thought. No wonder the king’s warriors were afraid to enter the Underworld. 天啊，杰克心想。 难怪国王的战士们害怕进入冥界。

“Do not be afraid,” said the Mayan girl, as if she had read his thoughts. “I will protect you.” “别害怕，”玛雅女孩说道，仿佛她读懂了他的想法。 “我会保护你。”

“Thank you,” whispered Annie, sounding a little nervous herself. “谢谢你，”安妮小声说道，听起来她自己也有些紧张。

Heart-of-the-Wind paddled through the twists and turns of the Underworld, until finally the canoe emerged onto a glistening pool that was open to the moonlit sky. 心之风划着独木舟穿过了冥界的曲折水道，直到最后，独木舟终于驶入了一个闪闪发光的池塘，池塘上方是月光照耀的天空。

“Whew. We made it,” said Annie. “哇—— 我们成功了，”安妮说道。

“And lived to tell about it!” Jack said, only half joking. “而且还能活下来讲述这件事！”杰克说道，语气半是开玩笑。

“That was great,” said Annie. “太棒了，”安妮说道。

“You are both more brave than my father’s men,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “你们两个比我父亲的手下都要勇敢，”心之风说道。

“Not really,” said Jack. But he secretly liked thinking of himself as being braver than an ancient Mayan warrior. “That was an incredible ride.” “其实不是，”杰克说。 但他偷偷地喜欢把自己想象得比一位古代玛雅战士还要勇敢。 “那真是一次惊人的旅程。”

Heart-of-the-Wind paddled the canoe to the edge of the water. “We have arrived,” she said. 心之风划着独木舟来到水边。 “我们到了，”她说。

Jack could hear ocean waves and the cries of gulls. The moon was going down. In the east, the sky was growing lighter. 杰克能听到海浪声和海鸥的叫声。 月亮正在落下。 在东方，天空渐渐亮了起来。

Heart-of-the-Wind held the canoe steady as Jack and Annie climbed out onto the beach. The Mayan girl followed behind them, and Jack and Annie helped her pull the log canoe out of the pool, onto the cool sand. “The light of the dawn will be bright very soon,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “What will you do now?” 心之风稳稳地掌控着独木舟，杰克和安妮爬上了沙滩。 玛雅女孩跟在他们后面，杰克和安妮帮她把木头做的独木舟从水池里拉到凉爽的沙滩上。 “黎明的光很快就会很明亮，”Heart-of-the-Wind说。 “你现在要做什么？”

“We need to find something we lost,” Annie said. “It’s next to a raft we left on the beach.” “我们需要找到我们丢失的东西，”安妮说。 “它在我们留在海滩上的木筏旁边。”

“At least we hope it is,” said Jack. “至少我们是这么希望的，”杰克说。

“Where is the raft?” the girl asked. “木筏在哪里？”女孩问。

“In the cove below the City of Dawn,” said Annie. “在黎明之城下面的海湾里，”安妮说。

“Follow me,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “I will take you there.” “跟我来，”Heart-of-the-Wind说。 “我会带你们去那里。”

The Mayan girl led Jack and Annie down the misty beach. When they came to a pile of stones jutting into the sea, she stopped. “Stay close to the rocks so the watchmen cannot see us,” she said. “They will not leave their post until the sun rises.” 玛雅女孩带领杰克和安妮沿着迷雾笼罩的海滩走下去。 当他们来到一堆突入海中的石头前，她停下了。 “靠近岩石点，这样守卫的人就看不见我们，”她说。 “他们要等到太阳升起才会离开岗位。”

With the mist hiding them, Heart-of-the-Wind led Jack and Annie over the rocks. Jack could hear the waves breaking against the shore. 在薄雾的掩护下，Heart-of-the-Wind带领杰克和安妮翻过岩石。 杰克能听到海浪拍打岸边的声音。

“And what will you do next?” Annie asked as they followed Heart-of-the-Wind. “Where will you go after we leave you?” “那接下来你们打算去哪儿呢？”安妮一边跟着Heart-of-the-Wind，一边问。 “我们离开之后，你们打算去哪儿呢？”

“I will return to my father,” said the girl. “I will tell him that you have gone home to your own true father and mother.” “我会回到我爸爸那里，”女孩说。 “我会告诉他，你们已经回家，和你们真正的爸爸妈妈在一起了。”

“Will your father be mad?” asked Jack. “你爸爸会生气吗？”杰克问。

“Perhaps,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “He believed you were the answer to a question he has been asking for a long time: who will lead our people after he dies?” “也许吧，”Heart-of-the-Wind说。 “他一直问一个问题，已经问了很久：在他去世之后，谁会带领我们的人民？”

“Okay. But I have a question for him,” said Annie. “好的。 但我也有一个问题要问他，”安妮说。

“What is your question?” said Heart-of-the-Wind. She stopped and looked back at Annie. “你的问题是什么？”Heart-of-the-Wind说。 她停下来，回头看着安妮。

“I would like to ask him, ‘Why can’t your daughter, Heart-of-the-Wind, lead your people after you are gone?’ ” said Annie. “我想问他，‘为什么你的女儿Heart-of-the-Wind不能在你离开之后带领你们的人民？’”安妮说。

The Mayan girl laughed the biggest laugh she’d laughed all night. “You are crazy,” she said. 玛雅女孩发出她整晚最大的笑声。 “你疯了，”她说。

“Seriously! That’s a great question,” said Jack. “Why can’t you be the next Great Sun of Palenque?” “真的！ “这是一个很好的问题，”杰克说。 “为什么不能让你成为帕伦克的下一位伟大太阳呢？”

“Yes!” said Annie. “Where we come from, women are leaders of many countries and cities and states.” “是！”安妮说。 “在我们来的那个地方，很多国家、城市和州的领导者都是女性。”

Heart-of-the-Wind looked shocked. “Truly? But that is not possible here. A female is not allowed to rule the Mayan people. Now you must hurry. Before the sun rises.” She moved away from them, climbing over the rocks again, as if the matter were closed. Heart-of-the-Wind看起来很震惊。 “真的吗？ 但这里不可能发生这样的事情。 女性不允许统治玛雅人。 现在你们必须赶快。 在太阳升起之前。” 她离开了他们，再次爬过岩石，仿佛这件事已经结束了。

Jack and Annie hurried to keep up with Heart-of-the-Wind. Finally the three of them climbed over a big boulder and dropped down onto the sandy beach of the cove. 杰克和安妮赶紧跟上心之风的步伐。 最后，他们三个人爬过一块大石头，跳到了海湾的沙滩上。

“Listen to me, Heart-of-the-Wind,” said Annie. “You’re as brave as any warrior—braver, really. You don’t seem at all afraid of the forest or the Underworld.” “听我说，心之风，”安妮说。 “你和任何战士一样勇敢——其实，你更勇敢。 你似乎一点都不害怕森林或冥界。”

“Right, and you know tons more than I do,” said Jack. “You know your people. I’ll bet you know a lot about farming, and you know how to use the Mayan calendar, right?” “对，而且你懂得的东西比我多得多，”杰克说。 “你了解你的族人。 我打赌你对农耕很在行，你也知道怎么使用玛雅日历，对吧？”

Heart-of-the-Wind nodded. “Yes.” 心之风点了点头。 “是的。”

“And you know how to talk to jaguars,” said Annie. “你知道怎么和美洲豹说话，”安妮说。

“And you’re not afraid of the Rain God or the ancestor spirits,” said Jack. “你也不怕雨神或者祖先的精灵，”杰克说。

“So why can’t you be a leader?” asked Annie. “那为什么你不能当领袖呢？”安妮问。

Heart-of-the-Wind turned to look at the sea and slowly shook her head. “No, no, no…it is not possible…never.” 心之风转向大海望去，慢慢地摇了摇头。 “不，不，不……这不可能……永远不可能。”

“Okay. But just answer one question for me,” said Annie. “Would you like to be the Great Sun of Palenque someday? If you could be, would you? Tell the truth.” “好的。 但请你回答我一个问题，”安妮说。 “你将来想成为帕伦克的大太阳吗？ 如果你能成为的话，你会吗？说实话。”

Heart-of-the-Wind looked back at Annie and Jack. She smiled. “Yes. I would like to be the leader of my people.” 心之风回头看着安妮和杰克。她微笑了。 “是的。” “我想成为我人民的领袖。”

“We know you would take good care of them!” said Annie. “Just like you’ve taken good care of us!” “我们知道你会好好照顾他们的！”安妮说。 “就像你一直好好照顾我们一样！”

“I would be fair and just,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “I would teach everyone to read our calendar and learn our writing. I would make certain that all of my people had enough fish, beans, and grains to eat.” “我会公正无私，”Heart-of-the-Wind说。 “我会教大家阅读我们的日历，学习我们的文字。 我会确保我的人民都有足够的鱼、豆子和谷物吃。”

“Heart-of-the-Wind, you must tell that to your father!” said Annie. “Heart-of-the-Wind，你必须把这个告诉你的父亲！”安妮说。

“He would laugh at me,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “He would never allow a girl to inherit his throne, not even a daughter he loves greatly. That would be against all the customs and traditions of our ancestors.” “他会嘲笑我的，”Heart-of-the-Wind说。 “他绝不会允许一个女孩继承他的王位，即使是他深爱的女儿也不会。” 那会违背我们祖先的所有习俗和传统。”

“Isn’t there any way you could change his mind?” said Jack. “你有没有办法让他改变主意？”杰克说。

“Only a miracle could change the mind of my father about this,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “只有奇迹才能让我父亲改变对这件事的看法，”Heart-of-the-Wind说。

“Okay,” said Annie. “I have an idea. What if Jack told him?” “好吧，”安妮说。 “我有个主意。 如果杰克去告诉他呢？

“Me?” Jack said to Annie. “我？”杰克对安妮说。

“Yes!” said Annie. “Heart-of-the-Wind, your father believes Jack came from a faraway place to teach new things to your people. So maybe Jack could teach him that girls can grow up to be great leaders, just like boys.” “是的！”安妮说。 “Heart-of-the-Wind，你父亲相信杰克是从遥远的地方来的，要教你们族人新东西。 所以也许杰克可以教他，女孩长大后也能成为伟大的领袖，就像男孩一样。”

“No, Annie, we can’t go back,” Jack whispered. “不，安妮，我们不能回去，”杰克小声说。

Heart-of-the-Wind looked thoughtful, and then slowly she nodded. “Yes. I believe words from Jack might help my father think a new thought,” she said. 心之风看起来若有所思，然后慢慢地点了点头。 “是的。 我相信杰克的话可能会让我父亲产生新的想法，”她说。

“Great!” said Annie. “太好了！”安妮说。

“Annie, we can’t go back,” Jack said again. “What if he won’t listen to me? What if he’s convinced I was sent here to be the Great Sun of Palenque?” “安妮，我们不能回去了，”杰克又说了一遍。 “如果他不听我的怎么办？ “如果他以为我是被派来当帕伦克大太阳的呢？”

“Don’t worry. You and I don’t have to go back,” said Annie. “And her father will listen to you. Because it’s going to seem like a miracle to him.” “别担心。 你和我都不用回去，”安妮说。 “而且她的父亲会听你的。” 因为对他来说，这看起来就像一个奇迹。

“What?” said Jack. “How?” “什么？”杰克说。 “怎么？”

“Give me the bag, please,” said Annie. “请把袋子给我，”安妮说。

Jack handed her their bag. Annie unzipped it. She reached in and pulled out their cell phone. 杰克把他们的包递给她。 安妮解开了拉链。 她伸手进去，拿出他们的手机。

“This will go back with Heart-of-the-Wind,” Annie said to Jack, “with an important message from you.” “这个要和心之风一起回去，”安妮对杰克说，“带着你的重要信息。”
