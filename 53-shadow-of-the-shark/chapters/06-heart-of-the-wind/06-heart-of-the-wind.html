6. Heart-of-the-Wind 6. 心之风

“Well, I wouldn’t exactly call this a luxury hotel,” said Annie. She shivered. A damp wind was blowing through the open window. “嗯，我可不会说这是豪华酒店，”安妮说。她打了个哆嗦。 潮湿的风吹进了敞开的窗户。

Jack was too stunned to speak. He reached into the swim bag and pulled out the travel guide. He walked to the window and studied the cover by moonlight. “Oh…,” he whispered, and then pointed to one of the small paintings. “I think I get it now.” 杰克惊讶得说不出话来。 他把手伸进游泳包，拿出了一本旅行指南。 他走到窗边，借着月光仔细看着封面。 “哦……”，他小声说，然后指着其中一幅小画。 “我想我现在明白了。”

“Get what?” said Annie. “明白什么？”安妮说。

Jack looked at her. “This isn’t a theme park.” 杰克看着她。 “这不是一个主题公园。”

“I was starting to think that, too,” said Annie. “But what is it?” “我开始也这么想了，”安妮说。 “但这到底是什么？”

“It’s real,” said Jack. “它是真的，”杰克说。

“Real?” said Annie. “What do you mean, real? “真的？”安妮说。 “你是说，真的吗？

“I think we’ve accidentally come to the actual time of ancient Mayans,” said Jack. “我想我们不小心来到了真正的古代玛雅人时代，”杰克说。

“Like when?” said Annie. “是什么时候呢？”安妮说。

“Like maybe over a thousand years ago, when they wore feathers and fought with spears.” “比如说，可能有一千多年前，他们戴羽毛，用矛战斗的时候。”

“But—how is that possible?” said Annie. “Teddy sent us to Cozumel now, in our time!” “但是——这怎么可能呢？”安妮说。 “泰迪现在把我们送到我们这个时代的科苏梅尔去了！”

“That’s what he was supposed to do,” said Jack. “But maybe when you pointed at the cover of the book, you pointed at the painting of the ancient pyramid the way it looked, like, over a thousand years ago. Teddy must not have known the pyramid painting would send us back to Cozumel then instead of now.” “这就是他应该做的事，”杰克说。 “但是也许当你指着书的封面时，你指着的是那幅古代金字塔的画，就像它一千多年前的样子。” “泰迪一定不知道那幅金字塔的画会把我们送到科苏梅尔，而不是现在这里。”

“Oh. Wow. You mean all this time we’ve been in an ancient world?” said Annie. “哦。哇。” “你是说，我们一直都在一个古代的世界里吗？”安妮说。

“Yep. We didn’t land in a modern nature preserve,” said Jack. “对。” “我们降落的不是现代的自然保护区，”杰克说。

“Oh,” said Annie. “哦，”安妮说。

“That’s why we didn’t see any tourists or ferries or cruise ships,” said Jack. “这就是为什么我们没看到任何游客、渡轮或游轮，”杰克说。

“Right,” said Annie, nodding. She looked back at the cover of the guide. “Too bad I didn’t point to the luxury hotel.” “对，”安妮点头说。 她又看了看指南的封面。 “太糟糕了，我刚才没指那个豪华酒店。”

“No kidding,” said Jack. “可不是嘛，”杰克说。

“These guys have probably never seen people like us before,” said Annie. “这些人可能以前从没见过我们这样的人，”安妮说。

“Probably?” said Jack. “Definitely. We’re from the future—more than a thousand years in the future.” “可能？”杰克说。 “肯定。 我们来自未来——一千多年后的未来。”

“What should we do?” said Annie. “我们该怎么办？”安妮说。

“There’s only one thing to do—we have to get out of here and back to the tree house,” said Jack. “Before the king comes.” “只有一件事可做——我们必须离开这儿，回到树屋，”杰克说。 “在国王来之前。”

“Can we escape through the window?” Annie walked over and looked out the open window. “Darn. Guards are patrolling the building.” “我们能从窗户逃出去吗？”安妮走过去，朝窗外望去。 “该死。” 卫兵正在巡逻大楼。

“So we can’t get out that way,” said Jack. “所以，我们不能从那边出去，”杰克说。

“Oh, I know what we can do!” said Annie. “We can use our last gold coin to help us!” “哦，我知道我们可以怎么做！”安妮说。 “我们可以用我们最后一枚金币来帮助我们！”

“Of course! Perfect!” said Jack. He grabbed their swim bag. “Yes, yes, yes. We’ll make a wish to escape all the way back to the tree house! With no warriors capturing us or sharks attacking us! I think that would qualify as fun.” “当然！ 完美！”杰克说。 他拿起了他们的游泳包。 “是的，是的，是的。 我们会许愿，一路回到树屋！” 没有战士抓我们，也没有鲨鱼攻击我们！ 我觉得那可以算作是有趣的。”

“Let’s make a wish to fly from this window, across the sea!” said Annie. “That would be so fun!” “让我们许个愿，从这扇窗户飞出去，飞过大海吧！”安妮说。 “那会非常有趣！”

“No kidding!” said Jack. He knelt on the floor and dug inside the bag for the velvet coin purse. “Where is it?” He pulled everything out: flippers, life vests, snorkels, and face masks. “Where is it? Where’s that coin purse?” “真的吗？”杰克说。 他跪在地上，从袋子里翻找那枚天鹅绒硬币钱包。 “它在哪里？”他把所有东西都倒了出来：脚蹼、救生衣、潜水面镜和面罩。 “它在哪里？ 那个硬币钱包在哪里？

Jack turned the bag upside down and shook it. He and Annie shook the flippers and life vests, too, but no small green velvet purse fell out. 杰克把袋子倒过来，使劲摇晃它。 他和安妮也摇晃了脚蹼和救生背心，但没有一个小绿色天鹅绒钱包掉出来。

“It’s not here,” said Jack. “Did you put it back in here?” “它不在这里，”杰克说。 “你是不是又把它放回这里了？”

Annie looked confused. “Did I?” 安妮看起来很困惑。 “我放了吗？”

“That’s what I’m asking,” said Jack. “Did you?” “这就是我在问你的，”杰克说。 “你放了吗？”

“I don’t know,” said Annie. “I remember I put the five hundred dollars in the purse…and then we pushed the raft…and then we heard the music and—” “我不知道，”安妮说。 “我记得我把那五百美元放进钱包里……然后我们把皮划艇推了出去……然后我们听到了音乐，——”

“So when did you put the purse back in the bag?” asked Jack. “那你是什么时候把钱包又放回包里的？”杰克问。

“I—I don’t know,” said Annie. “Maybe I didn’t…. Maybe I put it down on the sand…to push the raft….” “我……我不知道，”安妮说。 “也许我…… 也许我把它放在沙子上了……为了推动木筏……”，

“Seriously?” said Jack. “真的吗？”杰克说。

“That’s what I sort of remember now that I think about it…,” whispered Annie. “Oh, no! I lost all our money and the magic coin! I’m so sorry!” “现在我想起来，我好像是记得这些的……”，安妮小声说。 “哦，不！ 我把我们所有的钱和那枚魔法硬币都弄丢了！我太抱歉了！”，

“It’s okay,” said Jack. “It’ll be okay. We just need to get out of here and climb back down to the beach before the king comes.” “没关系，”杰克说。 “会没事的。 我们只需要赶快离开这里，爬回海滩去，在国王来之前。”

“And find our coin purse!” said Annie. “然后找到我们的钱袋！”安妮说。

“Right,” said Jack. He took a deep breath. “Okay, one thing at a time. First, let’s—” Before he could say more, the door swung open. Jack quickly crammed everything back into their bag. “对，”杰克说。 他深吸了一口气。 “好吧，一次只做一件事。 首先，我们—” 他还没说完，门就猛地打开了。 杰克赶紧把所有东西塞回背包里。

The room was suddenly filled with fiery light. Warriors with burning torches entered the room. 房间突然充满了火光。 拿着燃烧火把的战士们走进了房间。

Several women followed the warriors. The women had large jade ornaments in their ears and wore dresses woven with red, yellow, and purple threads. They placed flowers on the floor, along with bowls filled with corn, beans, chili peppers, and pineapple slices. 一些女人跟着战士们进来了。 这些女人的耳朵上戴着大大的翡翠饰品，穿着用红、黄、紫线织成的裙子。 他们在地板上放了花，还有装满玉米、豆子、辣椒和菠萝片的碗。

Two men then stepped into the room—the same two who had sat on the thrones. They must be kings, Jack thought. But why are there two of them? The two kings wore feather headdresses that were at least three feet high. Around their shoulders were jaguar skins. Their arms were covered with bracelets. The older-looking one had long white hair. A girl with black braids stood beside him—the same girl who’d spoken to Annie near the wall. 接着，有两个人走进了房间——就是之前坐在王座上的那两个人。 杰克想，他们一定是国王。 但是，为什么有两个国王呢？ 两位国王戴着至少三英尺高的羽毛头饰。 他们的肩膀上披着美洲豹的皮。 他们的手臂上戴着很多手镯。 看起来年纪较大的那个国王有一头长长的白发。 他旁边站着一个扎着黑辫子的女孩——就是之前在墙边跟安妮说话的那个女孩。

Everyone stared at Jack and Annie. 所有人都盯着杰克和安妮看。

“Hi,” said Annie. “你好，”安妮说。

“We come in peace,” said Jack. “我们是来和平访问的，”杰克说。

The younger of the two kings stepped forward. 两位国王中年纪较小的那位向前走了几步。

“Who are you?” the man asked. “你们是谁？”那人问。

“Jack and Annie of Frog Creek, Pennsylvania,” said Annie. “我们是宾夕法尼亚州蛙溪镇的杰克和安妮，”安妮说。

“How did you get here?” the king asked. “你们是怎么来到这里的？”国王问。

“By raft,” said Jack, “from Cozumel.” “我们是乘木筏来的，”杰克说，“从科苏梅尔岛。”

The older king picked up the travel guide from the floor. “What is this?” 年纪较大的国王从地上捡起了旅行指南。 “这是什么？”

Oh, no! thought Jack. He’d forgotten to put their book back in the bag! The king turned the book upside down and sideways. He stared at the back and the front. 哦，不！杰克心想。 他忘记把他们的书放回袋子里了！ 国王把书倒过来，又侧着看了看。 他盯着书的封面和封底看了又看。

“It’s a travel guide,” Annie said helpfully. “这是一本旅行指南，”安妮热心地说。

“A travel guide?” the older man repeated. His face was lined with wrinkles, and his eyes were bright and alert. He opened the book and stared at a page. Pointing to a photo of a plane landing at the Cozumel airport, he looked puzzled. “旅行指南？”那个年长的男人重复了一遍。 他的脸上布满皱纹，眼睛却明亮而警觉。 他翻开书，盯着一页看了起来。 他指着一张飞机在科苏梅尔机场降落的照片，一脸困惑。

“That’s a plane,” said Jack. “那是飞机，”杰克说。

“It flies,” said Annie. She moved her hand through the air. “Like a bird.” “它会飞，”安妮说。 她把手伸进空气里。 “像一只鸟。”

The older king looked at the younger king and shrugged. He ruffled the pages, and the book fell open to a photo of a cruise ship. 年长的国王看着年轻的国王，耸了耸肩。 他翻了翻书页，书本自动翻开，出现了一张游轮的照片。

“That’s an ocean liner,” said Jack. “那是一艘远洋轮船，”杰克说。

“It carries thousands of people across the ocean,” said Annie. “它能载着成千上万的人横跨海洋，”安妮说。

The king turned to more photos: one showed a water-skier, and another showed an underwater tourist boat. 国王翻到更多的照片：一张显示了一个水上滑板的人，另一张显示了一艘水下观光船。

“Speedboat,” said Annie. “Submarine.” “快艇，”安妮说。 “潜水艇。”

The king turned the page and stared at a photo of skyscraper hotels. On the opposite page was a photo of a guest room with a girl working on a computer and a boy watching a football game on TV. 国王翻过一页，盯着一张摩天大楼酒店的照片。 对面的那一页是一张客房的照片，照片里有个女孩正在用电脑，还有一个男孩在电视上看足球比赛。

Neither Annie nor Jack tried to explain. 安妮和杰克都没有试图解释。

As the king turned more pages in the book, Jack glanced around the room. He caught the gaze of the young girl. She was the only person smiling. 当国王继续翻书时，杰克环顾了一下房间。 他注意到了那个年轻女孩的目光。 她是唯一一个在微笑的人。

Finally the old king closed the book and looked at Jack and Annie for a long time. He handed the book to Jack. Then he turned and nodded to the younger ruler. The two kings and the girl walked out the door back into the night. The women followed, and then the warriors. 最后，老国王合上书，长时间地盯着杰克和安妮看。 他把书递给了杰克。 然后他转身，向年轻的统治者点了点头。 两位国王和那个女孩走出了门，回到了夜晚中。 妇女们随后跟了上去，接着是战士们。

One of the warriors left his flaming torch in a stone sconce. Then he closed the door, leaving Jack and Annie alone again in the dimly lit room. 战士之一把他的火把留在了石灯座上。 然后他关上了门，杰克和安妮再次独自留在昏暗的房间里。

“I wonder what they were thinking,” said Annie. “Are we their prisoners or their guests?” “我想知道他们当时在想什么，”安妮说。 “我们是他们的囚犯，还是客人？”

“I don’t know,” said Jack, shaking his head. This dream vacation had turned into a nightmare. “我不知道，”杰克摇着头说。 这次梦幻假期已经变成了噩梦。

“Should we eat the food?” asked Annie. “我们该吃点食物吗？”安妮问。

“I’m not hungry anymore,” said Jack. “我现在不饿了，”杰克说。

“Me neither,” said Annie. “I just thought it might be polite.” “我也是，”安妮说。 “我只是觉得这样可能比较有礼貌。”

“I guess we’d better learn more about ancient Mayans,” Jack said. He opened the guide and found a section titled Mayans. By the light of the torch, he read aloud:

Today many Mayans live in Mexico and other parts of Central America. Long ago, before Columbus discovered the “New World,” Mayans had their own civilization. They created their own calendar and a special form of picture writing. They were skilled farmers, astronomers, and architects. 今天，许多玛雅人住在墨西哥和中美洲的其他地方。很久以前，在哥伦布发现“新大陆”之前，玛雅人就有自己的文明。他们创造了自己的日历和一种特殊的图画文字。他们是擅长耕种、天文学和建筑的能手。

Before Jack could read more, the door opened again. The girl with the bangs and shiny black braids slipped into the room. She closed the door and smiled at Jack and Annie.

“Hi,” said Annie. “What’s your name?”

“Heart-of-the-Wind,” said the girl.

“That’s a beautiful name,” said Annie.

“Thank you,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “My father meets with his council now.”

“Who’s your father?” asked Annie.

“My father is the Great Sun,” said the girl. “He rules the kingdom of Palenque. It is many days from here, in the jungle.”

“Was the Great Sun the one who looked at our book?” asked Jack.

“Yes,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “He has been traveling the land to find his true heir. Weeks ago, we stopped here, in the City of Dawn, to visit with the king and rest. My father did not know that this would be the place where he would find what he was looking for.” She smiled at Jack. “Do you understand?”

“Um…I’m not sure,” said Jack. What am I supposed to understand? he thought.

“My father believes that you are the one he has been searching for,” said Heart-of-the-Wind.

“Me?” said Jack.

“My father just told me that he believes the gods have sent you to be our next king,” said Heart-of-the-Wind.

“Jack?” said Annie. “Your next king?”

“Jack,” the girl said, nodding. “I am my father’s only child. He has no sons. So he has decided to take Jack back to our kingdom and prepare him to be our ruler.”

“Me?” Jack said again.

“Yes,” said Heart-of-the-Wind. “You will be the next Great Sun of Palenque.”
