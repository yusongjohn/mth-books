5. Yucatán Theme Park 5. 玉加坦主题公园

“There! Those are the steps I was talking about,” said Annie. She pointed to a rickety wooden stairway clinging to the side of the cliff. “那儿！ “那些就是我刚才说的步骤，”安妮说。 她指着一条摇摇晃晃的木制楼梯，楼梯紧紧贴在悬崖的一侧。

“That?” said Jack. “那条？”杰克说。

“I know, it doesn’t look safe,” said Annie. “But I don’t see any other way to get up there.” “我知道，看起来不太安全，”安妮说。 “但是我看不出还有别的办法能上去。”

Jack looked up and down the beach. “Me neither. I guess we’ll have to try it.” 杰克抬头看了看海滩，又低头看了看。 “我也没看到别的办法。” 我想我们只能试试看啦。”

Lugging their bag, Jack followed Annie to the stairs. Annie started up first, the weathered boards creaking with her weight. 拖着他们的行李箱，杰克跟着安妮走上了台阶。 安妮先爬了上去，老旧的木板在她的重量下发出吱呀声。

“Careful,” said Jack. “Hold on to the railing.” “小心点，”杰克说。 “抓紧扶手。”

“You too!” Annie called down. “你也要小心！”安妮朝下喊道。

Jack started climbing the rickety stairway. Gripping the handrail, he took one step at a time as the wind shook the stairs, and the sound of drums and flutes floated down from above. 杰克开始爬那条摇摇晃晃的楼梯。 抓紧扶手，他一步一步地往下走，风把楼梯吹得摇摇晃晃，上面还传来鼓声和笛子的声音。

Suddenly Jack’s foot broke through one of the boards! Hanging on tightly to the railing, he leapt to the next step as the broken one crashed to the ground. 突然，杰克的脚踩断了一块木板！ 他紧紧抓住扶手，跳到了下一个台阶上，那块断掉的木板掉到了地上。

“You okay?” said Annie. “你没事吧？”安妮问。

“Yeah, barely,” Jack answered. “没事，就差一点，”杰克回答。

As he kept climbing, he clung to the railing and gingerly placed one foot after the other. Finally he followed Annie from the top step onto the rocky ground at the top of the cliff. 他一边往上爬，一边紧紧抓着栏杆，小心翼翼地把一只脚放在另一只脚前面。 最后，他跟着安妮从台阶顶部走到了悬崖顶部的岩石地面上。

“We made it!” Annie said. “我们成功了！”，安妮说。

“I can’t believe they built such a bad staircase!” said Jack. “我真不敢相信他们造了这么糟糕的楼梯！”，杰克说。

“They probably want it to look as real as possible,” said Annie, “like in olden times.” “他们可能是想让它看起来尽可能真实，”安妮说，“就像在古代那样。”

“Yeah, but how about safe?” said Jack. “If someone falls—or this whole thing comes crashing down—people could really get hurt.” “是啊，但安全吗？”杰克说。 “如果有人摔倒了——或者这整件事突然倒塌下来——人们真的会受伤的。”

“We should tell our hotel to post a warning or something,” said Annie. “我们应该告诉酒店贴个警告标志什么的，”安妮说。

“Yeah. Avoid the rickety staircase with the broken step,” said Jack. “是啊。 “避开那条有坏台阶的摇摇晃晃的楼梯，”杰克说。

As he caught his breath, he listened to the drum and flute music coming from behind a high stone wall. The smell of wood smoke filled the air. By now the full moon had risen over the park, giving everything a bluish tint. 他停下来喘了口气，听着从高高的石墙后面传来的鼓声和笛子声。 空气中充满了木头燃烧的烟味。 这时，满月已经升起来了，把公园里的一切都染上了一层蓝色的光。

“It sounds great, doesn’t it?” said Annie. “听起来真棒，对不对？”安妮说。

“Yeah, it does,” said Jack. He had to admit that the theme park show seemed really inviting. “是啊，真棒，”杰克说。 他不得不承认，这个主题公园的表演看起来真的很吸引人。

“I wonder how we get inside,” said Annie. “我想知道我们怎么才能进去，”安妮说。

“Hold on,” said Jack. He took out their travel guide. By the light of the moon, he studied the map. “It looks like a wall runs all the way around the park. And the entrance is on the other side, with a ticket booth…. Oh, and there’s a visitors bureau! They can help us find a hotel.” “等等，”杰克说。 他拿出了他们的旅行指南。 借着月光，他仔细研究了地图。 “看起来好像有一面墙围着整个公园。” 入口在另一边，还有一个售票处…… 哦，还有一个游客服务中心！ 他们可以帮我们找旅馆。”

“But we’re checking out the show first, right?” Annie said. “但是我们先去看演出，对吧？”安妮说。

“Sure, let’s go,” said Jack. “当然，走吧，”杰克说。

As they started along the wall, bellowing horn sounds came from inside the park. “Whoa, what’s that?” said Annie. She ran ahead. 当他们沿着墙走的时候，公园里传来了号角声。 “哇，那是什么？”安妮说。她跑到了前面。

When Jack caught up with her, she was looking through a narrow opening in the wall. “It’s definitely a song-and-dance spectacular,” she said. “Look at the great costumes!” 当杰克追上她时，她正透过墙上的一个狭窄开口往里看。 “这肯定是一场歌舞表演，”她说。 “看看这些漂亮的服装！”

Annie stepped aside so Jack could peek through the opening. He saw the long shadows of stone buildings. In front of the buildings was an open square, bright with firelight. Dancers and musicians were performing around a roaring bonfire. Dozens of people watched from the sides, some standing and some sitting on the ground. 安妮让到一边，好让杰克从开口处 peek 一下。 他看见了石头建筑长长的影子。 建筑前面是一个开阔的广场，火光闪闪的。 舞者和音乐家们在 roaring bonfire 周围表演。 几十个人从两边看着，有的站着，有的坐在地上。

“It looks great,” said Jack. “Let’s find the entrance.” “看起来真棒，”杰克说。 “我们去找入口吧。”

“Oh, let’s just sneak in through here,” said Annie, “before the show ends.” “哦，我们干脆从这儿偷偷溜进去吧，”安妮说，“在表演结束之前。”

“What about our tickets?” said Jack. “那我们的票呢？”杰克说。

“If we spend time looking for the ticket booth, we’ll miss the show!” said Annie. “We can pay on the way out. This is a chance of a lifetime.” “如果我们花时间找票亭，就会错过演出啦！”安妮说。 “我们可以在出去的时候付钱。 这可是千载难逢的机会。”

“Okay,” said Jack. “好吧，”杰克说。

“Yes!” said Annie. She turned sideways and slid through the narrow opening. “See? No problem,” she said from the other side. “对！”安妮说。 她侧着身子，从狭窄的开口滑了进去。 “看到了吗？ 没问题，”她从另一边说。

“Take this,” said Jack. He pushed their bulky bag through the opening. Then he squeezed sideways between the thick slabs of rock. “拿着这个，”杰克说。 他把他们那个又大又重的包推了过去。 然后他侧着身子挤过厚厚的岩石板。

Jack took his bag back from Annie, and they hurried across the grounds of the park. When they got close to the bonfire, they stood near the back of the square. 杰克从安妮那里拿回他的包，他们赶紧穿过公园的场地。 当他们靠近篝火时，他们站在广场的后面。

The male dancers had feathered headdresses and beautiful feathered cloaks. They wore arm and leg bracelets made of gold and silver. They had painted faces and tattoos. Some of them waved spears and shields as they shook their bodies. 男舞者们戴着羽毛头饰，穿着漂亮的羽毛斗篷。 他们手腕和脚腕上戴着由金银制成的手镯和脚镯。 他们的脸上画着图案，还有纹身。 有些人挥舞着矛和盾，随着身体摇摆。

Near the dancers, several musicians played wooden flutes. Others rattled hollow gourd instruments and rang loud bells. Some clapped sticks together, while still others banged huge turtle-shell drums and blew on giant conch shells that made deep bellowing sounds. 在舞者附近，几位音乐家正在吹奏木制笛子。 其他人摇晃着空葫芦乐器，敲响了响亮的铃铛。 有些人拍打木棍，还有些人敲打着巨大的龟壳鼓，并吹响巨大的海螺号，发出低沉的轰鸣声。

While the dancers danced and the musicians played, actors moved about in front of the crowd, miming a story. They wore jaguar masks, crocodile masks, and bird masks. They bowed to two regal-looking men who sat on thronelike wooden seats on a platform. The two men wore enormous headdresses and animal-skin capes. Behind them stood a row of feathered warriors. 跳舞的人跳着舞，音乐家们演奏着音乐，演员们在人群面前走来走去，模仿着一个故事。 他们戴着美洲豹面具、鳄鱼面具和鸟的面具。 他们向坐在平台上、看起来像王座的木椅上的两位高贵男子鞠躬。 这两个人戴着巨大的头饰，还披着动物皮制成的斗篷。 他们身后站着一排戴着羽毛装饰的战士。

“I’ll bet those two guys are supposed to be ancient Mayan kings,” whispered Annie, “and the ones behind them must be their bodyguards. This is spectacular!” “我敢打赌那两个人应该是古代的玛雅国王，”安妮小声说，“他们身后的那些人一定是他们的保镖。 这太精彩了！

Jack had to agree, even though none of it made sense to him. He looked around at the crowd watching the show. He noticed that the women and girls wore colorful dresses and large pieces of jewelry. The men and boys wore loincloths or feathered robes. Many men wore headdresses. 杰克不得不同意，尽管这一切对他来说都不太明白。 他环顾四周，看着观看表演的人群。 他注意到妇女和女孩们穿着色彩鲜艳的裙子，戴着大大的首饰。 男人们和男孩们穿着围裙或者有羽毛的长袍。 很多男人都戴着头饰。

“Don’t you think it’s a little weird that everyone in the audience is wearing a costume?” Jack whispered. “你不觉得观众都穿着戏服有点奇怪吗？”杰克小声说。

“I love it,” said Annie. “Maybe this show is like a reality show, where even the audience plays a part. I’ll bet when you buy a ticket, you get a costume. I’ll find out.” She stepped over to some young boys in feathered robes who were watching the dancers. “Excuse me,” she said. “我喜欢，”安妮说。 “也许这个节目就像真人秀一样，连观众都要参与表演。 我打赌你买票的时候，会得到一件戏服。 我要去打听一下。”她走到一些穿着有羽毛长袍的年轻男孩身边，他们正在看着跳舞的人。 “请问一下，”她说。

A couple of boys turned around and looked at her. They jumped back as if frightened. Before Annie could ask about their costumes, one of them darted to a woman, grabbed her hand, and pointed to Annie. The woman cried out in alarm. 有好几个男孩转过身来看她。 他们吓得跳了回去。 安妮还没来得及问他们的服装，其中一个人突然跑向一位女士，抓住她的手，指着安妮。 那位女士惊叫起来。

“Whoa, what’s that about?” said Jack. “哇，这是怎么回事？”杰克说。

“I think they’re just acting,” Annie said. “我想他们只是在表演，”安妮说。

More and more people were turning to look at Jack and Annie. Some whispered and pointed. All had expressions of fear and wonder. 越来越多的人开始朝杰克和安妮看去。 有些人小声议论，还指指点点。 每个人脸上都带着恐惧和惊奇的表情。

“I don’t think these people are acting,” said Jack. “我觉得这些人不是在表演，”杰克说。

“Really?” said Annie. “真的吗？”安妮说。

“Yeah, this feels really weird.” “是啊，这感觉真的很奇怪。”

The music stopped. The musicians put down their instruments and stared with the rest of the crowd. Several men with spears started toward Jack and Annie. 音乐停了下来。 乐手们放下乐器，和人群一起惊讶地望着他们。 几个拿着长矛的男人朝杰克和安妮走来。

“Let’s go!” said Jack. “我们走吧！”，杰克说。

“Wait,” said Annie. “It’s part of the show! Don’t you want to play a role in it?” “等等，”，安妮说。 “这是表演的一部分！ 难道你不想要参与其中吗？”

“No! Let’s go!” said Jack. He grabbed Annie’s hand and pulled her along, heading back the way they’d come. As he hurried toward the wall, he heard shouting behind them. “不！ 我们走吧！”，杰克说。 他抓住安妮的手，拉着她往他们来的路上走。 他赶紧朝墙那边跑，听到他们身后有人在喊叫。

When they reached the narrow opening, Jack looked over his shoulder. The feathered men with spears were coming right at them. A few curious children were following the spear carriers. 当他们到达狭窄的入口时，杰克回头看了看。 拿着矛的羽毛人正朝他们冲过来。 有几个好奇的孩子正跟着拿矛的人走。

Jack frantically tried to push the bag through the opening ahead of him, but it got stuck. He shoved as hard as he could, but the bag didn’t budge. 杰克拼命想把袋子推到前面去，但袋子卡住了。 他用尽全力去推，但袋子一动不动。

“Don’t you want to find out what’s going on?” said Annie. “你不想要弄清楚到底发生了什么吗？”安妮说。

“No! I just want to get away from this crazy show!” said Jack. “I don’t want to perform in it!” “不！ 我只是想离开这个疯狂的表演！”杰克说。 “我不想参加这个表演！”

“Too late!” said Annie. “太晚了！”安妮说道。

Jack looked back. The feathered warriors had nearly reached them. Jack pulled hard on the bag, yanking with such force that he fell backward, and the bag landed on top of him. A moment later, spear carriers were standing over him. Several surrounded Annie, too. 杰克回过头。 那些长着羽毛的战士已经快追上他们了。 杰克用力拉袋子，用这么大的力气，他往后一仰，袋子掉在他身上了。 过了一会儿，举着矛的人站在他上面了。 还有几个人也围住了安妮。

“Hi!” said Annie. “This is all part of the song-and-dance spectacular, right?” “你好！”安妮说道。 “这都是歌与舞的精彩表演的一部分，对吧？”

None of the men answered. 那些人都没有回答。

“We didn’t know where to get tickets,” she said. “We, uh, we thought maybe we could pay on the way out?” “我们不知道在哪里能买到票，”她说。 “呃，我们想 maybe 在离开的时候付钱？”

Again, no one answered. Instead the spear carriers turned to one another and spoke in frantic whispers. “Who are they?” “What are they?” “Where do they come from?” 还是没有人回答。 相反，长矛手们转而互相低声慌乱地交谈起来。 “他们是谁？”“他们是什么人？”“他们从哪儿来的？”

A girl about Annie’s age stepped forward. Like Annie, she had long, thick braids and bangs, but her hair was shiny black. The girl stared at Annie curiously and then smiled a big smile. 一个和安妮年龄相仿的女孩走了出来。 就像安妮一样，她也有长长的、厚实的辫子和刘海，但她的头发是闪亮的黑色。 女孩好奇地盯着安妮，然后露出了一个大大的微笑。

“Why did they stop the show?” Annie asked the girl. “What’s going on?” “为什么他们要停止表演？”安妮问那个女孩。 “发生了什么事？”

The girl kept staring at Annie. “Who are you?” she asked. Before Annie could answer, a woman rushed forward, grabbed the girl, and pulled her away. 女孩继续盯着安妮。 “你是谁？”她问。 安妮还没来得及回答，一个女人冲上前去，抓住女孩，把她拉走了。

One of the warriors pointed his spear at Jack. “Come,” he said. “To the House of Columns.” 一名战士用矛指着杰克。 “来吧，”他说。 “去柱子房子。”

“Um…actually, we’d rather go to the hotel zone,” Jack said. “呃……其实，我们更想去酒店区，”杰克说。

The man stared at him. 那人盯着他看。

“But whatever’s closest,” said Jack. “We’re not picky.” “不管是什么地方，”杰克说。 “我们不挑。”

The warrior gently prodded Jack with the tip of his spear. “Go,” he said. 战士用矛尖轻轻戳了戳杰克。 “走吧，”他说。

Without another word, Jack and Annie began marching across the grounds. If this is all part of the show, Jack thought, these guys are taking it way too seriously. 没有再说什么，杰克和安妮开始穿过院子。 如果这是演出的一部分，杰克想，这些人也太认真了。

Followed by the spear carriers, Jack and Annie walked through the blue moonlight, until they came to a stone building with columns. The head warrior ushered them up uneven steps, opened a heavy wooden door, and motioned for them to enter. 在举着矛的人后面，杰克和安妮走在蓝色的月光下，直到他们来到一座有柱子的石制建筑前。 头领战士让他们走上不平的台阶，打开一扇沉重的木门，示意他们进去。

“Wait for the king,” the warrior said. “等国王，”战士说。

The cold, empty room had only a single window, which let in a shaft of moonlight. The warrior closed the heavy door, leaving Jack and Annie alone in the House of Columns. 这个又冷又空的房间只有一个窗户，让一束月光照了进来。 战士关上沉重的门，留下杰克和安妮独自在柱子大厅里。
