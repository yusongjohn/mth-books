2. Cozumel 2. 科苏梅尔

Afternoon sunlight flooded into the tree house. Jack heard the sound of waves softly lapping the shore. He and Annie looked out the window. The tree house had landed in a palm tree overlooking snow-white sand and a turquoise-blue sea. Near the beach was the stone pyramid pictured on the cover of the travel guide. 下午的阳光照进了树屋。 杰克听到海浪轻轻拍打岸边的声音。 他和安妮看向窗外。 树屋降落在一棵棕榈树上，俯瞰着雪白的沙滩和碧蓝的海水。 在海滩附近，有一座石头金字塔，和旅行指南封面上的图片一模一样。

“Cozumel,” Jack said. “科苏梅尔，”杰克说。

“Paradise,” Annie said. “天堂，”安妮说。

“Where is everyone?” said Jack, looking around. There were no other tourists—no swimmers, surfers, or sunbathers. There were no sightseers. There were no vehicles parked near the Mayan pyramid. “这里怎么没有人？”杰克环顾四周，问道。 没有其他游客——没有游泳的人，冲浪的人，也没有晒太阳的人。 这里没有游客。 玛雅金字塔附近没有停着车辆。

“I don’t know…,” Annie said. “I thought there’d be, you know, luxury spa hotels and fancy restaurants.” “我不知道……”，安妮说。 “我以为这里会有，你知道的，豪华水疗酒店和高档餐厅。”

“It’s weird,” said Jack. “这太奇怪了，”杰克说。

“Maybe it’s just the perfect beach,” said Annie, shrugging. “也许只是完美的海滩吧，”安妮耸耸肩说。

“Yeah, but…” Jack looked through their travel guide until he found a photo that seemed to match the deserted shoreline with the pyramid. “This looks like the same place.” He read the caption: “是啊，不过……”杰克翻看他们的旅行指南，直到找到一张似乎与空旷海岸线和金字塔相匹配的照片。 “这看起来像是同一个地方。”他读了照片上的说明：

On the southwest Caribbean coast is a nature preserve. 在加勒比海西南海岸，有一个自然保护区。

“Oh, cool! We landed in a nature preserve!” said Annie. “That’s why it looks so natural.” “哦，太酷了！ 我们降落在一个自然保护区里！“安妮说。 “这就是为什么看起来这么自然。”

“Right…,” said Jack. He read on: “对……，”杰克说。他继续读下去：

Dazzling coral reefs are just a short distance offshore. Beginning snorkelers can swim without losing sight of land. 闪闪发光的珊瑚礁离海岸不远。刚开始学浮潜的人可以游泳，而不会看不到陆地。

“Yay!” said Annie. “We did come to the perfect place—a nature preserve with a coral reef, just like Randy and Jenny’s.” “太棒了！”安妮说。 “我们确实来到了一个完美的地方——一个有珊瑚礁的自然保护区，就像兰迪和珍妮的那样。”

“Yep!” said Jack. “Let’s snorkel first. Then maybe check out that pyramid. It looks cool.” “没错！”杰克说。 “我们先去浮潜吧。 然后去看看那边的金字塔。看起来很酷。”

“Good plan,” said Annie. She started down the rope ladder. “好主意，”安妮说。 她开始沿着绳梯往下爬。

Jack put the travel guide into the swim bag with their snorkel gear. He hung the bag on his shoulder and followed Annie. He stepped off the ladder and stood with her in the shade of the tree. 杰克把旅行指南和他们的浮潜装备一起放进游泳包里。 他把袋子挂在肩膀上，然后跟着安妮走了。 他从梯子上下来，和她一起站在树的阴影下。

“Oh, man…,” said Jack, sighing. “哦，天哪……”，杰克叹了口气说。

A fresh, salty ocean breeze wafted over them. White sand sparkled in the warm sunlight. 一股新鲜的、咸咸的海风拂过他们。 白色的沙子在温暖的阳光下闪闪发光。

“This is a good view,” said Annie. “I’ll take a picture. No, even better, a video.” She reached into the pocket of her tunic and pulled out their cell phone. Pointing the phone at the beach, she clicked the “record” button and spoke loudly: “Jack and I are in Cozumel, at a beautiful beach on a nature preserve, and this—” “这里的景色真好，”安妮说。 “我要拍一张照片。 不，更好的是拍视频。”她伸手到长袍的口袋里，拿出他们的手机。 她把手机对准海滩，按下“录制”按钮，大声说道：“杰克和我正在科苏梅尔，位于一个自然保护区的美丽海滩上，而这个——”

“Hey, stop!” said Jack. “What if Mom and Dad see that? You have to erase it.” “嘿，别拍了！”杰克说。 “如果妈妈和爸爸看到那怎么办？” 你必须把它擦掉。”

“Oh, you’re right!” said Annie. She laughed as she deleted the video. “No big deal, Mom and Dad, we just took a little detour to Mexico on our way home from the lake.” She put the phone in Jack’s bag. “Okay! No camera on this trip.” “哦，你没错！”安妮说道。 她一边笑着，一边删掉了视频。 “没什么大不了的，爸爸妈妈，我们只是从湖边回家的路上，顺便去墨西哥绕了小路。”她把手机放进了杰克的包里。 “好呀！ 这次旅行不带相机。”

“Let’s hit the water,” said Jack. As they started across the beach, their flip-flops sank into the fine, sugary sand. Seagulls cawed overhead. “让我们去水里玩吧，”杰克说。 当他们开始穿过海滩时，他们的人字拖鞋陷进了细腻如糖的沙子里。 海鸥在头顶上嘎嘎地叫着。

“It’s easier to go barefoot,” said Annie. She and Jack stopped and put their flip-flops into Jack’s bag. “光着脚走路更容易，”安妮说。 她和杰克停下来，把他们的拖鞋放进杰克的包里。

“Hey, what’s that?” Annie pointed to a raft lying on the sand. “嘿，那是什么？”安妮指着沙滩上躺着的一只木筏。

“Whoa,” said Jack. They hurried over to the raft. It was made of bamboo poles lashed together with leather strips. A paddle lay beside it. Jack looked around for its owner, but no one was in sight. “Who left it here?” “哇，”杰克说。 他们赶紧跑过去看那只木筏。 它是用竹竿用皮带绑在一起做成的。 旁边还躺着一根桨。 杰克四处张望寻找它的主人，但周围没有人。 “谁把它放在这里的？”

“It must belong to the nature preserve,” said Annie. “I’ll bet it’s for tourists to use. Why else would it just be lying here? It’s got a paddle and everything. Want to try it?” “它一定是自然保护区留下的，”安妮说。 “我打赌这是给游客用的。 要不然它怎么会就这样躺在这里呢？ 它有个桨，还带一切呢。想试试看吗？

“What? Put it in the water?” said Jack. “什么？ “把它放进水里？”杰克说。

“Yes,” said Annie. “It’ll be fun! Say yes.” “是的，”安妮说。 “这会很有趣！说好哦。”

“No!” Jack said. “不！”杰克说。

“Oh, Jack. Please! We can paddle out to the reef. Look how calm the water is! It’ll be super-fun,” said Annie. “哦，杰克。求你了！ 我们可以划船去珊瑚礁。 看，水多平静啊！ 这会超级好玩，”安妮说。

Jack started to say no again, but he stopped. It would actually be super- fun, he thought. “Well, if we take it, we have to stay close to shore, and keep a lookout for its owner, just in case—” 杰克又想说不，但停住了。 他觉得其实真的会超级好玩。 “好吧，如果我们拿走它，我们必须靠近岸边，还要留意它的主人，以防——”

“Okay, okay,” said Annie. “I’ll row!” “好吧，好吧，”安妮说。 “我来划船！”，

She grabbed the paddle, and Jack dragged the raft over the wet sand. He pushed it from the bubbling sea-foam into the cool, clear water. 她抓起了船桨，杰克把小船拖过了湿沙。 他把它从冒泡的海泡沫中推入清凉清澈的水中。

“She floats!” said Annie. “它漂起来了！”，安妮说。

Gentle waves lapped the sides of the raft. “Okay, you hop on first,” said Jack. He put their bag on the raft and then held it steady while Annie climbed onto the bamboo poles. The raft kept floating. 温柔的海浪轻轻拍打着筏子的两侧。 “好吧，你先上。”杰克说。 他把他们的袋子放在筏子上，然后稳住筏子，让安妮爬到竹竿上。 筏子继续漂浮着。

“Good, good…now me,” said Jack as he climbed aboard. “好，好……现在轮到我了。”杰克说着爬上了筏子。

The raft dipped a bit lower in the water, but it easily held them both. 筏子在水里稍微下沉了一点，但它轻松地载着他们两个人。

“Yay! Now let’s find the reef!” said Annie. “I’ll take us there.” As she began to paddle, the breeze helped push the raft over small ocean swells. “This is a great raft!” “太棒了！ 现在我们去找珊瑚礁吧！”安妮说。 “我来带路。”当她开始划桨时，微风帮助筏子顺利地穿过海上的小浪。 “这真是个很棒的筏子！”

“I’ll bet the ancient Mayans made them just like this,” said Jack. “我敢打赌，古代玛雅人就是这么做的，”杰克说。

“So who were the ancient Mayans exactly?” said Annie. “那古代玛雅人到底是谁呢？”安妮说。

“I don’t know a lot about them,” said Jack. “But I know they had a huge civilization over a thousand years ago. They built pyramids and designed a special calendar.” “我对他们的了解不多，”杰克说。 “但我知道他们一千多年前有一个非常庞大的文明。 他们建造了金字塔，还设计了一种特殊的日历。”

“Well, they sure made good rafts,” said Annie. As she pushed the paddle through the water, the raft moved farther out to sea. “嗯，他们做的木筏真的很棒，”安妮说。 当她把桨插入水中时，木筏向海中漂得更远了。

About a hundred yards out, Jack held up his hand. “Okay, let’s stop here,” he said. “We don’t want to get too far away from land. Let’s look underwater for the reef. The book said it was near the shore.” 大约漂出一百码时，杰克举起了手。 “好吧，我们就在这里停下，”他说。 “我们可不想离陆地太远。 让我们到水下看看珊瑚礁吧。 书上说它在靠近岸边的地方。

“Right,” said Annie. “You snorkel first. I’ll stay with the raft.” “对，”安妮说。 “你先用呼吸管， 我留在筏子上。”

“You sure?” “你确定吗？”

“Sure.” “确定。”

“Thanks.” Jack reached into the swim bag. Before he pulled out their snorkel gear, he looked up Cozumel, coral reefs in the travel guide. He read aloud: “谢谢。” 杰克伸手进了游泳包。 在拿出他们的水肺装备之前，他看了看旅行指南里关于科苏梅尔和珊瑚礁的部分。他大声读道：

The coral reefs of Cozumel are visited by an average of 1,500 tourists a day. Coral— 科苏梅尔的珊瑚礁每天平均有1500名游客参观。珊瑚—

“Really?” Annie interrupted. “I don’t see one single tourist.” “真的吗？” 安妮打断了。 “我一个游客都没看到。”

“I know. I don’t understand that,” said Jack. He kept reading: “我知道。 我不明白那是什么，”杰克说。他继续读下去：

Coral is made of animals, not plants. Since a coral reef is very delicate and takes a very long time to grow, swimmers and snorkelers must avoid touching it or kicking it with their flippers. 珊瑚是由动物组成的，不是植物。因为珊瑚礁非常脆弱，生长需要很长时间，所以游泳者和浮潜者必须避免触摸它，或者用脚蹼踢它。

“Makes sense,” said Jack, closing the book. “Look. Don’t touch. Just like at a museum.” “说得通，”杰克说，合上了书。 “看，别碰。 就像在博物馆里一样。”

“Got it,” said Annie. “明白了，”安妮说。

Jack put his glasses and the travel guide in the inside pocket of the swim bag. Then he unpacked all their swimming gear. While Annie held the raft steady with the paddle, Jack buckled on one of the life vests, squeezed his feet into flippers, and attached the snorkel to his face mask. Finally he pulled on the mask, covering his eyes and nose, and adjusted the strap. He inhaled through his nose, causing the mask to create a vacuum so water couldn’t seep in. 杰克把他的眼镜和旅行指南放进了游泳包的内袋里。 然后他把他们的所有游泳装备都拿了出来。 安妮用桨稳住橡皮艇，杰克系上了一件救生衣，把脚踩进脚蹼里，把呼吸管连接到他的面镜上。 最后，他戴上了面具，遮住了眼睛和鼻子，然后调整了头带。 他通过鼻子吸气，让面具形成真空，这样水就进不去了。

“Balance me, so the raft doesn’t flip over,” Jack said to Annie. “帮我保持平衡，这样小筏子就不会翻倒了，”杰克对安妮说。

Annie balanced the raft while Jack sat on the edge. As he slipped down into the sea, his life vest kept him from going under. Bobbing in the water, he placed the end of the snorkel tube into his mouth. 安妮让小筏子保持平衡，而杰克坐在边缘。 当他滑入海中时，救生衣让他不会沉下去。 在水里上下起伏时，他把呼吸管的一端放进嘴里。

“Have fun!” Annie called. “玩得开心点！”安妮喊道。

Jack waved to her. Then he started swimming facedown along the surface of the water, gently kicking his flippers. Looking through his mask, he saw only a few fish at first. But then he saw more…and more…and more! Suddenly he couldn’t believe his eyes. 杰克朝她挥手。 然后他开始面朝下在水面上游泳，轻轻踩着脚蹼。 透过面具望去，他一开始只看到几条鱼。 但是，他看见了更多……更多……更多！ 突然，他简直不敢相信自己的眼睛。
