5. Warships on the Waves 5. 海上的战船

“We found our story!” said Jack. “我们找到故事了！”杰克说。

“Yay!” said Annie. “耶！”安妮说。

“Indeed,” said Brother Patrick. “But alas, Brother Michael has not yet completed his work. You will have to return for it.” “确实如此。”帕特里克修士说。 “可是唉，迈克尔修士还没有完成他的工作。 你们得再回来取它。”

“Oh, shoot,” said Annie. “哎呀，糟糕。”安妮说。

Jack was disappointed, too. 杰克也很失望。

“I don’t know if we’ll be able to come back,” he said. “我不知道我们还能不能再回来。”他说。

“I don’t know if we can even leave without the story,” said Annie. “我不知道没有这个故事我们能不能离开。”安妮说。

Brother Patrick looked puzzled. 帕特里克修士一脸困惑。

The two kids looked at each other, then back at Brother Patrick. It was too hard to explain about the tree house and how the magic worked. 两个孩子互相看了看，又回头看着帕特里克修士。 要解释树屋和魔法是怎么运作的太难了。

Jack shrugged. 杰克耸了耸肩。

“We’ll just have to try,” he said. “我们只好试试看了。”他说。

Outside, the church bell began ringing. 外面，教堂的钟开始敲响。

“It is time now for our sunrise prayers,” said Brother Patrick. “Will you join us?” “现在是我们日出祷告的时间了。”帕特里克修士说。 “你们要加入我们吗？”

“Thanks, but we better try to go home now,” said Jack. “谢谢，不过我们最好现在就设法回家。”杰克说。

Brother Patrick nodded and led them into the garden. When he opened the gate, they stopped. 帕特里克修士点点头，领他们进了花园。 他打开大门时，他们停了下来。

The horizon glowed pink and purple. The sun had started to rise. 地平线闪耀着粉红和紫色。 太阳开始升起。

No one spoke as the great, fiery ball rose slowly over the ocean. 没有人说话，那巨大的火球慢慢从海上升起。

Finally, Brother Patrick broke the silence. “Shine, O light of the sun,” he said softly, “on this day filled with wonder.” 终于，帕特里克修士打破了沉默。 “照耀吧，哦，太阳之光，”他轻声说，“照耀在这充满奇迹的一天。”

“That’s beautiful,” said Annie. “真美。”安妮说。

Jack smiled. He agreed. 杰克笑了。他同意。

Brother Patrick turned to them. “It is such sights as this that inspire our bookmaking,” he said. “Now go, and may God be with you on your voyage home.” 帕特里克修士转向他们。 “正是这样的景象赋予我们制作书籍的灵感。”他说。 “现在去吧，愿上帝在你们回家的航程中与你们同在。”

“Thanks,” said Jack and Annie. “谢谢。”杰克和安妮说。

“Do you need me to guide you to your boat?” he asked. “需要我带你们去船上吗？”他问。

“I don’t think so,” said Jack. “我想不用了。”杰克说。

“Follow the path to the top of the cliff,” said Brother Patrick. “Then use my rope to help you down the steps.” “顺着小路走到悬崖顶。”帕特里克修士说。 “然后用我的绳子帮你们下台阶。”

“Okay,” said Annie. “Bye!” And she went through the gate. “好。”安妮说。 “再见！”她走出大门。

Jack wanted to go home, but he hated leaving the monastery. It was filled with people doing his favorite things: reading and learning. 杰克想回家，可又舍不得离开修道院。 那里满是做他最喜欢的事的人：阅读和学习。

“I really like it here,” he said to Brother Patrick. “我真的很喜欢这儿。”他对帕特里克修士说。

“I’m glad. But you must go now, while the weather is with you,” said the monk. “Everything can change in an instant.” “我很高兴。 但你们必须现在就走，趁着天公还作美。”修士说。 “一切都可能在瞬间改变。”

Then Brother Patrick turned and went into the church. 然后帕特里克修士转身进了教堂。

Jack hurried out the gate. Before he went further, he stopped and pulled out his notebook. 杰克匆匆走出大门。 再往前走之前，他停下来抽出笔记本。

He quickly made two lists: 他很快列了两个清单：

图中文字：To make a book

sheepskin

To make paint

goose quill paints

earth plants 图中文字：要做书用羊皮纸，做颜料用鹅毛笔、泥土和植物。

“Come on!” Annie called from the top of the steps. “快点！”安妮从台阶顶喊道。

“Coming,” Jack called. “来了。”杰克喊道。

He put away his notebook and ran along the dirt path to the edge of the cliff. 他收好笔记本，沿着土路跑向悬崖边。

Overhead, flocks of gulls circled in the purple sky. Their cries sounded like screams. 头顶上，一群群海鸥在紫色的天空中盘旋。 它们的叫声听起来像尖叫。

“What’s wrong with them?” Jack said. “它们怎么了？”杰克说。

“Maybe they always do this at sunrise,” said Annie. “Let me go first.” “也许它们总是在日出时这样。”安妮说。 “让我先下。”

Clutching the rope, she started down the steps. 她紧紧抓着绳子，开始走下台阶。

Jack grabbed the rope and started down. The birds’ cries went on. They worried him. They sounded like warnings. 杰克抓住绳子开始往下。 鸟儿的叫声还在继续。 那些声音听起来像警告。

Jack reached the rocky ledge and let go of the rope. 杰克走到岩石突出的平台上，松开了绳子。

“Let’s go!” Annie called from the tree house. “咱们走！”安妮从树屋喊道。

Jack looked at the horizon one last time. 杰克最后看了一眼地平线。

His heart nearly stopped. A ship was outlined against the sky! Behind it, he saw two smaller ships. 他的心几乎停住了。 一艘船的轮廓映在天空上！ 在它后面，他看见两艘较小的船。

As the ships came into view, their bright sails were filled with wind and their serpent prows blazed in the new sunlight. 当那些船映入眼帘时，明亮的船帆鼓满了风，蛇头船首在初升的阳光里闪耀。

“Oh, no,” Jack whispered. “Vikings!” “哦，不。”杰克小声说。 “维京人！”
