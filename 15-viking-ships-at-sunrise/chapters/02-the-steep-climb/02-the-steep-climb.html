2. The Steep Climb 2. 陡峭的攀登

Jack opened his eyes. 杰克睁开眼睛。

The light was still gray, but the air was even damper and colder than in Frog Creek. 光线仍是灰色的，可空气比蛙溪镇更潮湿、更冷。

“Wow, I’m in a long dress,” said Annie. “It’s scratchy. Hey, I’ve got a little purse on my belt. It has my library card in it!” “哇，我穿着长裙。”安妮说。 “穿着扎人。 嘿，我腰带上还有个小钱包。 我的图书卡就在口袋里！”

Jack looked down at his own clothes. 杰克低头看着自己的衣服。

He was wearing a shirt and trousers, made of heavy wool. He also wore leather slippers. And in place of his backpack was a leather bag. 他穿着一件衬衫和一条厚羊毛裤子。 他还穿着皮拖鞋。 取代他背包的是一个皮袋子。

“Wow,” said Annie, looking out the window. “This really looks like the Dark Ages.” “哇。”安妮看着窗外说。 “这儿真的像黑暗时代。”

Jack looked out, too. He couldn’t see anything through the mist. 杰克也往外看。 透过雾，他什么也看不见。

“It’s just because the sun’s not up yet,” he said. “I’d better check the book.” “只是因为太阳还没升起来。”他说。 “我最好查查书。”

Annie handed the Ireland book to Jack. He opened it and read aloud: 安妮把爱尔兰书递给杰克。 他打开书，大声读道：

The early Middle Ages were once known as the “Dark Ages” because learning and culture nearly vanished throughout Europe. Scholars today praise the brave Irish monks who helped keep Western civilization alive. 中世纪早期曾经被称为‘黑暗时代’，因为那时候欧洲的学习和文化几乎消失了。现在学者们赞扬勇敢的爱尔兰修士，他们帮助保存了西方文明。

“What do ‘civilization’ and ‘monks’ mean?” asked Annie. “‘文明’和‘修道士’是什么意思？”安妮问。

“I think civilization is when people have books and art and good manners,” said Jack. “Monks are religious people who spend their time praying and reading and helping people.” “我觉得文明就是人们有书籍、艺术和良好的礼仪。”杰克说。 “修道士是宗教信徒，把时间花在祈祷、阅读和帮助别人上。”

“Well, I don’t see any civilization or monks out there,” said Annie, pointing at the mist. “嗯，那儿我看不见任何文明，也看不见修道士。”安妮指着雾说。

Jack pulled out his notebook. He wrote: 杰克抽出笔记本，写道：

图中文字：brave monks in Ireland 图中文字：勇敢的爱尔兰僧侣

Then he looked at Annie. “If we find civilization, I think we’ll find the lost story,” he said. 然后他看着安妮。 “如果我们找到文明，我想就会找到失落的故事。”他说。

“Let’s go,” said Annie. She lifted her skirt and climbed out the window. “咱们走吧。”安妮说。 她提起裙子，爬出窗户。

Jack read more in the Ireland book. 在印刷术发明之前，所有的书都得用手书写和抄写。

The monks copied the ancient writings of the Western world. Before printing was invented, all books had to be written and copied by hand. 和尚们抄写了西方世界古老的文字。在印刷术发明之前，所有的书都必须用手写和抄写。

“Hey, we’re on a cliff!” Annie called from outside. “Above the ocean!” “嘿，我们在悬崖上！”安妮在外面喊道。 “在海洋上方！”

“Be careful!” said Jack. “小心！”杰克说。

He stuffed the Ireland book and his notebook into his leather bag. Then he climbed out the window. 他把爱尔兰的书和笔记本塞进皮袋里。 然后他也爬出窗户。

Annie was peering over the edge. Jack looked, too. 安妮正探头往悬崖边看。杰克也看了看。

There was a rocky shore twenty feet below. Waves slapped against the rocks. Sea gulls swooped and glided above the sea. 下面二十英尺处是岩石海岸。 海浪拍打着岩石。 海鸥在海面上俯冲、滑翔。

“It doesn’t look like there’s any civilization down there,” said Jack. “下面看起来不像有什么文明。”杰克说。

“Maybe we should climb those,” said Annie. She pointed to steep steps cut into the cliff. “也许我们应该爬那些台阶。”安妮说。 她指着开凿在悬崖上的陡峭台阶。

Jack looked up. The cliff also rose above them in the mist. 杰克抬起头。 悬崖也在雾中耸立在他们上方。

“We better wait till the sun comes up,” he said. “我们最好等到太阳升起来。”他说。

“Let’s just go super slow,” said Annie. She started up the stone steps. “咱们就特别慢地爬。”安妮说。 她开始登上石阶。

“Wait, Annie!” said Jack. “They might be slippery.” “等等，安妮！”杰克说。 “台阶可能很滑。”

“Whoa!” she said, almost falling backward. “I tripped on my darn dress!” “哇！”她说，差点向后摔倒。 “我被这讨厌的裙子绊了一下！”

“I told you to wait,” Jack said. “It’s too dangerous.” “我告诉你等一等。”杰克说。 “太危险了。”

Just then something fell from above. 就在这时，一样东西从上面掉下来。

“Watch it!” said Jack. He put his hands over his head. “小心！”杰克说。 他用手护住头。

“Hey, it’s a rope!” said Annie. “嘿，是绳子！”安妮说。

Jack saw a thick rope dangling down the stairs. 杰克看见一根粗绳子垂在台阶上。

“Where’d this come from?” he asked. “这是哪儿来的？”他问。

“It’s like when Morgan dropped the ladder to us,” said Annie. “I bet someone’s trying to help us.” “就像摩根把梯子扔给我们时一样。”安妮说。 “我敢肯定有人在帮我们。”

“Yeah, but who?” said Jack. “对，可是谁扔的？”杰克说。

“Let’s find out,” said Annie. She grabbed the rope. “I’ll use it first. Once I’m at the top, you can come after me.” “咱们去看看。”安妮说。 她抓住绳子。 “我先用。 我到了顶上，你再上来。”

“Okay, but hurry,” he said. “And be very careful.” Jack waited as Annie started climbing up the steps. “好，可要快。”他说。 “而且要特别小心。”杰克等着，安妮开始往台阶上爬。

Annie held on to the rope as she climbed slowly up the stairs. Soon she vanished over the top of the cliff. 安妮紧紧抓住绳子，慢慢爬上台阶。 很快她就消失在悬崖顶上。

“What’s up there?” Jack shouted. But his voice was lost in the sound of the waves. “上面有什么？”杰克喊道。 可他的声音被浪声淹没了。

He grabbed the rope and started up the steep steps. At the top of the cliff, he pulled himself over the edge. 他抓住绳子，开始登上陡峭的台阶。 到了悬崖顶，他把自己拉过边缘。

“Aha!” boomed a deep, jolly voice. “It’s another little invader!” “啊哈！”一个低沉欢快的声音轰然响起。 “又来了一个小入侵者！”
