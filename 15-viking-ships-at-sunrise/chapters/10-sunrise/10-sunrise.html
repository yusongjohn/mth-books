10. Sunrise 10. 日出

“Boy, I’m glad to be back in my jeans,” said Annie. “哎呀，真高兴又穿回牛仔裤了。”安妮说。

Jack opened his eyes. He still felt damp. But he was glad to be wearing his jeans again, too. 杰克睁开眼睛。 他仍觉得湿漉漉的。 可他也高兴又穿回了牛仔裤。

“Welcome home,” said Morgan. She was standing in the shadows. “Are you all right?” “欢迎回家。”摩根说。 她站在阴影里。 “你们没事吧？”

“Of course!” piped Annie. “当然！”安妮尖声说。

“And we brought back the lost book,” said Jack. “我们还带回了失落的书。”杰克说。

He reached in his backpack and took out the jeweled book of Brother Michael. He handed it to Morgan. 他伸手进背包，取出迈克尔修士那本镶着宝石的书。 他把它递给摩根。

The enchantress sighed. She ran her hand over the sparkling cover. 女魔法师叹了口气。 她用手轻轻抚过那闪亮的封面。

“A great work of art,” she said. “一件伟大的艺术品。”她说。

Morgan then put the book next to the scroll from Roman times and the bamboo book from ancient China. 摩根接着把书放在罗马时代的卷轴和古代中国的竹简书旁边。

“I’m afraid the story you wanted is not all there,” said Jack. “Brother Michael didn’t get the chance to finish it.” “恐怕你要的那个故事并不完整。”杰克说。 “迈克尔修士没来得及把它画完。”

Morgan nodded. 摩根点了点头。

“I know,” she said. “Sadly, we have only bits and pieces of many wonderful old stories.” “我知道。”她说。 “可惜的是，许多精彩的古老故事，我们只剩下零零碎碎的片段。”

“What’s the story about?” asked Annie. “故事讲的是什么？”安妮问。

“It’s an ancient Irish tale about a great serpent named Sarph,” said Morgan. “这是一个古老的爱尔兰传说，讲的是一条叫萨尔夫的巨蛇。”摩根说。

“He saved us by pushing our ship over the stormy waves!” Annie said. “它推着我们的船越过狂风巨浪，救了我们！”安妮说。

“Sarph was a huge, ugly monster,” said Jack. “萨尔夫是一头又大又丑的怪物。”杰克说。

Morgan smiled. 摩根笑了。

“Sometimes monsters can be heroes,” she said. “有时候怪物也可以是英雄。”她说。

“What about Vikings?” asked Jack. “那维京人呢？”杰克问。

“Oh, most definitely, Vikings could be heroes, too,” she said. “In fact, once the Vikings settled down, they became more than just a warrior people. They actually added a lot to civilization.” “哦，当然可以，维京人也可以是英雄。”她说。 “事实上，维京人一旦安定下来，就不再仅仅是一个尚武的民族。 他们其实为文明增添了很多东西。”

“We found civilization on our trip,” said Annie. “我们在旅途中找到了文明。”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack, “in the library of the monastery.” “对。”杰克说，“在修道院的图书馆里。”

Morgan smiled again. 摩根又笑了。

“Their library was a light in the Dark Ages, wasn’t it?” she said. “他们的图书馆是黑暗时代的一盏灯，对吧？”她说。

Jack nodded. He thought of Brother Michael and the other monks making their beautiful books by candlelight. 杰克点了点头。 他想起迈克尔修士和其他修道士在烛光下制作那些美丽的书。

“Thank you also for your great courage,” Morgan said. “You are both heroes, too.” “也谢谢你们巨大的勇气。”摩根说。 “你们俩也是英雄。”

Jack smiled shyly. 杰克羞怯地笑了。

“Go home now and rest,” said Morgan. “现在回家休息吧。”摩根说。

“Bye,” Jack and Annie said together. “再见。”杰克和安妮一起说。

They started down the tree house ladder. The sky was turning pink and gold. 他们开始顺着树屋梯子往下走。 天空正变成粉红和金色。

When they reached the ground, Morgan called out to them, “Come back in two weeks. I need you to find one more lost book.” 他们下到地面时，摩根朝他们喊道：“两个星期后再来。 我需要你们再找一本失落的书。”

“Where is it?” said Jack. “在哪儿？”杰克说。

“Ancient Greece,” said Morgan. “A place with the highest civilization— and the first Olympic games.” “古希腊。”摩根说。 “一个拥有最高文明的地方——还有最早的奥林匹克运动会。”

“Oh, wow!” said Annie. “哦，哇！”安妮说。

Jack was thrilled. He had always wanted to go to ancient Greece. 杰克高兴极了。 他一直想去古希腊。

Jack and Annie took off through the woods. 杰克和安妮匆匆穿过树林离开了。

The sun was rising when they got to their front porch. 他们走到前门廊时，太阳正在升起。

Annie opened the front door. She stuck her head inside and listened. 安妮打开前门。 她把头探进去听。

“Everything’s quiet,” she whispered. “I think Mom and Dad are still sleeping.” “一切都很安静。”她小声说。 “我想爸爸妈妈还在睡觉。”

She moved quietly inside. 她悄悄走进去。

Jack turned back to watch the red sun rise through a clear blue sky. 杰克转过身，看着红日从晴朗的蓝天中升起。

He thought about how it was the same sun that had risen in Ireland—over a thousand years ago. 他想，这和一千多年前在爱尔兰升起的是同一个太阳。

“Shine, O light of the sun,” Jack whispered, “on this day filled with wonder.” “照耀吧，哦，太阳之光，”杰克轻声说，“照耀在这充满奇迹的一天。”

Then he slipped inside his quiet house. 然后他悄悄走进安静的家。
