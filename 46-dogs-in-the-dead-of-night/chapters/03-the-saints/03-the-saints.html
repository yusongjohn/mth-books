3. The Saints 3. 圣徒们

When they reached the monastery, the monks lowered the two stretchers to the ground. They helped Jack and Annie to their feet and then led them up a short flight of steps to a big wooden door. 当他们到达修道院时，修士们把两个担架放到了地上。 他们扶起杰克和安妮，然后带他们走上一段台阶，来到一扇大木门前。

“You okay?” Jack asked Annie. “你还好吗？”杰克问安妮。

“Yes,” she said. “You?” “是的，”她说。 “你呢？”

“Just cold,” Jack said, shivering. “只是有点冷，”杰克说，发着抖。

One monk rang a large bell outside the door. Another handed Jack his leather bag. “We found this beside you,” the man said. 一个和尚在门外敲响了一个大钟。 另一个递给了杰克他的皮袋子。 “我们是在你旁边找到这个的，”那人说。

“Oh, thanks,” said Jack. He’d forgotten all about the bag. “哦，谢谢，”杰克说。 他已经完全忘记了这个袋子。

Someone inside pulled open the door. Jack and Annie limped into a torchlit hallway with a vaulted ceiling and a wide staircase. The monks and three dogs followed. 里面有人拉开了门。 杰克和安妮一瘸一拐地走进了一条有高天花板和宽楼梯的、被火把照亮的走廊。 修道士们和三条狗跟了上来。

The monks lifted their hoods, revealing friendly faces. For the first time, Jack got a good look at the huge, panting dogs. They were Saint Bernards with white and reddish-brown fur. They had powerful heads, square muzzles, and drooping jowls. 修道士们摘下兜帽，露出友好的面孔。 这是杰克第一次好好看看这些又大又喘气的狗。 它们是圣伯纳犬，毛色是白色和红棕色的。 它们有强壮的头，方方的嘴，还有下垂的胡须。

As the monks who had rescued Jack and Annie went down the hall with the dogs, two other monks stepped forward. They both wore dark robes and round black caps. 当救了杰克和安妮的修道士们带着狗沿着走廊走开时，另外两个修道士走了出来。 他们两个人都穿着深色的长袍，戴着黑色的圆帽。

“Welcome,” one said in a deep, warm voice. He was a large man with a rugged face. “I am Father Laurent, the head of the monastery.” “欢迎你们，”其中一个人用低沉而温暖的声音说道。 他是个身材高大的人，脸上布满皱纹。 “我是劳伦特神父，修道院的负责人。”

“And I am Brother Michael,” said the other monk. He was not much taller than Jack, and his face was smooth and rosy. “我是迈克尔神父，”另一个修道士说。 他并没有比杰克高多少，脸庞光滑红润。

“We’re Jack and Annie,” Jack said hoarsely. He still didn’t have his full voice. “我们是杰克和安妮，”杰克沙哑地说。 他还没有完全恢复声音。

“Thanks for saving us,” said Annie. “谢谢你们救了我们，”安妮说。

“We were happy to do so,” said Father Laurent. “You must come and sit by the fire in the parlor to warm yourselves and dry your clothes. Brother Michael will take you there while I ask our cook to warm some soup for you.” “我们很高兴这么做，”劳伦特神父说。 “你们必须到客厅里坐在火炉旁，让自己暖和起来，把衣服晾干。 迈克尔兄弟会带你们去，而我会让我们的厨师给你们热些汤。”

“Thanks,” said Annie. “That sounds great.” “谢谢，”安妮说。 “那听起来很棒。”

Jack nodded. He wanted to lie down, but at the same time he was hungry and cold. 杰克点了点头。 他想躺下，但同时他又饿又冷。

“Come with me,” said Brother Michael. “Are you able to walk on your own?” “跟我来，”迈克尔兄弟说。 “你能自己走路吗？”

“Sure,” said Jack. “当然可以，”杰克说。

Jack and Annie could walk without help, but they both limped a little as they followed Brother Michael down the hall into the dimly lit parlor. The three Saint Bernards were resting in front of a huge fireplace. “Wow,” breathed Annie. The dogs barely looked up. 杰克和安妮可以自己走路，但当他们跟着迈克尔兄弟沿着走廊走进光线昏暗的客厅时，两人都有点跛脚。 三只圣伯纳犬正躺在一个巨大的壁炉前休息。 狗们几乎连头都没抬。

“Please, sit by the fire,” said Brother Michael. “请坐到火炉边，”迈克尔兄弟说。

Jack and Annie sat on wooden chairs and stared in a daze at the crackling fire. A moment later, Father Laurent came into the room and handed them bowls of steaming soup. The two monks waited as Jack and Annie drank the hot broth. 杰克和安妮坐在木椅上，呆呆地看着噼啪作响的火堆。 过了一会儿，劳伦特神父走进房间，给他们端来了热气腾腾的汤碗。 两位修道士等着杰克和安妮喝下这热腾腾的汤。

By the time they had emptied their bowls, Jack was feeling much better. The hot liquid had warmed his insides, and the heat from the fire had nearly dried his clothes. 等他们喝完汤时，杰克感觉好多了。 热汤让他从内到外都暖和起来，火堆的热气几乎把他的衣服都烘干了。

“Thank you. I feel a lot better now,” Annie said to the two monks. “谢谢。” 我现在感觉好很多了，”安妮对那两个修女说。

“You must stay here and rest for the night,” said Father Laurent. “You can continue your journey in the morning when you are rested.” “你必须在这里休息一晚，”拉乌尔神父说。 “你可以在早上休息好后继续你的旅程。”

“Thank you,” said Jack. “谢谢，”杰克说。

The monk looked at them curiously. “You must be very brave to try to travel the pass in the dark,” he said. 那个修士好奇地看着他们。 “你们在黑暗中穿越山口一定很勇敢，”他说。

“Or not very smart,” Annie said. “或者一点都不聪明，”安妮说。

Father Laurent smiled. “Yes, but I did not want to say that,” he said. 劳伦特神父笑了。 “是的，但我不想这么说，”他说。

“No kidding; we were looking for flowers!” said Annie. “别开玩笑了；我们是在找花！”安妮说。

“Goodness,” said Brother Michael. “I am afraid you’ve come to the wrong place to pick flowers.” “天哪，”迈克尔兄弟说。 “恐怕你们来错地方了，这里不能摘花。”

Both monks chuckled, and Annie laughed with them. Jack could barely smile—he was worried. I knew we came to the wrong place! he thought. 两位修士都笑了，安妮也和他们一起笑。 杰克几乎笑不出来——他很担心。 我知道我们来错地方了！他想。

“Now tell me, why were you really traveling on the pass?” asked Father Laurent. “现在告诉我，你为什么真的要经过这个山口？”劳伦特神父问。

“Well … um … actually, we’re on a mission,” said Jack. “嗯……其实……我们是执行任务的，”杰克说。

“Indeed?” said Brother Michael. “真的吗？”迈克尔兄弟说。

“Yes … we … um …,” Jack said. “是……我们……嗯……，”杰克说。

“We’re on a mission to learn more about the Alps,” said Annie.

“That’s right,” said Jack. “We’re doing a report.”

“But you are very young,” said Brother Michael.

“We are young,” said Annie. “But we are serious researchers. In fact, we recently went to India and studied the Taj Mahal. It’s amazingly beautiful. Have you ever seen it?”

“Uh, no, we haven’t,” said Brother Michael. He seemed a little surprised, but Father Laurent only smiled.

Before either monk could say any more, Annie continued, “I have some questions right now about these mountain dogs. Jack, get out your notebook.”

Jack reached into his bag and took out his pencil and notebook.

“So,” Annie said in a serious voice, “exactly how did your dogs know that we needed help?”

“Well, the Saints always know if someone is trapped in the snow,” said Brother Michael.

“The Saints?” asked Jack.

The monk pointed to the dogs lying near the hearth. “Our Saint Bernard dogs,” he said. “We have fifteen of them. We call them simply the Saints. They were named after Saint Bernard, who founded the monastery.”

“Oh, right,” said Jack. “Saint Bernard of Menthon.” He pushed his glasses back and took notes:

“The Saints have saved over a thousand people from the White Death,” said Brother Michael.

“And the White Death is … what?” Jack asked, looking up.

“That is what we call blizzards and avalanches,” said Brother Michael. “You were directly in the path of a very small avalanche. They are quite common in the spring. When the snow begins to melt, it loses its grip on the mountainside and slides down.”

“You were lucky,” said Father Laurent. “Avalanche victims rarely survive if they are trapped for long in the snow. Even a very small avalanche can be deadly.”

“So there wasn’t much time for the Saints to find us and save us, was there?” said Annie.

“No,” said Father Laurent. “But they need no compass. Once they hear the voice of the snow, as we call it, their noses lead them in the right direction. They can find their way to a person buried six feet down.”

“Oh, man,” Jack murmured, and wrote:

“Wow, they’re amazing,” said Annie. Everyone looked at the dogs. “Thank you, Saints, for saving our lives,” Annie said to them.

The gentle giants didn’t seem to care about being heroes. One scratched his ear, another yawned, and the third one snored.

Father Laurent laughed at the dogs. “Well, Jack and Annie. I imagine you would like to sleep now,” he said.

Jack closed his notebook and nodded.

“We’re pretty tired,” said Annie.

“Come. I will take you to your room,” Brother Michael said.

“Good night, Jack and Annie,” said Father Laurent. “And farewell, for I will not be seeing you in the morning. I am setting out first thing to help the French army.”

What does that mean? wondered Jack.

“Thanks for everything,” said Annie.

“Of course,” said Father Laurent. “I am glad you are safe now. No one should ever be caught on these mountains in the dead of night.”
