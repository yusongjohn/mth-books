9. Lovers of Knowledge 9. 知识的爱好者

Jack and Annie crawled over to the soldier lying in the snow. “Hi there,” said Annie, gently shaking him. 杰克和安妮爬到雪地里躺着的士兵身边。 “你好，”安妮轻声说道，轻轻摇晃他。

The young man opened his eyes. “Who are you?” he asked in a hoarse voice. “Where are the dogs?” 年轻人睁开了眼睛。 “你们是谁？”他用沙哑的声音问道。 “狗狗们去哪儿了？”

“The dogs are gone. We’re Jack and Annie,” said Annie. “We won’t leave you.” “狗狗们走啦。 我们是杰克和安妮，”安妮说。 “我们不会丢下你的。”

“Thank you,” breathed the young soldier, and he closed his eyes again. He was trembling with cold. “谢谢你们，”年轻的士兵喘着气说，然后他又闭上了眼睛。 他冷得发抖。

Annie pulled off her monk’s robe and tucked it around the soldier. Jack did the same. Annie began rubbing one of the soldier’s shaking hands, and Jack rubbed the other. 安妮脱下她的僧袍，裹在士兵身上。 杰克也做了同样的事。 安妮开始搓着士兵颤抖的一只手，杰克则搓着另一只。

As the snow flew around them, Jack wondered what to do. Where was Barry? How could they get the soldier up on his feet? How would they find their way back to the monastery? 雪花在他们周围飞舞，杰克想知道该怎么办。巴里去哪儿了？ 他们怎么才能让士兵站起来呢？ 他们怎么才能找到回去修道院的路呢？

“Listen,” said Annie, as if answering Jack’s questions. “听着，”安妮说，好像在回答杰克的问题。

Jack heard distant voices. He heard booming barks. People were coming to help! And Barry was leading the way. 杰克听到了远处的声音。 他听到了响亮的狗叫声。 有人来帮忙了！ 而且巴里正在带路。

The huge dog bounded down the narrow passage to the young soldier. He seemed startled when he saw Jack and Annie. He sniffed them, and then barked his booming bark. 那只大狗蹦蹦跳跳地沿着狭窄的通道跑向年轻的士兵。 他看到杰克和安妮时似乎很惊讶。 他闻了闻他们，然后又发出响亮的狗叫声。

Jack wasn’t sure what Barry was saying now. He wondered if it might be: “HOW THE HECK DID YOU CHANGE FROM DOGS BACK INTO PEOPLE?” 杰克不确定巴里现在在说什么。 他想这可能是："天哪，你怎么从狗变回人了？"

“Magic,” Jack said. "魔法，"杰克说。

Barry just panted, but Jack had the feeling he understood. 巴里只是喘着气，但杰克觉得他明白了。

“Hello!” Brother Michael was hurrying up the narrow passage. Behind him were the two French soldiers who had stopped earlier at the monastery. The soldiers dropped to their knees and helped lift the avalanche victim to his feet. They wrapped Jack’s and Annie’s robes tightly around him. "你好！"迈克尔兄弟正匆匆沿着狭窄的通道走来。 他身后是那两个早先在修道院停留的法国士兵。 士兵们跪下来，帮忙把雪崩的受害者扶起来。 他们把杰克和安妮的长袍紧紧地裹在他身上。

“Did Barry find you?” Jack asked Brother Michael. “巴里找到你了吗？”杰克问迈克尔兄弟。

“Yes. We had just met these soldiers on the pass when Barry came running up to us,” said the monk. “How did he get out of the monastery?” “是的。” 我们刚在山口遇到这些士兵，巴里就跑着朝我们冲过来，”修道士说。 “他怎么从修道院逃出来的？”

“He escaped when we weren’t looking,” said Jack. “When we found him, he led us to the buried soldier.” “当我们没注意的时候，他逃走了，”杰克说。 “当我们找到他时，他带我们找到了埋着的士兵。”

“Amazing!” said Brother Michael. “太神奇了！”迈克尔修士说。

Barry wagged his tail. It didn’t seem to worry him any longer that Jack and Annie had turned back into people. Dogs accept whatever comes along, Jack thought. 巴里摇着尾巴。 似乎不再担心杰克和安妮又变回了人。 狗会接受一切发生的事情，杰克想。

“We must help this young man to the monastery,” said Brother Michael. The two soldiers lifted the rescued man to his feet and helped him walk. “Barry, lead us home!” “我们必须把这个年轻人送回修道院，”迈克尔修士说。 两个士兵把救下的男人扶起来，帮他走路。 “巴里，带我们回家！”

Barry took the lead with Jack and Annie. The three soldiers and Brother Michael followed. 巴里带着杰克和安妮走在前面。 三个士兵和迈克尔神父跟在后面。

“I loved being a dog,” Jack whispered to Annie. “I loved all of it. Didn’t you? Didn’t you love it?” “我喜欢当一条狗，”杰克小声对安妮说。 “我喜欢所有的一切。你不喜欢吗？ 你不喜欢吗？”

“Of course I did!” said Annie, grinning. “当然喜欢！”安妮笑着说。

“Playing with you and Barry was one of the happiest things I’ve done in my life,” said Jack. “And we helped teach him stuff, and we helped him save a life!” “和你还有巴里一起玩是我一生中最开心的事情之一，”杰克说。 “而且我们还教了他一些东西，还帮助他救了一条命！”

“I know,” said Annie. “我知道，”安妮说。

As he hurried through the snow beside Barry, Jack tried to remember how it felt to be a dog. He moved quickly, keeping in step with Barry’s prancing gait. He inhaled the cold, fresh wind, smelling woodsmoke and falling snow. 杰克一边快步走过积雪，一边跟在巴里旁边，努力回想当狗的感觉。 他快速移动，步伐和巴里跳跃的步调保持一致。 他吸入了寒冷新鲜的风，闻到了木柴燃烧和飘落的雪花的味道。

By the time they had all reached the monastery, the wind and snow had stopped. Jack held the door for Brother Michael and the three soldiers. Then he, Annie, and Barry followed them inside. 等他们全都到达修道院时，风和雪已经停了。 杰克为迈克尔神父和三个士兵扶着门。 然后他、安妮和巴里跟着他们走进了里面。

Father Laurent and another man were waiting for them in the torchlit hallway. The man wore a long gray overcoat and a large black hat in the shape of a triangle. He had a pale face, and his gray eyes were deeply set above a long, straight nose. Jack thought he looked familiar. 劳伦神父和另一个男人正在火炬照亮的走廊里等他们。 那个男人穿着一件长长的灰色外套，戴着一顶形状像三角形的黑色帽子。 他有一张苍白的脸，灰眼睛深陷在长长的直鼻上方。 杰克觉得他看起来很眼熟。

“So the missing officer has been found! Wonderful!” said Father Laurent. “Brother Michael, please make him comfortable by the fire in the parlor. Bring him some hot soup.” Brother Michael led the soldiers down the hall. “所以失踪的警官被找到了！ “太棒了！”劳伦特神父说。 “迈克尔哥哥，请让他在客厅的火炉旁坐得舒服些。” “给他端些热汤来。”迈克尔兄弟带领士兵们沿着走廊走下来。

“Jack and Annie,” said Father Laurent, “may I present you to Consul Napoléon Bonaparte?” “杰克和安妮，”劳伦特神父说，“让我来向你们介绍拿破仑·波拿巴参议员吧？”

Now Jack knew why the man looked so familiar! He had seen paintings of the famous military leader in history books. 现在杰克知道为什么那个人看起来这么眼熟了！ 他曾在历史书上见过这位著名军事领袖的画像。

Napoléon Bonaparte took off his hat and bowed his head. When he straightened up, he looked at Annie and Jack with a piercing gaze. 拿破仑·波拿巴摘下帽子，低头鞠了一躬。 当他直起身来时，用锐利的目光看着安妮和杰克。

“Am I to understand you have saved my man from a snowy grave?” Napoléon Bonaparte said. “我是不是应该理解，你们救了我的人，让他免于葬身雪地？”拿破仑·波拿巴说道。

“Not us, Consul, sir,” said Jack. “It was Barry.” “不是我们， consul 先生，”杰克说。 “是巴里救的。”

The big dog was swinging his tail and panting. 那只大狗摇着尾巴，喘着气。

“Barry saved the lost soldier?” exclaimed Father Laurent. “Oh, my! I knew that you were trying to train him. But I had no idea you could work miracles! How did you do it?” “巴里救了那个迷路的士兵？”劳伦特神父惊呼道。 “哦，天哪！ 我知道你一直在训练他。 但我没想到你竟然能创造奇迹！ 你是怎么做到的？”

“Well … I guess we know how to think like dogs,” said Jack. “嗯……我想我们知道自己该怎么像狗一样思考，”杰克说。

“Delightful! Good dogs! All three of you!” said Father Laurent, laughing. “太棒了！好样的，狗狗们！ 你们三个！”劳伦特神父笑着说。

Barry sneezed. Jack and Annie sneezed, too. 巴里打了个喷嚏。 杰克和安妮也打了个喷嚏。

“I think we all need some hot tea and a fire,” said Father Laurent. “Come. Let us go into my library.” “我想我们都需要喝点热茶，生个火。”劳伦特神父说。 “来吧。 我们去我的图书馆。”

“Thank you, but I fear I must be on my way,” Napoléon Bonaparte said. “I will allow my three men to rest here, but I must return to camp. I will see you tomorrow with all your dogs. And of course”––he patted Barry’s head—“I hope that this one leads the way. He is a great dog.” “谢谢，但我恐怕得走了，”拿破仑·波拿巴说。 “我可以允许我的三个手下在这里休息，但我必须回到营地。 我明天会带着所有的狗来找你们。 当然——他拍了拍巴里的头——我希望这只狗带路。” 他是一只很棒的狗。”

“Indeed he is,” said Father Laurent. “Good afternoon, Your Excellency.” “确实如此，”拉乌尔神父说道。 “下午好，阁下。”

The French ruler opened the door and stepped out into the cold. He turned back and saluted them, then strode away alone through the snow. 法国统治者打开门，走进了寒冷中。 他转过身，向他们敬礼，然后独自踏着雪走了。

Father Laurent closed the door. He looked at Jack and Annie and laughed. “This has been quite a day,” he said, shaking his head. “I met Napoléon Bonaparte for the first time—and Barry became a true Saint! Let us celebrate with a cup of tea. A fresh pot is waiting in the library.” 拉乌尔神父关上了门。 他看着杰克和安妮，笑了。 “今天真是不寻常的一天，”他说着，摇了摇头。 “我第一次见到了拿破仑·波拿巴——而巴里成了一位真正的圣人！ 让我们用一杯茶来庆祝吧。 一个新煮的茶壶正在图书馆里等着呢。

“Thank you, that sounds great,” said Annie. She looked at Jack. “谢谢，听起来很棒，”安妮说。 她看着杰克。

“Sure, cool,” said Jack. “当然，太酷了，”杰克说。

Jack, Annie, and Barry followed Father Laurent down the main hall. He opened the door to a large room lit with flickering candles and a blazing fire. Barry flopped down near the fireplace. 杰克、安妮和巴里跟着劳伦特神父沿着大厅走。 他打开了一扇门，走进了一个大房间，房间里有闪烁的蜡烛和熊熊燃烧的火炉。 巴里懒洋洋地躺在壁炉旁边。

Jack and Annie looked around at the room. Shadows played on shelves with rows and rows of leather-bound books. Along one of the walls were glass cabinets filled with displays of butterflies, other insects, feathers, and rocks. 杰克和安妮环顾四周。 阴影在摆满一排排皮面书籍的书架上跳跃。 沿着其中一面墙，摆放着玻璃柜，里面陈列着蝴蝶、其他昆虫、羽毛和石头。

“Wow,” breathed Jack. “This is a great room.” “哇，”杰克惊叹道。 “这是一个很棒的房间。”

“I confess it is my favorite room in all the world,” said Father Laurent. “I am a lover of knowledge.” “我承认，这是我世界上最喜欢的房间，”拉乌尔神父说道。 “我热爱知识。”

“So are we,” said Jack. “我们也是，”杰克说。

“Wonderful. I thought you might be,” said the monk. “Come, sit, please.” He led them to a small table. They all sat down, and Father Laurent poured tea from a silver pot into three china cups. “太棒了。 我想你一定是，”修道士说。 “来，坐下来，请。”他带他们走到一张小桌子旁。 他们都坐了下来，拉乌尔神父用一个银壶把茶倒进三个瓷杯里。

As they each took a sip of the hot, sweet tea, Barry barked. He stood up and wagged his tail, staring at them. He barked again. 当他们每人喝了一口又热又甜的茶时，巴里叫了起来。 他站起来，摇着尾巴盯着他们看。他又叫了一声。

“He wants attention,” said Father Laurent, shaking his head. “Quiet, Barry.” “他想要关注，”拉乌尔神父说着，摇了摇头。 “安静，巴里。”

But Barry barked again. 但巴里又叫了起来。

“Oh!” said Annie. “I know what he wants! He wants to show you something! Barry, stay.” She walked to the other side of the room. Barry didn’t move. “哦！”安妮说。 “我知道他想要什么！ 他想给你们看一样东西！ “巴里，待着。”她走到房间的另一边。 巴里没有动。

“Come, Barry,” said Annie. “来，巴里，”安妮说。

Barry walked to Annie and stood very still. 巴里走到安妮面前，一动不动地站着。

“Sit, Barry,” said Annie. “坐下，巴里，”安妮说。

Barry sat. His tail thumped the stone floor. 巴里坐下了。 他的尾巴拍打着石头地板。

“My! I can’t believe it!” said Father Laurent. “It’s like magic!” “我简直不敢相信！”劳伦特神父说。 “这就像魔法一样！”，

“You have no idea,” said Annie. “你根本想象不到，”安妮说。

Jack laughed. “Good dog, Barry,” he said. 杰克笑了。 “好狗，巴里，”他说。

“Perhaps you would like to brush him?” Father Laurent said to Annie. The monk got a large brush from a shelf and gave it to her. “Brushing always makes a Saint happy, and Barry has more than earned his happiness today.” “也许你想给他梳毛？”劳伦特神父对安妮说。 和尚从架子上拿了一把大刷子，递给了她。 “刷毛总是能让圣人开心，而巴里今天已经充分赢得了这份快乐。”

Annie got up from her chair and knelt beside Barry. As she gently brushed his beautiful, thick fur, Barry leaned against her and put his paw on her foot. 安妮从椅子上站起来，跪在巴里旁边。 当她轻轻刷着他漂亮又浓密的毛发时，巴里靠在她身上，把爪子放在她的脚上。

Father Laurent smiled. “A Saint leans against you to keep you warm,” he said, “and he’ll put his paw on your foot to keep it warm, too.” 劳伦特神父微笑了。 “有一位圣人靠在你身边，让你感到温暖，”他说，“他还会把爪子放在你的脚上，让你的脚也暖和起来。”

“You sound like you love dogs,” said Annie. “你听起来好像很喜欢狗，”安妮说。

“I do indeed,” said Father Laurent. “I love all of nature. In fact, I often leave the monastery and go on expeditions just to study the natural world.” “确实如此，”拉乌尔神父说。 “我热爱大自然。 事实上，我经常离开修道院，去探险，只是为了研究自然世界。”

“What do you do on your expeditions?” asked Jack. “你们在探险时都做什么？”杰克问。

“Oh, I have gathered many rare specimens of butterflies, other insects, rocks, and minerals, as you can see from my displays,” said Father Laurent, pointing to the cases in his library. “But I am a botanist first and foremost.” “哦，我收集了很多稀有的蝴蝶、其他昆虫、石头和矿物标本，就像你们看到的我图书馆里的这些展示柜一样，”拉乌尔神父说着，指着他的书架上的标本柜。 “不过，我首先是一名植物学家。”

“A botanist?” said Annie. “植物学家？”安妮问。

“A botanist studies plants,” said Father Laurent. He stood and walked over to a shelf and picked out a large book with a pale cloth cover. “This is my most precious treasure. On my mountain expeditions, I have found many rare and beautiful flowers. They are preserved in this book.” “植物学家研究植物，”拉乌尔神父说。 他站起来，走到一个书架前，拿出一本封面是浅色布料的大书。 “这是我的最珍贵的宝藏。 在我去山里探险时，我发现了许多稀有而美丽的花朵。 它们都被保存在这本书里。”

Jack’s heart started to pound. “You mean you have real flowers in that book?” he asked. 杰克的心开始怦怦直跳。 “你是说那本书里真的有花吗？”他问。

“Yes,” said Father Laurent. “Would you like to see them?” “是的，”拉乌尔神父说。 “你想看看吗？”

Jack and Annie nodded. 杰克和安妮点了点头。

Father Laurent smiled and brought the book over to the table. 拉乌尔神父微笑着，把书拿到桌边。
