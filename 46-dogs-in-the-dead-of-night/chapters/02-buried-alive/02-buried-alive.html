2. Buried Alive! 2. 被活埋了！

A cold wind swept through the window. The purple light of the setting sun filled the tree house. Jack and Annie wore scratchy wool pants, shirts, hats, scarves, and gloves, and leather shoes. Jack’s pack had turned into a leather bag. When he opened it, he saw the scroll and the blue bottle inside—along with his notebook and pencil and the emerald rose. 一阵冷风吹进了窗户。 夕阳的紫色光芒充满了树屋。 杰克和安妮穿着粗糙的羊毛裤子、衬衫、帽子、围巾和手套，还穿着皮鞋。 杰克的背包变成了一个皮包。 当他打开它时，他看见里面有一卷羊皮纸和一个蓝色瓶子——还有他的笔记本、铅笔和翡翠玫瑰。

“So these are the Swiss Alps,” said Annie, shivering and looking out the window. “Pretty, but cold.” “所以这就是瑞士阿尔卑斯山，”安妮一边发抖一边朝窗外看，说道。 “漂亮，但很冷。”

Jack looked out with her. The tree house was nestled between gray boulders on a mountain slope. Snowy peaks loomed overhead. Below the peaks was the snow-covered pass they had seen in the picture. Smoke rose from a tall building. 杰克也和她一起朝外看。 树屋坐落在山腰上，夹在灰色的岩石之间。 白色的山峰高高耸立在头顶。 山峰下方是他们曾在图片中看到的被雪覆盖的山口。 一团烟雾从一座高大的建筑物上升起。

“This must be the Great Saint Bernard Pass,” said Jack. He picked up their book and turned to the page with the bookmark and read: “这里一定是伟大的圣伯纳德山口，”杰克说。 他拿起他们的书，翻到夹着书签的那一页，读道：

The Great Saint Bernard Pass is an ancient road between the two highest peaks of the Alps. For thousands of years, it was the only route between Switzerland and Italy. The pass was named for Bernard of Menthon, who built a monastery there in the eleventh century. For hundreds of years, the monks at this Swiss monastery have welcomed cold and weary travelers who are crossing the pass. 伟大的圣伯纳德山口是连接阿尔卑斯山两座最高山峰的一条古老道路。 几千年来，它是瑞士和意大利之间唯一的通道。 这个山口以门东的伯纳德命名，他在11世纪时在那里建了一座修道院。 几百年来，这座瑞士修道院的修士们一直欢迎那些穿越山口、又冷又累的旅行者。

“So that building must be the monastery,” said Jack. “所以那栋建筑一定是修道院，”杰克说。

“Great,” said Annie. “We can start our mission by going there.” “太好了，”安妮说。 “我们可以从那里开始我们的任务。”

“Okay,” said Jack. “But I don’t get it. To save Penny we have to find a white and yellow flower. And we have to live its meaning, if only for an hour—whatever that means.” “好的，”杰克说。 “但我还不明白。” 为了救佩妮，我们必须找到一朵白色和黄色的花。 而且我们必须体会它的意义，哪怕只有一小时——不管那是什么意思。

“We’ll figure it out,” said Annie. “我们会弄明白的，”安妮说。

“Let’s hope,” said Jack. “But where do we find flowers here?” “但愿如此，”杰克说。 “但是我们在这里哪里能找到花呢？”

Jack and Annie looked out at the treeless landscape of ice, snow, and rock. “Well, there must be flowers somewhere,” said Annie. 杰克和安妮望着眼前没有树木的冰天雪地。 “好吧，一定有花在某个地方，”安妮说。

“I don’t know,” said Jack. “Maybe Teddy and Kathleen made a mistake and sent us to the wrong place.” “我不知道，”杰克说。 “也许泰迪和凯瑟琳犯了错误，把我们送到了错误的地方。”

“They’ve never made a mistake before,” said Annie. “他们以前从没犯过错误，”安妮说。

“Uh, excuse me. Teddy didn’t make a mistake turning Penny into stone?” said Jack. “呃，打扰一下。” “泰迪没把彭妮变成石头搞错吗？”杰克说。

“Okay, good point,” said Annie. “But let’s head to the monastery before it gets dark. We can ask about flowers there.” “好吧，说得对，”安妮说。 “不过在天黑之前我们最好去修道院， 在那里我们可以打听一下那些花。”

“But what if—” started Jack. “但是万一——”杰克开口说。

“Stop worrying,” interrupted Annie. “Our book says the monks welcome cold, weary travelers. I’m cold, and your questions are making me weary. Come on.” She climbed out the window into the snow. “别担心，”安妮打断了他。 “我们的书上说，修道士们欢迎又冷又累的旅行者。 我冷，你问的这些问题让我更累了。 来吧。”她爬出窗户，走进了雪地里。

“Funny,” said Jack. But he was ready to find shelter, too. His face was freezing. He packed up their book and slung the leather bag over his shoulder, then followed Annie out the tree house window. “真有趣，”杰克说。 但他也准备去找个地方避避寒了。 他的脸冻得发红。 他收拾好他们的书，把皮质的包背到肩上，然后跟着安妮从树屋的窗户爬了出去。

Jack’s feet crunched down onto the icy snow. As the sun went down behind the peaks, deep purple shadows climbed over the tall mountains. The monastery in the hollow below was completely hidden in darkness. 杰克的脚踩在结冰的雪地上，发出咯吱咯吱的声音。 太阳落山后，深紫色的影子爬上了高高的山峰。 山脚下空地里的修道院完全被黑暗笼罩了。

“We have to hurry,” said Annie. “我们必须快点，”安妮说。

“No, we have to move slowly down the slope, so we don’t slide,” said Jack. “不，我们必须慢慢往下走，这样才不会滑下去，”杰克说。

“Well, then let’s move slowly quickly,” said Annie. “那我们就慢慢快点走吧，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie started down the slope. As they carefully put one foot in front of the other, a strange sound came from above: whumph! 杰克和安妮开始往下走。 当他们小心翼翼地把一只脚放在另一只脚前面时，上面传来了一种奇怪的声音：砰！

“What was that?” said Jack, looking around. “那是什么？”杰克环顾四周，问。

Next came a low rumble like thunder. 接着，又传来一阵像雷声一样的低沉轰鸣。

“What’s that?” said Annie. “那是什么？”安妮问。

Then came a noise like the sound of glass breaking. 然后传来一声，像是玻璃碎裂的声音。

“Whoa!” cried Jack. “哇！”杰克喊道。

Directly above them on the mountain, big blocks of snow were breaking into smaller chunks and sliding down the slope. 在他们头顶的山上，大块的雪正在碎成小块，滑下山坡。

“Grab my hand!” shouted Jack. He reached for Annie, and she gripped his hand. “抓住我的手！”杰克喊道。 他伸手去抓安妮，她紧紧握住了他的手。

Suddenly snow was moving all around them! Even the snow beneath them was sliding! A block of icy snow slammed into Jack, knocking him away from Annie. 突然，四周的雪开始移动！ 就连他们脚下的雪也在滑动！ 一块冰冻的雪块撞上了杰克，把他从安妮身边撞开。

“Jack!” Annie cried. “杰克！”安妮喊道。

Another chunk of snow knocked Jack off his feet and sent him tumbling headfirst down the slope. Jack kept falling downhill until a wall of frozen snow stopped him. He tried to stand, but a giant wave of soft, powdery snow blew over him, burying him completely. 又一块雪撞倒了杰克，把他头朝下滚下了山坡。 杰克一直往山下滚，直到一堵冰冻的雪墙把他挡住了。 他试着站起来，但一阵柔软蓬松的雪浪扑过来，把他完全埋住了。

Jack kicked his arms and legs, trying to surface from the fluffy sea of snow. He kicked and flailed, but the harder he struggled, the more snow there seemed to be. Snow clogged Jack’s eyes, ears, and throat. Every time he coughed, he sucked in more snow. He felt as if he were drowning—until he finally pushed his head up through the snow into the cold air. 杰克踢着腿和胳膊，努力从这蓬松的雪海里浮出来。 他踢呀踢，挣扎呀挣扎，可他越挣扎，雪好像越多。 雪堵住了杰克的眼睛、耳朵和喉咙。 他每次咳嗽，就会吸进更多的雪。 他感觉就像要淹死了一样——直到他终于把头从雪里顶出来，呼吸到冰冷的空气。

He could breathe! 他能呼吸了！

But Jack still couldn’t see. A gust of snow powder blinded him. He couldn’t move his arms or hands, his legs or feet. The soft snow around his body had turned hard and solid. Jack felt as if he were trapped in cold concrete, buried up to his neck. Where was Annie? Had she been buried alive, too? 但是杰克还是什么也看不见。 一阵雪粉吹过来，把他弄得睁不开眼。 他动不了手臂、手、腿或脚。 他身体周围的软雪已经变硬了。 杰克感觉就像被困在冰冷的混凝土里，埋到了脖子。安妮在哪里？ 她也被活埋了吗？

Jack tried to yell, but no sound came from his throat. He kept trying to shout for Annie, but it was hopeless. His lips wouldn’t move. He couldn’t even feel his mouth. He couldn’t feel his arms or legs, feet or hands. He closed his eyes. He couldn’t feel anything, not even the wind that kept blowing snow in his face.… 杰克试着大喊，但喉咙里发不出声音。 他一直试着喊安妮的名字，但毫无希望。 他的嘴唇动不了。 他甚至感觉不到自己的嘴巴。 他感觉不到自己的手臂、腿、脚或手。 他闭上了眼睛。 他什么也感觉不到，连一直往他脸上吹雪的风也感觉不到。…

Owww! Jack screamed in his head. His eyes shot open. He was freezing cold, and creatures were attacking him! They were whimpering, snuffling, panting, whining. Wild dogs! Jack thought with terror. 杰克在心里尖叫。 他的眼睛猛地睁开了。 他冷得发抖，还有野兽在攻击他！ 它们在呜咽、嗅来嗅去、喘气、哀叫。是野狗！ 杰克吓得想。

Two dogs were scratching and digging all around Jack’s body. A third dog licked his eyes and ears and the top of his head! Jack felt as if he were about to be licked to death! 两只狗在杰克的身体周围抓呀挖呀。 第三只狗舔了他的眼睛、耳朵和头顶！ 杰克感觉好像要被舔死了！

Help! Jack tried to shout. But no sound came from his clogged throat. Help! Help! His mind roared. But the three huge, panting creatures kept licking him and pawing the icy snow that had trapped Jack’s body. 救命！ 杰克试着喊叫。 但他喉咙被堵住了，发不出声音。救命！ 救命！他的脑海里大声呼喊。 但那三只又大又喘的怪物还在舔他，用爪子刨着困住杰克身体的冰雪。

As the giant dogs hovered over him, Jack saw flames behind them. Figures in hooded robes were moving about in the fiery light, carrying torches. The figures were scarier than the dogs. 当那些巨大的狗在他头顶盘旋时，杰克看见它们后面有火焰。 穿着斗篷的人影在火光中走来走去，手里举着火把。 那些人影比狗还要可怕。

“Jack!” came a faint cry. “杰克！”一个微弱的声音传来。

“Annie?” Jack croaked. “安妮？”杰克沙哑地喊道。

A man shouted, and the dogs backed off. The flaming torches shone directly on Jack. He could see that he’d been mostly dug out of the snow, but he still couldn’t feel or move his body. 一个人喊了一声，狗们退开了。 火把的光直接照在杰克身上。 他看得出来，自己大部分已经被从雪里挖了出来，但他还是动不了，也感觉不到身体的任何部分。

Two of the hooded figures leaned down and tightly gripped Jack’s arms. They pulled him to his feet. When they let go of him, he started to fall. They grabbed him again and lifted him onto a cloth stretcher. 两个戴兜帽的人弯下腰，紧紧抓住了杰克的手臂。 他们把他拉了起来。 他们松开他时，他开始往下掉。 他们又抓住他，把他抬到了一张布担架上。

“My—my sister …,” Jack chattered. He managed to lift his head and look back. In the torchlight, he saw Annie lying on a stretcher, wrapped in a blanket. “我……我的姐姐……，”杰克结结巴巴地说。 他好不容易抬起头，朝后看了一眼。 在火光中，他看见安妮躺在另一张担架上，裹着一条毯子。

“She is safe,” said one of the men. “她没事，”其中一个人说。

“Who—who are you?” Jack asked, shivering uncontrollably. He had lost his hat and scarf in the snow. “谁……你是谁？”杰克问，不由自主地发抖。 他在雪里弄丢了帽子和围巾。

“We are monks from the Saint Bernard Monastery,” the man answered. He covered Jack with a heavy blanket. “我们是来自圣伯纳修道院的修士，”那人回答。 他用一条厚重的毯子裹住了杰克。

“Oh … thank you,” whispered Jack. “哦……谢谢你，”杰克小声说。

The three dogs led the way over the snowy pass, snorting and sneezing and panting. The monks followed, carrying Jack and Annie through the cold, windy dark. 三条狗在雪地的山口上领路，喷着鼻息，打着喷嚏，喘着粗气。 修士们跟在后面，带着杰克和安妮穿过寒冷、多风的黑暗。
