7. I Don’t Believe It 7. 我不信

Jack and Annie followed Barry inside. In the dining hall, Barry bounded over to one of the large dog bowls and slurped water. He splashed more outside of the bowl than he actually drank. When he finished, he galumphed toward Jack, water and drool flying out of his mouth. 杰克和安妮跟着巴里进去了。 在餐厅里，巴里蹦蹦跳跳地跑到一个大狗碗前，喝起水来。 他碗外溅出的水比他实际喝的还要多。 喝完后，他满嘴口水地朝杰克冲过去，水和口水从他嘴里飞溅出来。

“Stay away from me!” said Jack, pushing Barry away. “Annie, I’m going to go read now.” “离我远点！”杰克喊道，把巴里推开。 “安妮，我现在要去看书了。”

Jack hurried out of the dining hall and headed down the hallway toward their room. But Annie and Barry dashed ahead of him. The dog’s playful barks echoed through the monastery, making it seem full of life, despite its emptiness. 杰克赶紧从餐厅跑出去，沿着走廊朝他们的房间跑去。 但安妮和巴里冲到了他前面。 狗活泼的吠声在修道院里回荡，让这个空荡荡的地方看起来充满了生机。

As soon as they entered their bedroom, Jack closed the door and grabbed their book. Barry jumped onto Annie’s bed. As Jack started to read about flora and fauna in the Alps, Barry yelped. He leapt up and barked at the door. 他们一进入卧室，杰克就关上门，拿起他们的书。 巴里跳到了安妮的床上。 当杰克开始读关于阿尔卑斯山的动植物时，巴里突然叫了起来。 他跳起来，对着门狂吠。

“What now?” said Jack. “现在又怎么了？”杰克说。

“Someone’s ringing the bell!” said Annie. “The bell at the front door. Hear it?” “有人在按门铃！”安妮说。 “前门的铃铛响了。听到了吗？”

Jack could barely hear the bell clanging over Barry’s barking. 杰克几乎听不清铃铛声，因为巴里的吠叫太大了。

“We’d better go answer it,” said Annie. “No one else is here. Come on, Barry.” “我们最好去开门，”安妮说。 “这里没有其他人。来吧，巴里。”

“No, wait,” said Jack. “I’ll go with you. Barry should stay here, so he doesn’t jump all over whoever’s there.” “不，等等，”杰克说。 “我和你一起去。 巴里应该留在这里，这样他才不会扑向门口的人。”

“Okay, wait here, Barry,” said Annie. “好吧，巴里，你在这里等着，”安妮说。

Jack left the book on his bed, and he and Annie hurried out of the room. Before Barry could follow them, Jack closed the door tightly. The dog howled. 杰克把书放在床上，然后他和安妮赶紧离开了房间。 巴里还没来得及跟上他们，杰克就把门紧紧关上了。狗嚎叫起来。

“Don’t cry! We’ll be back soon!” shouted Annie. Then she and Jack hurried down the torchlit hallway to the front entrance. “别哭！” “我们很快就回来！”安妮喊道。 然后她和杰克赶紧沿着被火把照亮的走廊跑向正门。

The bell kept clanging. 钟声一直在响。

Annie opened the heavy wooden door, and she and Jack peered out. 安妮打开了那扇沉重的木门，她和杰克探头往外看。

Two men in military uniforms stood on the steps of the monastery. One had a black mustache, and the other had bushy sideburns. Both wore blue and white coats with red cuffs and red collars. White pants were tucked into their riding boots. Swords hung from their sides. 两个穿着军装的男人站在修道院的台阶上。 一个人有黑色的胡子，另一个人有浓密的鬓角。 他们都穿着蓝白相间的外套，袖子和领子是红色的。 白色的裤子塞进他们的骑马靴里。 剑挂在他们腰间。

Jack was startled. The soldiers looked as if they’d stepped out of a time long ago. 杰克吓了一跳。 那些士兵看起来就像是从很久很久以前走出来的一样。

“Good afternoon. We think one of our officers may be here,” said the soldier with the mustache. “下午好。 我们认为你们的一位军官可能在这里，”留着胡子的士兵说。

“Um, I’m afraid your officer isn’t here,” said Annie. “No one’s here but us. All the monks have left to help the French army.” “呃，恐怕你们的军官不在这里，”安妮说。 “这里除了我们，没有人。 所有修道士都离开去帮助法国军队了。”

“We are an advance party, and our officer got separated from us,” said the second soldier. “Consul Napoléon hoped he had made his way here on his own.” “我们是先遣队，我们的军官和我们分开了，”第二个士兵说。 “拿破仑总督希望他能自己找到这里。”

“Napoléon?” asked Jack. “拿破仑？”杰克问。

“First Consul Napoléon Bonaparte,” the soldier said. “First Consul Napoléon Bonaparte,” the soldier said.

Oh, man, thought Jack. Napoléon Bonaparte was a famous military leader of France! 哦，天哪，杰克心想。 拿破仑·波拿巴是法国一位著名的军事领袖！

“When our officer arrives, please tell him to wait for us,” said the man with the mustache. “We will return later.” The two soldiers turned and walked away from the monastery. “当我们的军官到达时，请告诉他让我们等一下，”那位有胡子的男士说道。 “我们稍后再回来。”两个士兵转身离开修道院。

Jack whirled around to Annie. “Did you hear the name of their leader? Napoléon Bonaparte! He lived two hundred years ago! Let’s go look in our book! Maybe it has information about Napoléon crossing the Alps!” 杰克转身对安妮说。 “你听到他们首领的名字了吗？ 拿破仑·波拿巴！ 他生活在两百年前！ 我们去书里查一查吧！ 也许它有关于拿破仑翻越阿尔卑斯山的信息！

Jack and Annie hurried down the torchlit hallway to their room. “Oh, wow,” Jack said, pointing at one of the torches. “I should have figured out we weren’t in our time! We don’t use torches and lanterns for lighting anymore.” 杰克和安妮赶紧沿着被火把照亮的走廊跑回他们的房间。 “哦，哇，”杰克指着其中一盏火炬说。 “我早该想到我们不在自己的时代！ 我们现在不用火炬和灯笼来照明了。”

“Well, you’ve had a lot on your mind,” said Annie. “好吧，你脑子里想了很多事情，”安妮说。

“Monks seem timeless,” Jack went on. “But as soon as you see a soldier, you can start to figure out the time in history. I can’t wait to read about this in our book. Maybe there will be a clue to help us with our mission.” “修道士看起来好像永远不会老，”杰克接着说。 “但只要你看到一个士兵，你就能开始猜出这是哪个历史时期了。 我迫不及待想在我们的书里读到这部分内容了。 也许会有线索能帮我们完成任务。”

Jack ran the rest of the way to their room. He opened the door and stopped. “Oh, no,” he said. “I … don’t … believe … it.” 杰克一路小跑冲回他们的房间。 他打开门，停住了。 “哦，不，”他说。 “我……不……相信……这是真的。”

Annie caught up to Jack and peered into the room. “Oh, Barry,” she said. 安妮追上杰克，往房间里张望。 “哦，巴里，”她说。

Barry was sitting on the floor, wagging his tail. Paper was hanging from his mouth. 巴里正坐在地板上，摇着尾巴。 纸张从他的嘴里垂下来。

“He ate our Alps book,” said Jack, stunned. “He ate it!” “他把我们的阿尔卑斯书吃掉了，”杰克惊呆了说。 “他真的吃掉了！”

All around the room were bits and pieces of paper. Jack started collecting the pieces, but it was hopeless. Most of the book was in Barry’s stomach. “I really don’t believe this,” said Jack. 房间里到处都是纸片和碎纸屑。 杰克开始捡起这些纸片，但根本捡不完。 这本书的大部分内容都在巴里的肚子里。 “我真的不敢相信这是真的，”杰克说。

“Bad dog, Barry!” yelled Annie. “坏狗，巴里！”安妮喊道。

Barry stopped wagging his tail. He lowered his head. 巴里停下了摇尾巴的动作。 他低下了头。

“Bad,” said Annie sternly. “Bad, bad, bad!” “坏，”安妮严厉地说。 “坏，坏，坏！”

Barry kept his head low and wiggled his way toward the door. Then he stood up, and with his tail drooping, he slunk out of the room. 巴里低下头，扭动着身子朝门口爬去。 然后他站了起来，尾巴低垂着，悄悄地溜出了房间。

Jack flopped down on his bed and stared at the ceiling. “There’s no hope now,” he said. “We’ll never read about flora and fauna in the Alps or about Napoléon or anything. I feel like we’ll never figure out this mission or save Penny.” 杰克扑通一声躺在床上，盯着天花板。 “现在没有希望了，”他说。 “我们再也读不到关于阿尔卑斯山的植物和动物，或者拿破仑，或者任何东西了。 我觉得我们永远也完不成这个任务，也救不了彭妮。”

Annie sat next to him. “I’m sorry,” she said. 安妮坐在他旁边。 “对不起，”她说。

“It’s not your fault,” said Jack. “这不是你的错，”杰克说。

“Well, I shouldn’t have offered to train Barry,” said Annie. “We should have studied the book when you wanted to.” “好吧，我不该主动提出要训练巴里，”安妮说。 “我们应该在你想要的时候就一起研究那本书。”

Jack shook his head. “Whatever,” he said. “I just don’t know what to do next. We can’t go home yet. We can’t give up. We have to save Penny.” 杰克摇了摇头。 “随便吧，”他说。 “我只是不知道接下来该做什么。 我们还不能回家，我们不能放弃。 我们必须救出佩妮。”

“I know, I know,” said Annie. “Maybe we should just go outside and walk around. Get some fresh air. Want to?” “我知道，我知道，”安妮说。 “也许我们应该出去走走。 呼吸点新鲜空气。想不想？”

“I guess,” said Jack. He picked up his bag and sighed. “Maybe if we just walk around, we’ll think of something.” “我想是吧，”杰克说。 他拿起他的包，叹了口气。 “也许如果我们到处走走，就能想出点什么主意。”

“Let’s find Barry first,” said Annie. “我们先去找巴里吧，”安妮说。

“Why?” said Jack. “So he can ‘help’ us some more?” “为什么？”杰克问。 “这样他就能‘帮’我们更多吗？”

“No, I just want to make sure he’s okay,” said Annie. “I feel kind of bad for yelling.” “不，我只是想确定他没事，”安妮说。 “我有点后悔刚才对他大喊大叫。”

Jack and Annie headed out of the room and down the hall. 杰克和安妮走出了房间，沿着走廊走下去。

“Barry!” Annie called. “巴里！”安妮喊道。

There was no sign of him. 没有任何他的踪迹。

“I wonder where he went,” Annie said. “Barry!” “我不知道他去哪儿了，”安妮说。 “巴里！”

“When he comes, just don’t tell him he’s a good dog,” said Jack. “等他来了，千万别告诉他他是一只好狗，”杰克说。

They passed the dining hall and looked inside. “Barry?” Annie called. But the room was empty. 他们经过了餐厅，往里面看了看。 “巴里？”安妮喊道。 但房间里空无一人。

Jack and Annie kept walking, until they got to the parlor. “Barry?” Annie called, peering in. But that room was empty, too. 杰克和安妮继续往前走，直到来到客厅。 “巴里？”安妮喊道，朝里面张望。 但那个房间也是空的。

Jack felt a cold wind blowing. It grew colder as he and Annie kept walking down the hallway, heading for the front door. 杰克感觉到一阵冷风吹来。 随着他和安妮继续沿着走廊往门口走，天气变得越来越冷。

“Barry!” Annie called. “巴里！”安妮喊道。

The whole monastery felt quiet and empty and cold. Jack and Annie were both shivering by the time they reached the front hall. 整个修道院都显得安静、空荡和寒冷。 杰克和安妮到达前厅时，都已经在发抖了。

“I wonder where— Oh, no!” said Annie. “Look!” She pointed to the front door. It was open. “We must not have closed the door hard enough and the wind blew it open! Barry must have gotten out!” “我想知道——哦，不！”安妮说道。 “看！”她指着大门。门是开着的。 “我们刚才关门的时候可能没关紧，风把它吹开了！” 巴里一定跑出去了！

Annie rushed outside. “Barry! Barry!” 安妮冲了出去。 “巴里！巴里！”

“Oh, man,” said Jack. He hurried out into the cold, too. “Barry! Barry!” “哦，天哪，”杰克说。 他赶紧跑出去，外面冷极了。 “巴里！巴里！”

The only sound they heard was the wind whistling over the pass. The only sight was snow flying through the air. 他们听到的唯一声音，是风在山口呼啸。 他们看到的唯一景象，是雪花在空中飞舞。

“He’s run away!” wailed Annie. “I know it!” “他跑掉了！”安妮哭喊道。 “我知道！”

“Barry!” Jack called. “巴里！”杰克喊道。

“He’ll get lost!” said Annie. “Brother Michael said he should never run loose—he’ll get lost and never find his way home!” “他会迷路的！”安妮说。 “迈克尔哥哥说过，他不该放任自由——他会迷路，再也找不到回家的路！”

“He couldn’t have gone far,” said Jack. “Don’t panic. We’ll find him.” “他不可能走得太远，”杰克说。 “别慌，我们会找到他的。”

Jack and Annie began wandering through the cold, shouting, “Barry! Barry!” 杰克和安妮开始在寒冷中四处走动，大声喊着：“巴里！巴里！”

Gusts of wind blew over the pass, swirling the snow around them. 一阵阵狂风吹过山口，把周围的雪吹得四处飘扬。

“Barry! Barry!” they called. “巴里！ 巴里！”他们喊道。

Jack kept expecting to see the big dog bounding through the white powder. How could he just disappear? 杰克一直期待着看到那只大狗在白色的雪粉中蹦蹦跳跳地跑过来。 他怎么会突然消失呢？

“I don’t know what to do,” Annie said, hugging herself. They both stopped walking. They were shivering in their robes, and their teeth chattered. “我不知道该怎么办，”安妮说，紧紧地抱住自己。 他们俩都停下了脚步。 他们穿着长袍在发抖，牙齿也在不停地打颤。

“Maybe we should go back and look through the monastery again,” said Jack. “也许我们应该回去再仔细看看修道院，”杰克说。

“No, I know he’s not there!” said Annie. “I can feel it! He’s lost somewhere, I’m sure of it!” Tears filled her eyes. “I really hurt his feelings when I said bad, bad, bad. He didn’t know our book was so important. He thought he was a good dog. I kept telling him he was a good dog. But really he wasn’t a very good dog, Jack. He was just a funny, happy dog! I should have closed the door all the way!” Tears ran down Annie’s red cheeks. “I feel terrible.” “不，我知道他不在那里！”安妮说。 “我能感觉到！ 他一定迷路了，我确定！”她眼里充满了泪水。 “我真的伤害了他的感情，当时我说他坏，坏，坏。 他不知道我们的书这么重要。 他以为自己是一只乖狗。 我一直告诉他他是一只乖狗。 但其实他并不是一只很乖的狗，杰克。 他只是一只有趣又快乐的狗！ “我应该把门完全关上！”安妮的红脸蛋上流下了泪水。 “我感觉糟透了。”

“Hey, hey, it wasn’t your fault,” said Jack. “I feel terrible, too. I could have closed the door. But don’t worry. When the others come back, the dogs can help us find him.” “嘿，嘿，这不是你的错，”杰克说。 “我也感觉糟透了。” 我本来可以关上门的。但别担心。 当其他人回来时，狗可以帮助我们找到他。”

“That’s a long time from now,” said Annie, still crying. “It’ll be really dark.” “这还要等很久呢，”安妮一边说，一边还在哭。 “天会变得非常黑。”

“I know,” said Jack. He put his hand on Annie’s back and spoke gently. “But the Saints can find him in the dark, like they found us. If they can smell people really far away, they can smell a big dog like Barry.” “我知道，”杰克说。 他把手放在安妮的背上，温柔地说话。 “但是圣徒们即使在黑暗中也能找到他，就像他们找到我们一样。” 如果他们能闻到很远的人，他们也能闻到像巴里这样的大狗。”

“I wish we could smell him right now,” said Annie. “I wish we were dogs. Then we could find him.” She covered her face and sobbed. “我希望我们现在能闻到他，”安妮说。 “我希望我们是狗。” 那样的话，我们就能找到他。”她捂住脸，抽泣起来。

“You wish we were dogs?” said Jack. “你希望我们是狗？”杰克说。

“Yes,” said Annie, sniffling, “so we could find him.” “是的，”安妮抽泣着说，“这样我们就能找到他。”

“Well …” Jack took a deep breath. “Okay. I think I know what we can do,” he said. “嗯……”杰克深吸了一口气。 “好吧。” 我想我知道我们现在能做什么，”他说。

“What? What can we do?” asked Annie, wiping her face. “什么？”， “我们该怎么办？”安妮一边擦着脸一边问道。

“This is what we can do,” said Jack. He reached into his bag and pulled out the tiny bottle from Teddy and Kathleen. “这就是我们能做的事，”杰克说。 他从包里拿出摩根和泰迪给他的小瓶子。

Annie stared at Jack. “Oh, wow!” she breathed. 安妮盯着杰克看。 “哦，哇！”她惊叹道。

“We can use this to turn into dogs and find Barry,” said Jack. “我们可以用这个变成狗，找到巴里，”杰克说。

“Jack! That’s the best idea you’ve ever had!” said Annie. “杰克！”， “这是你有史以来最好的主意！”安妮说。

Jack smiled. “Okay, here’s what we’ll do,” he said. “We’ll each take a sip. Then together, we’ll say: ‘Turn us into Saint Bernard dogs!’ ” 杰克笑了。 “好，我们这么办，”他说。 “我们每个人都喝一口。 然后一起说：‘把我们变成圣伯纳犬！’”

“And we’ll turn into dogs for an hour!” said Annie. “我们会变成狗一小时！”安妮说。

“Dogs for an hour,” said Jack. He took another deep breath. Then he pulled the cork out of the bottle. “变成狗一小时，”杰克说。 他又深吸了一口气。 然后他把瓶子的软木塞拔了出来。
