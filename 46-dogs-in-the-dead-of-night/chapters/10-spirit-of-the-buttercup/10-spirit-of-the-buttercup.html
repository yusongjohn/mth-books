10. Spirit of the Buttercup 10. 金盏花之灵

Father Laurent sat down at the table and opened his book of flowers. Jack and Annie looked over the monk’s shoulder. Even Barry stepped closer to the table and pushed his giant head under Father Laurent’s elbow to look at the book. 拉乌尔神父在桌边坐下，打开他的花书。 杰克和安妮从修道士的肩膀上看过去。 就连巴里也走近了桌子，把巨大的脑袋伸到拉乌尔神父的肘下，想看看那本书。

The monk carefully turned the rough linen pages. The pages were blank, but pressed between them were leaves and flowers. Father Laurent gently held up a cluster of tiny pink flowers. The flowers were completely flat and dry, but they had kept all their petals and color. 修道士小心地翻动着粗糙的亚麻纸页。 书页是空白的，但夹在里面的有树叶和花朵。 劳伦特神父轻轻地举着一簇小小的粉红色花朵。 这些花已经完全变平、变干了，但它们还保留着所有的花瓣和颜色。

“These are Androsace alpina. That’s their scientific name. Their popular name is Alpine rock-jasmine,” said the monk. “Several summers ago, I found these hidden among mountain rocks.” “这些是高山仙女木。 那是它们的科学名称。 它们的俗名叫阿尔卑斯岩茉莉，”和尚说。 “几年前的夏天，我在山岩中发现了这些花。”

“Nice,” said Annie. “真漂亮，”安妮说。

Jack held his breath. Could there be a white and yellow flower in the book? 杰克屏住了呼吸。 书里会不会有白色和黄色的花呢？

Father Laurent turned the page and very carefully picked up a dark golden flower. “This is Caltha palustris, better known as marsh marigold,” he said. “I found this two years ago in the southern Alps.” 劳伦特神父翻过一页，非常小心地捡起一朵深金色的花。 “这是Caltha palustris，也叫沼泽金盏花，”他说。 “我两年前在南阿尔卑斯山发现的。”

“Lovely,” said Annie. “真漂亮，”安妮说。

Father Laurent turned the page again. He held up a dried white flower. “Cerastium fontanum, the mouse-ear chickweed,” the monk said. “Its tiny petals look like the ears of a mouse.” 劳伦特神父又翻了一页。 他举起一朵干枯的白色花。 “这是Cerastium fontanum，也叫鼠耳芥，”神父说。 “它小小的花瓣看起来就像老鼠的耳朵。”

“Cute,” said Annie. “真可爱，”安妮说。

“Yes,” said Father Laurent. He gently closed the cloth-covered book. “Since you like flowers so much, I assume you like butterflies, too. Let me show you my favorites.” He started to stand up. “是的，”劳伦特神父说。 他轻轻合上了那本封面有布料的书。 “既然你这么喜欢花，我想你也一定喜欢蝴蝶吧。 让我给你看看我最喜欢的那些。” 他开始站起来。

“No, wait, please,” said Jack. He tried to keep his voice steady. “Do you happen to have a white and yellow flower in that book?” “不，等等，请别走，”杰克说。 他努力让自己的声音保持平稳。 “你那本书里有没有白色的花，中间是黄色的？”

Father Laurent frowned. “Hmm … let me see. Oh, yes, of course.” He opened the book again and turned the pages until he found what he was looking for. He carefully held up a flower with white petals and a yellow center. It shone in the firelight, as if it were still alive. 劳伦特神父皱起了眉头。 “嗯……让我想想。 哦，对了，当然有。” 他再次打开书，翻动着页面，直到找到他要找的那一页。 他小心翼翼地举起一朵花瓣是白色、花心是黄色的花。 它在火光中闪闪发光，好像还活着一样。

“Ranunculus glacialis, the glacial buttercup,” said the monk. “One of the highest flowering plants in the Alps. A perfect specimen! I found it not far from our monastery just last summer.” “Ranunculus glacialis，冰川毛茛，”和尚说。 “这是阿尔卑斯山最高的开花植物之一。 完美的标本！ 我去年夏天就在我们修道院附近发现它的。”

“That’s it,” whispered Jack. “That’s exactly what we’re looking for!” “就是这个，”杰克小声说。 “这正是我们找的东西！”，

“You’re looking for the glacial buttercup?” Father Laurent asked. “Why?” “你们在找冰川蒲公英？”，劳伦特神父问。 “为什么？”，

“Why?” said Jack. He wasn’t sure what to say. “为什么？”，杰克说。 他不知道该说什么。

“Uh, we have some friends who really love flowers,” said Annie. “And when we were heading out to do our research in the Alps, they asked us to please try to find one of those and bring it back to them.” “呃，我们有一些朋友特别喜欢花，”安妮说。 “当我们去阿尔卑斯山做研究的时候，他们让我们试着找一种花带回去给他们。”

“I see,” said the monk. “Well, then allow me to give you this glacial buttercup to take back to your friends. I’m sure I can find another later in the summer. It is the least I can do to thank you for the wonderful work you did with Barry today.” “我明白了，”修士说。 “那我这就把这株冰川毛茛送给你，带回去给你朋友吧。 我相信我夏天晚点还能再找到另一株。 为了感谢你今天和巴里一起做的出色工作，这是我能做的最起码的事。”

“Thank you!” said Jack. “谢谢！”杰克说。

“Do you know if the glacial buttercup has any special meaning?” said Annie. “你知道冰川毛茛有没有什么特殊的意义吗？”安妮问。

Father Laurent squinted at the flower. “Well, throughout history many flowers have had special meanings,” he said. “Let me think.” 劳伦特神父眯着眼睛看着这朵花。 “嗯，历史上很多花都有特别的含义，”他说。 “让我想想。”

“Wait, please,” said Jack. He quickly pulled out his notebook and pencil. “Okay. Ready.” “等等，”杰克说。 他赶紧拿出笔记本和铅笔。 “好了，准备好了。”

“If I recall, buttercups stand for childhood, the carefree, open spirit of childhood,” said Father Laurent. “Such a joyful spirit can brighten even the most bleak and barren places.” “如果我没记错的话，蒲公英代表童年，那种无忧无虑、开朗的童年精神，”劳伦特神父说。 “这样快乐的精神，即使是最灰暗、最荒凉的地方也能变得明亮。”

Jack quickly wrote: 杰克快速写下：

图中文字：Buttercups:

Spirit of childhood

Joyful, carefree, open 图中文字：Buttercups：童年的精神，快乐、无忧无虑、开朗

Jack looked at Father Laurent. “That sounds like the spirit of a dog,” he said. He stroked Barry’s ear. It felt like warm velvet. 杰克看着劳伦特神父。 “这听起来像是狗的精神，”他说。 他抚摸着巴里的耳朵。 感觉像温暖的天鹅绒。

Father Laurent smiled. “Yes. Yes, it does,” he said. “Dogs and children share a common spirit. I do agree with that.” 劳伦特神父笑了。 “是的。 是的，确实如此，”他说。 “狗和孩子有着共同的精神。 我同意这一点。”

Jack and Annie looked at each other. “Well, I guess we’d better leave now,” said Jack. He put away his pencil and notebook. 杰克和安妮互相看了看。 “好吧，我想我们最好现在离开了，”杰克说。 他收起了他的铅笔和笔记本。

“Can you not stay another night?” asked Father Laurent. “It will be dark soon.” “你不能多待一晚吗？”拉乌尔神父问道。 “很快天就要黑了。”

“That’s okay. We can find our way home, even in the dark,” said Jack. “没关系。” 即使在黑暗中，我们也能找到回家的路，”杰克说。

“And where is home?” asked the monk. “家在哪里？”僧人问。

“Oh … it’s down the mountain a ways,” said Annie. “It’s not exactly in the first village you come to, but just outside that village, you know, before you see the church steeple. There you turn left onto a winding path and then go awhile, and then turn right—and then left …” She shrugged. “Well, we know how to find it.” “哦……在山下一点的地方，”安妮说。 “它不在你第一个看到的村庄里，而在那个村庄外面，你知道的，在你看到教堂尖顶之前。 在那里你向左转，走上一条蜿蜒的小路，走一会儿，然后向右转……再向左转……”她耸了耸肩。 “好吧，我们知道怎么找到它。”

“I see,” said Father Laurent, though he looked a little bewildered. He stood up. “Let’s carefully pack your flower, so you can carry it to your friends.” The monk took two pieces of thick paper from the flower book and placed the glacial buttercup between them. Then he handed it to Jack. “我明白了，”劳伦特神父说，尽管他看起来有点困惑。他站了起来。 “我们小心地把花包好，这样你就可以把它带给你的朋友了。”和尚从花书里拿出两张厚纸，把冰川毛茛夹在中间。 然后他把它交给了杰克。

Jack gently placed the flower at the bottom of his bag. Then Father Laurent led Jack and Annie out of the library. 杰克轻轻地把花放在包的最下面。 然后劳伦特神父带着杰克和安妮走出了图书馆。

Barry followed them down the hallway. When they reached the front hall, Annie looked at the big dog. “You have to stay here, Barry,” she said. 巴里跟着他们沿着走廊走。 当他们到达前厅时，安妮看着那只大狗。 “你必须留在这里，巴里，”她说。

Barry’s tail stopped wagging. 巴里的尾巴停止了摇动。

“Perhaps he can walk with you part of the way,” said Father Laurent. “Then just send him back to me. I think now that you’ve trained him, he can come back home by himself.” “也许他可以和你一起走一段路，”劳伦特神父说。 “那你就让他先陪你们走一段，然后再送他回来。” 我觉得现在你已经训练过他了，他可以自己回家了。”

“Great, thank you!” said Annie. “太好了，谢谢你！”安妮说。

Father Laurent opened the front door. “Farewell, my friends. Travel safely,” he said. “Come back for dinner, Barry.” 劳伦特神父打开了前门。 “再见了，我的朋友们。 一路平安，”他说。 “回来吃晚饭吧，巴里。”

Barry barked as if he understood and then bounded out into the snow. Jack and Annie followed him. The snow had completely stopped, and the sky was clear. The setting sun cast a brilliant light over the mountains. 巴里好像听懂了，汪汪叫了几声，然后蹦蹦跳跳地跑进了雪地里。 杰克和安妮跟着他走了。 雪已经完全停了，天空也很晴朗。 落日的余晖给群山披上了一层灿烂的光辉。

Jack remembered that the tree house had landed on a slope above the wide pass. His sense of direction seemed keener than before. “I think we originally came from that way,” he said. “Let’s try it.” 杰克记得树屋落在了宽广山口上方的一处斜坡上。 他的方向感似乎比以前更敏锐了。 “我想我们最初就是从那个方向来的，”他说。 “我们试试看吧。”

Jack led Annie and Barry away from the monastery. His eyes searched the pass for signs of the tree house, until finally he thought he saw it: brown wood nestled on a slope among gray boulders. “That’s it!” he said, pointing. “Over there!” 杰克带着安妮和巴里离开了修道院。 他的眼睛在山口四处搜寻树屋的踪迹，直到终于看到它：棕色的木头静静地躺在灰白色的岩石之间的一处斜坡上。 “就是那里！”他指着说。 “在那里！”

Jack started to run, and Annie and Barry ran with him. As they raced through the clean, crisp air, Jack remembered what it felt like to be a dog. The cold didn’t bother him at all; he didn’t feel sore or stiff. Nothing hindered his body. And most of all, he felt a wild joy. He felt as if he could run forever. 杰克开始跑起来，安妮和巴里也跟着他一起跑。 当他们穿过清新、凉爽的空气奔跑时，杰克想起了当狗的感觉。 寒冷对他一点影响都没有；他一点也不觉得酸痛或僵硬。 没有任何东西阻碍他的身体。 最重要的是，他感到一种狂野的快乐。 他感觉好像能一直跑下去。

Finally, laughing and panting, Jack, Annie, and Barry reached the base of the slope below the tree house. The setting sun covered the mountaintops with purple light. 最后，笑着、喘着气，杰克、安妮和巴里到达了树屋下方山坡的底部。 落日将山顶染成了紫色的光。

Barry barked. Jack could tell he wanted to climb into the tree house with them. “You have to go back to Father Laurent now, Barry,” Jack said softly. He tugged on Barry’s velvety ear. “But we think you’re a great dog, and so does Napoléon.” 巴里叫了起来。 杰克能感觉到，他想和他们一起爬进树屋。 “巴里，你现在得回去找劳伦特神父了，”杰克轻声说道。 他拽了拽巴里那柔软的耳朵。 “但是我们认为你是一只很棒的狗，纳波莱昂也这么认为。”

Barry made a snuffling sound. Annie turned her face away from Barry and Jack. 巴里发出了一声鼻音。 安妮把脸转向了巴里和杰克。

“Annie, aren’t you going to say good-bye to him?” said Jack. “安妮，你不打算和他道别吗？”杰克说。

Annie looked at Jack. Her eyes were filled with tears. “How do I do that?” she said. “How do I say good-bye to him?” 安妮看着杰克。 她的眼睛里充满了泪水。 “我该怎么说呢？”她说。 “我该怎么和他道别呢？”

“You tell him how you feel about him. Tell him about all the good things waiting for him back at the monastery,” said Jack. “The fire, the food, Father Laurent and Brother Michael, and all the other dogs. You tell him he’s going to save lots of lives.” “你要告诉他你对他的感受。 告诉他修道院里等着他的一切美好事物，”杰克说。 “火，食物，劳伦特神父和迈克尔神父，还有其他所有狗。” 你告诉他，他将会拯救很多生命。

Annie knelt down and put her arms around Barry’s giant head. He licked her as she clutched him. 安妮跪下来，把双臂环住巴里的巨大脑袋。 他舔着她，而她紧紧抱住他。

“Tell him, Annie,” Jack urged her. “告诉他，安妮，”杰克催促她。

Annie lifted Barry’s ear and whispered into it for a long time. Jack couldn’t hear all that she said, but he caught the words love and all my life. 安妮抬起巴里的耳朵，长时间地在耳边轻声细语。 杰克听不清她说的所有话，但他听到了‘爱’和‘我一生’这几个词。

Then Annie stood up and pointed toward the monastery. Smoke was rising from the chimney. “Go home now, Barry,” she said. “It’s where you belong.” 然后安妮站了起来，指着修道院。 烟囱里升起了一缕烟。 “现在回家去吧，巴里，”她说。 “这是你该待的地方。”

Barry looked from Annie to Jack. He sneezed. Then he climbed down the slope and took off for home. 巴里从安妮看向杰克。他打了个喷嚏。 然后他沿着斜坡爬下来，朝家的方向跑去了。

Annie turned away quickly and wiped her eyes. “Let’s go,” she said, and she scrambled up the slope and climbed through the tree house window. 安妮迅速转过身，擦了擦眼睛。 “我们走吧，”她说，然后她赶紧爬上去，从树屋的窗户爬了进去。

Jack followed her. Inside the tree house, he picked up the Pennsylvania book and pointed to a picture of the Frog Creek woods. “I wish we could go home, where we belong,” he said. 杰克跟在她后面。 在树屋里，他捡起了宾夕法尼亚的书，指着一张蛙溪镇森林的图片。 “我希望我们能回家，去我们该待的地方，”他说。

Booming dog barks came from the distance. Echoes of the barks filled the air. Barking echoed from all directions––the sky, the clouds, the rocky slopes, and the purple mountain peaks of the Great St. Bernard Pass. 远处传来狗的吠叫声。 吠叫声的回声充满了空气。 从四面八方都传来狗叫声——天空、云朵、岩石山坡，还有圣伯纳大山口那紫色的山峰。

Then the wind began to blow. 然后，风开始吹了。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后，一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。

Jack and Annie were wearing their jeans and sweatshirts again. Jack’s bag was a backpack. No time at all had passed in Frog Creek. Dawn was still breaking. Dogs were barking in the distance, and the woods were filled with birdsong. 杰克和安妮又穿上了他们的牛仔裤和运动衫。 杰克的包是一个背包。 在蛙溪镇，一点时间都没有过去。 黎明仍然正在到来。 远处传来狗叫声，树林里到处都是鸟儿的歌声。

“You okay?” Jack asked Annie. “你还好吗？”杰克问安妮。

She nodded. 她点了点头。

“Barry’s going to save lots of lives,” said Jack. “I know it.” “巴里会救很多人的生命，”杰克说。 “我知道。”

Annie smiled. “He was so funny,” she said wistfully. 安妮笑了。 “他真有趣，”她若有所思地说。

“Yeah, and he was happy,” said Jack. He reached into his backpack and carefully lifted out the papers with the glacial buttercup. He handed the dried flower to Annie, then pulled out their rhyme and reread it: “是啊，他也很开心，”杰克说。 他伸手从背包里小心地拿出那张写着冰川毛茛的纸。 他把那朵干花递给安妮，然后拿出他们的韵文，又读了一遍：

The second thing to break the spell

is a white and yellow flower.

Live its meaning for yourself,

if only for an hour. 打破这个咒语的第二件事是一朵白色和黄色的花。你要自己体会它的意义，哪怕只有一小时。

“I guess we did live its meaning for an hour,” said Jack. “我想我们确实体会了它的意义一小时，”杰克说。

“That joyful spirit that Father Laurent talked about?” said Annie. “那个父亲劳伦特提到的快乐精神？”安妮说。

Jack nodded. “For exactly an hour, while I was a dog, I was perfectly happy,” he said. 杰克点了点头。 “当我变成狗的时候，整整一小时我非常快乐，”他说。

“Only for an hour, perfectly happy?” said Annie. “只有一小时，就非常快乐？”安妮说。

Jack shrugged. “The rest of the time, I was pretty worried.” 杰克耸了耸肩。 “其余的时间，我挺担心的。”

“About what?” said Annie. “担心什么？”安妮问。

“Well, in the beginning, of course, I worried about the avalanche and losing you,” said Jack. “Then I worried that Teddy and Kathleen had sent us to the wrong place. I worried about finding the flower. I worried about wasting time training Barry. Then I worried about finding him.” “一开始，当然，我担心雪崩和失去你，”杰克说。 “然后我担心泰迪和凯瑟琳把我们送到错误的地方了。 我担心找不到那朵花。 我担心花时间训练巴里是浪费时间。 然后我担心找不到他。”

“You worry a lot, Jack,” said Annie. “你总是很担心，杰克，”安妮说。

“I know,” said Jack. “I needed a dog to teach me how to have a child’s joyful spirit.” “我知道，”杰克说。 “我需要一条狗来教我如何拥有孩子的快乐精神。”

“And Barry needed you to learn how to be responsible,” said Annie. “You taught him how to listen to others and how to find avalanche victims. You helped bring out Barry’s instincts to be a rescue dog, Jack. You did a great job.” “而巴里需要你来学习如何负责任，”安妮说。 “你教会了他如何倾听别人，如何找到雪崩的幸存者。 你帮助激发了巴里作为救援犬的本能，杰克。 你做得真棒。”

“Oh …” Jack felt his face getting red. “Well, you did a great job, too. You really did.” “哦……”杰克感觉自己的脸变得红红的。 “好吧，你做得也很棒，真的。”

“Whatever,” Annie said modestly. “At least we found the second thing to help save Penny.” She carefully placed the glacial buttercup in the corner of the tree house. “随你便吧，”安妮谦虚地说。 “至少我们找到了第二样能帮助拯救彭妮的东西。”她小心地把冰川毛茛放在树屋的角落里。

Jack reached into his bag, took out the emerald rose, and placed it beside the flower. 杰克把手伸进包里，拿出翡翠玫瑰，把它放在花旁边。

“We have two things so far,” said Annie. “到目前为止，我们已经有两样东西了，”安妮说。

“And two more to go,” said Jack. “还有两样要找，”杰克说。

“But right now, we have to hurry home and sneak back into our rooms and put on our pajamas and get into our beds, so Mom and Dad can wake us up and tell us to get ready for school,” said Annie. “但现在，我们必须赶快回家，偷偷回到我们的房间，穿上睡衣，上床睡觉，这样爸爸妈妈才能叫醒我们，告诉我们该准备去上学了，”安妮说。

Jack laughed. “We live a weird life,” he said. 杰克笑了。 “我们过着很奇怪的生活，”他说。

“Yep,” said Annie, “but I like it all.” “是啊，”安妮说，“但我喜欢这一切。”

“Me too,” said Jack. And they started down the rope ladder, headed for home. “我也是，”杰克说。 他们开始顺着绳梯往下爬，朝家的方向走去了。
