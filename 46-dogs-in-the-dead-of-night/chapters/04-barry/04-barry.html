4. Barry 4. 巴里

Jack and Annie followed Brother Michael out of the parlor. The monk carried two lanterns and chatted while he led them down a chilly hall. 杰克和安妮跟着迈克尔兄弟走出了客厅。 那个修士拿着两盏灯，一边走一边和他们聊天。

“This monastery was built on the highest part of the pass almost eight hundred years ago,” Brother Michael said. “We have a dog kennel and a chapel, as well as a library filled with books and exhibits of rocks, minerals, and butterflies. Father Laurent is quite well known for his research and his nature expeditions.” “这个修道院建在山口最高的地方，差不多有八百年了，”迈克尔兄弟说。 “我们有狗舍和教堂，还有一个装满书籍和展品的图书馆，展品包括石头、矿物和蝴蝶。” 拉乌尔神父因为他的研究和野外探险而非常有名。

“That’s cool,” murmured Jack. But he was too worried about their mission to say any more. How will we ever find a flower here? he wondered again. “真酷，”杰克小声说。 但他太担心他们的任务了，没再说什么。 我们怎么可能在这里找到一朵花呢？ 他再次想道。

Brother Michael opened a door to a small room. “We have many rooms for travelers,” he said. “But tonight you are our only visitors.” 迈克尔神父打开了一扇通向小房间的门。 “我们有很多房间给旅行者住，”他说。 “但今晚你们是唯一的客人。”

The monk placed a lantern on a table between two small beds. “I hope you will sleep well,” he said. “And if you have more questions, I will be glad to answer them tomorrow.” 修道士把一盏灯放在两张小床之间的桌子上。 “希望你们睡得好，”他说。 “如果你还有更多问题，我很乐意明天回答你。”

“Thanks. Good night,” said Annie. “谢谢。 晚安，”安妮说。

“Good night,” said Brother Michael, and he left the room and closed the door. “晚安，”迈克尔兄弟说，然后他离开了房间，关上了门。

Jack and Annie sat on their beds. The lantern light was casting shadows on the white walls of the small room. The wind rattled the windowpanes. 杰克和安妮坐在他们的床上。 灯笼的光在小房间的白色墙壁上投下影子。 风把窗户的玻璃震得哗哗响。

“I’m so glad to be here,” Annie said, sighing, “and not lost outside in the dead of night.” She lay back and covered herself with a brown woolen blanket. “我很高兴来到这里，”安妮叹了口气说，“而不是在深夜迷失在外面。”她躺下，用一条棕色的羊毛毯子盖住自己。

“No kidding,” said Jack. “But it still makes no sense that Teddy and Kathleen sent us here.” “真高兴你这么说，”杰克说。 “但泰迪和凯瑟琳为什么要把我们送到这里，还是让人搞不懂。”

“We’ll figure it out tomorrow,” said Annie. “我们明天再想办法，”安妮说。

Jack reached into his bag and took out the small scroll. He unrolled it and read aloud by the lantern light: 杰克把手伸进他的包里，拿出了一卷小卷轴。 他展开卷轴，借着灯笼的光大声读起来：

The second thing to break the spell

is a white and yellow flower.

Live its meaning for yourself,

if only for an hour. 打破咒语的第二件事是一朵白黄相间的花。亲自体会它的含义，哪怕只有一小时。

“It’s a mystery,” said Jack. “No flower can live in a cold, snowy place like this. And live its meaning? What’s that about? And why just an hour?” “这是一个谜，”杰克说。 “这里又冷又雪，没有花能活下来。” 活出它的意义？ 那是什么意思？ 为什么只有一小时？

“I don’t know,” murmured Annie. She closed her eyes and yawned. “Aren’t you tired?” “我不知道，”安妮低声说。 她闭上眼睛，打了个哈欠。 “你不累吗？”

“Yeah, very,” said Jack. “But I’m also worried about our mission.” He put the scroll back into his bag and pulled out their research book. “是啊，很累，”杰克说。 “不过我还在担心我们的任务。”他把卷轴放回背包里，拿出他们的研究书。

Jack flipped through it until he came to a section with photos of plants and animals. He read the chapter title aloud: “Flora and Fauna of the Alps.” He looked at Annie. “ Flora and fauna means plants and animals. Did you know that?” 杰克翻着书，直到翻到一页有植物和动物照片的地方。 他大声读出章节标题：“阿尔卑斯的植物和动物。”他看着安妮。 “Flora和Fauna就是植物和动物。 你知道吗？”

Annie didn’t open her eyes or say anything. 安妮没有睁开眼睛，也没有说话。

“Are you asleep?” asked Jack. “你睡着了吗？”杰克问。

“Yes,” said Annie. “You should go to sleep, too. You can read all this stuff in the morning.” “是的，”安妮说。 “你也该去睡觉了。 明天早上你可以读所有这些东西。”

“But nothing makes sense,” said Jack. “I don’t know if I can sleep.” “什么都不明白，”杰克说。 “我不知道我能不能睡着。”

“Try,” said Annie. “试试看，”安妮说。

Jack sighed. He closed the research book and placed it on the floor. Then he blew out the lantern flame and pulled up his wool blanket. 杰克叹了口气。 他合上研究书，把它放在地上。 然后他吹灭了提灯的火焰，拉起了他的羊毛毯子。

Jack kept his eyes open, but he couldn’t see a thing. The room was pitch-black. From somewhere in the monastery came soft, beautiful singing. Two or three Saints barked. The panes of their window rattled as the wind moaned outside. 杰克睁着眼睛，但什么也看不见。 房间里一片漆黑。 从修道院的某个地方传来柔和而美丽的歌声。 两三个圣人叫了起来。 风在外面呜咽着，他们的窗户玻璃也跟着晃动。

“We forgot to ask what year it is,” said Jack. “It’s hard to tell, don’t you think?” “我们忘了问现在是什么年份，”杰克说。 “很难说清楚，你不觉得吗？”，

“Shhh,” whispered Annie. “嘘——，”安妮小声说。

“But it feels timeless here,” Jack went on. “Everything could be in a time long ago or in our time: sun, snow, mountains, monks, soup, singing … wind … wool … night …” Jack closed his eyes. “但这里感觉像是永恒的，”杰克继续说道。 “一切可能都是很久以前的，也可能就是我们现在所处的时代：太阳、雪花、山脉、僧侣、汤、歌声……风……羊毛……夜晚……”杰克闭上了眼睛。

“And dogs …,” murmured Annie. “还有狗……，”安妮低声说道。

“Dogs …,” Jack repeated in a whisper. “狗狗……，”杰克小声重复道。

And he fell asleep. 他睡着了。

Jack opened his eyes. Cold gray light came through a paned window. Where am I? he wondered. He sat up, put his glasses on, and looked around the white-walled room. Annie was still asleep. 杰克睁开了眼睛。 冷灰色的光从一扇窗户透进来。我这是在哪里？ 他想道。 他坐起来，戴上眼镜，环顾四周这间白墙的房间。 安妮还在睡觉。

Oh, yeah, the monastery, Jack thought. “Hey, Annie,” he whispered. “Wake up.” 哦，对了，修道院，杰克心想。 “嘿，安妮，”他小声说。 “醒醒。”

“Where are we?” Annie asked, opening her eyes. “我们在哪里？”安妮睁开眼睛问道。

“In the Swiss Alps,” said Jack. “我们在瑞士阿尔卑斯山，”杰克说。

“We are? Why?” Annie asked groggily. “我们是？“ “为什么？”安妮迷迷糊糊地问。

“Good question,” said Jack. “Let’s read about flora and fauna of the Alps and figure it out.” “好问题，”杰克说。 “我们来读一读阿尔卑斯山的植物和动物，然后想办法解决它。”

Jack reached for the book on the floor. Just as he grabbed it, the door banged open, and a Saint Bernard dog burst into the room. The dog flung himself onto Jack’s bed. 杰克伸手去拿地板上的书。 就在他拿到书的时候，门猛地被推开，一只圣伯纳犬冲进了房间。 那只狗跳到了杰克的床上。

“Ahhh!” Jack yelled. “啊！”杰克喊道。

“Whoa!” cried Annie. “哇！”安妮喊道。

The giant dog plopped down on top of Jack and panted, HEH-HEH-HEH! His head was as big as a basketball. His breath smelled like fish and crackers. His fur smelled like wet wool. 那只巨大的狗扑通一声坐在杰克身上，喘着气，呼哧呼哧的！ 他的头有篮球那么大。 他呼出的气闻起来像鱼和饼干。 他的毛发闻起来像湿羊毛。

“Okay, get off, please!” said Jack. He tried to push the dog off, but the dog just wrinkled his brow and looked curiously into Jack’s eyes. Drool hung from his long jowls. “好了，求你下来吧！”杰克说道。 他试着把狗推开，但狗只是皱了皱眉头，好奇地盯着杰克的眼睛。 长长的下巴上还挂着口水。

“Eww! Annie, help!” said Jack. “呕—— 安妮，帮帮我！”杰克说道。

“Hey, you! ” Annie called to the huge, panting dog. “Come to me!” “嘿，你！”安妮对那只又大又喘气的狗喊道。 “来我这儿！”

The Saint Bernard jumped off Jack and bounded onto Annie’s bed with the same joy and energy. Annie couldn’t stop giggling as the dog licked her face. 圣伯纳犬从杰克身上跳下来，用同样的快乐和精力跳到了安妮的床上。 安妮忍不住笑嘻嘻地，狗用它的大头舔她的脸。

“Barry!” said Brother Michael. He stood in the doorway. “巴里！”迈克尔兄弟说道。 他站在门口。

Ignoring the monk, the dog nuzzled Annie with his giant head. “Stop! Stop!” she said, laughing. 无视修道士，狗用它巨大的头蹭着安妮。 “停！ 停！”她笑着说。

“Barry! Off!” the monk said. “巴里！ “走开！”和尚说。

“Barry!” said Jack, trying to help. “巴里！”杰克喊道，试图帮忙。

The dog looked over at Jack. Then he leapt off Annie’s bed. 那只狗朝杰克看了过来。 然后它从安妮的床上跳了下来。

“Oh, no!” cried Jack. “Don’t come back to me!” He covered his head with his blanket. “哦，不！”杰克喊道。 “别再回来找我！”他用毯子盖住头。

But it was too late. Barry jumped back onto Jack’s bed and started pawing at the blanket, trying to dig Jack out from underneath. 但已经太晚了。 巴里又跳回杰克的床上，开始用爪子刨毯子，试图把杰克从下面挖出来。

“Barry, come!” commanded Brother Michael. “Now!” “巴里，过来！”迈克尔兄弟命令道。 “现在！”

Finally the Saint Bernard leapt off Jack’s bed and bounded into the hallway. 终于，圣伯纳犬从杰克的床上跳下来，冲进了走廊。

Brother Michael quickly closed the door behind the rowdy giant. “Well! Now you’ve met Barry!” he said. 迈克尔兄弟迅速关上了那扇吵闹的巨人离开后的门。 “哇！ “现在你已经见到巴里了！”他说。

Out in the hallway, Barry whined and pawed at the door, begging to come back into the room. 在走廊里，巴里一边抱怨一边用爪子拍打房门，恳求着想回到房间里来。

“Oh, poor puppy,” said Annie. “哦，可怜的小狗，”安妮说。

“Barry may be young,” said Brother Michael. “But he is a two-hundred-pound dog who needs to grow up.” “巴里虽然还小，”迈克尔哥哥说。 “但他是一只两百磅重的狗，需要长大。”

“Aww,” said Annie. She laughed tenderly at the mournful sounds Barry was making. “啊，”安妮说。 她温柔地笑了，听着巴里发出的悲伤声音。

“Perhaps you do not realize this,” said Brother Michael, “but it is past noon. You have both been asleep for many hours.” “也许你没意识到这一点，”迈克尔兄弟说，“但现在已过了中午。” 你们俩已经睡了很长时间了。

“Really? Oh, man!” said Jack. He jumped off the bed and grabbed his shoes and put them on. He couldn’t believe they’d wasted so much time! “真的吗？ 哦，天哪！”杰克说。 他跳下床，抓起鞋子穿上。 他简直不敢相信他们浪费了这么多时间！

“We are just completing our noontime meal in the dining hall before we hike down the pass,” said Brother Michael. “Thousands of French soldiers will be crossing soon on their way to Italy. Father Laurent left to assist the first troops. I thought perhaps you would like to eat something before the rest of us leave to help.” “我们刚刚在餐厅吃完午饭，准备去山口徒步，”迈克尔兄弟说。 “很快就有成千上万的法国士兵会穿过这里前往意大利。 劳伦特神父已经出发去协助第一批部队了。 我想也许在我们其他人去帮忙之前，你们可能会想吃点东西。”

“Uh—sure, thanks,” said Jack. “呃——当然，谢谢，”杰克说。

“I will wait for you in the dining hall next to the parlor,” said the monk. “我会在客厅旁边的餐厅等你，”修道士说。

When Brother Michael slipped out the door, Barry greeted him with a happy yelp. Then the giant dog’s barking faded down the hallway. 当迈克尔兄弟溜出门时，巴里高兴地喊着欢迎他。 然后那只大狗的吠声沿着走廊渐渐消失了。
