1. The Second Thing 1. 第二件事情

“Jack, Jack!” whispered Annie. “杰克，杰克！”安妮小声说。

Jack opened his eyes. He’d been dreaming about running away from cobras. “What’s wrong?” he said, sitting up in bed. It was still dark outside. 杰克睁开了眼睛。 他刚才梦见自己在躲避眼镜蛇。 “怎么了？”他说，坐起来在床上。 外面仍然很黑。

“Nothing’s wrong,” whispered Annie. “We have to go to the tree house and come back before it’s time to get ready for school.” “没什么不对，”安妮小声说。 “我们必须去树屋，然后在上学前回来。”

“The tree house?” said Jack sleepily. “树屋？”杰克迷迷糊糊地说。

“Teddy and Kathleen may have translated the next lines of the spell,” said Annie. “We have to see if they’re there, or if they’ve sent a message.” “泰迪和凯瑟琳可能已经翻译了咒语的下一部分。”安妮说。 “我们必须去看看他们是不是在那里，或者有没有留下信息。”

“Huh?” said Jack. He was still half-asleep. “什么？”杰克说。 他还在半睡半醒的状态。

“We have to find the second thing to break the spell that turned Penny to stone!” said Annie. “Remember? Come on, Jack, wake up!” She shook his shoulder. “我们必须找到第二样东西，才能打破让彭妮变成石头的咒语！”安妮说。 “还记得吗？ 快醒醒，杰克！“她摇晃他的肩膀。

“Okay, okay. We have to get the second thing!” Jack jumped out of bed. “I’m ready!” “好吧，好吧。 “我们必须拿到第二样东西！”杰克从床上跳了起来。 “我准备好了！”，

“No, you’re not,” said Annie. “You have to put your clothes on. I’ll meet you downstairs.” “不，你还没准备好，”安妮说。 “你得先穿上衣服。 我在楼下等你。”

Annie left the room, and Jack quickly changed out of his pajamas and into jeans and a sweatshirt. He picked up his backpack and reached into an inside pocket. He pulled out the emerald rose they’d found in India. It was the first thing Teddy and Kathleen needed to break the spell and bring Penny back to life. 安妮离开了房间，杰克赶紧换下睡衣，穿上牛仔裤和运动衫。 他拿起书包，伸手摸了摸里面的口袋。 他拿出他们在印度找到的翡翠玫瑰。 这是泰迪和凯瑟琳需要的第一样东西，用来打破咒语，把彭妮救回来。

Jack grabbed his notebook and a pencil from his desk. He put them into his backpack along with the emerald rose. Then he slipped quietly out of his bedroom and down the stairs. 杰克从书桌上的抽屉里拿起了他的笔记本和一支铅笔。 他把翡翠玫瑰和别的东西一起放进背包里。 然后他悄悄地从卧室里溜出来，走下楼梯。

Annie was waiting on the front porch. The sky was just starting to become light. The spring air was damp and cool. Jack was glad that he had worn his sweatshirt. 安妮正在前门廊上等着。 天空刚刚开始变亮。 春天的空气又湿又凉。 杰克很高兴自己穿了毛衣。

“All set,” said Jack. “Let’s go.” “准备好了，”杰克说。 “我们走吧。”

Jack and Annie ran across the wet grass of their front yard and dashed down the sidewalk. The houses they passed were all quiet, but the world of nature was awake with birdsong and dogs barking in the distance. 杰克和安妮跑过他们家门前湿漉漉的草地，冲下人行道。 他们经过的房屋都很安静，但大自然的世界已经苏醒，鸟儿在唱歌，远处还有狗在吠叫。

Jack and Annie crossed the street and headed into the Frog Creek woods. It was hard to see in the shadowy dark, but they were so familiar with the path to the tallest oak that they quickly found their way. 杰克和安妮穿过街道，走进了蛙溪镇的树林里。 在阴暗的黑暗中很难看清楚，但他们对通往最高橡树的小路非常熟悉，很快就找到了路。

The tree house was there, waiting for them. But no one was looking out the window. 树屋就在那里，等着他们。 但没有人从窗户里往外看。

“Darn, no Teddy and Kathleen,” said Annie. “哎呀，泰迪和凯瑟琳不在，”安妮说。

“Well, at least they sent the tree house,” said Jack. “That must mean they were able to translate the next part of the ancient spell. They must have stayed in Camelot to work on the rest.” “好吧，至少他们把树屋送过来了，”杰克说。 “这一定意味着他们成功翻译了古老咒语的下一部分。 他们一定留在卡梅洛特，继续研究剩下的部分。”

“Yeah, probably,” said Annie. She grabbed the rope ladder and started up. Jack followed her. “是啊，大概如此，”安妮说。 她抓住绳梯，开始往上爬。 杰克跟了上去。

Inside the tree house, daylight was starting to creep through the windows. Jack saw a small scroll on top of a book in the shadows. “That’s it!” he said. He picked it up and read aloud: 树屋里， daylight 开始从窗户透进来。 杰克在阴影中看到一本书上放着一张小卷轴。 “就是它！”他说。 他捡起来，大声读道：

The second thing to break the spell is a white and yellow flower. Live its meaning for yourself,if only for an hour. 打破咒语的第二件事是一朵白黄相间的花。亲自体会它的意义，哪怕只有一小时。

“A white and yellow flower?” said Annie. “Well, that sounds easier than finding an emerald shaped like a rose.” “一朵白黄相间的花？”安妮说。 “嗯，这听起来比找到一朵玫瑰形状的翡翠要容易些。”

“Okay. But where do we go to look for it?” said Jack. He picked up the book from the floor and read the title aloud: “好吧。 但我们去哪儿找它呢？”杰克说。 他从地板上捡起书，大声读出书名：

“What’s that mean?” asked Annie. “The Swiss Alps?” “那是什么意思？”安妮问。 “瑞士阿尔卑斯山？”

“Those are mountains in the country of Switzerland,” said Jack. “People ski there and stuff. The Alps are the mountains they climb in The Sound of Music.” “那些是瑞士国家里的山，”杰克说。 “人们在那里滑雪和做其他事情。 阿尔卑斯山就是音乐之声里他们攀登的山。”

“Oh, that place!” said Annie. “Great!” “哦，那个地方！”安妮说。 “太棒了！”，

“Look, there’s a bookmark,” said Jack. He turned to a page marked with a blue velvet ribbon. There was a picture of tall mountain peaks and an open, snowy area surrounded by rocky slopes. The caption read: The Great Saint Bernard Pass. “看，这里有个书签，”杰克说。 他翻到用蓝色天鹅绒丝带标记的那一页。 这里有一幅高耸山峰的图片，还有被岩石山坡环绕的开阔雪地。 标题写着：“伟大的圣伯纳德山口”。

“That must be the exact place we’re supposed to go,” said Jack. “Ready?” “那一定就是我们应该去的 exact 地方，”杰克说。 “准备好了吗？

“Hold on—there’s something else,” said Annie. She picked up a small blue bottle from the floor of the shadowy corner. The bottle had a label on it. She read aloud: “等等——还有别的东西，”安妮说。 她从阴影角落的地板上捡起一个小蓝瓶子。 瓶子上贴着一张标签。她大声读出来：

图中文字：One sip with a wish will turn you into anything

you want for one hour. Use only once.

—Teddy and Kathleen 图中文字：喝一口，许个愿，你就能变成你想成为的任何东西，持续一小时。只能用一次。——Teddy和Kathleen

“Whoa,” said Jack. “哇，”杰克说。

“Anything we want?” said Annie. “我们想要什么都可以吗？”安妮说。

“That’s what it says,” said Jack. “标签上就是这样写的，”杰克说。

“This is going to be so much fun!” said Annie. “Let’s get going.” “这会非常有趣！”安妮说道。 “我们出发吧。”

Annie handed Jack the bottle. He carefully put it and the scroll into his backpack. Then he pointed at the picture of the Great Saint Bernard Pass in their Alps book. “I wish we could go there!” he said. 安妮把瓶子递给杰克。 他小心地把瓶子和卷轴放进背包里。 然后他指着他们阿尔卑斯山书籍中格雷特圣伯纳德山口的图片。 “我希望我们能去那里！”他说。

The wind started to blow. 风开始吹起来。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切又恢复了平静。

Absolutely still. 完全静止不动。
