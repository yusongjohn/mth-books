3. Magic? Magicians? 3. 魔法？魔术师？

“Wow, ” said Jack and Annie together. “哇，”杰克和安妮异口同声地说。

Inside the crowded fairgrounds, below the looming Eiffel Tower, a band played a lively march. Fountains shot colored water high into the sky. A small train chugged through the crowd, blowing its whistle. 在拥挤的博览会场地里，高耸的埃菲尔铁塔下，一支乐队正演奏着欢快的进行曲。 喷泉将彩色的水柱高高射向天空。 一列小火车穿过人群，吹着汽笛。

People of all ages from many different countries bustled about in the twilight. Everyone seemed to be having fun, reading from guide books, strolling from one exhibit to another, or buying refreshments and souvenirs. 不同国家、不同年龄的人都在暮色中忙碌着。 每个人似乎都很开心，有人在看指南书，有人在展览之间散步，还有人在买零食和纪念品。

“We can’t see much from here,” said Annie. “It’s hard to know what’s going on.” “我们从这儿看不到什么，”安妮说。 “很难知道到底发生了什么。”

“What about that little train?” said Jack. “Maybe we could ride on it and get a good look at everything.” “那辆小火车怎么样？”杰克说。 “也许我们可以坐上去，好好看看周围的一切。”

“Great idea,” said Annie. “好主意，”安妮说。

The train whistle blew again. 火车的汽笛声又响了起来。

“Over there,” said Jack. He pointed to a clear space where passengers were getting off the train and other passengers were boarding. “在那里，”杰克说。 他指着一个空旷的地方，那里有乘客正在下车，其他乘客正在上车。

“Hurry!” said Jack. “快点！”杰克说。

They raced to the train and jumped on. Annie dug into her pocket and took out some coins. She held them out to the conductor. He took a few, and Jack and Annie squeezed into seats on a wooden bench. The whistle blew, steam sputtered out of the smokestack, and the small train began to move. 他们跑向火车，跳了上去。 安妮把手伸进口袋，拿出了一些硬币。 她把硬币递给了列车员。 列车员拿了几枚，杰克和安妮挤到木制长椅上坐了下来。 汽笛响了，蒸汽从烟囱里喷出，小火车开始动了。

“Look for anything about magic or magicians,” said Jack. “找找有没有关于魔法或魔法师的东西，”杰克说。

As the train slowly chugged through the World’s Fair, a tour guide’s voice blared from a megaphone: “Welcome to the World’s Fair Sightseeing Train! On your ride, you will witness the astounding history of human structures as seen nowhere else! In every age, buildings had style and beauty.” 当火车缓缓穿过世界博览会时，一个导游的声音从扩音器里传来：“欢迎乘坐世界博览会观光火车！ 在你们的旅程中，你们将见证人类建筑的惊人历史，这是其他地方都看不到的！ 在每一个时代，建筑物都有自己的风格和美丽。”

The train chugged by cave dwellings, canvas tents, and huts made of mud. 火车隆隆驶过洞穴里的住所、帆布帐篷和泥巴做的小屋。

Magic? Magicians? Jack thought as he looked at the different structures. No, no, no. 魔法？魔术师？ 杰克一边看着不同的建筑一边想。不，不，不。

The train passed a thatched cottage, a mansion with columns, and a palace with a huge golden dome. 火车经过了一座茅草小屋、一座有柱子的大房子和一座有巨大金色圆顶的宫殿。

No, no, no, Jack thought. 不，不，不，杰克心想。

“Now we will visit the many lands of the world,” said the tour guide. “First, Egypt!” “现在我们将参观世界上的许多地方，”导游说。 “首先，是埃及！”，

The train chugged past an outdoor café. Smells of grilled meat and rich coffee filled the air. Three women with veils over their faces danced to flute music. 火车隆隆驶过一家户外咖啡馆。 烤肉和浓郁咖啡的香味充满了空气。 三位蒙着面纱的女士随着笛子音乐跳舞。

No magicians there, thought Jack. 杰克心想，那里没有魔法师。

“Next we have an African village on the beautiful Serengeti Plain,” said the tour guide. They passed a cluster of huts surrounded by tall grass. People played drums and shook gourd rattles. Still no magicians, thought Jack. “接下来我们要去美丽塞伦盖蒂平原上的一个非洲村庄，”导游说。 他们经过一簇茅草屋，周围长满了高高的草。 人们敲着鼓，摇着葫芦响铃。 杰克心想，还是没有魔法师。

“And now we visit a New Year’s festival in faraway China,” said the guide. The train chugged by Chinese acrobats and a huge dancing red dragon. “现在我们要去遥远的中国参加一个新年庆典，”导游说。 火车经过了中国杂技演员和一条巨大的红色舞龙。

“Dragons are sort of magic, aren’t they?” said Annie, looking back. “龙有点像魔法，对吧？”安妮回头说。

“It’s just a couple of guys in a costume,” said Jack. “That doesn’t count.” “那只是两个穿着戏服的人而已，”杰克说。 “那不算。”

“On our left is a Muslim mosque,” the tour guide said. “On the right, a Buddhist temple. Here is an exquisite Japanese garden….” “我们左边是一座穆斯林的清真寺，”导游说。 “右边是一座佛教寺庙。 这里有一座精致的日本花园……”，

“No, no, no,” murmured Jack. “不，不，不，”杰克小声说。

The train passed by a show with dolls dressed in outfits from all around the world. It rolled by a giant brown statue of a woman. “This amazing creation is the Roman goddess Venus,” said the tour guide, “made entirely of chocolate.” 火车经过了一场表演，里面的小人偶穿着来自世界各地的服装。 火车经过了一座巨大的棕色女性雕像。 “这个惊人的作品是罗马女神维纳斯，”导游说，“它完全是由巧克力做的。”

“That is amazing!” said Annie. “太棒了！”，安妮说。

“It is, but it’s not magic,” said Jack. “是的，但它不是魔法，”杰克说。

The train rode by a globe of the world at least three stories high. The globe was turning slowly. “See the lovely mountains, deserts, rivers, and oceans of Earth,” said the tour guide. 火车经过了一个至少有三层楼高的世界地图球。 地球慢慢地转动着。 “看看地球上的美丽山脉、沙漠、河流和海洋，”导游说。

“This fair really is a living encyclopedia!” Annie said. “这个博览会真的就像一本活的百科全书！”安妮说。

“But the encyclopedia doesn’t have what we need to find,” said Jack. He sighed and started thumbing through the guide book. “但百科全书里没有我们找的东西，”杰克说。 他叹了口气，开始翻看指南书。

“Ahh, fantastic!” a train passenger said. “啊，太棒了！”一名火车乘客说。

“Shocking!” said another. “令人震惊！”另一个人说。

“Magical!” exclaimed another. “神奇！”另一个人喊道。

Jack looked up. “Did someone just say magical? ” he asked Annie. 杰克抬起头。 “刚刚有人说是神奇吗？”他问安妮。

“False alarm,” said Annie. “They’re talking about the Eiffel Tower.” “虚惊一场，”安妮说。 “他们在谈论埃菲尔铁塔。”

The train came to a stop. All the passengers were gazing up. Pink lights lit the huge arches at the base of the tower. 火车停了下来。 所有乘客都抬头看着。 粉红色的灯光照亮了铁塔底部的巨大拱门。

“The Eiffel Tower was built especially for this World’s Fair,” said the tour guide. “The tower is nearly one thousand feet high, making it the tallest structure in the world today. Some of you may want to leave us here to get a closer look at Paris’s newest miracle.” “埃菲尔铁塔是专门为这次世界博览会建造的，”导游说。 “这座塔几乎有一千英尺高，让它成为当今世界上最高的建筑。 你们中的一些人可能想在这里下车，近距离看看巴黎最新的奇迹。”

People began climbing off the train. “Maybe we should get off here, too,” said Jack. “This train isn’t much help.” 人们开始从火车上下来。 “也许我们也应该在这里下车，”杰克说。 “这火车帮不上忙。”

Jack and Annie jumped off the train just before its whistle blew and it started moving again. 杰克和安妮在火车鸣笛并再次开动之前跳下了火车。

“That’s a tall tower,” said Jack, looking up. “那是一座很高的塔，”杰克抬头看着说。

“Really tall,” breathed Annie. “真的很高，”安妮轻声说。

Iron rods rose in crisscross patterns high into the sky. Large elevators clanked up through the tower’s lacy ironwork. Powerful spotlights beamed from the top of the tower, sweeping long fingers of light over the city. 铁杆以交叉的图案高高升向天空。 大型电梯在塔的铁艺结构中发出咔咔声向上移动。 强大的探照灯从塔顶射出，长长的光束扫过城市。

“It would be fun to ride to the top in one of those elevators,” said Annie. “坐电梯到塔顶一定很有趣，”安妮说。

“I know, but we don’t have time,” said Jack. “We have to find the four new magicians before the evil sorcerer finds them.” “我知道，但我们没时间，”杰克说。 “我们必须在邪恶的巫师找到他们之前找到四位新魔法师。”

“I wonder if he’s here yet,” said Annie. “他是不是已经来了呢？”安妮说。

Jack and Annie looked around at all the people busily moving about the fairgrounds, going from one exhibit to another. Parents were holding their children’s hands and pointing to the tower. Couples were strolling arm in arm. Everyone seemed happy and excited. 杰克和安妮环顾四周，看到许多人在游乐场里忙碌地穿梭，从一个展览走到另一个展览。 家长们牵着孩子的手，指着那座塔。 一对对情侣手挽着手散步。 每个人看起来都又开心又兴奋。

No one looks like an evil sorcerer, thought Jack. No one looks like a Magician of Sound, or a Magician of Light, or a Magician of the Invisible, or a Magician of Iron, either. 杰克心想：这里没有人看起来像邪恶的巫师。 这里也没有人看起来像声音魔术师，或者光的魔术师，或者隐形魔术师，或者铁的魔术师。

Jack’s thoughts were interrupted by a young girl’s voice—“You see, Papa? It is magic!” 杰克的思绪被一个年轻女孩的声音打断了——“你看到了吗，爸爸？这是魔法！”

“Magic?” said Jack. He and Annie looked at each other. “魔法？”杰克说。 他和安妮互相看了看。

“Over there,” said Annie. She pointed to a nearby exhibit. A little girl was laughing as her father pressed a pair of earpieces against his ears. “那边，”安妮说。 她指着附近的展品。 一个小女孩笑着，她的父亲把一副耳塞按在自己耳朵上。

Jack and Annie walked closer to the exhibit. “It is positively unbelievable, Mimi!” the man said, shaking his head. 杰克和安妮走近了展品。 “这简直难以置信，米米！”，那个男人说着，摇了摇头。

“It’s magic, isn’t it, Papa?” said the girl. “It can send a voice a thousand miles!” “这是魔法，对不对，爸爸？”小女孩说。 “它能把声音传到一千里之外！”

Annie grabbed Jack’s arm. “Did you hear what she just said?” she whispered. “ Send a voice a thousand miles—that’s what the Magician of Sound does!” 安妮抓住了杰克的手臂。 “你听到她刚才说的话了吗？”她小声说。 “把声音传到一千里之外——这就是声音魔术师做的事！”

“Right!” said Jack. He and Annie looked at the sign above the exhibit. It said: “对！”杰克说。 他和安妮看着展览上方的标牌。标牌上写着：

Telephone:

A New Invention by Alexander Graham Bell 电话：亚历山大·格雷厄姆·贝尔的新发明

“She’s talking about a telephone! ” said Jack. “I guess it’s just been invented!” “她在说电话！”杰克说道。 “我想它刚刚被发明出来！”

“So Alexander Graham Bell must be the Magician of Sound!” said Annie. “所以亚历山大·格雷厄姆·贝尔一定是声音的魔术师！”安妮说道。

“Oh, man, do you think Alexander Graham Bell is here in person?” said Jack. “哦，天哪，你觉得亚历山大·格雷厄姆·贝尔真的在这里吗？”杰克说。

“I’ll ask,” said Annie. She walked to a gray-haired woman helping with the exhibit. “Excuse me, but do you know where we can find Alexander Graham Bell?” “我去问问。”安妮说。 她走到一位正在帮忙布置展览的灰头发女士面前。 “打扰一下，您知道我们能在哪儿找到亚历山大·格雷厄姆·贝尔吗？”

“I’m afraid he just left,” said the woman. “恐怕他刚刚离开了。”女士说。

“Where did he go?” asked Annie. “他去哪儿了？”安妮问。

“I do not know,” said the woman. “A strange man gave me an invitation to give to Mr. Bell. When Mr. Bell read it, he left at once. That is all I know. Excuse me.” The woman turned away to answer someone else’s question. “我不知道，”女人说。 “一个陌生人给了我一封邀请函，让我交给贝尔先生。 当贝尔 先生读完后，立刻离开了。 那我只知道这些。 请问一下。” 女人转身去回答别人的问题。

“Alexander Graham Bell!” Jack said to Annie. “He’s a famous inventor, not a magician!” “亚历山大·格雷厄姆·贝尔！” 杰克对安妮说。 “他是个著名的发明家，不是魔术师！”

“The evil sorcerer must’ve heard about the telephone and thought it was magic,” said Annie. “那个邪恶的巫师一定听说了电话，还以为那是魔法，” 安妮说。

“I wonder what that invitation said,” said Jack. “And why did that lady say the messenger was strange?” “我好奇那封邀请函上写了什么，”杰克说。 “为什么那位女士说送信的人很奇怪？”

“Let’s ask,” said Annie. “我们去问她吧，”安妮说。

Annie tapped the gray-haired woman on the arm. “Excuse me, we have two more questions,” she said. “Do you know what was on the invitation? And why did you say the messenger was strange?” 安妮轻轻拍了拍那位灰头发女士的胳膊。 “对不起，我们还有两个问题，”她说。 “你知道邀请函上写了什么吗？ 还有，为什么你说送信的人很奇怪？”

“I do not know what the invitation said,” replied the woman, “but the man who delivered it was dressed in a long, dark cloak. He wore a hood that hid most of his face, and he spoke in a deep, whispery voice.” “我不知道邀请函上写了什么，”那位女士回答，“但送信的那个人穿着一件长长的、深色的斗篷。 他戴着兜帽，遮住了大部分脸，还用一种低沉而轻声的声音说话。”

Jack felt a chill go down his spine. So that’s what the evil sorcerer looks like, he thought. Just like you’d expect! 杰克感到一阵寒意顺着脊背流了下来。 所以那个邪恶的巫师看起来就是这个样子，他心想。 就跟你想的一样！

“Sounds like the sorcerer,” Annie whispered to Jack. “听起来像是巫师，”安妮对杰克小声说。

“I know, I know,” Jack said, looking around. “我知道，我知道，”杰克一边说一边四处张望。

“Do you have any idea where the strange man in the cloak went?” Annie asked the woman. “你知道那个穿斗篷的奇怪男人去哪儿了吗？”安妮问那个女人。

“He asked for directions to the Hall of Machines,” said the woman. “他问路去机器大厅，”女人说。

“Where’s that?” asked Jack. “Is that here at the fair?” “那在哪儿？”杰克问。 “那是在这次博览会这儿吗？”，

“Yes, of course. It is the gigantic building made of glass. Can you see the roof?” The woman pointed to an arched glass rooftop looming above other fair buildings in the distance. “是的，当然。 那是由玻璃建成的巨大建筑。 “你能看到屋顶吗？”那个女人指着远处一座拱形玻璃屋顶，它高高耸立在其他漂亮的建筑之上。

“I see it,” said Annie. “我看到了，”安妮说。

“Good,” said the woman. “Excuse me now. I must help some other people.” “很好，”那个女人说。 “现在请原谅我。 我得去帮其他一些人。”

“Sure, thanks,” said Annie. “Let’s go,” she said to Jack. She started walking quickly across the fairgrounds. “当然，谢谢，”安妮说。 “我们走吧，”她对杰克说。 她开始快速穿过游乐园。

“Wait, wait, wait,” said Jack, hurrying after her. “等一等，等一等，”杰克一边跑一边说。

“The messenger is the sorcerer, I just know it!” said Annie. “信使就是那个巫师，我确定没错！”安妮说。

“Of course he is,” said Jack. “But what do we do when we find him?” “当然啦，”杰克说。 “但是我们找到他之后该怎么办呢？”

“I don’t know yet,” said Annie. “我还不知道，”安妮说。

“He could be dangerous,” said Jack. “We need to make a plan.” “他可能很危险，”杰克说。 “我们需要制定一个计划。”

“We need to find him first,” said Annie, “before he gets away! Hurry!” She broke into a run and dashed toward the Hall of Machines. “我们得先找到他，”安妮说，“不然他就跑掉了！ 快点！”她突然加速，朝机器大厅跑去。
