9. The Sorcerer 9. 邪法师

Jack’ s knees turned to jelly. 杰克的膝盖变得像果冻一样软。

The knocking came again. 敲门声又响起来了。

Mr. Eiffel laughed. “Goodness, another unexpected guest!” He started toward the door. 埃菲尔先生笑了。 “天哪，又来了一个意想不到的客人！”，他朝门口走去。

“Don’t open it!” Jack shouted. “别开门！”，杰克喊道。

Everyone looked at Jack as if he were crazy. 大家都看着杰克，好像他疯了一样。

“It’s the sorcerer!” Jack said. “My sister was telling the truth! He thinks you’re all magicians!” “是邪法师！”，杰克说。 “我姐姐说的是真的！ 他以为你们都是魔术师！

“Don’t be afraid, son,” Dr. Pasteur said to Jack. “I’m sure it’s just another guest.” “Don’t be afraid, son,” Dr. Pasteur said to Jack. “I’m sure it’s just another guest.”

Mr. Eiffel stepped toward the door. Mr. Eiffel stepped toward the door.

“No, please!” shouted Jack. “不，求求你！”杰克喊道。

Mr. Eiffel opened the door. There was a deafening clap of thunder! A ball of fire blasted into the room! 先生。 埃菲尔打开了门。 突然传来一声震耳欲聋的雷声！ 一团火球冲进了房间！

Jack covered his face. 杰克用手遮住了脸。

Then all was quiet. 然后一切都安静下来了。

“Jack?” Annie said in a small voice. “杰克？”安妮用很小的声音说。

Jack looked up. A golden haze had filled the room. Annie stepped quickly to Jack’s side. But none of the others moved. Mr. Eiffel, Mr. Bell, Mr. Edison, and Dr. Pasteur were all as still as stone. 杰克抬起头。 房间里充满了金色的光晕。 安妮迅速走到杰克身边。 但其他人没有动。先生。 埃菲尔先生，贝尔先生。 爱迪生先生，和巴斯德博士。 都像石头一样一动不动。

Jack could barely make out a dark figure in a long cloak standing in the doorway. 杰克勉强看出门口站着一个穿着长斗篷的黑影。

“It’s him!” Jack cried. “We have to say our rhyme!” Jack shouted the line he had memorized: “是他！”杰克喊道。 “我们必须念出我们的咒语！”杰克大声念出他记下来的句子：

Thing before us, now we see— 出现在我们面前的事物——

Jack waited for Annie to finish the rhyme. But she didn’t say her line. Oh, no! She’s forgotten it! Jack thought wildly. 杰克等着安妮念完咒语。 但她没有念她的句子。哦，不！ 她忘记啦！ 杰克疯狂地想着。

Suddenly he heard Annie laugh. “It’s you,” she said. 突然他听到安妮笑了。 “是你，”她说。

Jack looked up. The haze had cleared. The sorcerer’s face glowed in the light. It was a familiar face, craggy with electric-blue eyes. 杰克抬头看了看。 雾气散开了。 巫师的脸在光中闪闪发亮。 那是一张熟悉的面孔，布满皱纹，眼睛是电光蓝的。

“Merlin?” breathed Jack. “梅林？”杰克低声说道。

The master magician answered him with a smile. 这位伟大的魔术师微笑着回答他。

“Merlin! Hi!” said Annie. She rushed over and hugged him. “梅林！你好！”安妮说道。 她跑过去，紧紧地拥抱了他。

Jack just stared at Merlin. “What happened?” he asked. “Where’s the evil sorcerer?” 杰克只是盯着梅林看。 “发生了什么？”他问。 “恶法师在哪里？”

“There are evil sorcerers in my world,” Merlin said in his deep voice. “But I assure you none of them were here at the World’s Fair today.” “在我的世界里有恶法师，”梅林用他低沉的声音说道。 “但我可以向你保证，今天在世界博览会的时候，他们一个都不在这里。”

“So you were the messenger?” said Annie. “ You delivered the invitations for everyone to come to the top of the tower?” “所以你就是信使？”安妮说道。 “你把邀请函送给每个人，让他们都来到塔顶？”

“Yes, I was the messenger,” said Merlin. “I wanted to gather these remarkable men together so you could meet them all in the short time you had to spend in Paris.” “是的，我就是信使，”梅林说道。 “我想把这些杰出的人聚集在一起，这样你们就能在短暂的巴黎之行中见到他们所有人。”

“But why did you tell us that we had to find them before an evil sorcerer did?” said Jack. “但是，为什么你告诉我们必须在恶法师之前找到他们？”杰克说道。

Merlin smiled. “Without that challenge, would you have used all your powers of thinking and courage?” he asked. “Would you have been so determined to find the ‘new magicians’ and learn their secrets?” 梅林笑了。 “如果没有这个挑战，你们还会用尽你们的思考能力和勇气吗？”他问。 “你是不是真的很想找到那些‘新魔法师’，并学会他们的秘密？”

“Well, maybe not,” Jack said honestly. “也许不是吧，”杰克诚实地说道。

“Problems make us focus our energy,” said Merlin. “They can help us think more sharply and act more swiftly. Never wish for all your problems to disappear. Problems can help you achieve your goals. Do you understand?” “问题能让我们集中精力，”梅林说道。 “它们能帮助我们更敏锐地思考，更迅速地行动。 千万不要希望所有的问题都消失。 问题可以帮助你实现你的目标。 你明白了吗？”

Jack and Annie nodded. 杰克和安妮点了点头。

“So now, what are the secrets of these remarkable men?” asked Merlin. “I truly wish to know.” “那么现在，这些非凡之人的秘密是什么？”梅林问道。 “我真的很想知道。”

“If you want to reach your goal, you have to love adventure and responsibility,” said Jack. “如果你想实现目标，就必须喜欢冒险和承担责任，”杰克说。

“You have to study and be prepared so luck will favor you,” said Annie. “你必须努力学习，做好准备，这样好运才会眷顾你，”安妮说。

“You have to work really hard, because genius is ninety-nine percent perspiration and only one percent inspiration,” said Jack. “你必须非常努力地工作，因为天才百分之九十九是汗水，只有百分之一是灵感，”杰克说。

“And you should never lose hope,” said Annie, “because when one door closes, another one opens, and you don’t want to miss it.” “你永远不要失去希望，”安妮说，“因为当一扇门关上时，另一扇门会打开，你可不想错过。”

“Wonderful!” said Merlin. “These are excellent secrets! And I believe you not only learned them on this mission, but you lived each of them as well. Do you understand?” “太棒了！”梅林说。 “这些是极好的秘密！ 我相信你们不仅在这次任务中学到了它们，还真正地实践了每一个。 你们明白了吗？

“I guess,” said Jack. “我想是的，”杰克说。

Annie looked at the four frozen men. “What about them, Merlin?” she said anxiously. “Will they be okay?” 安妮看着那四个被冻结的男人。 “他们怎么办，梅林？”她紧张地说。 “他们会没事吗？”

“Yes, they will awaken as soon as I leave. Do not worry,” said Merlin. “是的，我一离开他们就会醒来。 别担心，”梅林说。

“I’m sorry I almost made you disappear,” said Jack. “对不起，我差点让你消失了，”杰克说。

Merlin smiled. “That is quite all right. But now we have a little problem. One must never leave a magic rhyme hanging in the air unfinished.” 梅林笑了。 “那完全没问题。” 但是我们现在有一个小问题。 一个魔法咒语绝不能在空中未完成地悬着。

“Oh,” said Jack. “So Annie needs to finish the rhyme and make something disappear?” “哦，”杰克说。 “所以安妮需要完成这个押韵，让某样东西消失吗？”

“Precisely,” said Merlin. “Perhaps you could use the rhyme to speed me back to Camelot.” “没错，”梅林说。 “也许你可以用这个押韵把我送回卡梅洛特。”

“Sure,” said Annie. “But do you have to leave so soon?” “好的，”安妮说。 “但是你必须这么快就离开吗？”

“Yes, I must be on my way,” said Merlin. “I would not want to confuse these kind gentlemen. Do not worry. I will send for you both again in the very near future. But now it is time for me to … disappear.” “是的，我必须动身了，”梅林说。 “我不想让这两位善良的先生感到困惑。别担心。” 我会很快再叫你们两个人来的。 但现在，是时候让我……消失了。”

Jack smiled. “Good-bye, Merlin,” he said. 杰克笑了。 “再见，梅林，”他说。

Annie took a deep breath. Then she looked at Merlin and slowly said her line: 安妮深吸了一口气。 然后她看着梅林，慢慢地念出她的台词：

Thee-be-wan-new-ee-vee! Thee-be-wan-new-ee-vee！

There was a clap of thunder and a blast of fiery light—and Merlin was gone. 一道雷声响起，一道火光闪现——梅林消失了。

Just as suddenly, the new magicians came back to life. Mr. Eiffel pointed to the open doorway as wind gusted inside. “You see, son,” he said to Jack, “it was only the wind.” 就在那一瞬间，新的魔法师们恢复了生命。先生。 埃菲尔指着敞开的门道，一阵风吹了进来。 “你看，儿子，”他对杰克说，“只是风而已。”

“Oh, yeah,” said Jack, pretending to be embarrassed. “I’m sorry.” “哦，是啊，”杰克假装感到尴尬地说。 “对不起。”

“Don’t worry,” said Mr. Eiffel. “You and your sister are perfectly safe. We live in the wondrous new world of science, and not in the old world of magic and sorcerers.” “别担心，”埃菲尔先生说。 “你和你姐姐完全安全。 我们生活在科学这个奇妙的新世界，而不是魔法和巫师的旧世界。”

Mr. Eiffel moved toward the open doorway. “Come, let us all step outside and take a look at our new world.” 先生。 埃菲尔先生朝敞开的门口走去。 “来吧，让我们都走出去，看看我们的新世界。”

Everyone joined him on the windy terrace and looked over the railing. 大家都跟着他走到风大的露台上，俯身看向栏杆外。

“Paris is a lovely city, is it not?” Mr. Eiffel said. “巴黎是一座美丽的城市，不是吗？”埃菲尔先生说。

Jack and Annie and the others watched the giant spotlights sweep over Paris like white comets. The lights shined down on the domes and treetops, the grand monuments and church spires, the colorful fountain waters and rippling river. The boat lights twinkled like fireflies. 杰克、安妮和其他人看着巨大的探照灯像白色彗星一样扫过巴黎。 灯光照在圆顶和树顶上，照在宏伟的纪念碑和教堂尖顶上，照在五彩缤纷的喷泉和波光粼粼的河面上。 船上的灯光像萤火虫一样闪烁。

“Thanks to Mr. Eiffel and his tower, we can see the entire city,” Mr. Edison shouted above the wind. “多亏了埃菲尔先生 和他的埃菲尔铁塔，我们才能看到整个城市，”埃菲尔先生 在风声中大声喊道。

“Thanks to Mr. Edison, ten thousand gas streetlamps in Paris will soon be replaced by electric lights!” said Mr. Eiffel. “多亏了先生 “爱迪生，巴黎的十万个煤气路灯很快就会被电灯取代了！”埃菲尔先生说道。

“Thanks to Dr. Pasteur’s institute, we will soon have cures for many more deadly diseases,” said Mr. Bell. “感谢博士 “巴斯特研究所，我们很快就能找到更多致命疾病的治疗方法，”贝尔先生说道。

“And thanks to Mr. Bell, I’ll be able to call you all on the telephone and tell you about it!” joked Dr. Pasteur. “而且要感谢先生。 贝尔，我可以通过电话给你们所有人打电话，告诉你们这件事！”巴斯德博士开玩笑说。

Everyone laughed. 大家都笑了。

“And this is just the beginning!” said Annie. “Someday people will carry tiny telephones in their pockets and talk to other people anywhere in the world.” “这只是个开始！”安妮说。 “有一天，人们会在口袋里装上小小的电话，和世界各地的人说话。”

“Uh, Annie, we’d better be going,” said Jack. He didn’t want the others to know he and Annie were from the future. “呃，安妮，我们最好走了，”杰克说。 他不希望其他人知道他和安妮来自未来。

But Annie kept talking. “And there’ll be these things called computers,” she said, “that can give you instant information about anything, anytime—” 但安妮还在继续说话。 “还会有这些东西叫电脑，”她说，“它们可以随时给你任何事情的即时信息——”

“Annie!” said Jack. “安妮！”杰克说。

“And get this!” she said. She pointed to the full moon overhead. “Someday people will actually walk on that moon up there!” “还有更厉害的！”她说。 她指着头顶的满月。 “总有一天，人们真的会走到天上那个月亮上！”

The men all chuckled. “You have a most delightful imagination,” said Mr. Eiffel. 男人们都笑了起来。 “你有非常棒的想象力，”埃菲尔先生说。

“And that is a wonderful thing!” said Mr. Edison. “Without imagination, none of us would be standing here tonight.” “这是一件非常好的事情！”爱迪生先生说。 “没有想象力，我们此刻都不可能站在这里。”

“Well, we’d better be getting home now,” said Jack. “那我们最好现在回家了，”杰克说。

“And where is your home? The moon?” teased Mr. Eiffel. “你的家在哪里？ 是月亮吗？”埃菲尔先生开玩笑地问。

“No, it’s Frog Creek, Pennsylvania, in the United States,” said Jack. “不，是在美国宾夕法尼亚州的蛙溪镇，”杰克说。

“How will you get there?” said Mr. Bell. “你们怎么回去呢？”贝尔先生说。

“In our magic tree house,” said Annie. “在我们的魔法树屋里，”安妮说。

The men laughed. Jack tried to laugh with them. “ Ha. Good one, Annie,” he said. “Well, let’s go.” 那些人笑了。 杰克试着和他们一起笑。 “好啊，安妮，”他说。 “那我们走吧。”

“Annie, I hope you and your brother have a safe trip in your magic tree house,” said Mr. Eiffel. “You have both been most entertaining guests. Please come visit me anytime.” “安妮，我希望你和你哥哥在你们神奇的树屋里旅行平安，”埃菲尔先生说。 “你们俩都是最有趣的客人。 随时欢迎你们来拜访我。”

Jack and Annie waved good-bye to the four men. Then they climbed carefully down the spiral staircase and started down the 1,652 steps of the Eiffel Tower. 杰克和安妮向那四个人挥手告别。 然后他们小心地沿着螺旋楼梯往下爬，开始走埃菲尔铁塔的1,652级台阶。
