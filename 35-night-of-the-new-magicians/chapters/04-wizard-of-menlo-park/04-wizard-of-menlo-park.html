4. Wizard of Menlo Park 4. 莫尼洛帕克的巫师

J ack ran after Annie. He caught up to her outside the huge building made of glass. She was standing in line waiting to buy tickets. 杰克追着安妮跑。 他追上她，来到了那座由玻璃建成的巨大建筑外面。 她正排着队，等着买票。

“Listen,” Jack said breathlessly, “we … we’ve got to make a plan. What if we suddenly find the sorcerer? What do we say to him? What if he tries to use his powers against us?” “听着，”杰克气喘吁吁地说，“我们……我们必须想个计划。 如果我们突然遇到那个巫师怎么办？ 我们该对他说什么？ 如果他想用他的魔法对付我们怎么办？“

“We use a rhyme,” said Annie. “我们用一首歌谣，”安妮说。

“Which rhyme?” said Jack. “哪一首歌谣？”杰克问。

“How many, children?” interrupted the ticket seller. They’d reached the front of the line. “孩子们，”售票员插话道，“要几张票？ 他们已经到了队伍的最前面。

“Two, please,” said Annie, holding out some coins. The man gave her two tickets, and Annie turned back to Jack. “Let’s go inside and see if we can find the sorcerer. Then we can figure out which rhyme.” “两张，谢谢，”安妮说着，递出了一些硬币。 那人给了她两张票，安妮转身对杰克说。 “我们进去看看能不能找到那个巫师。 然后我们就能弄清楚是哪一句咒语。”

“Okay, but be cool,” said Jack, “so the sorcerer doesn’t notice us.” “好吧，但要冷静，”杰克说，“这样巫师就不会发现我们。”

Jack and Annie walked through the entrance of the glass building. “Oh, man,” whispered Jack. 杰克和安妮走进了玻璃建筑的入口。 “哦，天啊，”杰克小声说。

The Hall of Machines was the size of a football stadium. It was filled with thousands of people— and thousands of machines! Motors roared, wheels spun, gears clanked. 机器大厅的大小就像一个足球场。 里面挤满了成千上万的人——还有成千上万的机器！ 马达轰鸣，轮子转动，齿轮咔咔作响。

“What kind of place is this?” asked Annie. “这是什么地方？”安妮问。

Jack pulled out their guide book and read aloud: 杰克拿出他们的指南书，大声读起来：

In the Hall of Machines you will see machines from all over the world, bringing alive the world of engineers and inventors. You will see how cloth is sewn by machines to make clothes! Youwill see an exhibit of an automobile powered by gasoline! And of course, you will see a collection of inventions by the American grand-prize winner from Menlo Park, New Jersey— 在机器大厅里，你会看到来自世界各地的机器，展现工程师和发明家的世界。你会看到机器如何把布料缝制成衣服！你还会看到一辆由汽油驱动的汽车展品！当然，你还会看到一位来自新泽西州门洛帕克的美国大奖得主发明的发明收藏——

“Look at that!” Annie interrupted. She pointed to a mechanical walkway overhead. The walkway circled the entire exhibit hall. Sightseers gazed down on all the exhibits. “We can get a look at everything from up there.” “快看那！”安妮打断了他。 她指着头顶上的一条机械步道。 这条步道环绕着整个展览大厅。 游客们从上面俯视着所有的展品。 “我们从那里可以看遍所有的东西。”

“Good,” said Jack. “Maybe we can spot the sorcerer.” “好，”杰克说。 “也许我们能发现那个巫师。”

He put away the guide book and led the way up the stairs. They stepped onto the crowded moving walkway and peered down at all the people milling about the exhibit hall below. 他收起指南书，带头走上楼梯。 他们踏上拥挤的移动步道，低头看着展览大厅里来来往往的人群。

There were lots of men dressed in black coats and top hats. There were American cowboys and bearded men in Arab robes and headcloths. But Jack didn’t see a single scary-looking guy in a hooded cloak. 那里有很多穿着黑色外套和礼帽的男人。 那里有美国牛仔，还有穿着阿拉伯长袍、戴着头巾的有胡子的男人。 但杰克没有看到一个穿着斗篷、看起来吓人的男人。

As Jack and Annie moved slowly over the exhibits, the air grew hotter and the sounds in the hall grew louder. Hammers hammered, sirens blew, bells rang, whistles whistled. The voices of other sightseers swirled around Jack and Annie. “What genius!” “The Age of Machines!” “He’s the Wizard of Menlo Park!” 杰克和安妮慢慢地走过展品时，空气变得越来越热，大厅里的声音也变得越来越大。 锤子敲打着，警报器响着，钟声响起，哨子也吹响了。 其他游客的声音在杰克和安妮周围回荡。 “多么聪明啊！”“这是机器的时代！”“他就是门洛帕克的魔术师！”

“Did you hear that?” Annie shouted to Jack. “Someone said something about a wizard!” “你听到吗？”安妮对杰克喊道。 “有人提到了一个魔术师！”

“I heard!” said Jack. “ The Wizard of Menlo Park! We just read something about Menlo Park.” He pulled out their guide book and found the page he’d been reading. He read aloud: “我听到了！”杰克说道。 “梅诺帕克的巫师！” “我们刚读到有关门洛帕克的内容。”他拿出他们的指南书，翻到他刚才读的那一页。他大声读道：

And of course, you will see a collection of inventions by the American grand-prize winner from Menlo Park, New Jersey—Mr. Thomas Alva Edison! 当然，你还会看到来自新泽西州门洛帕克的美国大奖得主——托马斯·阿尔瓦·爱迪生先生发明的一些作品！

“Thomas Alva Edison!” said Jack. “He’s one of the most brilliant inventors who ever lived! Where’s his exhibit?” They looked down at all the booths. Directly below them was a booth with a big sign that said EDISON. “托马斯·阿尔瓦·爱迪生！”杰克说道。 “他是有史以来最聪明的发明家之一！ 他的展览在哪里？”他们低头看着所有的展位。 他们正下方有一个摊位，上面有一个大牌子，上面写着‘爱迪生’。

“There! Let’s go down!” said Annie. “看那儿！ “我们下去吧！”安妮说。

When the moving walkway came to a staircase, Jack and Annie jumped off and hurried down to the main floor, squeezing past people as they went. 当自动人行道来到楼梯口时，杰克和安妮跳了下去，赶紧跑向一楼，边跑边挤过人群。

“Okay, where is it?” said Annie, looking around. “好吧，它在哪里？”安妮环顾四周，说道。

“Follow me,” said Jack. He led her down a wide aisle until they came to the Edison exhibit. Lots of people were gathered around the booth. “跟我来，”杰克说。 他带她沿着一条宽宽的通道走，直到他们来到爱迪生的展览区。 很多人围在展台周围。

Jack and Annie slipped through the crowd to get a good look. Many of Thomas Edison’s inventions were on display. One of them had a big tube and lots of switches. Above it was a sign: 杰克和安妮挤过人群，想看得更清楚一些。 许多托马斯·爱迪生的发明都展出来了。 其中一件有一个大管子和很多开关。 上面有一块牌子：

Phonograph 留声机

“What’s a phonograph?” Annie asked. “留声机是什么？”安妮问。

“I think it’s like an old-fashioned CD player,” said Jack. “It was the first thing that played recordings of music.” “我觉得它就像一个老式的CD播放器，”杰克说。 “这是第一个能播放音乐录音的东西。”

A man with earphones was listening to the phonograph. Tears ran down his wrinkled face. “It’s incredible!” he said to the woman beside him. “Now we can hear the dead sing!” 一个戴着耳机的人正在听留声机。 泪水从他布满皱纹的脸上流下来。 “太不可思议了！”他对旁边的女士说。 “现在我们能听到死人唱歌了！”

“What does he mean?” Annie asked Jack. “他是什么意思？”安妮问杰克。

“I guess he means that even after people die, you’ll still be able to hear their voices on the recordings,” said Jack. “我想他的意思是，即使人们去世了，你仍然能从录音中听到他们的声音，”杰克说。

“I never thought of it like that,” said Annie. “我从来没这么想过，”安妮说。

“Shh!” someone said. People were trying to listen to a man delivering a speech to the crowd. His name tag said: HENRI. “嘘——！”有人说道。 人们正试图听一个男人向人群发表演讲。 他的名牌上写着：亨利。

“Yes, indeed,” Henri was saying. “Thomas Alva Edison of Menlo Park, New Jersey, U.S.A., invented the phonograph, shown to the public for the first time here at the Paris World’s Fair. Mr. Edison has invented many other things, too.” Henri moved to another display in the booth: a lightbulb with a switch. He clicked the switch, turning the bulb on and off. “是的，确实如此，”亨利正在说。 “托马斯·阿尔瓦·爱迪生，来自美国新泽西州门洛帕克，发明了留声机，第一次向公众展示是在巴黎世界博览会这里。先生。 爱迪生还发明了许多其他东西。”亨利走到展位的另一个展示台前：一个带开关的灯泡。 他按下了开关，把灯泡打开又关掉。

“Ten years ago, after years of work and thousands of experiments, Thomas Alva Edison invented the incandescent lightbulb,” said Henri. “When electricity passes through the thread, it gets very hot. There is no oxygen in the glass bulb, however. So the fire glows, but it does not burn.” “十年前，经过多年的努力和数千次实验，托马斯·阿尔瓦·爱迪生发明了白炽灯泡，”亨利说。 “当电流通过灯丝时，它会变得非常热。 然而，玻璃灯泡里没有氧气。 所以火焰会发光，但不会燃烧。”

As others moved closer to the lightbulb to get a good look, Jack turned to Annie. “ His fires glow, but they do not burn!” he whispered. “Thomas Edison is the Magician of Light!” 当其他人走近灯泡仔细看时，杰克转向了安妮。 “他的火光闪烁，但不会燃烧！”他低声说。 “托马斯·爱迪生是光明的魔术师！”

“I know!” said Annie. She turned to Henri. “Excuse me—is Mr. Edison in Menlo Park now?” she asked. “我知道！”安妮说。 她转向亨利。 “对不起——请问先生。 爱迪生现在在门洛帕克吗？”她问。

“No, as a matter of fact, Mr. Edison was here at this exhibit just a short while ago,” said Henri. “不，事实上，先生。 爱迪生刚刚在这个展览上出现过，”亨利说。

“Do you know where he is now?” asked Jack. “你知道他现在在哪里吗？”杰克问。

“No. All I know is that he was invited to a party and he left,” said Henri. “不知道。” “我只知道他被邀请去参加一个派对，然后就离开了，”亨利说。

Jack felt the hair on his neck go up. “The sorcerer,” he whispered. 杰克感觉他脖子上的汗毛都竖了起来。 “那个巫师，”他小声说。

“Did a strange messenger in a cloak deliver the invitation?” asked Annie. “是穿着斗篷的奇怪信使送的邀请函吗？”安妮问。

“Why, yes,” said Henri. “哦，是的，”亨利说。

“Do you know where the messenger went after he left here?” Jack asked. “你知道他离开这里之后去了哪里吗？”杰克问。

“He asked for directions to the Pasteur Institute. That is all I know,” said Henri. “他问了去巴斯德研究所的路。 “这就是我所知道的，”亨利说。

“The Pasteur Institute?” said Jack. “Where’s that?” “巴斯德研究所？”杰克说。 “那是在哪儿？”

But Henri didn’t answer. Another boy had asked him a question about the lightbulb. 但是亨利没有回答。 另一个男孩问他关于灯泡的问题。

“Come on,” Annie said to Jack. “We’ll find it somehow!” “快点，”安妮对杰克说。 “我们一定会找到它！”，

As Jack and Annie left the Edison exhibit, they could hear Henri repeating his speech word for word: “Ten years ago, after years of work and thousands of experiments, Thomas Alva Edison invented the incandescent lightbulb….” 当杰克和安妮离开爱迪生的展览时，他们能听到亨利一字不差地重复着他的演讲：‘十年前，在多年的工作和数千次实验之后，托马斯·阿尔瓦·爱迪生发明了白炽灯泡……’
