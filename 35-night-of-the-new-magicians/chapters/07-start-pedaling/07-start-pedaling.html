7. Start Pedaling! 7. 开始踩踏！

“W ow,” whispered Jack. He reached into his satchel and pulled out their rhyme book. “哇，”杰克小声说。 他把手伸进他的帆布包，拿出他们的咒语书。

“I’ll say the first line of the rhyme,” Jack said to Annie. “You say the second. Then we’ll start pedaling as fast as we can. The street’s empty. No one will see us. So we can—” “我会说咒语的第一句，”杰克对安妮说。 “你来说第二句。” 然后我们就赶紧开始踩踏板。 街上一个人也没有。 没人会看到我们。所以我们可以—”，

“Good,” interrupted Annie. “Let’s get going.” “好，”安妮打断了他。 “我们快出发吧。”

Jack held up the rhyme book so they could both read by the light of a streetlamp. He read his line first: 杰克举起韵律书，这样他们就能借着路灯的光一起读了。 他先读了他的那句：

Whirl and twirl and swirl and spin! 旋转，转圈，打转，旋转！

Then Annie read the second line: 然后安妮读了第二句：

Tee-roll-eye-bee-eye-ben! Tee-roll-eye-bee-eye-ben！

Jack shoved the book back into his satchel. “Pedal!” he cried. 杰克把书塞回了他的帆布包里。 “踩踏板！”，他喊道。

Jack and Annie balanced themselves on the bike and pedaled hard. The bike rattled over the cobblestones. 杰克和安妮坐在自行车上，努力地踩踏板。 自行车在鹅卵石路上颠簸着前进。

“Faster!” shouted Jack. He pedaled as hard as he could. “快点！”，杰克喊道。 他拼命地踩着踏板。

The bike shot forward! The front wheel began rising off the stone pavement! 自行车猛地向前冲了出去！ 前轮开始离开石板路！

“Whoa!” cried Annie. “停住！”，安妮喊道。

“Hold on tight!” cried Jack. “抓紧了！”，杰克喊道。

Jack gripped his handlebars as the wheels spun faster and faster and the bicycle rose into the air. It rose higher and higher above the dark street, above the rooftops, and into the moon-bright sky. 杰克紧紧抓住车把，车轮转得越来越快，自行车飞了起来。 自行车越飞越高，飞过了漆黑的街道，飞过了屋顶，飞向了月光闪耀的天空。

“Turn left!” shouted Jack. “向左转！”杰克喊道。

Annie turned her handlebars, and the flying bicycle headed straight toward the Eiffel Tower. The white beams of the tower’s spotlights swept over Paris, shining on chimneys, church steeples, and domes. But Jack kept his eyes fixed on the glowing iron tower. That was where they had to go. That was their goal. 安妮转了转车把，飞天自行车直直地朝埃菲尔铁塔飞去。 铁塔白色的探照灯光扫过巴黎，照在烟囱、教堂尖顶和圆顶上。 但杰克一直盯着那座闪闪发光的铁塔看。 他们必须去那里。 那里就是他们的目标。

As Jack and Annie pedaled, the warm Paris air embraced them, holding the bike steady. With very little effort, they drew closer and closer to the tower. Soon they were almost there. 杰克和安妮一边踩着踏板，巴黎温暖的空气就温柔地拥抱了他们，让自行车稳稳地飞着。 他们用很少的力气，就越来越接近那座塔了。 很快他们就快到了。

“We have to land!” shouted Jack. “我们必须着陆！”杰克喊道。

“I know!” shouted Annie. “Lean forward!” “我知道！”安妮喊道。 “往前倾！”

They both leaned forward. The front wheel of the bike dipped. Annie steadied her handlebars as the bike zoomed down toward the base of the tower. 他们两个都往前倾了。 自行车的前轮向下倾斜了。 自行车飞速向下冲向塔的底部时，安妮稳住了车把。

“Stop pedaling!” shouted Jack. He was afraid they would dive straight into the ground. “停下踩踏！”杰克喊道。 他担心他们会直接冲进地面里。

But the bike seemed to have a mind of its own. As it drew nearer to the base of the tower, it began to drop softly and slowly, like a falling feather. 但自行车似乎有自己的想法。 它慢慢靠近塔的底部，像一片羽毛一样轻柔地落下来。

The bike floated closer and closer to the ground. Its wheels brushed the grass of a shadowy garden not far from the tower. Jack and Annie pushed on the brakes and the bike slowed to a stop. Then it fell gently onto its side, dumping Jack and Annie onto the soft, wet grass. 自行车缓缓地向地面靠近。 它的轮子碰到了塔附近一个阴暗花园里的草。 杰克和安妮踩下刹车，自行车慢慢停了下来。 然后，自行车轻轻倒在地上，把杰克和安妮摔在了柔软潮湿的草地上。

Jack looked up. The Eiffel Tower loomed above them, reaching toward the bright Paris moon. 杰克抬头看了看。 埃菲尔铁塔高高地耸立在他们面前，向着明亮的巴黎月亮伸展。

“We made it,” Annie said breathlessly. “我们成功了，”安妮喘着气说。

“Not yet,” said Jack. “We still have to find that party.” He and Annie stood up. “还没呢，”杰克说。 “我们还得找到那个派对。”他和安妮站了起来。

“But first we have to leave the bike under the tower, like we promised,” said Annie. “不过首先我们要把自行车留在塔下面，就像我们答应过的那样，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie picked up the big bike. They jumped back on and started pedaling toward the Eiffel Tower. The bike felt a lot clunkier on the ground than it had in the air. As they bumped over the grass, they saw people streaming away from the fairgrounds. 杰克和安妮捡起了那辆大自行车。 他们跳上去，开始朝埃菲尔铁塔骑去。 自行车在地面上骑起来比在空中时要笨重得多。 当他们骑过草地时，看到人们正从游乐园里往外走。

“It looks like the fair’s closing,” said Annie. “看起来游乐园要关门了，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie parked the bike in a bike stand beneath the tower. The area looked deserted. There was no sign of a party or of the new magicians. A single guard stood under one of the tall arches. 杰克和安妮把自行车停在塔下面的一个自行车架上。 周围看起来空无一人。 没有任何派对的迹象，也没有新魔术师的踪影。 一个守卫站在其中一个高大的拱门下。

“Excuse me!” Annie called to the guard. “Do you know what time it is?” “对不起！”安妮对卫兵喊道。 “你知道现在几点了吗？”

“Almost ten,” answered the guard. “快到十点了，”卫兵回答道。

“Is the tower closed for the day?” said Jack. “今天塔楼关门了吗？”杰克问道。

“Yes, I’m afraid it is,” said the guard. “是的，恐怕是的，”卫兵说道。

“We heard there was going to be a party at the Eiffel Tower tonight,” said Annie. “我们听说今晚埃菲尔铁塔要举办一场派对，”安妮说道。

The guard shook his head. “No, sorry. As you can see, there is no party here—unless you mean the private affair at the top of the tower.” 卫兵摇了摇头。 “不，抱歉。 正如你所看到的，这里没有派对——除非你指的是塔顶的私人聚会。”

“There’s a private party at the very top?” said Annie. She and Jack looked up. The top of the tower seemed a mile away. “塔顶有私人派对吗？”安妮说道。 她和杰克抬头看了看。 塔顶看起来远得像一英里之外。

“Yes, with some very important guests,” said the guard. He leaned closer and whispered, “Mr. Thomas Edison, Dr. Louis Pasteur, and Mr. Alexander Graham Bell.” “是的，还有一些非常重要的客人，”守卫说。 他凑近了些，低声说：“先生。 托马斯·爱迪生，博士。 路易斯·巴斯德，还有先生。 亚历山大·格雷厄姆·贝尔。”

“That’s our party!” exclaimed Annie. “那就是我们的聚会！”安妮喊道。

“Is there a fourth guest?” Jack asked. “还有第四位客人吗？”杰克问。

“There may be others, but I did not see anyone else go up,” said the guard. “可能还有其他人，但我没看到有人再上去，”守卫说。

“We need to be there, too,” said Annie. “How do we get up?” “我们也得去那里，”安妮说。 “我们怎么上去呢？”

The guard smiled. “I am sorry,” he said, “but the elevators are all shut down for the night. Even if you had an invitation, the only way you could get to the top would be to climb the steps.” The guard looked up. “And that is quite a few steps, indeed. Come back bright and early tomorrow and you can ride the elevators.” The guard tipped his hat and strolled away. 卫兵微笑了。 “对不起，”他说，“但电梯今晚都关了。” 即使你有邀请函，唯一能到达顶部的方法就是爬这些台阶。”守卫抬头看了看。 “这确实要走很多很多台阶呢。” 明天一大早来，你可以坐电梯上去。”守卫摘下帽子，慢悠悠地走开了。

“Excuse me, sir!” Annie called after him. “Just how many steps are there?” “先生，请等一下！”安妮追着喊道。 “到底有多少级台阶？”

“To be exact, there are 1,652 steps to the platform at the top of the Eiffel Tower,” the guard said. Then he disappeared into the dark. “确切地说，埃菲尔铁塔顶部的平台有1,652级台阶。”守卫说道。 然后他消失在黑暗中。

“That’s too many steps,” said Jack. “台阶太多了，”杰克说。

“Let’s fly up on the bike!” said Annie. “我们骑自行车飞上去吧！”，安妮说。

“We can’t,” said Jack. “We can only use a rhyme once, remember?” He pulled out their book of rhymes and read the ones they hadn’t used. “ Find a Treasure You Must Never Lose.” “我们不能，”杰克说。 “我们只能用一次押韵，还记得吗？”他拿出他们的押韵书，读了那些还没用过的。 “你必须永远珍惜的宝藏。”

“That doesn’t help,” said Annie. “这也没用，”安妮说。

“Pull a Cloud from the Sky,” read Jack. “从天空中拉出一朵云，”杰克读道。

“No help there, either,” said Annie. “那儿也没用，”安妮说。

“Turn into Ducks,” read Jack. “变成鸭子，”杰克读道。

Annie smiled. 安妮笑了。

“Forget it. I’m not meeting Thomas Edison as a duck,” said Jack. “忘掉它吧。 我不会以一只鸭子的身份去见托马斯·爱迪生，”杰克说。

“So …?” said Annie. “那……呢？”安妮说。

“The steps,” said Jack. “台阶，”杰克说。

Jack and Annie moved quickly around the base of the tower, searching for steps. “There!” said Jack. They hurried to a staircase tucked inside one of the legs of the tower. 杰克和安妮迅速绕着塔的底部移动，寻找台阶。 “在那里！”杰克说。 他们赶紧跑到塔腿里面藏着的一段楼梯前。

Jack gripped the iron railing. “Ready?” he said. 杰克抓住了铁栏杆。 “准备好了吗？”他说。

“Yep,” said Annie. “Let’s go.” “是啊，”安妮说。 “我们走吧。”

Together they started up the 1,652 steps that led to the top platform of the Eiffel Tower. 他们一起开始爬那1,652级台阶，这些台阶通往埃菲尔铁塔的顶层平台。
