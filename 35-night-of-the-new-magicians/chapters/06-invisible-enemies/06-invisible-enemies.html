6. Invisible Enemies 6. 不可见的敌人

Jack and Annie whirled around. A dark figure was standing at a side door of the institute. The sorcerer! Jack thought. He frantically tried to remember his line of the rhyme. 杰克和安妮猛地转过身。 一个黑影正站在研究所的侧门旁。那是个巫师！ 杰克想。 他拼命努力回想自己该念的那句咒语。

“Can I help you?” the figure said. He stepped forward into the light of a gas lamp. He was an old man with stooped shoulders. His hair was white and he had a friendly smile. “我能帮上忙吗？”那个影子说。 他向前走，走进了煤气灯的光里。 他是个肩膀佝偻的老人。 他的头发是白色的，还带着友好的微笑。

“Hi! Who are you?” asked Annie. “你是谁？”安妮问。

“I am the night watchman,” the man said. “The institute is closed for the night. Have you been bitten by a dog? Have you come for the rabies treatment?” “我是夜班看守员，”那人说。 “研究所今晚已经关门了。” 你被狗咬过吗？ 你是来接受狂犬病治疗的吗？

“No, we’re fine,” said Annie. “没有，我们很好，”安妮说。

“Is that what you do here?” asked Jack. “You treat people for rabies?” “你们在这里是做这个的吗？”杰克问。 “你们是治疗狂犬病的吗？

“Yes. Not I, of course, but Dr. Pasteur. He treats other diseases as well,” said the old man. “He is the world’s foremost medical researcher.” “是的。 不是我，当然不是，而是巴斯德医生。 “他还会治疗其他疾病，”老人说。 “他是世界上最顶尖的医学研究员。

“Really?” said Jack. “What does he research?” “真的吗？”杰克说。 “他研究什么？”

“Microbes,” said the night watchman. “微生物，”守夜人说。

“Microbes?” said Annie. “微生物？”安妮说。

“Germs,” explained Jack. “细菌，”杰克解释道。

“Yuck,” said Annie. “真恶心，”安妮说。

“Microbes are invisible to the eye,” said the old man. “Some are useful and necessary, but others can cause great harm. Dr. Pasteur battles the deadly ones with research and vaccines and new medicines.” “微生物是肉眼看不见的，”老人说。 “有些是有用且必要的，但有些会造成很大的伤害。杜布瓦博士 用研究、疫苗和新药与致命的微生物作斗争。”

Annie gasped. “ He battles deadly enemies no one can see!” she said. “He’s the Magician of the Invisible!” 安妮惊呼了一声。 “他与看不见的致命敌人作斗争！”她说。 “他就是隐形魔术师！”

“Yes!” said Jack. “对！”杰克说。

The old man smiled. “I suppose you could say that,” he said. “Dr. Pasteur has certainly helped a lot of people.” 老人笑了。 “也许这么说没错，”他说。 “博士。 巴斯德确实帮助了很多人。”

“We have to find him,” said Annie. “Do you know where he is now?” “我们必须找到他，”安妮说。 “你知道他现在在哪里吗？”，

“Unfortunately, you have just missed him,” said the night watchman. “Earlier, a messenger left an invitation for him.” “不幸的是，你们刚刚错过了他，”守夜人说。 “早些时候，有人给他送了一封邀请函。”

“A strange man in a black cloak?” said Annie. “一个穿着黑斗篷的奇怪男人？”安妮说。

“You know him?” said the night watchman. “你认识他吗？”守夜人说。

“Not really,” said Jack. “But we think we know who he is. What did the invitation say?” “不 really，”杰克说。 “但我们觉得我们知道他是谁。 邀请函上写了什么？”

“I do not know,” said the old man. “But when Dr. Pasteur read it, he left immediately. He said he had to get to the Eiffel Tower by ten p.m.” “我不知道，”老人说。 “但是当杜布瓦博士 读到它时，他立刻离开了。 他说他必须在晚上十点前到达埃菲尔铁塔。”

“The Eiffel Tower?” said Annie. “埃菲尔铁塔？”安妮说。

“By ten p.m.?” said Jack. “Do you know what time it is now?” “十点之前？”杰克说。 “你知道现在几点了吗？”

The old man pulled out a pocket watch. “It is about twenty-five minutes until ten.” 老人掏出一块怀表。 “现在离十点还有二十五分钟左右。”

“Yikes, we’d better get going!” said Annie. “天啊，我们得赶快走了！”，安妮说。

“Thanks for your help,” Jack said to the night watchman. “谢谢你帮忙，”杰克对守夜人说。

“You’re welcome,” the old man said. Then he stepped back inside the institute and closed the door. “不用谢，”老人说。 然后他走进研究所，关上了门。

“Hurry!” said Annie. She and Jack ran down the steps to the street. “快点！”安妮说。 她和杰克跑下台阶，来到街上。

“Dr. Louis Pasteur!” said Jack. “I’ve heard of him, too! This is crazy. None of these guys are really magicians. They’re all famous for doing great things in science and stuff!” “杰克，快看！树屋里有东西在发光！” “路易·巴斯德！”杰克说道。 “我也听说过他！这太疯狂了。” 这些人都不是真正的魔术师。 他们全都因为科学方面做了很多伟大的事情而出名！

“I wonder who the fourth ‘ magician’ is,” said Annie. “The Magician of Iron, who bends the metals of earth and triumphs over the wind. Is he a magician or a scientist or what?” “我好奇第四个‘魔术师’是谁，”安妮说。 “铁之魔术师，他能弯曲大地上的金属，战胜风。 他是个魔术师，还是科学家，或者其他什么人呢？

“I don’t know,” said Jack. “But we have to get to that tower fast! We have to find the magicians and learn their secrets—before the sorcerer finds them!” “我不知道，”杰克说。 “但我们得赶快去那座塔！ 我们必须找到那些魔术师，学会他们的秘密——在巫师找到他们之前！

Jack and Annie looked up and down the lamp-lit street. A man was pushing a cart over the cobblestones. A couple on a two-seater bicycle rode by and disappeared. Then a horse and carriage clattered up the street. 杰克和安妮抬头看了看这条被灯光照亮的街道，又低头看了看。 一个男人正推着一辆手推车，在鹅卵石路上前进。 一对情侣骑着一辆双人自行车经过，然后就不见了。 接着，一匹马和一辆马车叮叮当当地沿着街道驶来。

“Taxi!” yelled Jack. “出租车！”杰克喊道。

But the horse and carriage kept going. There was no sign of another one. The street was empty except for Jack and Annie. 但马车继续向前走了。 没有看到另一辆出租车。 街上除了杰克和安妮，空无一人。

“Let’s start walking,” said Jack. “我们开始走路吧，”杰克说道。

“Look,” said Annie. “看，”安妮说。

The couple on the two-seater bicycle rattled back down the road. They stopped near a yellow streetlamp. 那对骑双人自行车的夫妇颠颠地沿路往回骑。 他们停在一根黄色的路灯旁。

“We heard you call for help. Do you need assistance?” the man asked in a gruff voice. “我们听到你呼救。 你需要帮忙吗？”男人用粗哑的声音问道。

Jack and Annie stepped closer to the bike. The riders were an odd-looking couple. The man was short. He wore a tall black hat and had a bushy beard and a long mustache. The woman was short, also. She wore a hat with a veil that hid most of her face. 杰克和安妮走近了自行车。 骑车的夫妇看起来很奇怪。 那个男人很矮。 他戴着一顶高高的黑帽子，有一把浓密的胡子和一条长长的胡须。 那个女人也很矮。 她戴着一顶帽子，帽子上有个面纱，遮住了她大部分的脸。

“We need to know the quickest way to the Eiffel Tower,” said Annie. “We have to get there by ten. It’s an emergency!” “我们需要知道去埃菲尔铁塔最快的路，”安妮说。 “我们必须在十点前到那里。 这是个紧急情况！

“An emergency! Oh, dear!” exclaimed the woman in a high, squeaky voice. “紧急情况！ 哦，天哪！”，一个声音尖锐地喊道。

The man cleared his throat and spoke in his low, gruff voice. “It would take quite a long time to walk to the Eiffel Tower from here,” he said. “Perhaps you should take our bicycle.” 男人清了清嗓子，用他低沉沙哑的声音说话。 “从这里走到埃菲尔铁塔要花很长时间，”他说。 “也许你们应该骑我们的自行车。

“Really?” said Jack. “真的吗？”杰克说。

“Of course,” said the man, “if it’s truly an emergency.” “当然可以，”那人说，“如果真是紧急情况的话。”

“It’s an emergency, all right,” said Annie. “But how can we get your bike back to you?” “这确实是个紧急情况，”安妮说。 “但是我们怎么才能把你的自行车还给你呢？”

“Just leave it for us under the arches at the bottom of the tower,” said the man. “只要把它留在塔底拱门下面就行，”那人说。

“We can pay you for letting us borrow it,” said Annie. She pulled coins out of her pocket and held them out to the couple. “You can have them all.” “我们可以付钱给你们让我们借一下，”安妮说。 她从口袋里掏出一些硬币，递给那对夫妇。 “你们可以全部拿走。”

“No, please, we are happy to help,” said the man as the couple climbed off their bicycle. “不用了，真的，我们很乐意帮忙，”那人说，同时那对夫妇下了自行车。

“This is really nice of you!” said Annie. “你们真是太好了！”安妮说。

“Good luck!” the woman squeaked. Then she and the man started walking away. “祝你们好运！”女人尖叫道。 然后她和那个男人开始走开。

“You were our good luck!” shouted Annie. “Thanks!” “你们给我们带来了好运！”安妮喊道。 “谢谢！”

“Yes, thanks a lot!” shouted Jack. “是啊，非常感谢！”杰克喊道。

The man turned back. “You had better hurry!” he called over his shoulder. “If you want to be there by ten, you will have to spin like a whirlwind!” Then he and the woman rounded the corner and were gone. 那个男人转过身。 “你们最好快点！”他回头喊道。 “如果你们想在十点前到那儿，就得像龙卷风一样飞快地骑！”然后他和那个女人转过拐角，消失不见了。

“I love this bike!” said Annie. She climbed onto the front seat, and Jack climbed onto the one in back. “Ready?” “我太喜欢这辆自行车了！”安妮说道。 她爬上了前排座位，杰克则爬上了后排座位。 “准备好了吗？”

“Go easy till we get the hang of it,” said Jack. “慢慢来，等我们掌握了再加速，”杰克说。

Jack and Annie started pedaling. At first, the large bike was very wobbly and they almost fell over. “We have to pedal at the same speed,” said Jack. 杰克和安妮开始踩踏板。 一开始，这辆大自行车非常摇晃，他们差点摔倒。 “我们必须以同样的速度踩踏板，”杰克说。

Jack and Annie balanced themselves on the bike and tried to pedal together. The bike bumped over the cobblestones a little more smoothly. 杰克和安妮在自行车上保持平衡，努力一起踩踏板。 自行车在鹅卵石路上颠簸得稍微平稳了一些。

“I think I’ve got the hang of it now!” said Annie. “我想我现在掌握了，”安妮说。

“Me too!” said Jack. “It isn’t that different from riding a regular bike.” “我也是！”杰克说。 “这和骑普通自行车差别不大。”

“Which way do we go?” said Annie. “我们该往哪个方向走？”安妮说。

“We have to find that busy street with the cafés,” said Jack. “我们必须找到那条有咖啡馆的热闹街道，”杰克说。

They rode the bike to the corner and looked right and left. “That way” said Annie. She pointed to the right, where there was a busy block with lots of gaslit restaurants and people strolling about. 他们骑着自行车到了拐角处，左右看了看。 “那边，”安妮说。 她指向右边，那里有一块热闹的街区，有很多煤气灯照着的餐厅，人们在四处散步。

“Okay go,” said Jack. “好，出发，”杰克说。

Annie turned the front handlebars, and she and Jack pedaled down the bumpy street. Annie steered them carefully around couples walking arm in arm. People at outdoor cafés waved at them as they rode by. 安妮转向前轮把手，她和杰克一起骑着自行车沿着颠簸的街道往下骑。 安妮小心地绕过手牵手走路的情侣。 他们骑车经过户外咖啡馆时，人们朝他们挥手。

But the street grew more deserted as Jack and Annie kept riding. By the time they came to the end, there was no one around. They pushed back on their pedals and brought their bike to a shaky stop. 但随着杰克和安妮继续骑车，街道变得越来越冷清。 等他们骑到街道尽头时，周围已经一个人也没有了。 他们踩着踏板，把自行车停了下来，车子有点摇晃。

“Which way now?” said Annie. “现在往哪走？”安妮说。

Jack looked to the right and left. Both ways were dimly lit, with closed shops and dark houses. Jack didn’t recognize anything. “I don’t know,” he said. “I wasn’t paying attention during the carriage ride.” 杰克向左和向右看了看。 两边的路都灯光昏暗，商店都关着，房子也黑乎乎的。 杰克什么都没认出来。 “我不知道，”他说。 “我坐马车的时候也没注意看。”

“Me, either,” said Annie. “我也是，”安妮说。

Jack could see the Eiffel Tower rising into the sky behind other buildings. It didn’t look that far away, but he had no idea how to get there. “Let’s try going left,” he said. 杰克看到埃菲尔铁塔在其他建筑物后面耸立在天空中。 它看起来并不太远，但他不知道怎么才能到那里。 “我们试试往左走，”他说。

Jack and Annie turned left and rattled over the cobblestones until they came to an empty square at the end of the street. 杰克和安妮向左转，颠簸地穿过鹅卵石街道，直到来到街尾的一个空广场。

“It’s a dead end,” said Jack. “这是死胡同，”杰克说。

“We have to go back!” said Annie. “Hurry!” “我们得回去！”安妮说。 “快点！”

Jack and Annie turned the bike around and sped back up the street. They pedaled until they came to another dead end. 杰克和安妮调转自行车，飞快地沿街冲了回去。 他们一直骑车，直到来到另一个死胡同。

“Oh, no!” said Jack. “Where’s that busy street with all the cafés?” “哦，不！”杰克说。 “那条有很多咖啡馆的热闹街道在哪里？”

“We must have missed it somehow,” said Annie. “We’re completely lost! And it’s almost ten o’clock!” “我们一定是哪里走错了，”安妮说。 “我们完全迷路了！” 而且现在快十点了！

“This is so annoying!” said Jack. “The tower is right there! ” He pointed to the Eiffel Tower looming over Paris. “It’s really not that far away! We just don’t know how to get there!” “这实在太烦人了！”杰克说道。 “那座塔就在那儿！”他指着巴黎上空高耸的埃菲尔铁塔。 “其实并没有那么远！ 我们就是不知道怎么去那儿！

“Wait a minute,” said Annie. “That guy said that to get there by ten, we’d have to ‘spin like a whirlwind.’” “等一下，”安妮说。 “那个人说，要是十点前到那儿，我们就得‘像龙卷风一样转圈’。”

“I know, but we’re lost!” said Jack. “We don’t know which way to go!” “我知道，但我们迷路了！”杰克说。 “我们不知道该往哪个方向走！”

“It doesn’t matter!” said Annie. “We have to spin! Spin into the Air! That’s one of our magic rhymes! We have to spin our bike into the air!” “没关系！”安妮说。 “我们必须旋转！ 转到空中！ 那是我们的魔法咒语之一！ 我们必须把自行车转到空中！”，
