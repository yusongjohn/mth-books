8. The Tomb 8. 陵墓

Jack and Annie stood frozen. 杰克和安妮站在那儿惊呆了。

The silent soldiers did, too. 那些沉默的士兵也一样。

Finally, Annie spoke. 终于，安妮开口了。

“They’re fake,” she said. “它们是假的，”她说。

“Fake?” whispered Jack. “假的？”杰克小声说。

“They’re not real,” she said. “它们不是真的，”她说。

“They look real,” said Jack. “他们看起来像真的。”杰克说。

Annie walked straight toward the front row of soldiers. 安妮径直朝前排士兵走去。

Jack held his breath. 杰克屏住呼吸。

Annie pulled the soldier’s nose. 安妮拉了拉士兵的鼻子。

“Fake!” she said. “假的！”她说。

“Oh, brother,” said Jack. He walked over to the soldier and touched his painted face. It was as hard as stone. “哎呀，真是的。”杰克说。 他走到士兵旁，摸了摸他上了彩的脸。 硬得像石头。

“It’s amazing,” Jack said. “太神奇了。”杰克说。

Annie nodded. “It’s like a museum.” 安妮点了点头。 “就像一座博物馆。”

She walked down a row between two lines of soldiers. 她沿着两排士兵之间的通道走下去。

“Wait. This is spooky,” said Jack. “What is this place?” “等等。 这儿真阴森，”杰克说。 “这是什么地方？”

He put down his sack and pulled out the China book. He found a picture of the frozen army and read aloud: 他放下布袋，抽出中国书。 他找到一张僵立军队的图，大声读了起来：

The Dragon King had 7,000 life-size clay figures made for his burial tomb. The clay was baked and painted. 龙王为他的陵墓制作了7000个真人大小的陶土人像。陶土被烧制并涂上了颜色。
The Dragon King hoped that the clay army would protect him after he died. 龙王希望他死后，陶土军队能保护他。

“It’s like the pyramid in ancient Egypt,” said Jack. “Remember? The queen was buried with a boat and lots of things to take to the afterlife.” He looked around. “Annie?” “这就像古埃及的金字塔。”杰克说。 “记得吗？ 女王下葬时带着一条船和许多要带到来世的东西。” “安妮？”

“I’m here,” she called. She was far down another row. “我在这儿。”她喊道。 她在另一排很远的地方。

“Come back here,” yelled Jack. “回到这儿来。”杰克喊道。

“No, you come here,” said Annie. “It’s so cool. All their faces are different.” “不，你过来。”安妮说。 “太酷了。 他们的脸都不一样。”

Jack threw the book into his sack. Then he hurried down the row to Annie. 杰克把书扔进布袋。 然后他匆匆沿着那排走到安妮身边。

“Look,” she said. “Just look.” “看。”她说。 “你就看看。”

In the flickering lamplight, they wandered down the rows of soldiers. No two soldiers had the same nose, the same eyes, or the same mouth. 在忽明忽暗的灯光下，他们沿着一排排士兵走去。 没有两个士兵长着相同的鼻子、眼睛或嘴巴。

“Oh, man. No wonder so many people had to work on this tomb,” said Jack. “天哪。 难怪这么多人得为这座陵墓干活，”杰克说。

“They really did a good job,” said Annie. “他们做得真好，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. “嗯。”杰克说。

There were kneeling archers and foot soldiers dressed in red and black armor. 有跪着的弓箭手，还有穿着红黑盔甲的步兵。

There were real bronze swords, daggers, axes, spears, bows, and arrows. 那里有真正的青铜剑、匕首、斧头、长矛、弓和箭。

There were even life-size wooden chariots with horses. The horses looked completely real. They were different colors with white teeth and red tongues. 甚至还有真人大小的木制战车和马。 那些马看起来完全是真的。 它们颜色各异，有白牙和红舌头。

“I have to take some notes about all this,” said Jack. “我得把这些记下来。”杰克说。

He pulled out his notebook and pencil. Then, kneeling on the brick floor, he wrote: 他抽出笔记本和铅笔。 然后跪在砖地上，写道：

图中文字：no two faces the same not even the horses 图中文字：没有两张脸是一样的，连马儿的面孔都不一样

“Ja-ack,” said Annie. “You know what?” “杰——克。”安妮说。 “你知道吗？”

“What?” “什么？”

“I think we’re lost,” she said. “我觉得我们迷路了，”她说。

“Lost?” Jack stood up. “We’re not lost.” “迷路了？”杰克站起来。 “我们没迷路。”

“Yeah? Then which way is out?” said Annie. “是吗？ 那出路在哪边？”安妮说。

Jack looked around. All he could see were rows of soldiers. In front of them, to the right, to the left, behind them—nothing but clay soldiers. 杰克四处看。 他只能看见一排排士兵。 他们前面、右边、左边、身后——除了陶土士兵，什么也没有。

“Which way did we come?” said Annie. “我们是从哪边来的？”安妮说。

“I don’t know,” said Jack. “我不知道。”杰克说。

All the rows looked the same. They stretched out endlessly. 所有的排看起来都一样。 它们无尽地延伸出去。

Jack tried not to panic. 杰克努力不让自己惊慌失措。

“I’d better look this up,” he said. “我最好查查书。”他说。

“Forget it,” said Annie. “Morgan said the research book would guide us. But in our darkest hour, only the ancient legend would save us.” “算了。”安妮说。 “摩根说过研究书会指引我们。 但在最黑暗的时刻，只有古代传说能救我们。”

“Is this our darkest hour?” asked Jack. “这就是我们最黑暗的时刻吗？”杰克问。

Annie nodded. “Yeah, it’s pretty dark.” 安妮点了点头。 “对，挺黑的。”

It does seem to be getting darker in here, thought Jack. The air was getting thicker, too. It seemed harder to breathe. 这儿好像真的越来越暗了，杰克心想。 空气也变得更浓了。 呼吸好像更困难了。

“Let’s ask for help,” said Jack. “我们去求助吧，”杰克说。

He reached into the sack and pulled out the bamboo book. He held it up and said, “Save us!” 他伸手进布袋，抽出那本竹简书。 他把它举起来说：“救救我们！”

As Jack waited, the tomb seemed unbearably quiet. 杰克等着的时候，陵墓安静得让人无法忍受。

Jack held the book up again. “Please help us find our way out,” he said. 杰克又把书举了起来。 “请帮我们找到出去的路，”他说。

He and Annie kept waiting. But nothing happened. 他和安妮继续等着。 可什么也没发生。

The air was growing even thicker. The light was getting dimmer. The countless rows of soldiers seemed creepier. 空气变得更浓了。 光线越来越暗。 那无数排士兵显得更吓人了。

Help did not come. 帮助没有到来。

Jack felt faint. “I—I guess we’ll just have—have to—” 杰克觉得自己快要晕倒了。 “我——我想我们只好——只好——”

“Look!” Annie said. “看！”安妮说。

“What?” “什么？”

“The ball of thread! It rolled out of your sack!” she said. “那团线！ 它从你的袋子里滚出来了！”她说。

“So what?” said Jack. “那又怎样？”杰克说。

He looked at his cloth sack lying on the floor. The ball of yellow silk thread had rolled out. And it was still rolling, leaving a trail of yellow thread! 他看着地上的布袋。 那团黄色的丝线已经滚了出来。 而且它还在滚，留下一道黄色的丝线痕迹！
