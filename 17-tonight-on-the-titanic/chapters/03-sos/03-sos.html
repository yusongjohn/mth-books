3. SOS 3. 求救信号

Jack stared at the name of the ship. 杰克盯着船名。

“You know what happened to the Titanic, don’t you?” he said softly. “你知道泰坦尼克号后来怎样了，对吧？”他轻声说。

Annie nodded. 安妮点点头。

“It hit an iceberg and sank,” she said. “But I don’t get it. I thought this ship was unsinkable.” “它撞上冰山沉了，”她说。 “可我不明白。 我还以为这艘船是不会沉的。”

“That’s what people thought when the Titanic was built,” said Jack. “But they were wrong.” “泰坦尼克号建成时人们就是这么想的，”杰克说。 “可他们错了。”

Suddenly, steam gushed out of the ship’s smokestacks. Then the engines cut off. The Titanic stopped moving. 突然，蒸汽从船的大烟囱里喷涌而出。 接着引擎停了。 泰坦尼克号停住了。

“We’d better go home,” said Jack. “There’s nothing we can do here. We’ll have to find another lost ship—a safer one.” “我们最好回家，”杰克说。 “我们在这儿什么也做不了。 我们得另找一艘沉没的船——一艘更安全的。”

“No! We have to stay and help,” said Annie. “We can escape in the tree house anytime we want.” “不！ 我们必须留下来帮忙，”安妮说。 “我们随时都可以坐树屋逃走。”

“But what can we do?” said Jack. “This ship’s going to sink no matter what. We can’t change history. And we can’t take anyone into our time with us.” “可我们能做什么？”杰克说。 “这艘船不管怎样都会沉。 我们无法改变历史。 我们也不能带任何人一起回到我们的时代。”

“Yeah, but maybe there’s some way we can help,” said Annie. “是啊，但也许有办法我们能帮忙，”安妮说。

“How?” said Jack. “怎么帮？”杰克说。

“I don’t know,” said Annie. “Let’s look around.” Holding Teddy, she disappeared down a small stairway. “我不知道，”安妮说。 “咱们四处看看吧。”她抱着泰迪，消失在一小段楼梯上。

Jack followed her to a lower level. 杰克跟着她走到下面一层。

Annie put Teddy down on the deck. The dog sniffed the chunks of ice that had fallen off the iceberg. 安妮把泰迪放到甲板上。 狗闻了闻那些从冰山上掉下来的冰块。

The ship seemed strangely empty. 船上出奇地空。

“Where is everyone?” Annie asked. “人都去哪儿了？”安妮问。

“Maybe they’re still asleep,” said Jack. “They probably don’t even know the ship hit something.” “也许他们还在睡觉，”杰克说。 “他们大概还不知道船撞到了什么。”

Annie and Jack started toward the front of the Titanic. 安妮和杰克朝泰坦尼克号船头走去。

“Come on, Teddy,” said Annie. “来，泰迪，”安妮说。

The little dog scampered after them. 小狗欢快地跟在他们后面跑。

They passed the round windows of different rooms on the ship. Jack looked through them as they went by. 他们经过船上不同房间的圆窗。 他们经过时，杰克透过那些圆窗往里看。

He saw exercise bikes and rowing machines in one room. In another, there were palm trees, bamboo chairs, and tables. Next, they passed a library filled with books. 他看见一个房间里有健身自行车和划船机。 另一间里有棕榈树、竹椅和桌子。 接着，他们经过一间摆满书的图书室。

“This ship is like a town,” said Jack. “这艘船像一座小镇，”杰克说。

Near the end of the deck, they peeked through another round window. Inside a little room, a man was wearing earphones. 靠近甲板尽头时，他们透过另一扇圆窗往里看。 一间小房间里，一个男人戴着耳机。

Jack heard a clicking noise as the man tapped a lever on a black box. 杰克听见咔嗒声，那人正敲着黑盒子上的操纵杆。

“What’s he doing?” whispered Annie. “他在干什么？”安妮低声说。

Jack shrugged. 杰克耸耸肩。

Another man stood nearby. He had a white beard and wore a fancy uniform. 另一个男人站在旁边。 他留着白胡子，穿着精致的制服。

“Send the international call for help,” he ordered the man with headphones. “Tell all ships close by to come at once. We’re sinking.” “发出国际求救信号，”他命令那个戴耳机的人。 “通知附近所有船只立刻赶来。我们正在下沉。”

“Yes, Captain,” said the man. “是的，船长，”那人说。

“Great, they’re calling for help!” Annie whispered. “太好了，他们在求救！”安妮低声说。

Jack shook his head. “I don’t think help will come,” he said. 杰克摇了摇头。 “我觉得救援不会来，”他说。

He shined the flashlight on the book and found a picture of the radio operator. 他用手电照着书，找到一张无线电发报员的图画。

He read quietly to Annie: 他轻声对安妮读道：

After the Titanic hit the iceberg at 11:40 P.M., the ship’s radio operator sent out an SOS. An SOS is the international distress signal in Morse code. Unfortunately, the only ship near the Titanic had turned off its radio for the night. All the other ships who received the message were too far away to help. When the Titanic sank around 2:20 A.M., she was all alone. 泰坦尼克号在晚上11点40分撞上冰山后，船上的报务员发出了SOS求救信号。 SOS是用摩尔斯电码发出的国际求救信号。 不幸的是，离泰坦尼克号最近的那艘船当晚关掉了无线电。 其他收到电文的船都太远，赶不过来帮忙。 大约凌晨2点20分泰坦尼克号沉没时，它完全孤身一人。

“That’s terrible,” said Annie. “太可怕了，”安妮说。

“I wonder what time it is now,” said Jack. “不知道现在几点了，”杰克说。

“I don’t know,” said Annie. “我不知道，”安妮说。

Jack pulled out his notebook and wrote: 杰克抽出笔记本写道：

图中文字：SOS sent out

Titanic sinks at 2:20 A.M. 图中文字：发出SOS信号，泰坦尼克号在凌晨2:20沉没。

“He’s leaving,” whispered Annie. “他要走了，”安妮低声说。

“Hide!” whispered Jack. “躲起来！”杰克低声说。

He and Annie moved quickly into the shadows. 他和安妮飞快躲进阴影里。

The captain stepped out onto the deck. 船长走上了甲板。

“Tell the men to start loading the lifeboats now,” the captain ordered a deck hand. “告诉船员现在开始装救生艇，”船长命令一名甲板水手。

“Yes, Captain!” the man answered. “是的，船长！”那人回答。

The captain and the deck hand left. Annie turned to Jack. 船长和甲板水手离开了。 安妮转向杰克。

“At least that’s good,” she whispered. “Everyone can get into the lifeboats.” “至少这一点还不错，”她低声说。 “人人都能上救生艇。”

“I don’t think it’s good enough,” said Jack. He read from the book again: “我觉得这还不够好，”杰克说。 他又从书里读道：

On the Titanic, there were 20 lifeboats. To save all the passengers, the ship needed twice as many. But with all the confusion on board, a number of the lifeboats were not even full when they left the ship. Many third-class passengers did not have a chance to get into any of the lifeboats because they were on the lower decks and didn’t know where to go. 泰坦尼克号上有20艘救生艇。 要救下所有乘客，船上需要两倍数量的救生艇。 可船上一片混乱，有几艘救生艇离开时甚至没有坐满。 许多三等舱乘客根本没有机会登上任何救生艇，因为他们在下层甲板，不知道该往哪儿走。

Jack wrote in his notebook: 杰克在笔记本上写道：

图中文字：Need twice as many lifeboats 图中文字：需要两倍多的救生艇

“Hey, I know what we can do to help,” said Annie. “嘿，我知道我们能怎么帮忙了，”安妮说。

“What?” asked Jack. “什么？”杰克问。

“We can help someone find the lifeboats,” she said. “我们可以帮人找到救生艇，”她说。

“You’re right!” said Jack. “With our book, we can find our way to a lower deck.” “你说得对！”杰克说。 “有了我们的书，我们能找到去下层甲板的路。”

He turned the page to a map of the ship. He and Annie studied it. 他翻到船的地图那一页。 他和安妮一起研究。

“We’ll start at the grand stairway,” said Jack. He traced a path with his finger. “Then we’ll go down to the third-class cabins this way.” “我们从大楼梯开始，”杰克说。 他用手指勾画出一条路线。 “然后我们从这条路下到三等舱。”

“Good plan!” said Annie. “好主意！”安妮说。

Jack looked back through the door. The radio operator was still tapping out his message—over and over and over. 杰克回头透过门看去。 报务员还在一遍又一遍敲着电文。

“SOS,” whispered Jack. “SOS，”杰克低声说。

He took a deep breath. 他深吸一口气。

“Okay,” he said. “Let’s go.” “好，”他说。 “走吧。”
