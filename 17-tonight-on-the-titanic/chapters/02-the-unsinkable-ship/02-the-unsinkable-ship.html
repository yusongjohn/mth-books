2. The Unsinkable Ship 2. 永不沉没的船

Jack opened his eyes. He shivered. Wherever they were now, it was cold— very cold. 杰克睁开眼睛。他打了个寒颤。 无论他们现在身在何处，天气都很冷——非常冷。

Teddy barked again. 泰迪又叫了起来。

“Shhh,” said Jack. “嘘，”杰克说。

Annie shined the flashlight on her clothes. 安妮把手电照在自己的衣服上。

“Wow, we’re dressed like old-fashioned kids!” she said. “哇，我们穿得像旧时的小孩！”她说。

Instead of pajamas and a poncho, she wore a sailor dress and a long wool cloak. 她没有穿睡衣和雨披，而是穿着水手裙和一件长羊毛斗篷。

Jack had on an overcoat and knee-length pants with long socks. His backpack had turned to leather. He also had on a shirt and tie. 杰克穿着大衣和过膝短裤，还配了长袜。 他的背包变成了皮制的。 他也穿着衬衫和领带。

“Where are we?” he wondered aloud. “我们在哪儿？”他大声想道。

Jack and Annie looked out the window. 杰克和安妮朝窗外望去。

The moonless sky glittered with stars. 没有月亮的天空里繁星闪烁。

There was a soft wind and the sound of water lapping. 微风轻拂，还能听见水轻轻拍打的声音。

The tree house seemed to be resting on a wooden deck between two giant columns. 树屋好像停在两根巨大圆柱之间的木甲板上。

Jack looked up and saw smoke coming out of the columns. 杰克抬头看见烟柱冒着烟。

“We must have landed on the ship between the smokestacks,” he said. “我们一定是落在了船上两根大烟囱之间，”他说。

Then Jack looked straight ahead and saw a box high in the air, near the front of the ship. 接着杰克朝正前方看，看见船头附近高空中有一个箱子。

“That must be the lookouts’ nest,” he said. “那一定是瞭望哨，”他说。

Jack sat back in the tree house and opened their book. Annie handed him the flashlight. 杰克在树屋里坐回来，打开他们的书。 安妮把手电递给他。

“Let’s find out where we are,” he said. “咱们弄清楚自己在哪儿吧，”他说。

He turned to a picture of a huge ocean liner. By the light of the flashlight, he read: 他翻到一幅巨大远洋客轮的图画。 借着手电的光，他读道：

Late at night, on April 14, 1912, an English ocean liner was making her first voyage across the Atlantic Ocean. She was going to New York City. Carrying 2,200 passengers, the ship was four city blocks long. Most people believed the ship was unsinkable. 深夜，1912年4月14日，一艘英国远洋客轮正在进行她横渡大西洋的首航。 它正驶往纽约市。 船上载着两千二百名乘客，船长达四个街区。 大多数人相信这艘船永不沉没。

“Oh, man, we’re in 1912,” said Jack. He pulled out his notebook and wrote: “天哪，我们在1912年，”杰克说。 他抽出笔记本写道：

图中文字：April 14, 1912 图中文字：1912年4月14日

“This ship is huge,” said Annie. “How will we ever find the gift to help free Teddy?” “这艘船好大，”安妮说。 “我们怎么才能找到帮助解救泰迪的礼物？”

“You can’t go looking for a gift,” said Jack. “You have to wait until someone gives it to you.” “你不能主动去找礼物，”杰克说。 “你得等到有人把它送给你。”

“Right,” said Annie with a sigh. “Well, I guess we just have to be ourselves, like Morgan said. And maybe we’ll get lucky.” “对，”安妮叹了口气说。 “嗯，我想我们只要做我们自己，就像摩根说的。 也许我们会走运。”

“This is hard,” said Jack. “这很难，”杰克说。

The little dog whined. 小狗发出了哀鸣。

“Don’t worry, Teddy,” said Annie. “We’ll free you from your spell.” “别担心，泰迪，”安妮说。 “我们会把你从咒语里解救出来。”

Just then, a shout came from the lookouts’ nest: “Iceberg ahead!” 就在这时，瞭望哨里传来一声喊：“前方有冰山！”

Jack and Annie turned back to the window—just in time to see a huge iceberg looming out of the sea. 杰克和安妮转回窗口——刚好看见一座巨大的冰山从海里隐约浮现。

The iceberg was dark with a fringe of white at the top. And it was right in front of the ship! 冰山是暗色的，顶部有一圈白边。 而且就在船的正前方！

Jack felt a jolt. Then he heard a grinding sound. The ship was scraping against the mountain of ice. 杰克感到一阵震动。 接着他听见一阵刺耳的摩擦声。 船正在那座冰山上刮擦而过。

“Arf! Arf!” Teddy barked. “汪！ 汪！”泰迪叫道。

“Shhh, don’t be scared,” said Annie. “嘘，别害怕，”安妮说。

She picked the dog up and hugged him. 她抱起小狗搂住它。

The scraping sound stopped. The ship slid past the iceberg until they lost sight of it. 刮擦声停了。 船滑过冰山，直到他们看不见它了。

The night was calm again. 夜晚又平静下来。

“See?” Annie said to Teddy. “It was just a little bump. This ship is unsinkable.” “看见没？”安妮对泰迪说。 “不过是轻轻撞了一下。 这艘船是不会沉的。”

But Jack was worried. “Wait. I have to read more about this,” he said.

“Don’t read now, Jack,” said Annie. “It’s time to get the gift. Come on, Teddy.”

She picked up the little dog and the flashlight. And she climbed out the tree house window.

“Hey—don’t take the flashlight!” said Jack.

But Annie was gone.

“Annie!” called Jack.

He heard a soft “Yikes.”

Annie stuck her head back in the tree house.

“Bad news,” she said in a whisper. “I think you’d better see this.”

Jack threw his stuff into his knapsack. He put it on and climbed out the window.

Annie was standing by the ship’s railing, holding Teddy.

Without a word, she shined the flashlight on a life preserver hanging from the railing.

In big black letters were the words:

R.M.S. TITANIC
