7. Something Very Weird 7. 非常奇怪的事情

Her trunk high in the air, Morning Breeze kept sniffing the hot wind. 晨风把长鼻子高高扬起，继续嗅着热风。

“We’ll get her away from here!” Jack said to Annie. “Then you and I can walk back to the tree house!” “我们会把她带离这里！”杰克对安妮说。 “那你就和我一起走回树屋吧！”，

“Great!” said Annie. “太好了！”安妮说。

“Go!” Jack yelled again at the elephant. “Go home! Go home!” He looked over his shoulder. He was afraid the Great Mogul might change his mind and send his guards after them. “快走！”杰克又对大象喊道。 “回家去！ “回家！”他回头看了看。 他担心大莫卧儿可能会改变主意，派人去追他们。

Morning Breeze let out a long, deep rumble and flapped her ears, as if she’d finally heard a distant call or caught a special scent. Her rumbling cries grew louder and louder—rrrrrrRRRRRR! She lumbered away from the drawbridge of the Red Fort. 晨风发出一声长长的、低沉的轰鸣，抖了抖耳朵，仿佛终于听到了远处的呼唤，或者闻到了一种特殊的气味。 她的隆隆叫声越来越响亮——rrrrrrRRRRRR！ 她从红堡的吊桥旁慢吞吞地走开了。

Jack was sure the elephant would start down the street filled with horses and oxcarts. But Morning Breeze headed up the other street, the one that went through the bazaar. 杰克肯定，大象会沿着那条满是马车和牛车的街道走下去。 但晨风却沿着另一条街道走，那条穿过集市的街道。

“No, no! Go the other way!” cried Jack. “不，不！” “往另一边走！”杰克喊道。

The elephant began to run. Her flat oval feet thumped against the road as she headed toward the tents and stalls of the bazaar. The street was crowded with merchants and shoppers—bearded men in colorful coats and women in outfits that completely hid their faces and bodies. 大象开始奔跑起来。 她那又扁又圆的脚重重地踩在路上，朝集市的帐篷和摊位跑去。 街上挤满了商人和购物者——穿着五颜六色外套的有胡子的男人，还有穿着完全遮住脸和身体的衣服的女人。

“Watch out, everyone!” Annie cried. “大家小心！”安妮喊道。

As the elephant ran between the busy stalls, everyone scrambled out of her way. 当大象在热闹的摊位间奔跑时，大家都赶紧躲开。

“Sorry! Sorry!” yelled Jack. “对不起！ “对不起！”杰克喊道。

“Rogue elephant!” a banana seller shouted. “野蛮的大象！”一个卖香蕉的人喊道。

“Sorry! Sorry!” Jack kept shouting. “对不起！ “对不起！”杰克一直喊着。

Morning Breeze knocked over wooden poles that supported white tents. The tents collapsed onto burlap bags filled with figs, rice, and peas. She knocked over baskets of lemons, oranges, and pineapples. 晨风撞倒了支撑白色帐篷的木桩。 帐篷倒塌在装满无花果、大米和豌豆的麻袋上。 她撞倒了装满柠檬、橙子和菠萝的篮子。

Angry merchants yelled and shook their fists. 生气的商人们大喊大叫，挥舞着拳头。

“She’s from the wild! She can’t help it!” Annie cried. “她来自野外！ 她没办法控制自己！”安妮喊道。

“Sorry! Sorry!” Jack said. “对不起！ 对不起！”杰克说。

Morning Breeze pulled down hanging silk shawls and carpets with her trunk. The silk merchant shouted with rage. He and the other sellers grabbed sticks and charged at the elephant. Jack and Annie scrunched down in their small straw carriage. 晨风用她的长鼻子把挂着的丝绸披肩和地毯都扯了下来。 丝绸商人愤怒地大喊起来。 他和其他商人们拿起棍子，朝大象冲了过去。 杰克和安妮缩在他们的小稻草马车里。

“Hurry! Go! Go!” Jack called to Morning Breeze. “快点！走！ 走！”杰克对晨风喊道。

With the merchants all chasing after her, Morning Breeze bolted from the bazaar. She started down a narrow pebbled road that soon became a dirt path. She left the bearded merchants shouting angrily in the dust. 随着商人们在后面追着她，晨风从集市上猛地冲了出去。 她沿着一条狭窄的鹅卵石小路跑，很快变成了泥土小路。 她把满脸胡须的商人们远远地甩在了尘土中。

Morning Breeze kept running down the path, passing farms with small mud huts. Sheep and goats bleated and scuttled out of her way. Chickens clucked and scattered. 晨风继续沿着小路奔跑，经过了一些有小泥屋的农场。 羊和山羊发出咩咩声，慌忙躲到一边。 母鸡咯咯叫着四处逃散。

Her bells jangling wildly, the elephant clomped past women and girls tending outdoor cooking fires. She lumbered past men and boys unloading hay from wooden carts. 大象的铃铛叮当作响，它大摇大摆地从正在户外生火做饭的妇女和女孩们身边走过。 它慢悠悠地从正在从木制推车上卸干草的男人和男孩们身边经过。

“Hi! Hi! Excuse us!” Annie yelled, waving. Jack tried to smile as he clung to the sides of the swaying carriage. “你好！你好！”， 对不起！“安妮大喊着，挥了挥手。 杰克努力地笑了笑，紧紧抓住摇晃的马车两边。

No one smiled or waved back. Everyone stared in shock as the elephant thundered by. 没有人回以微笑或挥手。 大家惊呆了，看着大象轰隆隆地从旁边跑过。

Morning Breeze ran to the end of the dirt path. But she didn’t stop there. She kept running, cutting her own path through a sun-scorched field. Jack felt as if they were riding a big gray ship on waves of tall yellow grass. 晨风跑到泥路上的尽头。 但她并没有在那里停下。 她一直跑着，穿过一片被太阳晒得干裂的田野，开辟出自己的道路。 杰克感觉他们好像正骑着一艘巨大的灰色船，在高高的黄色草浪上航行。

“This is fun!” said Annie. “这真好玩！”安妮说道。

Not really, thought Jack. Where in the world were they going? 其实并不好玩，杰克心想。 他们到底要去世界的哪个地方呢？

A million insects hummed and buzzed. Butterflies and dragonflies darted about. The elephant burst from the sea of grass and charged into a scrubby forest. As she plowed through shrubs and trees, birds cawed and flapped out of the brush. 成千上万的昆虫嗡嗡地飞来飞去。 蝴蝶和蜻蜓四处飞舞。 大象从草地里冲出来，冲进了灌木丛森林。 当它冲过灌木和树木时，鸟儿们惊叫着从灌木丛中飞了出来。

“Okay, slow down, Morning Breeze!” yelled Jack. “You’re back in the wild, so you can let us off now! We need to—” “好了，慢点，晨风！”杰克大喊。 “你们又回到野外了，所以现在可以让我们下车了！我们需要—”

Before he could finish, the elephant lifted her trunk and let out a loud shriek. She reared up on her hind legs. Jack and Annie tumbled out of the carriage. They slid down the elephant’s back and fell onto the ground. Morning Breeze shrieked again and tore away through the forest. 他还没说完，大象就抬起鼻子，发出一声响亮的尖叫。 她用后腿站起来。 杰克和安妮从马车里滚了出来。 他们顺着大象的背滑下来，掉在了地上。 晨风再次尖叫着，穿过森林狂奔而去。

Jack and Annie lay in the dirt. They heard the elephant trampling plants and crashing through bushes. They heard her bells jangling. Then the sounds faded away. 杰克和安妮躺在泥地上。 他们听到大象踩踏植物，撞倒灌木丛的声音。 他们听到她的铃铛叮当作响。 然后，这些声音渐渐消失了。

“Are you okay?” Annie asked. “你还好吗？”安妮问。

“Yep,” said Jack. “But that wasn’t very polite of her.” “没事，”杰克说。 “不过她那样做不太礼貌。”

Annie laughed. “Well, you asked her to let us off,” she said. 安妮笑了。 “好吧，是你让她让我们下车的，”她说。

“Yeah, but not dump us off,” said Jack. He waved away flies and slapped at mosquitoes. He felt sweaty and thirsty and exhausted. “I wonder how long it will take us to get back to the tree house?” “是啊，但不是把我们扔在这里，”杰克说。 他挥手赶走苍蝇，拍打蚊子。 他感觉又热又渴，累得不行。 “我想知道我们得多久才能回到树屋？”

“I don’t know—let’s just start retracing our steps,” said Annie. “我不知道——我们还是先回头走吧，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie stood up and brushed the dirt off their coats. 杰克和安妮站起来，把外套上的泥土刷掉。

“Hey, where’s your bag?” asked Annie. “嘿，你的包去哪儿了？”安妮问。

“My bag?” said Jack. Where was it? He whirled around. He saw it lying in the grass. “我的包？”杰克说。它去哪儿了？ 他转过身。 他看见包躺在草地上。

“There!” he said. He hurried to his bag and picked it up. It was open. “Oh, no!” He reached in and pulled out their research book, the blue bottle, and Teddy and Kathleen’s note. He searched frantically for the emerald rose. “在那里！”他说。 他赶紧跑过去，捡起包。包是开着的。 “哦，不！”他把手伸进去，拿出他们的研究书、蓝色瓶子，还有泰迪和凯瑟琳的便条。 他焦急地翻找着翡翠玫瑰。

“I don’t believe it! The emerald’s missing!” cried Jack. “我不相信！” “翡翠不见了！”杰克喊道。

“It must have slipped out when we fell off Morning Breeze,” said Annie. “We’ll find it. It has to be here somewhere.” “我们掉下晨风号的时候，它一定掉出去了，”安妮说。 “我们会找到它的。 它肯定就在这附近。”

Jack shoved everything back into his bag. Then he and Annie got down on their hands and knees. Jack looked intently at the forest undergrowth—the dried vegetation, sticks, clumps of dirt, and rotten leaves. 杰克把所有的东西都塞回了他的包里。 然后他和安妮跪在地上，手脚着地。 杰克仔细地看了看森林的地面上——干枯的植物、树枝、泥土块和腐烂的叶子。

“I can’t believe this!” said Jack. They had been so close to returning home, their mission done. Now they were stuck in the middle of nowhere and the emerald rose was missing. “I don’t see it. It’s not here. Maybe it fell out at the bazaar when—” “我简直不敢相信！”杰克说。 他们本来离回家已经很近了，任务也完成了。 现在他们却被困在了荒无人烟的地方，而且翡翠玫瑰也不见了。 “我没看到。它不在这里。” “也许它在集市上掉下来了——”

“Bingo!” said Annie. “There it is!” “ bingo！”安妮说。 “在那里！”

“Where? Where?” said Jack. “哪里？ “哪里？”杰克说。

“There!” Annie said, pointing. “在那里！”安妮指着说。

Jack saw it. The emerald rose glittered like a tiny green light in a small, sunlit clearing. It was lying next to a mound of earth and dead leaves. 杰克看到了它。 翡翠玫瑰在一小片阳光照耀的空地上闪闪发光，就像一盏小小的绿色灯。 它躺在一堆泥土和枯叶旁边。

“Yes!” said Jack. He and Annie scrambled to their feet and headed for the mound. “是的！”杰克说。 他和安妮赶紧站起来，朝土堆跑去。

But when they got closer, Annie grabbed his arm. “Wait! There’s something weird happening there,” she whispered. “Very weird. See?” 但当他们走近时，安妮抓住了他的手臂。 “等一下！ 那里好像有什么奇怪的事情在发生，”她小声说。 “非常奇怪。你看？”

“What? Where?” said Jack. “什么？ 在哪里？”杰克说。

Annie pointed to the mound. Something very weird was happening. The earth and leaves seemed to be moving! Then Jack saw speckled yellow bands and two shiny black eyes. 安妮指着土堆。 那里真的发生了一件非常奇怪的事情。 地面和树叶好像在动！ 然后杰克看见了有斑点的黄色条纹，还有两只闪亮的黑眼睛。

Jack gasped. “Oh, no,” he whispered. “A king cobra!” 杰克惊叫了一声。 “哦，天哪，”他小声说。 “是眼镜蛇！”
