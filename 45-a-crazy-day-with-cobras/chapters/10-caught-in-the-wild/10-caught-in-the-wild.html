10. Caught in the Wild 10. 陷入野境

Instantly the straw carriage felt small and cramped. Jack and Annie sat up and looked at each other. 瞬间，稻草马车感觉又小又拥挤。 杰克和安妮坐起来，互相看着对方。

“We’re big again,” said Annie. “我们又变回原来的样子了，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack, dazed. “是的，”杰克迷迷糊糊地说。

“Where’s the emerald?” said Annie. “翡翠在哪里？”安妮问。

Jack opened his bag. The jewel looked tiny again, but it was there. “No problem. I have it,” he said. 杰克打开他的包。 那颗宝石看起来又变得很小，但确实在那里。 “没问题， 我有，”他说。

“It was kind of fun being so little, wasn’t it?” said Annie. “变成那么小还挺有趣的，不是吗？”安妮说。

“Yeah, until we nearly got killed by humongous, superpoisonous snakes,” said Jack. “是的，直到我们差点被又大又毒的蛇吃掉，”杰克说。

“Thanks for saving us, Morning Breeze!” called Annie. She reached out from the carriage and patted the elephant’s wrinkled skin. “谢谢你救了我们，晨风！”安妮喊道。 她从马车里伸出胳膊，轻轻拍了拍大象皱巴巴的皮肤。

“Yeah, thanks,” said Jack. “嗯，谢谢，”杰克说。

Morning Breeze made a trumpet sound and swayed from side to side as she tramped through the low, scraggly brush. 晨风吹出喇叭般的声音，一边走一边左右摇晃，她踩着低矮、稀疏的灌木丛走过去。

“I wonder where she’s going,” said Jack. “我想知道她要去哪儿，”杰克说。

The elephant slowed down. She sniffed the air with the tip of her trunk. Then she stopped near a clump of bushes and made a low purring sound. 大象放慢了脚步。 她用象鼻的尖端闻了闻空气。 然后她靠近一丛灌木停了下来，发出低低的呼噜声。

A snuffling noise came from behind the bushes. Branches and leaves shook and parted. 灌木丛后传来一阵窸窸窣窣的声音。 树枝和树叶摇晃着，分开来了。

“Ohhhh, wow! ” breathed Annie. “哦——哇！”安妮惊叹道。

A baby elephant bumbled out into the open. He had a fuzzy head and bright, shining eyes. 一头小象笨拙地从灌木丛后面走了出来。 他有一头毛茸茸的脑袋，还有明亮闪亮的眼睛。

Morning Breeze lowered herself onto her knees. She stretched out her trunk and stroked her baby. The little elephant nuzzled against her. 晨风缓缓蹲下身子。 她伸出长鼻子，轻轻抚摸着她的宝宝。 小象蹭了蹭她。

“So that’ s why Morning Breeze had to escape the fort!” said Annie. “When they captured her in the wild, they took her away from her baby. She was desperate to get back to him.” “原来这就是为什么晨风必须从堡垒里逃走！”安妮说。 “当他们把她从野外抓走时，把她和宝宝分开了。 她非常着急，想回到宝宝身边。”

“Yeah,” said Jack, “and after she found him, she came back to help us.” “是啊，”杰克说，“后来她找到宝宝后，又回来帮助我们了。”

Annie climbed out of the straw carriage and carefully slid down off the elephant’s back. Jack followed her. 安妮从稻草马车里爬出来，小心地从大象背上滑下来。 杰克跟在她后面。

Annie patted the elephant’s back. “I guess you’ll be staying here now that you’ve found your baby.” 安妮拍了拍大象的背。 “我想你现在找到宝宝了，得在这里住下了。”

Jack rubbed his hand over Morning Breeze’s skin. Her huge, wrinkled body smelled of grass and heat and trees. The elephant actually looked as if she were smiling. 杰克用手抚摸着晨风的皮肤。 她那巨大的、布满皱纹的身体散发着草、热和树的气味。 大象看起来好像在笑。

“I don’t think you need to wear this stuff anymore,” said Annie. She reached up and lifted the rope of silver bells off the elephant’s neck. The bells jangled as Annie dropped them into the grass. “我想你不需要再穿这些了，”安妮说。 她伸手向上，把脖子上银铃的绳子取了下来。 安妮把铃铛丢到草地上时，铃铛发出了一阵叮当声。

“Or this,” said Jack. He took the fringed cloth off the elephant’s head. “还有这个，”杰克说。 他把大象头上的镶边布取了下来。

“And especially this,” said Annie. Together she and Jack unbuckled the strap that held the straw carriage in place on Morning Breeze’s back. They pulled the carriage off and set it on the grass. “尤其是这个，”安妮说。 安妮和杰克一起解开了固定在晨风背上稻草车的皮带。 他们把车拉下来，放在草地上。

“Doesn’t that feel better?” said Jack. “这样感觉好些了吗？”杰克说。

Morning Breeze stared at Jack and Annie. Her eyes were bright and clear. She trumpeted and rose to her full height. 晨风盯着杰克和安妮看。 她的眼睛又亮又清澈。 她吹了声响亮的口哨，然后站直了身子。

“Before you go, can we pet your baby?” asked Annie. “在你们离开之前，能摸摸你的宝宝吗？”安妮问。

The large elephant didn’t seem to mind as Annie reached out and stroked the baby elephant. “Whoa,” Annie breathed. “Pet him, Jack.” 那只大象似乎不介意，安妮伸手摸了摸小象。 “哇，”安妮轻声说。 “摸摸他，杰克。”

Jack rubbed his hand slowly over the baby’s head. The elephant’s fuzzy hair felt coarse and tickly. Jack laughed. 杰克慢慢地用手掌抚摸着小象的头。 大象毛茸茸的头发摸起来又粗糙又痒痒的。杰克笑了。

“Enjoy being home in the wild again,” Annie said to Morning Breeze. “Jack and I have to go back to our home now.” “安妮对晨风说：‘享受再次回到野外的家吧。’ “杰克和我得回我们的家了。”

“Thanks, Morning Breeze,” said Jack. “Good luck.” “谢谢您，晨风，”杰克说。 “祝你好运。”

“We love you,” said Annie. “我们爱你，”安妮说。

Morning Breeze flapped her ears and waved her trunk at them. Then she turned and lumbered off with her baby at her heels. The two of them kicked up grass and dirt as they disappeared into the brush. 晨风扇了扇耳朵，用象鼻向他们挥了挥。 然后她转过身，带着宝宝慢悠悠地走开了。 他们俩一边消失在灌木丛中，一边踢起草和泥土。

“Wow,” said Annie. “哇，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. “是啊，”杰克说。

Annie turned to Jack and laughed. “We look like we’ve been in a train wreck,” she said. 安妮转身对杰克笑了起来。 “我们看起来好像刚经历了一场火车事故，”她说。

“No kidding,” said Jack. They’d both lost their pointy shoes, and their coats were torn and dirty. “可不是嘛，”杰克说。 他们俩都失去了尖头鞋，外套也被撕破弄脏了。

“At least we have our emerald rose,” said Annie. “And Morning Breeze is back with her baby.” “至少我们还有翡翠玫瑰，”安妮说。 “而且晨风和她的宝宝也回来了。”

“Mission done,” said Jack. “任务完成，”杰克说。

“So let’s go back to the tree house,” said Annie. “那我们回树屋吧，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. He looked around. “But … where the heck are we?” “对，”杰克说。他环顾四周。 “但是……我们到底在哪里？”

“I think the big field we came through is over there,” said Annie, pointing. “I hear a lot of bug noise. Let’s look.” “我想我们刚才穿过的那片大草地就在那边，”安妮指着说。 “我听到很多虫子的声音。我们去看看。”

Jack and Annie headed for the field. “Oww! Oww!” Weeds and rocks jabbed their bare feet. Jack just missed stepping on a large anthill. He and Annie picked their way through the scrubby forest, until they came to the edge of the yellow field. 杰克和安妮朝草地走去。 “哦！ 哦！”杂草和石头扎得他们赤裸的脚生疼。 杰克差点踩到一个大蚂蚁窝。 他和安妮小心地穿过这片灌木丛，直到来到那片黄色草地的边缘。

The sounds of buzzing and chirping filled the air. The tall grasses rustled in the dry, hot wind. 空气里充满了嗡嗡声和叽叽喳喳的声音。 干燥炎热的风吹动着高高的草地。

“That’s it,” said Jack. “So to get back to the tree house, we cross the field, then go on the dirt path, then head back through the bazaar and down the road to the Red Fort. Got it?” “就是这里，”杰克说。 “所以，要回到树屋，我们先穿过田野，然后走上土路，接着再穿过集市，再沿着路走到红堡。明白了吗？”，

“Got it,” said Annie. “明白了，”安妮说。

“I just hope we don’t get eaten alive by bugs,” said Jack, “or get heatstroke or get clobbered by those angry guys in the bazaar or—” “我只是希望我们不会被虫子活活吃掉，”杰克说，“或者中暑，或者被集市里那些生气的人打一顿，或者——”，

“Enough, stop,” said Annie, grinning. “One thing at a time. Come on, don’t be chicken. The bugs are more afraid of us than we are of them. Run!” “够了，住口，”安妮笑着说。 “一次只做一件事。 来吧，别当胆小鬼。 那些虫子比我们还怕我们。跑！”

Annie and Jack started running through the dry, dusty field. Their torn silk coats billowed behind them. Jack could feel grasshoppers and all sorts of buzzy and jumpy things bashing against him, but nothing really hurt. His feet crushed the dry, tough grass, until he and Annie finally reached the dirt path. 安妮和杰克开始在干干的、灰尘满满的田野里奔跑。 他们破旧的丝绸外套在他们身后飘扬。 杰克能感觉到蚱蜢和各种嗡嗡叫、蹦蹦跳跳的小东西撞在他身上，但其实一点都不疼。 他的脚踩碎了干干的、硬硬的草，直到他和安妮终于到达了那条泥路。

“Yes! We—we made it!” Annie said, out of breath. “Oh! Look …” “是的！ 我们……我们成功了！”安妮气喘吁吁地说。 “哦！看……”

Jack was burning up. He felt dizzy. “Whew, we—” 杰克热得不行，他觉得头晕。 “哇，我们——”

“Look!” Annie said again. She grabbed Jack and pointed to a farmer hauling hay. The farmer was talking to an imperial guard in a horse-drawn carriage. It was the same guard who had escorted them to the Great Mogul’s balcony! “看！”安妮又说了一遍。 她抓住杰克，指着一个正在运干草的农夫。 农夫正在和一辆马车里的帝国卫兵说话。 这正是之前带他们去大莫卧儿阳台的那位卫兵！

The farmer pointed back at Jack and Annie. The guard turned and saw them. 农夫指着杰克和安妮。 卫兵转过身，看到了他们。

“Oh, no! He must be searching for us,” said Jack. “Hide!” “哦，不！ 他一定在找我们，”杰克说。 “躲起来！”，

But it was too late. The guard had jumped out of the carriage. He was striding toward the edge of the field. “Stop, ambassadors from Frog Creek!” he called. 但已经太晚了。 卫兵已经从马车里跳了出来。 他正大步走向田地的边缘。 “停住，来自蛙溪镇的使者！”，他喊道。

Jack held his breath as he and Annie waited for the guard. 杰克屏住呼吸，他和安妮等着守卫。

The guard looked about as he approached them. “Where is the elephant?” he asked. 守卫走近他们时四处张望。 “大象在哪里？”，他问。

“She threw us off,” said Annie. “她把我们甩开了，”安妮说。

“Yeah, and then she ran that way,” said Jack. He pointed to the right, while Annie pointed to the left. “是啊，然后她朝那边跑掉了，”杰克说。 他指向右边，而安妮指向左边。

But the guard didn’t seem to care about the elephant, nor did he seem to care about their bare feet and torn coats. “You must come with me,” he said gruffly. 但守卫似乎并不在意大象，也不在意他们光着的脚和破旧的外套。 “你们必须跟我来，”他粗声粗气地说。

“Why?” asked Jack. “为什么？”杰克问。

The guard wrapped his hand around the hilt of his sword. “Because if you do not, you will be put in jail for the rest of your lives,” he said. 卫兵握紧了剑柄。 “因为如果你们不这么做，你们将永远被关进监狱，”他说。

“Oh,” said Jack. “Good reason.” “哦，”杰克说。 “这是个好理由。”
