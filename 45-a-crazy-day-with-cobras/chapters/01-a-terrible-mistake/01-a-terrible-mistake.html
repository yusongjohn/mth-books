1. A Terrible Mistake 1. 一个可怕的错误

“The weather’s getting warmer,” said Jack. He and Annie were walking home from school under a cloudless May sky. A warm breeze blew through the leaves on the newly green trees. “天气越来越暖和了，”杰克说。 他和安妮正走在回家的路上，五月的天空没有一丝云彩。 温暖的微风吹过新发绿芽的树上的叶子。

“I love it,” said Annie. “It makes me feel like something good’s going to happen.” “我太喜欢了，”安妮说。 “这让我感觉好像好事就要发生了。”

“Something good already happened,” said Jack. “My teacher finally gave back my story today.” “好事已经发生了，”杰克说。 “我的老师今天终于把我的故事还给我了。”

“The one about our adventures in the tree house?” asked Annie. “关于我们在树屋里冒险的那本吗？”安妮问。

“Yep. She said I have a fantastic imagination,” said Jack. “对。” 她说我有非常棒的想象力，”杰克说。

“Great!” said Annie. “太棒了！”，安妮说。

“And she loved your drawings for the story, too,” said Jack. “She said you’re really talented.” “而且她也很喜欢你为故事画的画，”杰克说。 “她说你真的很擅长。”

“That’s nice!” said Annie. “Maybe she loved the sparkle pens I used.” “那太好了！”安妮说。 “也许她喜欢我用的闪亮笔。”

“The only thing she didn’t love was the type I chose,” said Jack. “She said it was hard to read because it was too fancy.” “她唯一不喜欢的是我选的字体，”杰克说。 “她说太花哨了，很难读。”

“I really like that curly type,” said Annie. “我真的很喜欢那种卷卷的字体，”安妮说。

“Me too,” said Jack. “But it’s no big deal—she still gave me an A plus.” “我也是，”杰克说。 “不过没关系——她还是给了我一个A加。”

“Wow, cool,” said Annie. “哇，真酷，”安妮说。

As they reached the edge of the Frog Creek woods, a gust of wind shook the tree branches. Jack’s baseball cap blew off. He grabbed his cap from the sidewalk. The wind blew harder. 当他们到达蛙溪镇森林边缘时，一阵风吹动了树枝。 杰克的棒球帽被吹掉了。 他从人行道上捡起了帽子。 风刮得更猛了。

“What’s happening?” said Annie. “这是怎么回事？”安妮说。

Suddenly two figures rushed out of the woods. Their dark cloaks flapped behind them as they hurried toward Jack and Annie. 突然，有两个人从森林里冲了出来。 他们的黑斗篷在他们跑向杰克和安妮时在背后飘动。

“Teddy!” said Annie. “泰迪！”安妮说。

“Kathleen!” said Jack. “凯瑟琳！”杰克说。

The red-haired teenage boy and the beautiful girl with dark wavy hair ran to Jack and Annie and hugged them. 那个红头发的少年和那个有着深棕色波浪发的漂亮女孩跑向杰克和安妮，然后拥抱了他们。

“You must come with us to the tree house!” said Kathleen. “Hurry!” “你们必须和我们一起去树屋！”，凯瑟琳说。 “快点！”，

“Why? What’s up?” said Jack. “为什么？”， “怎么了？”杰克说。

“We’ll explain when we get there!” said Teddy. He and Kathleen turned and started back into the woods. As Jack and Annie raced after them, sunlight slanted through the leafy treetops. “等我们到了那里再解释！”，泰迪说。 他和凯瑟琳转身，跑回了树林里。 杰克和安妮追着他们跑，阳光从树叶繁茂的树顶斜射进来。

Soon they all came to the tallest oak tree. “Up, up!” cried Teddy. 很快，他们都来到了最高的橡树前。 “往上爬，往上爬！”泰迪喊道。

One by one, Teddy, Kathleen, Jack, and Annie scrambled up the rope ladder and climbed into the magic tree house. When they were all inside, Teddy heaved a sigh. “My friends, we desperately need your help,” he said. 一个接一个，泰迪、凯瑟琳、杰克和安妮爬上了绳梯，爬进了神奇的树屋。 当他们都进去了，泰迪叹了口气。 “我的朋友们，我们非常需要你们的帮助，”他说。

“What’s wrong?” asked Annie. “怎么了？”安妮问。

“We made a terrible mistake!” said Kathleen. “我们犯了一个可怕的错误！”凯瑟琳说。

“No, it was me, just me,” said Teddy. “I made the mistake.” “不，是我在犯错，只是我一个人，”泰迪说。 “是我犯了错误。”

“What did you do?” said Jack. “你做了什么？”杰克说。

“I turned Penny into a stone statue,” said Teddy. “我把彭妮变成了一座石像。”泰迪说。

“Penny?” said Annie. “A statue?” “彭妮？”安妮说。 “一座石像？”

“Oh, no,” said Jack. He loved the little orphan penguin that he and Annie had found on one of their adventures. Penny had helped save Merlin’s life. “哦，不。”杰克说。 他很喜欢那只小企鹅，那是他和安妮在一次冒险中发现的孤儿企鹅。 彭妮曾帮助救过梅林的命。

“It was an accident,” said Kathleen. “这是个意外，”凯瑟琳说。

“It was stupidity!” said Teddy. “We were in Morgan’s library, and I was looking at spells in her books. Morgan forbids us to try any spells on our own, but I disobeyed when I found a simple one that turns things into stone. I thought I’d just give it a quick try—I turned an apple, a goblet of water, and a writing quill all to stone!” “这是愚蠢！”泰迪说。 “我们当时在摩根的图书馆里，我正在看她书里的咒语。 摩根禁止我们自己尝试任何咒语，但我发现了一个简单的咒语，能把东西变成石头，我就偷偷试了一下。 我想只是试一下——我把一个苹果、一杯水和一支羽毛笔都变成了石头！

“Teddy was pointing at a walking stick by the doorway, reciting the words of the spell,” said Kathleen. “And just as he finished, Penny wandered in, and the spell hit her!” “泰迪正指着门口的一根手杖，念着咒语的词句，”凯瑟琳说。 “就在他念完的时候，彭妮走进来了，咒语立刻击中了她！”，

“And now she’s a stone statue,” Teddy said miserably. “现在她变成了一座石像，”泰迪难过地说。

“That’s terrible,” said Annie. “这太糟糕了，”安妮说。

“Well, can’t you just ask Merlin or Morgan to use their magic to bring Penny back to life?” asked Jack. “那你不可以让梅林或摩根用魔法把彭妮复活吗？”杰克问。

“No, no, they must never learn anything about this! If they do, I …” Teddy shook his head and looked away. “不，不行，他们绝不能知道这件事！ 如果他们知道了，我……”泰迪摇了摇头，把头转过去。

“Merlin and Morgan are both in Avalon for the Festival of May,” said Kathleen. “If they discover what Teddy has done, we fear Merlin will banish him from the kingdom.” “梅林和摩根都在阿瓦隆参加五月节，”凯瑟琳说。 “如果他们发现泰迪做了什么，我们担心梅林会把他赶出王国。”

“Really?” said Annie. “真的吗？”安妮说。

“Yes, Merlin will be enraged,” said Kathleen. “Penny is the very heart of our kingdom. We all love her very much.” “是的，梅林会非常生气，”凯瑟琳说。 “彭妮是我们王国的心脏。 我们都非常爱她。”

“Me too,” said Jack. He thought about the penguin’s fuzzy head, the funny way she peeped, the way she loved and trusted everyone. “我也是，”杰克说。 他想起了企鹅毛茸茸的脑袋，她好奇地偷看的样子，以及她对每个人的喜爱和信任。

“The good news is that we have found a rhyme that tells us how to reverse the stone spell,” said Kathleen. She took a note from her cloak. “好消息是，我们找到了一首诗，它告诉我们如何解除石头的咒语，”凯瑟琳说。 她从斗篷里拿出一张纸条。

“The bad news is that it is written in an ancient language,” said Teddy. “We have only translated eight lines so far.” “坏消息是，它是用一种古老的语言写的，”泰迪说。 “到目前为止，我们只翻译了八行。”

Kathleen read from the note: 凯瑟琳从纸条上读道：

Ye say that ye wish

your spell be reversed?

Four things ye must find.

Here is the first:



In the shape of a rose

is an emerald stone

that uncovers the heart

of one who’s alone. 你说你想让咒语解除？你必须找到四样东西。第一样在这里：一朵玫瑰形状里有一块翡翠，能揭示孤独之人的内心。

“An emerald stone shaped like a rose?” said Jack. He took the note from Kathleen and looked at it. “That’s what you have to find first?” “一颗形状像玫瑰的翡翠石头？”杰克说道。 他从凯瑟琳手里接过纸条，仔细看了起来。 “这就是你要先找到的东西？”

“Yes, only we must spend our time trying to translate the rest of the spell before Merlin and Morgan return,” said Kathleen, “so we need you and Annie to look for the emerald rose.” “是的，不过我们得先花时间把梅林和摩根回来之前剩下的咒语翻译完，”凯瑟琳说，“所以你需要你和安妮去寻找那颗翡翠玫瑰。”

“Got it,” said Jack. “明白了，”杰克说。

“Where do we look?” asked Annie. “我们去哪儿找呢？”安妮问道。

“We have done some research,” said Teddy, “and we think you should go back almost four hundred years and visit one of the Great Moguls of India.” “我们做了一些研究，”泰迪说，“我们认为你们应该回到将近四百年前，去拜访印度的一位大莫卧儿。”

“Great Moguls?” said Annie. “大莫卧儿？”安妮说道。

“They are the emperors who ruled India’s Mogul Empire,” said Teddy. “One of them had the largest collection of precious gems in all the world.” “他们是统治印度莫卧儿帝国的皇帝，”泰迪说。 “其中一位拥有世界上最大的珍贵宝石收藏。”

“And his stonecutters cut many of his jewels into the shape of leaves and flowers!” said Kathleen. “而且他的石匠把他的许多宝石雕刻成了叶子和花朵的形状！”，凯瑟琳说。

“Perfect!” said Annie. “太棒了！”，安妮说。

“Our research says he often gave gifts of his precious gems to visiting ambassadors,” said Teddy. “You will have to pretend to be ambassadors.” “我们的研究说，他经常把珍贵的宝石作为礼物送给来访的使节，”泰迪说。 “你们必须假装自己是使节。”

“And I am afraid, Annie, that you will have to pretend to be a boy again,” said Kathleen. “In Mogul India, girls were not allowed to show their faces in public.” “而且，安妮，恐怕你又得假装自己是个男孩了，”凯瑟琳说。 “在莫卧儿印度，女孩是不允许在公共场合露脸的。”

“No problem, I’m getting used to it,” Annie said. “没问题，我已经习惯了，”安妮说。

“Wait, even if Annie’s a boy, they’re probably not going to believe that we’re ambassadors,” said Jack. “等等，即使安妮是个男孩，他们可能也不会相信我们是使节，”杰克说。

Teddy and Kathleen gave each other blank looks. “I do not know why not,” said Teddy, “though I admit we do not really know very much about ambassadors.” 泰迪和凯瑟琳互相看着，一脸困惑。 “我不知道为什么不行，”泰迪说，“不过我承认，我们其实对大使了解得不多。”

“They’re people who visit other countries to represent their own country,” said Jack. “大使是去其他国家代表自己国家的人，”杰克说。

“Splendid!” said Teddy. “So that’s what you’ll do—you’ll represent Frog Creek four hundred years ago.” “太棒了！”泰迪说。 “所以你们要做的就是——在四百年前代表蛙溪镇。”

“Except ambassadors are usually grown-ups,” said Jack. “不过大使通常是成年人，”杰克说。

“Oh. Well. I suppose you must just do the best you can,” said Kathleen. “哦。那好吧。” “我想你们只能尽力而为了，”凯瑟琳说。

“Perhaps this will help you,” said Teddy. “If you have knowledge, you will seem older.” He reached into his cloak and pulled out a book. “也许这能帮到你们，”泰迪说。 “如果你有知识，就会显得更成熟。”他伸手到斗篷里，拿出了一本书。

“And we thought perhaps some magic might help, too,” said Kathleen, “if you find yourselves in danger.” She reached into her cloak again and pulled out a tiny blue bottle. “A potion to make you very small.” “而且我们认为也许一些魔法也能帮上忙，”凯瑟琳说，“如果你们遇到危险的话。”她又伸手到她的斗篷里，拿出了一只小小的蓝色瓶子。 “一种能让你们变得非常小的药水。”

“Wow, great!” said Annie. “哇，太棒了！”安妮说。

“Uh … very small?” said Jack. He remembered how their friend Augusta had been made tiny by the fairies of Ireland. He remembered that he’d been afraid it might happen to them, too. All kinds of ordinary things could seem huge and scary, he thought. “How long would we stay small?” he asked. “呃……非常小？”杰克说。 他记得他们的朋友奥古斯塔曾被爱尔兰的仙女变得非常小。 他记得自己曾担心这件事也可能发生在他们身上。 他想，各种各样的普通事情看起来都可能非常巨大和可怕。 “我们能变小多久？”他问。

“It depends on how many sips you take,” said Kathleen, giving the bottle to Jack. “One sip will make you small for ten minutes, two for twenty, three for thirty, and so on.” “这取决于你喝了多少口，”凯瑟琳说着，把瓶子递给了杰克。 “喝一口，你会变小十分钟，两口二十分钟，三口三十分钟，依此类推。”

“Thanks,” he said. He put the bottle and the rhyme into his backpack. “谢谢，”他说。 他把瓶子和那首诗放进背包里。

“Go now,” said Teddy. “Find the emerald rose while we try to translate the rest of the spell.” “现在走吧，”泰迪说。 “在我们试着翻译剩下的咒语时，去找那朵翡翠玫瑰。”

“We’ll do our best,” said Annie. “我们会尽力的，”安妮说。

“Thank you,” said Kathleen. “When you need strength, just think of Penny.” “谢谢，”凯瑟琳说。 “当你需要力量时，就想想彭妮。”

“We will,” said Jack. He took a deep breath and pointed at the cover of their research book. “I wish we could go there!” “我们会的，”杰克说。 他深吸了一口气，指着他们研究书的封面。 “我们真希望可以去那里！”

Annie waved at Teddy and Kathleen. “See you later!” she said. 安妮向泰迪和凯瑟琳挥手。 “再见啦！”她说。

The wind started to blow. 风开始吹起来了。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。
