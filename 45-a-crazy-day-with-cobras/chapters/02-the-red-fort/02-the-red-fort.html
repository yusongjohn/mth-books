2. The Red Fort 2. 红色堡垒

Kathleen and Teddy were gone. The air was hot. Flies buzzed around Jack’s head. 泰迪和凯瑟琳不见了。空气很热。 苍蝇在杰克的头上嗡嗡飞。

“These are ambassador clothes?” said Annie. “这些是大使的衣服吗？”安妮说。

Jack and Annie were dressed alike. They both wore wide-brimmed felt hats, stockings, buckled shoes, short jackets, and puffy short pants. Jack’s backpack had turned into a leather bag. 杰克和安妮穿得一模一样。 他们俩都戴着宽边毡帽，穿着长袜、系带鞋、短夹克和蓬松的短裤。 杰克的背包变成了一只皮包。

“I guess,” said Jack. “This must be how we would’ve dressed almost four hundred years ago in Frog Creek.” “我想是吧，”杰克说。 “这一定是我们在四百年前几乎会穿的衣服，就在蛙溪镇。”

“If we’d both been boys,” said Annie. “So, where are we?” “如果当时我们两个都是男孩的话，”安妮说。 “那我们现在在哪儿？”

Jack and Annie looked out the window. The tree house had landed in a row of tall dark trees. The trees stood next to a red fort with a moat, a drawbridge, and massive red battlements. 杰克和安妮看向窗外。 树屋降落在一排高大的黑树中间。 这些树旁边有一座红色的城堡，城堡有护城河、吊桥和巨大的红色城墙。

“Cool fort,” said Jack. “这城堡真酷，”杰克说。

“Yeah, and look—elephants,” said Annie. “I love elephants!” “是啊，你看——有大象，”安妮说。 “我超喜欢大象！”，

Leading away from the fort were two streets. One was filled with oxen pulling carts and people riding horses and elephants. Veiled women sat in carriages on the elephants’ backs. 从城堡延伸出去的有两条街道。 一条街道上有很多牛拉着车，人们骑着马和大象。 一些戴面纱的妇女坐在大象背上乘坐马车。

“Yeah, and there’s a bazaar like the one we saw in Baghdad,” said Jack. He pointed to the other street, which was lined with tents and stalls. “是啊，这里还有一个集市，就像我们在巴格达看到的那样，”杰克说。 他指着另一条街道，街道两旁都是帐篷和摊位。

“So where do you think we find an emerald stone in the shape of a rose?” said Annie. “你觉得我们该去哪里找一颗形状像玫瑰的翡翠石呢？”安妮说。

Jack picked up their research book and opened to the first page. He read aloud: 杰克拿起他们的研究书，翻到第一页。他大声读起来：

In the 1600s, India was a vast land of crowded cities and countless villages. A great deal of India, though, was still covered by wilderness. Wild creatures such as cheetahs, elephants, and Bengal tigers lived in its forests. India’s wilderness was also home for many snakes, including the king cobra, one of the deadliest snakes on earth. 在1600年代，印度是一个拥有拥挤城市和无数村庄的广阔土地。不过，印度的大部分地区仍然是荒野。猎豹、大象和孟加拉虎等野生动物生活在它的森林里。印度的荒野也是许多蛇的家园，其中包括世界上最致命的蛇之一——眼镜王蛇。

“Yikes,” said Annie. She and Jack looked at pictures of a Bengal tiger and a king cobra. The growling tiger had a huge head and enormous teeth. The cobra had speckled yellow bands around its long body. Its open jaws revealed two deadly fangs. “天哪，”安妮说。 她和杰克看着孟加拉虎和眼镜王蛇的图片。 咆哮的老虎有一个巨大的头和巨大的牙齿。 眼镜王蛇的长身体上围着斑驳的黄色条纹。 它张开的嘴巴里露出两颗致命的毒牙。

“Don’t worry,” said Jack with a shiver. “We definitely didn’t land in the wilderness.” He turned the pages until he found a drawing of the red fort. “Yes! Here’s exactly where we landed.” He read: “别担心，”杰克打了个哆嗦说。 “我们肯定没有降落到荒野里。”他翻动书页，直到找到一座红色城堡的图画。 “对！ 我们降落的地点就在这里。”他读道：

For several centuries, mighty rulers, known as Great Moguls, ruled over much of India. The wealthiest of the Great Moguls was named Shah Jahãn. He lived inside the Red Fort, where he was protected night and day by imperial guards. 好几个世纪以来，强大的统治者被称为大莫卧儿，他们统治着印度的大部分地区。最富有的大莫卧儿名叫沙贾汗。他住在红堡里，日夜都有帝国卫兵保护他。

“So the Great Mogul lives right here!” said Annie. “How lucky is that?” “所以大莫卧儿就住在这里！”安妮说道。 “这运气也太好了吧？”

She and Jack looked at the fort again. “I guess those guys must be imperial guards,” Jack said. He pointed to men in white coats and leggings guarding the drawbridge. Some carried spears; others had bows and arrows. 她和杰克再次看向那座堡垒。 “我想那些人一定是帝国卫兵，”杰克说。 他指着穿着白外套和长裤、守卫吊桥的男人。 有些人拿着长矛；有些人拿着弓和箭。

“Right. But who do you think those guys are?” Annie asked, pointing. Two carts pulled by pairs of white oxen had stopped at the entrance to the bridge. Eight men were climbing out of the carts. They wore outfits like Jack’s and Annie’s—puffy pants, short jackets, and wide hats. Two of the guards greeted them with deep bows. “对的。 但你觉得那些人是谁呢？”安妮指着问。 两辆由两头白牛拉着的马车停在桥入口处。 八个人正从马车里爬出来。 他们穿着像杰克和安妮一样的衣服——蓬松的裤子、短夹克和宽大的帽子。 两名卫兵向他们深深鞠躬表示欢迎。

“They’re dressed like us,” said Jack. “So I guess they must be ambassadors, too.” “他们穿得和我们一样，”杰克说。 “那我想他们也一定是大使了。”

“If they’re visiting the Great Mogul, we should join them!” said Annie. “如果他们要去拜访大莫卧儿，我们也应该和他们一起去！”安妮说。

“Hold on,” said Jack. He opened his bag. As he put their book inside, he noticed that everything that had been in his backpack was still there: his story from school, the note about the emerald rose, and the blue bottle with the potion to make them small. “Okay, we’ve got everything,” he said. “抓紧了，”杰克说。 他打开自己的背包。 当他把他们的书放进去时，他注意到背包里原来的东西还在：学校里的故事，关于翡翠玫瑰的便条，还有装着让他们变小的药水的蓝色瓶子。 “好了，我们什么都有了，”他说。

“Come on, before we miss our chance!” Annie called, starting down the rope ladder. “快点，不然我们就要错过机会了！”安妮喊道，开始顺着绳梯往下爬。

“Wait!” said Jack. He put his bag over his shoulder and hurried after Annie. By the time he stepped off the ladder, Annie was already heading toward the drawbridge of the fort. “等一下！”杰克说。 他把背包背到肩上，赶紧追上安妮。 等他从梯子上下来时，安妮已经朝堡垒的吊桥走了过去。

“Annie, hold on! We have to talk about something important!” said Jack. He didn’t want her to say anything crazy to the guards or the ambassadors. “安妮，等等！ 我们得谈谈一件重要的事情！”杰克说。 他不希望她对卫兵或大使们说一些疯狂的话。

“What?” said Annie, waiting for him. “什么？”安妮说，等着他解释。

“If they’re real ambassadors, we shouldn’t get too close to them,” Jack said. “They’ll figure out we’re fakes.” “如果他们是真正的大使，我们不应该离他们太近，”杰克说。 “他们就会发现我们是假的。”

“Oh. Good point,” said Annie. 说得对，”安妮说。

“So let’s wait until they start across the bridge,” said Jack. “Then we’ll run to the gate and tell the guards that we’re supposed to be with them. And don’t forget, if anyone asks, you’re my brother. Oh, you should stick your braids under your hat.” “那我们等他们开始过桥的时候再行动，”杰克说。 “然后我们跑到大门那里，告诉守卫我们本来应该和他们在一起。” 还有啊，如果有人问起，你可是我的哥哥。 哦，你应该把你的发辫塞到帽子下面。

“Right,” said Annie. She tucked her pigtails under her hat. “How’s that?” “对，”安妮说。 她把她的两个辫子塞到帽子下面。 “这样呢？”

“Fine, I guess,” said Jack. He was still worried about the fact that he and Annie looked too young. “We have to try to act like ambassadors. So stand up straight and speak in a low voice.” “我想还不错，”杰克说。 他仍然担心他和安妮看起来太年轻了。 “我们必须试着表现得像大使。 所以，站直了，用低沉的声音说话。”

“Okay, don’t worry,” said Annie. “好的，别担心，”安妮说。

“And we should hold up our chins,” said Jack. “Try to look taller.” “我们应该抬起头，”杰克说。 “试着看起来更高一些。”

“Okay, okay. Oh, look, they’re leaving,” said Annie. “Let’s go!” “好的，好的。 哦，看，他们要离开了，”安妮说。 “我们走吧！”

The ambassadors had climbed back into their oxcarts. As the carts started across the moat, Jack and Annie walked quickly toward the bridge. 大使们已经爬回了他们的牛车里。 当牛车开始穿过护城河时，杰克和安妮快速朝桥走去。

The first guard held up his spear. “Who are you?” he asked. He had a long purple scar on his cheek and silver rings in his ears. 第一个守卫举起了他的长矛。 “你是谁？”他问。 他的脸颊上有一道长长的紫色伤疤，耳朵里戴着银色的耳环。

“We are ambassadors,” Jack said in a deep voice. “We are with the gentlemen who just crossed the bridge.” “我们是大使，”杰克用低沉的声音说。 “我们和刚刚过桥的那些先生在一起。”

The guard gave Jack and Annie a sharp look. “You are with the ambassadors from Europe?” he said. 卫兵严厉地看了杰克和安妮一眼。 “你们是来自欧洲的大使吗？”他问。

“Yes”—Jack stood straighter—“but we’re from Frog Creek.” “是的”—杰克挺直了身子—“但我们来自蛙溪镇。”

“I imagine you are thinking that we look very young,” Annie said in a low voice. “Well, it is true, we are young. But we are very learned.” “我想你们可能觉得我们看起来很年轻，”安妮低声说。 “嗯，确实，我们很年轻。 但我们非常博学。”

“I see,” said the guard. “我明白了，”卫兵说。

“And imaginative and creative,” Annie added. “而且富有想象力和创造力，”安妮补充道。

Oh, brother, thought Jack. 哦，天哪，杰克心想。

“I see,” said the guard again. He looked at Jack’s bag. “And is that where you carry your treasure? Your gifts for the Great Mogul?” “我明白了，”卫兵又说了一遍。 他看了看杰克的包。 “那你是把宝藏带在这里吗？” 你给大莫卧儿的礼物？

“Um … well …,” said Jack. “嗯……好吧……，”杰克说。

“What gifts do ambassadors from Europe usually give Great Moguls?” Annie asked. “来自欧洲的使节通常会送给大莫卧儿什么礼物？”安妮问。

“The gifts sent by the kings and queens of Europe are always rare and beautiful,” said the guard. “欧洲国王和女王送来的礼物总是稀有又美丽，”卫兵说。

“Such as …?” said Annie. “比如说……？”安妮说。

“Silver swords, golden goblets, treasure chests filled with jewels and coins,” said the guard, “or perhaps … the fastest horses from Arabia.” “银剑、金杯、装满珠宝和金币的宝箱，”卫兵说，“或者……来自阿拉伯的最快的马。”

Forget it, thought Jack. 杰克想，算了。

But Annie smiled. “I see,” she said. “Well, I am pleased to tell you that we have brought a gift far greater than any of those things.” 但安妮笑了。 “我明白了，”她说。 “那么，我很高兴告诉你，我们带来了一份比所有这些都更珍贵的礼物。”
