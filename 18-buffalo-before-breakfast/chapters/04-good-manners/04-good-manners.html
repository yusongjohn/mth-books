4. Good Manners 4. 好礼貌

Black Hawk led Jack and Annie toward the tepees. Everyone kept watching them. 黑鹰领着杰克和安妮走向圆锥帐篷。 人人都在看着他们。

Jack couldn’t tell what anyone was thinking. No one looked angry. But no one looked happy, either. 杰克看不出任何人在想什么。 没有人看起来生气。 但也没有人看起来高兴。

Jack wondered how to appear brave. 杰克想知道怎样才能显得勇敢。

He glanced at Annie. She walked tall and straight. Her chin was up. Her face was calm. 他瞥了一眼安妮。 她走得身形挺拔。 她的面容平静。

Jack straightened his shoulders. He lifted his chin, and he felt braver. 杰克挺直了肩膀。 他抬起下巴，觉得更勇敢了。

Black Hawk stopped and slid off his pony. The pony headed for the grazing pasture. 黑鹰停下，滑下小马。 小马朝牧场走去。

Then Black Hawk led them to a tepee. It was covered with buffalo designs. 然后黑鹰带他们来到一座帐篷。 上面覆盖着野牛图案。

“Grandmother is inside,” Black Hawk said to Jack and Annie. “奶奶在里面，”黑鹰对杰克和安妮说。

Inside, the tepee looked like a small round room. A fire burned in the center. Smoke rose through a hole at the top. 帐篷里面像一间小小的圆屋。 中央燃着火。 烟从顶部的洞口升起。

An old woman sat on animal skins. She was sewing beads onto a moccasin. 一位老妇人坐在兽皮上。 她正在把珠子缝到一只软皮鞋上。

She looked up at Jack and Annie. 她抬头看着杰克和安妮。

“Grandmother,” said Black Hawk. “This is Jack and Annie from Frog Creek, Pennsylvania.” “奶奶，”黑鹰说。 “这是来自宾夕法尼亚州蛙溪的杰克和安妮。”

Jack and Annie both held up two fingers for “friend.” 杰克和安妮都举起两根手指表示“朋友”。

Grandmother raised two fingers also. 祖母也举起了两根手指。

Then Jack took off his coonskin cap. He gave it to Grandmother. 然后杰克摘下浣熊皮帽。 他把它递给奶奶。

She put the cap on her head, then laughed. Jack and Annie laughed, too. 她把帽子戴到头上，然后笑了。 杰克和安妮也笑了。

Grandmother’s laughter and kind face reminded Jack of his own grandmother. 祖母的笑声和慈祥的面容让杰克想起了自己的祖母。

“You wish to learn our ways,” she said. “你们想学习我们的生活方式，”她说。

Jack and Annie nodded. Jack could tell she was wise. 杰克和安妮点点头。 杰克能看出她很有智慧。

Grandmother stood and left the tepee. They followed her. 奶奶站起来，走出帐篷。 他们跟着她。

Outside, everyone was busy again. They all seemed to know that Jack and Annie weren’t enemies. 外面，大家又忙碌起来。 他们似乎都知道杰克和安妮不是敌人。

Jack looked around the camp. 杰克环顾营地。

Men and boys carved bows. Women and girls pounded meat and sewed clothes. One girl was adding claws to a buckskin shirt. 男人和男孩在雕刻弓。 女人和女孩在捣肉、缝衣服。 一个女孩正把爪子缝到鹿皮衬衫上。

“The bear claws will give her the strength of the bear,” said Grandmother. “She will sew on hawk feathers, elk teeth, and porcupine quills, too. All will give her the power of the animals.” “熊爪会给她熊的力量，”祖母说。 “她还会缝上鹰的羽毛、麋鹿的牙齿，还有豪猪的刺。” “所有这些都会给她动物的力量。”

Jack pulled out his notebook and wrote: 杰克抽出笔记本写道：

图中文字：sew bear claws to shirt 图中文字：把熊爪缝在衬衫上

“I have strong animal power when I go on a buffalo hunt,” Black Hawk said proudly. “我去猎野牛时有很强的动物力量，”黑鹰骄傲地说。

“What do you mean?” asked Jack. “什么意思？”杰克问。

“I will show you,” said Black Hawk. “Wait.” “我给你看，”黑鹰说。 “等等。”

Black Hawk went back inside the tepee. 黑鹰走回帐篷里。

Annie turned to Grandmother. 安妮转向奶奶。

“Why does he hunt the buffalo?” she asked. “他为什么要猎杀野牛？”她问。

“The buffalo gives our people many gifts,” said the old woman. “Food from his body. Tepees from his skin, tools from his bones.” “野牛给了我们的族人许多礼物，”老妇人说。 “食物来自它的身体。 帐篷来自它的皮，工具来自它的骨头。”

Jack started making a list. 杰克开始列清单。

“Cups from his horns,” Grandmother went on. “Ropes from his hair. Even winter sleds from his ribs.” “用它的角做杯子，”祖母接着说。 “绳子来自它的毛发。 “甚至用它的肋骨做冬天的雪橇。”

Jack finished his list. 杰克写完了他的清单。

图中文字：buffalo

skin-tepee

bones-tools

horns-cups

hair-ropes

ribs-sleds 图中文字：水牛皮——帐篷、骨头——工具、角——杯子、头发——绳子、肋骨——雪橇

“That reminds me of the seal hunter in the Arctic,” said Annie. “He used all the gifts from the seal’s body. He didn’t waste a thing.” “这让我想起北极的海豹猎人，”安妮说。 “他用了海豹身体带来的所有礼物。 “他一点也没有浪费。”

Just then, Teddy began growling and barking. 就在这时，泰迪开始低吼和吠叫。

Jack and Annie turned around. They both gasped. 杰克和安妮转过身。两人都倒吸一口气。

Coming out of Grandmother’s tepee was a huge wolf! 从奶奶帐篷里出来的是一头巨大的狼！
