3. Black Hawk 3. 黑鹰

Jack stared at a circle of tepees ahead. Busy people in buckskins moved about the circle. Horses and ponies grazed nearby. 杰克盯着前方一圈圆锥形帐篷。 忙碌的人们穿着鹿皮衣服，在帐篷圈里走来走去。 马和马驹在附近吃草。

Jack took out their research book and found a picture of the tepees. 杰克拿出研究书，找到一幅圆锥帐篷的图画。

He read: 他读道：

In the early 1800s, many different Native American tribes lived on the Great Plains. The Lakota were the largest tribe. They lived mostly in the areas we now call North Dakota, South Dakota, and Minnesota. 1800年代初，许多不同的美洲原住民部落住在大平原上。拉科塔族是最大的部落，他们主要住在我们现在称为北达科他州、南达科他州和明尼苏达州的地区。

Jack pulled out his notebook and wrote: 杰克抽出笔记本写道：

图中文字：early 1800s-Lakota were largest tribe of Great Plains 图中文字：19世纪初——拉科塔是大平原上最大的部落

Behind Jack and Annie, a horse neighed. 在杰克和安妮身后，一匹马嘶鸣起来。

They turned. A horse and rider were heading toward the tepee camp. 他们转过身。 一匹马和骑手正朝帐篷营地走来。

The sun was very bright behind the rider. Jack could only see the outline of a body with a bow and a quiver of arrows on his back. 骑手背后的阳光非常刺眼。 杰克只能看见一个身影的轮廓，背上背着弓和一筒箭。

Jack quickly flipped through the book. He found a picture of a man on horseback carrying a bow and arrows. Below the picture it said LAKOTA WARRIOR. 杰克飞快翻着书。 他找到一幅骑马男子拿着弓箭的图画。 图画下面写着：拉科塔勇士。

Jack read: 杰克读道：

Everything changed for the Native Americans of the Great Plains after white settlers arrived in the mid-1800s. Fighting broke out between Lakota warriors and white soldiers. By the end of the 1800s, the Lakota were defeated. They lost both their land and their old way of life. 19世纪中期，白人定居者来到大平原地区，印第安原住民的生活发生了巨大变化。拉科塔战士和白人士兵之间爆发了战斗。到19世纪末，拉科塔人被打败了，他们失去了土地，也失去了原来的生活方式。

Jack looked back at the rider. The warrior was coming closer. 杰克回头看那个骑手。 那位武士越来越近了。

“Get down,” he whispered. “趴下，”他低声说。

“Why?” said Annie. “为什么？”安妮说。

“This might be a time when the Indians are fighting with the settlers,” said Jack. “这也许是印第安人和殖民者打仗的时候，”杰克说。

The grass rustled as the warrior passed by them. His horse neighed again. 勇士从他们身边经过时，草丛沙沙作响。 他的马又嘶鸣起来。

Arf! Arf!

“Shh!” whispered Jack. “嘘！”杰克低声说。

But it was too late. The warrior had heard Teddy’s barking. He galloped toward them, grabbing his bow. 可已经太晚了。 勇士听见了泰迪的叫声。 他抓起弓朝他们疾驰而来。

“Wait!” shouted Jack. He jumped up from the grass. “We come in peace!” “等等！”杰克喊道。 他从草里跳起来。 “我们是为和平而来！”

The rider halted. 骑马的人停住了。

Now Jack saw that he was only a boy on a pony. He couldn’t have been more than ten or eleven. 现在杰克看清了，那只是一个骑着小马的男孩。 他最多不过十岁或十一岁。

“Hey, you’re just a kid,” Annie said, smiling. “嘿，你只是个小孩，”安妮笑着说。

The boy didn’t smile back. But he did lower his bow while he stared at Annie. 男孩没有笑回去。 可他一边盯着安妮，一边放下了弓。

“What’s your name?” she asked. “你叫什么名字？”她问。

“Black Hawk,” he said. “黑鹰，”他说。

“Cool name,” said Annie. “We’re Jack and Annie. We’re just visiting. We live in Frog Creek, Pennsylvania.” “酷名字，”安妮说。 “我们是杰克和安妮。 我们只是来做客的。 我们住在宾夕法尼亚的蛙溪。”

Black Hawk nodded. Then he turned his pony around and started toward the Lakota camp. 黑鹰点了点头。 然后他掉转小马，朝拉科塔营地走去。

“Hey, can we come with you?” called Annie. “嘿，我们能跟你一起去吗？”安妮喊道。

Black Hawk looked back. 黑鹰回头看。

“Yes,” he said. “Meet my people.” “可以，”他说。 “来见见我的族人。”

“You mean your parents?” asked Annie. “你是说你的父母？”安妮问。

“No, they died long ago,” said Black Hawk. “I live with my grandmother.” “没有，他们很久以前就去世了，”黑鹰说。 “我和奶奶住在一起。”

“Oh, I’d like to meet your grandmother,” said Annie. “I’m going to see my grandmother today, too.” “哦，我想见见你奶奶，”安妮说。 “我今天也要去看我的奶奶。”

Black Hawk nudged his pony forward again. Annie followed with Teddy. 黑鹰又轻轻推了一下小马，让它往前走。 安妮带着泰迪跟上去。

Jack didn’t move. 杰克没动。

What if the Lakota are at war with the white settlers? he worried. What if they think we’re enemies? 要是拉科塔人正在和白人殖民者打仗怎么办？他担心。 要是他们把我们当成敌人怎么办？

“Annie!” Jack called softly. “We don’t know if it’s safe or not!” “安妮！”杰克轻声喊道。 “我们还不知道安不安全！”

But Annie just waved for him to come on. 可安妮只是挥手让他跟上。

Jack sighed. He opened the research book and quickly flipped through the pages. He wanted information about how to act with the Lakota. 杰克叹了口气。 他打开研究书，飞快翻着书页。 他想找关于如何与拉科塔人相处的资料。

On one page, he read: 在一页上，他读到：

Good manners to the Lakota mean speaking as few words as possible and sharing gifts when visiting. 对拉科塔人来说，礼貌意味着说话要少，拜访时要送礼物。

On another page, he read: 在另一页上，他读到：

The Lakota admire those who do not show fear. 拉科塔人钦佩那些不表现恐惧的人。

Jack’s favorite piece of information was: 杰克最喜欢的一条信息是：

Holding up two fingers means “friend.” 比出两个手指头表示“朋友”。

Jack put the book away. He ran to catch up with Annie. 杰克把书收起来。 他跑去追上安妮。

Annie was telling Black Hawk all about their grandmother. The boy listened silently. 安妮正把她们奶奶的事全讲给黑鹰听。 男孩静静地听着。

“Annie,” Jack whispered. “I just read that it’s good manners to be quiet. And we should give gifts and not show fear. Also, holding up two fingers means ‘friend.’ ” “安妮，”杰克低声说。 “我刚读到，保持安静才是有礼貌的表现。” 而且我们应该送礼物，不要露出害怕。 还有，举起两根手指表示‘朋友。’”

Annie nodded. 安妮点点头。

“Got that?” said Jack. “明白了吗？”杰克说。

“Sure,” she said. “No talking, no fear, no problem.” “好，”她说。 “不说话，不害怕，没问题。”

Jack looked up. He caught his breath. 杰克抬起头。 他倒吸一口气。

Ahead of them, the people at the campsite had stopped what they were doing. All eyes were turned to Jack and Annie. 前面营地里的人都停下了手里的事。 所有目光都转向杰克和安妮。

Jack quickly held up two fingers. Annie did the same. 杰克赶紧举起两根手指。 安妮也一样。
