9. Lakota School 9. 拉科塔学校

Jack felt Teddy licking his cheek. 杰克感觉到泰迪在舔他的脸颊。

He opened his eyes. Gray light came through the smoke hole. 他睁开眼睛。 灰色的光从烟孔照进来。

The fire was out. The tepee was empty. 火灭了。 帐篷是空的。

Jack jumped up. He grabbed his bag and hurried outside with Teddy. 杰克跳起来。 他抓起袋子，带着泰迪急忙走到外面。

In the cool light before dawn, everyone was taking down their tepees. They were loading them onto wooden platforms strapped to two poles. The poles were pulled by horses. 在黎明前凉爽的光线里，人人都在拆帐篷。 他们正把帐篷装到绑在两根杆子上的木台上。 杆子由马拉着。

Grandmother and Black Hawk piled tools and clothes onto their platform. 奶奶和黑鹰把工具和衣服堆到他们的平台上。

Annie stuffed buffalo meat into a rawhide bag. 安妮把野牛肉塞进一个生皮袋子。

“What’s happening?” Jack asked. “怎么了？”杰克问。

“It is time to follow the buffalo,” said Grandmother. “We will camp somewhere else for a few weeks.” “是时候跟随野牛了，”奶奶说。 “我们会到别的地方扎营几个星期。”

Jack pulled out his notebook. He still had many questions. But he tried to choose just a few. 杰克抽出笔记本。 他还有很多问题。 可他试着只选几个来问。

“Can you camp anywhere?” he asked. “Even when you don’t own the land?” “你们可以在任何地方扎营吗？”他问。 “即使你们并不拥有那片土地？”

Black Hawk laughed. 黑鹰笑了。

“People cannot own land,” he said. “The land belongs to the Great Spirit.” “人不能拥有土地，”他说。 “土地属于伟大圣灵。”

Jack wrote in his notebook: 杰克在笔记本上写道：

图中文字：land owned by Great Spirit, not people 图中文字：属于伟大精灵的土地，不是人们的

“What about school?” said Jack. “Don’t you have to go to school?” “那学校呢？”杰克说。 “你们不用上学吗？”

“What is school?” Black Hawk said. “什么是学校？”黑鹰说。

“It’s a place where kids go to learn things,” Jack explained. “是孩子们去学习东西的地方，”杰克解释道。

Black Hawk laughed again. 黑鹰又笑了。

“There is not only one place to learn,” he said. “In camp we learn to make clothes, tools, and tepees. On the plains we learn to ride and hunt. We look at the sky and learn courage from the eagle.” “学习的地方不止一个，”他说。 “在营地里，我们学习做衣服、工具和帐篷。” 在平原上我们学习骑马和打猎。 我们看天空，向鹰学习勇气。”

Jack wrote: 杰克写道：

图中文字：Lakota school is everywhere 图中文字：拉科塔学校到处都是

Grandmother turned to Jack and Annie. 奶奶转向杰克和安妮。

“Will you walk with us toward the sunset?” she asked. “你们愿意和我们一起朝日落的方向走吗？”她问。

Jack shook his head. 杰克摇了摇头。

“We have to go the other way,” he said, “toward the sunrise.” “我们得朝另一个方向走，”他说，“朝日出的方向。”

“Thank you for the eagle’s feather,” said Annie. “谢谢你们的鹰羽，”安妮说。

“Let your thoughts rise as high as that feather,” said Grandmother. “It is good medicine.” “让你的思想像那根羽毛一样高高升起，”祖母说。 “这是好医药。”

“What does that mean?” Jack asked. “Good medicine?” “那是什么意思？”杰克问。 “好医药？”

“Good medicine connects you to the world of the spirits,” she said. “好药把你和精灵的世界连在一起，”她说。

Jack nodded. But he still didn’t really understand. 杰克点点头。 可他还是不太明白。

“Good-bye, Buffalo Girl and Rides-Like-Wind,” said Grandmother. “We wish you a safe journey.” “再见，野牛女孩和骑风者，”奶奶说。 “祝你们一路平安。”

Jack and Annie waved. Then they started walking back the way they’d come. 杰克和安妮挥手。 然后他们开始沿来路往回走。

Teddy ran ahead of them. 泰迪跑在他们前面。

At the top of the rise, they looked back. 在高坡顶上，他们回头看。

Grandmother, Black Hawk, and the rest of the tribe were watching. 祖母、黑鹰，还有部落其余的人都在看着。

Jack and Annie both held up two fingers for “friend.” Then they took off down the slope. 杰克和安妮都举起两根手指表示“朋友”。然后他们冲下斜坡。

They ran across the prairie … through the tall, whispering grass … all the way back to the tree house. 他们跑过草原……穿过高高的、低语的草……一路跑回树屋。

Annie put Teddy in the leather bag. She and Jack climbed up the rope ladder. 安妮把泰迪放进皮袋子。 她和杰克爬上绳梯。

They looked out the window one last time. The ocean of grass was golden in the early sunlight. 他们最后一次朝窗外望去。 草之海在清晨阳光里一片金黄。

By now, the Lakota are walking west, Jack thought. 此时拉科塔人该在往西走了，杰克想。

“Soon everything will change,” he said sadly. “The buffalo will vanish. The old way of life for the Lakota will vanish, too.” “很快一切都会改变，”他伤感地说。 “野牛将会消失。” 拉科塔人古老的生活方式也会消失。”

“But the Great Spirit won’t ever vanish,” said Annie. “It will always take care of Black Hawk’s people.” “可大灵永远不会消失，”安妮说。 “它会永远照顾黑鹰的族人。”

Jack smiled. Annie’s words made him feel better. 杰克笑了。 安妮的话让他感觉好多了。

Arf, arf! Teddy barked, as if to say Let’s go! 泰迪叫了叫，好像在说：走吧！

“Okay, okay,” said Jack. “好啦，好啦，”杰克说。

He picked up the Pennsylvania book and pointed at a picture of Frog Creek. 他拿起宾夕法尼亚的书，指着一幅蛙溪的图画。

“I wish we could go home to our people,” he said. “我希望我们能回到我们的族人那里，”他说。

The wind started to blow. 风开始刮起来。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster. 转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 绝对静止。
