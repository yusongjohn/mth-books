5. The Ghost-Queen 5. 幽灵女王

“A ghost,” Annie whispered. “幽灵，”安妮小声说。

But Jack could only stare in horror. 可杰克只能惊恐地盯着。

The ghost began to speak. She spoke in a hollow, echoing voice. 幽灵开始说话。 她的声音空空的，还带着回音。

“I am Hutepi,” she said. “Queen of the Nile. Is it true that you have come to help me?” “我是哈特皮，”她说。 “尼罗河的女王。 你们真的是来帮我的吗？”

“Yes,” said Annie. “是的，”安妮说。

Jack still couldn’t speak. 杰克还是说不出话。

“For a thousand years,” said the ghost-queen, “I have waited for help.” “一千年来，”幽灵女王说，“我一直在等人来帮我。”

Jack’s heart was pounding so hard he thought he might faint. 杰克的心跳得太厉害，他觉得自己都快晕过去了。

“Someone must find my Book of the Dead,” she said. “I need it to go on to the Next Life.” “必须有人找到我的《亡灵书》，”她说。 “我需要它，才能去到来世。”

“Why do you need the Book of the Dead?” asked Annie. She didn’t sound scared at all. “你为什么需要《亡灵书》？”安妮问。 她听起来一点也不害怕。

“It will tell me the magic spells I need to get through the Underworld,” said the ghost-queen. “它会告诉我穿过阴间需要的咒语，”幽灵女王说。

“The Underworld?” said Annie. “阴间？”安妮说。

“Before I journey on to the Next Life, I must pass through the horrors of the Underworld.” “在我去到来世之前，必须先穿过阴间里那些可怕的东西。”

“What kinds of horrors?” Annie asked. “什么样的可怕东西？”安妮问。

“Poisonous snakes,” said the ghost-queen. “Lakes of fire. Monsters. Demons.” “毒蛇，”幽灵女王说。 “火湖。怪物。 还有恶魔。”

“Oh.” Annie stepped closer to Jack. “哦。”安妮往杰克身边靠了靠。

“My brother hid the Book of the Dead. So tomb robbers would not steal it,” said the ghost-queen. “Then he carved this secret message on the wall, telling me how to find it.” “我弟弟把《亡灵书》藏了起来。 这样盗墓贼就偷不走，”幽灵女王说。 “然后他把这个秘密留言刻在墙上，告诉我怎么找到它。”

She pointed to the wall. 她指了指墙壁。

Jack was still in shock. He couldn’t move. 杰克还在发愣。他动不了。

“Where?” asked Annie. “Here?” She squinted at the wall. “What do these tiny pictures mean?” “在哪儿？”安妮问。 “这儿？”她眯着眼看墙壁。 “这些小小的图画是什么意思？”

The ghost-queen smiled sadly. “Alas, my brother forgot my strange problem. I cannot see clearly that which is close to my eyes. I have not been able to read his message for a thousand years.” 幽灵女王难过地笑了笑。 “唉，我弟弟忘了我有个奇怪的问题。 离眼睛太近的东西，我看不清楚。 这一千年来，我一直读不了他留下的话。”

“Oh, that’s not a strange problem,” said Annie. “Jack can’t see anything either. That’s why he wears glasses.” “哦，那可不算奇怪的问题，”安妮说。 “杰克也什么都看不清。 所以他才戴眼镜。”

The ghost-queen stared in wonder at Jack. 幽灵女王惊奇地盯着杰克。

“Jack, lend her your glasses,” said Annie. “杰克，把眼镜借给她，”安妮说。

Jack took his glasses off his nose. He held them out to the ghost-queen. 杰克把眼镜从鼻子上摘下来。 他把眼镜递给幽灵女王。

She backed away from him. “I fear I cannot wear your glasses, Jack,” she said. “I am made of air.” 她从他面前往后退。 “恐怕我戴不了你的眼镜，杰克，”她说。 “我是空气做成的。”

“Oh. I forgot,” said Annie. “哦。 我忘了，”安妮说。

“But perhaps you will describe the hieroglyphs on these walls,” said the ghost-queen. “不过也许你们可以描述这些墙上的象形文字，”幽灵女王说。

“Hi-row-who?” said Annie. “嗨——罗——谁？”安妮说。

“Hieroglyphs!” said Jack, finally finding his voice. “It’s the ancient Egyptian way of writing. It’s like writing with pictures.” “象形文字！”杰克终于找回了自己的声音。 “这是古埃及人写字的方法。 就像用图画来写字。”

The ghost-queen smiled at him. “Thank you, Jack,” she said. 幽灵女王朝他微笑。 “谢谢你，杰克，”她说。

Jack smiled back at her. He put his glasses on. Then he stepped toward the wall and took a good long look. 杰克也朝她笑了笑。 他把眼镜戴上。 然后他走近墙壁，好好看了好久。

“Oh, man,” he whispered. “天哪，”他小声说。
