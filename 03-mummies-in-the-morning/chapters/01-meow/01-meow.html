1. Meow! 1. 喵！

“It’s still here,” said Jack. “它还在这儿，”杰克说。

“It looks empty,” said Annie. “看上去是空的，”安妮说。

Jack and his seven-year-old sister gazed up at a very tall oak tree. At the top of the tree was a tree house. 杰克和他七岁的妹妹抬头望着一棵很高很高的橡树。 树顶上有一座树屋。

Late-morning sunlight lit the woods. It was almost time for lunch. 快到中午的阳光照着树林。 快到吃午饭的时间了。

“Shhh!” said Jack. “What was that noise?” “嘘！”杰克说。 “那是什么声音？”

“What noise?” “什么声音？”

“I heard a noise,” Jack said. He looked around. “It sounded like someone coughing.” “我听见有声音，”杰克说。他四处看了看。 “听起来像有人在咳嗽。”

“I didn’t hear anything,” said Annie. “Come on. Let’s go up.” “我什么都没听见，”安妮说。 “走吧。我们上去。”

She grabbed onto the rope ladder and started climbing. 她抓住绳梯，开始往上爬。

Jack tiptoed over to a clump of bushes. He pushed aside a small branch. 杰克踮着脚走到一片灌木丛边。 他把一根小树枝拨开。

“Hello?” he said. “Anybody there?” “有人吗？”他说。 “有人在那儿吗？”

There was no answer. 没有人回答。

“Come on!” Annie called down. “The tree house looks the same as it did yesterday.” “快点！”安妮朝下喊。 “树屋看起来跟昨天一模一样。”

Jack still felt that someone was nearby. Could it be the person who’d put all the books in the tree house? 杰克还是觉得附近有人。 会不会就是那个把书都放进树屋的人？

“Ja-ack!” “杰——克！”

Jack gazed over the top of the bushes. 杰克从灌木丛顶上望过去。

Was the mysterious person watching him now? The person whose name began with M? 那个神秘人现在正在看着他吗？ 那个名字以M开头的人？

Maybe M wanted the gold medallion back. The one Jack had found on their dinosaur adventure. Maybe M wanted the leather bookmark back. The one from the castle book. 也许M想把金色徽章要回去。 就是杰克在恐龙冒险里捡到的那个。 也许M想把皮书签要回去。 就是城堡那本书里的那个。

There was an M on the medallion. And an M on the bookmark. But what did M stand for? 徽章上有一个M。 书签上也有一个M。 可是M代表什么呢？

“Tomorrow I’ll bring everything back,” Jack said loudly. “明天我会把东西都还回来，”杰克大声说。

A breeze swept through the woods. The leaves rattled. 一阵微风吹过树林。 叶子沙沙作响。

“Come on!” called Annie. “快点！”安妮喊道。

Jack went back to the big oak tree. He grabbed onto the rope ladder and climbed up. 杰克走回那棵大橡树。 他抓住绳梯，爬了上去。

At the top he crawled through a hole in the wooden floor. He tossed down his backpack and pushed his glasses into place. 到了顶上，他从木地板的洞口爬进去。 他把背包往下一扔，把眼镜推正。

“Hmmm. Which book is it going to be today?” said Annie. “嗯—— 今天会是哪一本书呢？”安妮说。

She was looking at the books scattered around the tree house. 她看着树屋里散落一地的书。

Annie picked up the book about castles. 安妮拿起那本讲城堡的书。

“Hey, this isn’t wet anymore,” she said. “嘿，这本已经不湿了，”她说。

“Let me see.” “让我看看。”

Jack took the book from her. He was amazed. It looked fine. Yesterday it had gotten soaked in a castle moat. 杰克把书从她手里接过来。他很吃惊。 书看起来好好的。 昨天它在城堡的护城河里湿透了。

The castle book had taken Jack and Annie back to the time of knights. 那本城堡书曾把杰克和安妮带回骑士的时代。

Jack silently thanked the mysterious knight who had rescued them. 杰克在心里感谢那位救了他们的神秘骑士。

“Watch out!” warned Annie. “小心！”安妮提醒他。

She waved a dinosaur book in Jack’s face. 她把一本恐龙书在杰克脸前晃来晃去。

“Put that away,” said Jack. “把那本收起来，”杰克说。

The day before yesterday the dinosaur book had taken them to the time of dinosaurs. 前天，那本恐龙书把他们带到了恐龙的时代。

Jack silently thanked the Pteranodon who had saved him from a Tyrannosaurus rex. 杰克在心里感谢那只无齿翼龙，是它把他从霸王龙嘴里救了出来。

Annie put the dinosaur book back with the other books. Then she gasped. 安妮把恐龙书放回其他书里。接着她倒吸了一口气。

“Wow,” she whispered. “Look at this.” “哇，”她小声说。 “看这个。”

She held up a book about ancient Egypt. 她举起一本讲古埃及的书。

Jack caught his breath. He took the book from her. A green silk bookmark stuck out of it. 杰克屏住了呼吸。 他从她手里把书接过来。 一根绿色的丝绸书签从书里露出来。

Jack turned to the page with the bookmark. There was a picture of a pyramid. 杰克翻到书签夹着的那一页。 上面有一幅金字塔的画。

Going toward the pyramid was a long parade. Four huge cows with horns were pulling a sled. On the sled was a long gold box. Many Egyptians were walking behind the sled. At the end of the parade was a sleek black cat. 一支很长的队伍正朝着金字塔走去。 四头长着角的大牛拉着一辆雪橇。 雪橇上放着一个长长的金盒子。 很多埃及人走在雪橇后面。 队伍的最后是一只油亮的黑猫。

“Let’s go there,” whispered Annie. “Now.” “我们去那儿吧，”安妮小声说。 “现在就去。”

“Wait,” said Jack. He wanted to study the book a bit more. “等等，”杰克说。 他还想再把这本书看一会儿。

“Pyramids, Jack,” said Annie. “You love pyramids.” “金字塔啊，杰克，”安妮说。 “你最爱金字塔了。”

It was true. Pyramids were high on his list of favorite things. After knights. But before dinosaurs. Way before dinosaurs. 这倒是真的。 金字塔在他最喜欢的东西里排得很靠前。就在骑士后面。 但排在恐龙前面。 比恐龙靠前多了。

He didn’t have to worry about being eaten by a pyramid. 他不用担心会被金字塔吃掉。

“Okay,” he said. “But hold the Pennsylvania book. In case we want to come right back here.” “好吧，”他说。 “不过拿好宾夕法尼亚那本书。 万一我们想马上回来呢。”

Annie found the book with the picture of their hometown in it. Frog Creek, Pennsylvania. 安妮找到那本里面有家乡图画的书。 宾夕法尼亚的蛙溪镇。

Then Jack pointed to the pyramid picture in the Egypt book. He cleared his throat and said, “I wish we could go to this place.” 接着杰克指着埃及书里那幅金字塔的画。 他清了清嗓子，说：“我希望我们能去这个地方。”

“Meow!” “喵！”

“What was that?” Jack looked out the tree house window. “那是什么？”杰克朝树屋窗外看去。

A black cat was perched on a branch. Right outside the window. The cat was staring at Jack and Annie. 一只黑猫蹲在一根树枝上。 就在窗户外面。 那只猫正盯着杰克和安妮。

It was the strangest cat Jack had ever seen. He was very sleek and dark. With bright yellow eyes. And a wide gold collar. 这是杰克见过的最奇怪的猫。 它的毛又滑又黑。 眼睛是亮亮的黄色。 还戴着一圈宽宽的金项圈。

“It’s the cat in the Egypt book,” whispered Annie. “就是埃及书里的那只猫，”安妮小声说。

Just then the wind started to blow. The leaves began to shake. 就在这时，风开始刮起来。 叶子开始晃动。

“Here we go!” cried Annie. “要出发啦！”安妮喊道。

The wind whistled louder. The leaves shook harder. 风呼呼地刮得更响了。 叶子晃得更厉害了。

Jack closed his eyes as the tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转，杰克闭上了眼睛。

It spun faster and faster! And faster! 它转得越来越快！更快！

Suddenly everything was still. 忽然，一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止。

Not a sound. Not a whisper. 一点声音也没有。连一声耳语都没有。

Jack opened his eyes. 杰克睁开眼睛。

Hot bright sunlight nearly blinded him. 又热又亮的阳光差点把他晃瞎。

“Me-ow!”
