Jack raced down the steep stadium steps, trying to get ahead of the crowd. But by the time he reached the middle section, he had to slow down. The aisle was packed with people buying sodas and flags and soccer caps from vendors. 杰克沿着陡峭的体育场台阶飞快地跑下去，努力在人群中冲在前面。 但等他跑到中间部分时，不得不放慢了脚步。 过道上挤满了人，他们正在从摊位上买苏打水、旗帜和足球帽。

Jack couldn’t get past the human traffic jam. He kept looking at his watch as he waited for the steps to clear. After almost five minutes, he squeezed through a gap in the crowd. “ A-26, A-26!” he repeated as he struggled to get down to the sections near the playing field. 杰克无法穿过这堵人墙。 他一边等台阶上的人散开，一边不停地看手表。 等了将近五分钟，他终于从人群中挤出一条缝钻了过去。 “A-26，A-26！”他一边挣扎着往靠近操场的区域爬，一边重复着说。

Finally Jack reached the last rows of section A. “Annie? Roberto?” he whispered. As he looked down the steep rows of seats, all the wide-brimmed hats made it impossible to see. 最后，杰克到达了A区的最后一排。 “安妮？” 罗伯托？”他小声说。 当他低头看向一排排陡峭的座位时，所有宽边帽让视线完全被遮住了。

“Annie! Roberto!” Jack yelled. “Annie! Roberto!” “安妮！” “罗伯托！”杰克喊道。 “安妮！罗伯托！”

There was no sign of them anywhere. Jack hadn’t expected all the hats—or all the people milling around, standing, stretching, talking to neighbors, coming and going. 到处都看不到他们的踪影。 杰克没想到会有这么多帽子——也没想到会有这么多人在四处走动、站着、伸懒腰、和邻居聊天、来来往往。

Jack looked at his watch again. There were five minutes left until the end of halftime. Stadium officials were telling everyone to return to their seats. “Take your seats! Halftime’s almost over! Take your seats!” 杰克又看了看他的手表。 上半场结束还有五分钟。 体育场的工作人员让大家回到座位上。 “请坐好！ 中场休息快结束了！请坐好！”

Jack didn’t know what to do. Suddenly he was caught in another traffic snarl as people started climbing back up to the higher tiers. “Keep moving!” an usher shouted at everyone. “Keep moving!” 杰克不知道该怎么办。 突然，他又被堵在了人群中，因为人们开始往更高的看台爬。 “继续往前走！”一个工作人员朝大家喊道。 “继续往前走！”

A huge roar came from the crowd. The players were taking their positions again! 观众席上传来一阵巨大的欢呼声。 球员们又站好了位置！

Kickoff! The second half of the game began! The Brazilian and Italian soccer stars were running in all directions. Even peering through the crowd, Jack loved watching them up close! 开球！ 比赛的下半场开始了！ 巴西和意大利的足球明星们向四面八方跑了起来。 即使隔着人群，杰克也喜欢近距离地看着他们！

Suddenly an usher was by his side. “Where’s your ticket?” the man asked. 突然，一个工作人员站在了他身边。 “你的票呢？”那个人问。

Now’s my chance! thought Jack. He showed his ticket. “Please help me. I’m looking for A-26,” he said. “My sister—” 现在是我的机会了！杰克心想。 他出示了他的票。 “请帮帮我。” “我在找A-26，”他说。 “我姐姐——”

“A-26? That’s not your seat! Your seat’s at the top!” the stadium official said. “Please go there now!” “A-26？ 这不是你的座位！ 你的座位在最上面！”，体育场工作人员说。 “请现在马上过去！”，

“But—” started Jack. “但是——”杰克开始说。

“There’s no time!” barked the frazzled man. “The game has started again! Go back to your seat!” “没时间了！”那个慌乱的男人吼道。 “比赛又开始了！ 回到你的座位去！”

Jack did as the official said. Annie won’t know why I didn’t show up, he thought as he climbed the steep steps. She’ll be really worried. He kept peering back down at the first rows of section A, looking for Annie and Roberto. 杰克按照官员说的做了。 他爬着陡峭的台阶，心想：安妮不知道我为什么没出现。 她会非常担心的。 他一直往回看A区的第一排，寻找安妮和罗伯托。

Finally Jack arrived back at the top of the stadium. He felt frustrated and anxious as he sat down in his seat. Not only would he never get a good look at the World Cup players now—but how was he going to find Annie again? Jack wished he had a cell phone, but he knew cell phones hadn’t been invented yet. How did people ever find each other in 1970? he wondered. 最后，杰克又回到了体育场的顶部。 他坐下来时感到很沮丧和焦虑。 现在他不仅没法好好看看世界杯的球员了——而且他怎么才能再找到安妮呢？ 杰克希望他有手机，但他知道那时候手机还没被发明出来。 他想知道，1970年的人们是怎么找到彼此的？

As the fans of the Brazilian team kept whistling and cheering, Jack caught more snatches of the radio commentary: “Brazil attacks! … Gerson scores long, low shot! … Brazil keeps attacking, passing, dribbling.…” 当巴西队的球迷们不停地吹口哨和欢呼时，杰克又听到了一些广播解说：‘巴西队进攻了！’ …格森踢出一个又长又低的射门！ …巴西队继续进攻、传球、盘带。…

Jack barely listened to the game anymore. He was too worried about what to do when it was over. If the stadium was hectic at halftime, it’ll be insane when the championship game ends, he thought. He had Merlin’s magic mist in his backpack. Should he use it now? he wondered. What great talent would help him find Annie? What great talent would help them accomplish their mission? 杰克几乎不再听比赛了。 他太担心比赛结束后该怎么办了。 如果中场休息时体育场已经很热闹，那么决赛结束时一定会疯狂到极点，他心想。 他的背包里装着梅林的魔法薄雾。 他应该现在就用它吗？他心想。 什么神奇的本领能帮助他找到安妮？ 什么神奇的本领能帮助他们完成任务？

“Pelé follows with a hard shot!” the radio commentator’s voice crackled. “ Here comes Alberto!” “贝利跟上一脚大力射门！”收音机里的解说员声音传来。 “看，是阿尔贝托！”

As the game went on, Jack couldn’t stop worrying about finding Annie. She must be worried about finding me, too, he thought. Knowing her, she might have tried to get help from the ushers or stadium guards. Maybe she talked to the police. Jack looked up and down the aisles for any officials who might be searching for him. 比赛继续进行，杰克一直担心着怎么才能找到安妮。 他想，安妮一定也在担心怎么才能找到他。 他了解安妮，她可能已经去向 usher 或者体育场的保安求助了。 也许她还跟警察谈过了。 杰克抬头又低头看了看过道，看有没有官员在找他。

“Four minutes left!” the announcer finally yelled. “还剩四分钟！”广播员终于喊道。

“Tostao gets the ball!” the teenager with binoculars yelled. “Someone passes to Pelé! … Now Jairzinho has it! Brazil drives the ball and scores!” “托萨奥拿到球了！”那个拿着望远镜的青少年喊道。 “有人把球传给贝利了！ …现在贾伊津霍拿到了球！ 巴西带球突破，进球啦！“

As soon as it’s over, I’ll find seat A- 26, Jack thought. I’ll keep looking as long as I have to! Jack just hoped Annie would wait at her seat. He hoped she had discovered the secret of greatness during the game. He hoped Roberto would stick around and take them back to the U.S. Embassy. 比赛一结束，我就能找到A-26号座位，杰克心想。 我一定会一直找下去，直到找到为止！ 杰克只是希望安妮能坐在她的座位上等着。 他希望她在比赛中已经发现了伟大秘密。 他希望罗伯托能留下来，带他们回美国大使馆。

Jack’s thoughts were interrupted by a huge roar from the crowd. 杰克的思绪被人群发出的巨大吼声打断了。

“Final score four to one! … The Beautiful Team has won! … Champions of the world! … Best World Cup of all time!” “最终比分四比一！ … 美丽队赢了！ … 世界冠军！ … 有史以来最好的世界杯！”

All the spectators around Jack were screaming and jumping up and down and hugging each other. 杰克周围的观众们都在尖叫、跳上跳下，还互相拥抱。

As Jack squeezed past the people in his row, the sportscaster shouted from the radio, “Spectators are pushing past police and news reporters! Mobs are rushing onto the field. They’re tearing off the players’ shirts, their shoes! The fans have gone wild!” 当杰克挤过他那一排的人时，收音机里的体育播音员喊道：“观众们正在挤过警察和新闻记者！ 怪物们正冲向场地。 它们把球员们的衬衫、鞋子都撕了下来！ 粉丝们疯狂了！

Jack hoped that Annie wouldn’t get impatient in all the chaos and leave her seat to look for him. They’d never find each other if they were both moving around! 杰克希望安妮在所有混乱中不要不耐烦，离开她的座位来找他。 如果他们都在到处走动，就永远找不到彼此了！

“Pelé’s team members have hoisted him onto their shoulders! Celebrations will go on for days! The players will be honored at a giant banquet tonight in Mexico City!” “贝利的队友们已经把他扛在肩膀上啦！ 庆祝活动会持续好几天！ 今晚在墨西哥城将举行盛大的宴会，向这些运动员们表示敬意！

“Excuse me, excuse me,” Jack mumbled as he tried to move past people in his row. He made his way through the crowd of celebrating fans and started down the steep stairs. He was bumped and pushed by people spilling from the stands. He moved as quickly as he could so he wouldn’t get trampled. “对不起，对不起，”杰克小声地说，他试图从自己那一排的人群中挤过去。 他穿过庆祝的粉丝人群，开始走下陡峭的台阶。 他被从看台上挤下来的人撞了又推。 他尽可能快地移动，以免被踩踏。

Finally Jack reached the lower section of seats. As he looked around, he was accidentally knocked over by rowdy teenagers dancing in the aisle, screaming and waving flags. He scrambled back to his feet and jumped onto a stadium bench. 终于，杰克到达了座位的下层部分。 当他环顾四周时，一群在过道里跳舞的吵闹青少年不小心把他撞倒了，他们一边尖叫，一边挥舞着旗帜。 他赶紧爬起来，跳到了体育场的长椅上。

Finally Jack saw them: Annie and Roberto. They were only a few rows in front of him. They, too, were standing on a bench. But they were facing the field, pumping their arms in the air and shouting and cheering along with the crowd. 终于，杰克看到了他们：安妮和罗伯托。 他们只坐在他前面几排。 他们也站在长椅上。 但他们正面对着田野，一边挥舞着双臂，一边和人群一起大声喊叫、欢呼。

Jack was flooded with relief—but then he felt angry. What the heck? he thought. Annie wasn’t searching for him at all! She didn’t seem to be a bit worried. She looked like she was having a great time! 杰克感到一阵如释重负——但接着他又感到很生气。到底怎么回事？ 他心想。 安妮根本就没在找他！ 她看起来一点都不担心。 她看起来好像玩得非常开心！

“Annie!” Jack yelled. He hurried down to her. “Annie!” “安妮！”杰克大喊。 他赶紧跑过去。 “安妮！”

But when you didn’t come back, we figured your seat must have been okay! I hope it was!” 当安妮和罗伯托转身看到杰克时，他们开心地挥手。 “哦，杰克，见到你我太高兴了！”，安妮喊道。 “我们等了你！ 但当你没有回来时，我们认为你的座位一定没问题！我希望真的没问题！

Jack took a deep breath. And then, for the second time that day, he chose to do the right thing: “Yeah, sure. My seat was good,” he said with a sigh. “It was fine, totally fine.” 杰克深吸了一口气。 然后，那天第二次，他选择了做正确的事：“嗯，当然可以。” “我的座位很好，”他叹了口气说。 “很好，完全没问题。”
