2. Lucky Ducks 2. 幸运的鸭子

T he air in Mexico City was hot, humid, and smoggy. Jack heard screeching car tires, honking horns, and roaring truck motors. He looked at his clothes. He was wearing his own shorts and T-shirt. “Our clothes didn’t change,” he said. 墨西哥城的空气又热又潮湿，还有雾霾。 杰克听到刺耳的汽车轮胎声、按喇叭的汽车声和轰隆的卡车引擎声。 他看了看自己的衣服。 他穿着自己的短裤和T恤。 “我们的衣服没变，”他说。

“That’s because kids wore shorts and T-shirts in the 1970s. Remember the photos of Mom and Dad?” said Annie. “那是因为上世纪70年代，孩子们都穿短裤和T恤。” “还记得妈妈和爸爸的照片吗？”安妮说。

“Right,” said Jack. He and Annie looked out the window. The tree house had landed in a row of tall, leafy trees lining a busy street. Beyond the branches was a jagged skyline with towers and tall buildings. Below the branches, traffic moved noisily up an avenue. “对，”杰克说。 他和安妮看向窗外。 树屋降落在一条繁忙街道旁的一排高大、枝叶茂密的树上。 树枝后面是一片崎岖的天际线，上面有高高的塔楼和大楼。 树枝下方，车辆在一条大道上嘈杂地行驶着。

“It looks like we landed in the middle of the city,” said Jack. He opened their World Cup program to the first page and read: “看起来我们降落在城市中间了，”杰克说。 他打开他们的世界杯手册，翻到第一页并读道：

Mexico City is one of the largest cities in the world. Its citizens are overjoyed that their city has been chosen to host the 1970 World Cup games. Held every four years, the games are played for several weeks. This year, sixteen countries will compete in the matches to determine the final winner. 墨西哥城是世界上最大的城市之一。 市民们非常高兴，因为他们的城市被选中举办1970年世界杯比赛。 每四年举办一次，比赛会持续几周时间。 今年，有十六个国家将参加比赛，争夺最终的胜利者。

“And we’ve got tickets to see the final winner!” Jack said, grinning. “I wonder which two teams will be playing!” He thumbed through the booklet and saw page after page of black-and-white photos of the teams competing in the World Cup games. “Mexico, Belgium, Sweden, Israel, Italy, England …” “我们有票去看最终的胜者！”杰克笑着说。 “我想知道哪两支队伍会比赛！”他翻着手册，看到一页又一页的黑白照片，都是参加世界杯比赛的队伍。 “墨西哥、比利时、瑞典、以色列、意大利、英格兰……”

“Romania … West Germany … Brazil …,” Annie said, looking over his shoulder. “罗马尼亚……西德……巴西……，”安妮说，一边看着他的肩膀。

“There he is!” said Jack. “Arantes Nascimento—Pelé!” “在那里！”杰克说。 “阿兰特斯·纳西门托——贝利！”

“Well, we know Brazil made it to the final game, or we wouldn’t be here,” said Annie. “But I wonder who they’re playing.” “嗯，我们知道巴西队进入了决赛，否则我们也不会在这里，”安妮说。 “但我不知道他们正在和谁比赛。”

“We’ll soon find out.” Jack closed the program. As he put it into his backpack, he saw his notebook and pencil, the bottle of magic mist—and some coins and bills inside. “Look, Mexican money—pesos. We have five- and ten-peso coins, and bills worth fifty pesos.” “我们很快就会知道。”杰克合上了节目单。 当他把它放进背包时，他看到了他的笔记本和铅笔，还有那瓶魔法雾——以及里面的一些硬币和纸币。 “看，墨西哥的货币——比索。 我们有五比索和十比索的硬币，还有面值五十比索的纸币。”

“Sounds like a lot,” said Annie. “We can buy Mexican food when we get hungry. Tacos. Enchiladas. Burritos. I love Mexican food!” “听起来有很多，”安妮说。 “我们饿的时候可以买墨西哥食物。玉米饼。 恩奇拉达，布里托斯。 我超爱墨西哥食物！

“Who doesn’t?” Jack said. “Who doesn’t love everything we’re about to do?” He pulled on his backpack and led the way down the rope ladder. “谁会不喜欢呢？”杰克说。 “谁不爱上我们马上要做的所有事情呢？”他背上背包，顺着绳梯往下爬。

When Jack and Annie reached the ground, they saw a large white building with an American flag. Engraved on the front wall of the building were the words EMBASSY OF THE UNITED STATES. A uniformed guard stood under a green awning at the entrance. 杰克和安妮到达地面时，他们看到一座大型的白色建筑，上面飘着美国国旗。 建筑正面墙上刻着EMBASSY OF THE UNITED STATES（美国大使馆）的字样。 入口处的绿色雨棚下站着一名穿制服的警卫。

“Perfect. We landed in front of the U.S. Embassy,” said Jack. “完美。 我们降落在美国大使馆前面，”杰克说。 “Embassy,” said Jack.

“Why is that perfect?” asked Annie. “为什么这太完美了？”安妮问。

“A country’s embassy is supposed to help its citizens when they visit another country,” said Jack. “一个国家的使馆应该在它的公民去另一个国家时帮助他们，”杰克说。

“Oh, that is perfect,” said Annie. “Come on.” “哦，这太完美了，”安妮说。 “来吧。”

Jack and Annie headed toward the entrance. Before they got to the door, the guard stepped out from under the awning. 杰克和安妮朝入口走去。 他们还没走到门口，守卫就从雨棚下走了出来。

“Sorry, kids,” he said. “The embassy is closed on Sundays.” The man looked very formal in his blue uniform jacket and white hat and gloves, but he sounded friendly. He spoke with a Southern accent. “对不起，孩子们，”他说。 “大使馆星期天休息。”穿蓝色制服夹克、戴白色帽子和手套的男人看起来非常正式，但说话听起来很友好。 他说话带着南方口音。

“Oh, we were just looking for information,” said Jack. “We’re Americans.” “哦，我们只是在找信息，”杰克说。 “我们是美国人。”

“Groovy. Where y’all from?” the guard asked. “酷毙了。 ‘你们是从哪儿来的？’守卫问。

“We’re Jack and Annie from Frog Creek, Pennsylvania,” said Annie. “我们是来自宾夕法尼亚州蛙溪镇的杰克和安妮，”安妮说道。

“I’m Benny from Valdosta, Georgia,” said the guard. “Pleased to meet you.” “我是来自佐治亚州瓦尔多斯塔的贝尼，”守卫说道。 “很高兴见到你。”

“Hi, Benny,” said Jack. “We’re going to the World Cup game that’s being played today. And—” “你好，本尼，”杰克说。 “我们要去看今天举行的世界杯比赛。而且——”

“Lucky ducks!” Benny broke in. “It’s going to be the game of a lifetime!” “幸运的鸭子！”本尼插话道。 “这将是一生中最重要的比赛！”

“Game of a lifetime?” Jack said. “一生中最重要的比赛？”杰克说道。

“Today’s the final match—best two teams in the world. Italy’s playing Brazil!” said Benny. “今天是决赛——世界上最强的两支队伍。 意大利对巴西！”本尼说道。

“Brazil? That’s Pelé’s team, right?” said Jack. “巴西？ 那是贝利的队伍，对吧？”杰克说道。

“Sure is,” said Benny. “Pelé the Great.” “当然对。”本尼说道。 “伟大的贝利。”

Jack grinned at Annie. “This is so cool!” he said. 杰克对安妮笑了笑。 “这太酷了！”他说。

“No kidding!” said Annie. “可不是嘛！”安妮说。

“Y’all seem to really love soccer,” said Benny. “你们似乎真的很喜欢踢足球，”本尼说。

“We do. We love to watch it and play it,” said Annie. “是的。 我们既喜欢看也喜欢踢。”安妮说。

“Well, y’all are unusual,” said Benny. “Most American kids hardly know anything about soccer.” “嗯，你们确实很特别，”本尼说。 “大多数美国孩子对足球几乎一无所知。”

“Um … yeah … right,” said Jack. His dad had told them that kids rarely played soccer when he was growing up. “呃……对……没错，”杰克说。 他爸爸告诉过他们，他小时候几乎没人踢足球。

“Don’t worry,” said Annie. “It’ll become more popular with time. In fact, by 2014—” “别担心，”安妮说。 “随着时间的推移，它会变得越来越受欢迎。” 事实上，到2014年—”

“Excuse me,” Jack interrupted, “but do you know when the match starts? And where it is?” “打扰一下，”杰克打断了他，“你知道比赛什么时候开始吗？在哪儿？”

Benny looked at his watch. “Well, it’s almost ten a.m. now,” he said. “So you’ve got two hours till kickoff at Aztec Stadium. That’s about ten miles south of here.” 本尼看了看他的手表。 “嗯，现在快到上午十点了，”他说。 “所以你们还有两个小时就要在阿兹特克体育场开赛了。” 那是在这里往南大约十英里的地方。”

“Ten miles?” said Jack. “十英里？”杰克说。

“Don’t worry,” said Benny. “Public transportation can get you there pretty fast. Just walk a few blocks to the Metro Insurgentes station. That’s where you catch the metro.” “别担心，”本尼说。 “公共交通可以很快带你到那里。 只要走几个街区到Metro Insurgentes车站。 你在那里坐地铁。

“Metro?” said Jack. “地铁？”杰克说。

“It’s like what we’d call a subway train in the States,” said Benny. “Y’all need to catch the Red Line in the direction of Pantitlán. And then—” “就像我们在美国叫的地铁列车，”本尼说。 “你们需要坐红线路往Pantitlán方向，然后——”，

“Wait, please,” said Jack. He pulled out his notebook and pencil. “Could you spell that?” “等等，请等一下，”杰克说。 他拿出他的笔记本和铅笔。 “你能拼写一下吗？”，

“Here, I’ll write it down,” said the guard. “来，我写下来，”卫兵说。

Jack gave his notebook and pencil to Benny. The friendly guard mumbled the directions to himself as he wrote them down: “Walk to Metro Insurgentes … then Red Line to Pino Suaréz … then Blue Line to Taxqueña, last stop … then a tram to Aztec Stadium.” 杰克把他的笔记本和铅笔给了本尼。 友好的警卫一边写一边小声对自己说：“走到Metro Insurgentes……然后坐红线到Pino Suaréz……再坐蓝线到Taxqueña，最后一站……然后坐电车到Aztec Stadium。”

“It sounds complicated,” said Jack. “听起来好复杂，”杰克说。

“Just ask somebody if you get lost,” said Benny. “Folks here are super nice—they’ll be glad to help.” The guard handed the notebook and pencil back to Jack. “如果你迷路了，就问问别人，”本尼说。 “这里的人超级友善——他们会很乐意帮忙的。”警卫把笔记本和铅笔还给了杰克。

“Thanks,” said Jack. He tore out the sheet with Benny’s directions and put it in the pocket of his shorts. “谢谢，”杰克说。 他撕下写有本尼路线的那一页，放进短裤的口袋里。

“I hope y’all have seats for the game,” said Benny. “From what I hear, it’s completely sold out.” “希望你们有座位看比赛，”本尼说。 “据我所知，票已经全部卖光了。”

“Don’t worry—we have tickets,” said Annie. She showed them to Benny. “别担心——我们有票，”安妮说。 她把票展示给本尼看。

“Far out!” said Benny. “Y’all have two of the best seats in the house!” “太远了！”本尼说道。 “你们俩在屋里有两张最好的座位！”

“Really?” said Jack. He couldn’t believe it. “真的吗？”杰克说。 他简直不敢相信。

“How in the world did you get these?” said Benny. “天哪，你们是怎么弄到这些票的？”本尼问。

Annie shrugged. “We know somebody who knows somebody,” she said. 安妮耸了耸肩。 “我们知道一个人，那个人又知道另一个人，”她说。

“Well, don’t let anybody see those tickets,” said Benny. “People would sell their mothers for those seats.” “千万别让别人看到这些票，”本尼说。 “人们为了得到这些座位，连妈妈都能卖掉。”

Annie laughed as she put the tickets back in her pocket. “So how do we get to the metro train, Benny?” she asked. 安妮一边把票放回口袋里，一边笑了。 “那我们怎么去地铁站，本尼？”她问。

“Go up this street to the big statue of the angel, turn left onto Florencia, and after a couple of blocks, ask somebody to direct you to Insurgentes—that’s in-sur-HEN- tes,” said the guard, sounding out the word. “沿着这条街走到天使的大雕像那里，左转到弗洛伦西亚街，走几个街区后，问别人怎么去伊努尔亨特斯——发音是因-苏-亨-特斯，”卫兵一边说一边把单词的发音念了出来。

“Thanks, Benny! You’ve been a big help,” said Annie. “谢谢，本尼！ 你帮了大忙，”安妮说。

“Yeah, thanks,” said Jack. “是啊，谢谢，”杰克说。

“You’re welcome! Anytime!” said the guard. “不客气！ 随时愿意帮忙！”卫兵说。

Jack put the program, notebook, and pencil back into his backpack. Then he and Annie waved good-bye and headed away from the embassy. When they got to the sidewalk, they looked up and down the busy avenue. 杰克把节目单、笔记本和铅笔放回了他的背包里。 然后他和安妮挥手告别，朝大使馆外走去。 当他们走到人行道上时，他们抬头看了看这条繁忙的大街，又低头看了看。

“There’s the angel!” said Annie. She pointed to the huge statue of a winged angel looming in the distance. “看，有天使！”安妮说。 她指着远处一座巨大的有翅膀的天使雕像。

“Good, it’s not that far,” said Jack. “好，不远，”杰克说。

The air was hot and humid and smelled like car exhaust. But Jack was so excited, nothing bothered him as he and Annie walked past banks, airline offices, and fancy hotels. When they reached the golden angel in the middle of a traffic circle, Jack checked Benny’s directions, then looked around. 空气又热又潮湿，还有一股汽车尾气的味道。 但杰克太兴奋了，他和安妮走过银行、航空公司办公室和豪华酒店时，什么都没在意。 当他们来到交通环岛中间的金色天使雕像前，杰克查看了本尼的路线图，然后四处张望。

“There it is,” he said, pointing to a sign that said FLORENCIA AVENUE. They hurried across the street and headed up Florencia. “在那里，”他说着，指着一块写着“FLORENCIA AVENUE”的牌子。 他们赶紧穿过马路，沿着弗洛伦西亚大道走。

“Benny said that when we get to this street, we should ask someone for help,” said Annie. “Excuse me,” she said to a woman waiting at a stoplight, “could you please tell us where we can find …” She turned to Jack. “What was the name?” “本尼说，当我们走到这条街时，应该向人求助，”安妮说。 “请问，”她对在红绿灯前等待的一位女士说，“您能告诉我们……”她转头看向杰克。 “名字叫什么？”

“In-sur-HEN-tes,” Jack said, sounding it out like Benny had. “伊努尔-亨特斯，”杰克说，像本尼那样把名字拼读出来。

“Yes,” said the woman with a smile. “Just past Londres Street.” She pointed straight ahead. “对，”那个女人微笑着说。 “就在朗德斯街的前面。”她指着正前方。

“Thank you,” said Annie. She and Jack started walking again. “谢谢，”安妮说。 她和杰克又开始走路了。

“I can’t believe this—we have the best seats in the stadium!” Jack said. “我简直不敢相信——我们在体育场里有最好的座位！”杰克说。

“It makes sense,” said Annie. “Merlin gave us those seats to help us with our mission.” “这说得通，”安妮说。 “梅林给了我们这些座位，是为了帮助我们完成任务。”

“Right, like maybe Pelé will be playing near us and we’ll get to meet him,” said Jack. “Then somehow he’ll reveal a secret of greatness. And then we go back to Camelot to meet up with Merlin!” “对，比如说可能贝利会就近比赛，我们就能见到他，”杰克说。 “然后，他就会透露一个伟大的秘密。” 接着，我们回到卡梅洛特去见梅林！”，

“We’re super-lucky ducks,” said Annie. “Quack-quack.” “我们真是超级幸运的鸭子，”安妮说。 “嘎嘎。”
