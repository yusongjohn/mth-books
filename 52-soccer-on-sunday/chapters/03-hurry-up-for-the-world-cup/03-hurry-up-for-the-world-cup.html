3. Hurry Up for the World Cup! 3. 快点，为世界杯加油！

“Look, Insurgentes.” Jack pointed to a sign on a large building that said MERCADO INSURGENTES. “That’s it—the metro we’re supposed to catch. Come on!” “看，Insurgentes。”杰克指着一座大楼上的牌子，上面写着MERCADO INSURGENTES。 “就是它——我们要坐的地铁。快点！”

Annie and Jack joined the crowd heading into the Insurgentes building. They strolled down a corridor between stalls selling ponchos, beaded bags, wooden animals, baskets woven with palm leaves, and silver jewelry—tons of silver jewelry. The smell of sizzling meat came from food stands. Nothing looked like a train or subway station. 安妮和杰克加入了进入Insurgentes大楼的人群。 他们沿着走廊走，两边是摊位，卖着斗篷、串珠包、木制动物、用棕榈叶编织的篮子和很多很多的银制首饰。 食物摊位上传来烤肉的香味。 什么看起来都不像火车或地铁站。

Finally Jack stopped near a group of men in sombreros playing guitars, violins, and trumpets. He turned to Annie. “This doesn’t feel right!” he yelled above the noise of the music. 最后，杰克在一群戴着宽边帽、弹吉他、小提琴和小号的男人附近停了下来。 他转向安妮。 “这感觉不对劲！”，他在音乐声中大喊。

“We must have made a mistake!” Annie yelled back. “我们肯定搞错了！”，安妮也大喊着回应。

“Let’s get out of here,” said Jack. “We’re wasting time!” He and Annie turned and hurried back down the corridor between the stalls. They wove around shoppers until they escaped through the entrance of the giant market. “我们得离开这儿，”杰克说。 “我们浪费时间了！”，他和安妮转身，赶紧沿着摊位之间的走廊跑回去。 他们绕过购物的人，直到从巨大的市场入口逃了出来。

A soft, warm rain had started to fall. 一场柔和温暖的雨开始下起来。

“I don’t get it,” said Jack. He pointed to the sign on the building. “It says MERCADO INSURGENTES.” “我搞不懂，”杰克说。 他指着建筑物上的牌子。 “上面写着MERCADO INSURGENTES。”

“Look at Benny’s notes again,” said Annie. “再看看本尼的笔记，”安妮说。

Jack pulled out their directions. “Oh, no!” he said. “Benny wrote Metro Insurgentes, not Mercado Insurgentes!” 杰克拿出了他们的路线图。 “哦，不！”他说。 “本尼写的是Metro Insurgentes，不是Mercado Insurgentes！”

“I’ll ask for help!” said Annie. She took the directions and walked over to an old man reading a newspaper on a bench. “Excuse me, sir, where is Metro Insurgentes, please?” “我去问问别人吧！”安妮说。 她拿着路线图，走到长椅上一位正在看报纸的老人身边。 “请问，先生，Metro Insurgentes在哪儿？”

The old man pointed up the street. 老人指着街道上方。

“Thanks!” said Annie. She ran back to Jack and handed him the sheet of directions. Jack stuffed it into his backpack. “谢谢！”安妮说。 她跑回杰克身边，把那张地图递给他。 杰克把它塞进了他的背包里。

“Hurry, or we’ll be late!” Jack said. “快点，不然我们要迟到了！”杰克说。

Jack and Annie ran up the street through the warm, drizzling rain. They stopped when they came to an overpass jammed with trucks and cars. In a sunken plaza below was a round concrete building covered with posters and murals. One sign on the building said PLAZA INSURGENTES. Another said METRO STATION. 杰克和安妮沿着街道跑起来，穿过温暖的细雨。 他们来到一个被卡车和汽车堵住的高架桥前停了下来。 在下面的一个下沉广场上，有一座圆形的混凝土建筑，上面贴满了海报和壁画。 建筑上的一面牌子写着“PLAZA INSURGENTES”。 另一面写着“METRO STATION”。

“That’s it!” said Jack. He and Annie ran down to the plaza. They hurried by a fountain, vendors, and snack bars until they came to the rounded entrance of the metro. “就是这里！”杰克说。 他和安妮跑到了广场上。 他们匆匆跑过喷泉、小贩和小吃摊，直到来到地铁圆形入口处。

When they stepped into the station, the noise was deafening, even louder than in the market. Hordes of people were rushing up and down the stairs. Jack and Annie joined the stream of traffic going down. Beyond the stairs was a row of turnstiles. 当他们走进车站时，噪音震耳欲聋，甚至比市场里还要吵。 成群的人上下楼梯，匆忙地走来走去。 杰克和安妮加入了向下走的人流中。 楼梯的另一端是一排检票闸机。

“How do we do this?” said Annie. “我们该怎么过？”安妮说。

“Watch what everyone else is doing,” said Jack. “看看别人是怎么做的，”杰克说。

One at a time, people were dropping a single coin into slots in the turnstiles and then passing through. Jack pulled two pesos out of his backpack and gave one to Annie. Copying everyone else, they passed through the turnstiles onto the metro platform. 人们一个接一个地把一枚硬币投进闸机的投币口，然后通过闸机。 杰克从背包里拿出两枚比索，给了安妮一枚。 他们模仿着别人，穿过检票闸机，来到地铁站台。

“That wasn’t so hard,” said Annie. “这还不算太难，”安妮说。

Another huge crowd was waiting on the sweltering platform for the next train. Jack pulled out their directions again. “Red Line to Pino Suaréz!” he shouted to Annie above the noise. “Then the Blue Line to Taxqueña! Then we get off at the last stop and get a tram to Aztec Stadium!” 一大群人正在闷热的站台上等着下一班地铁。 杰克又拿出了他们的路线图。 “红线到皮诺·索雷斯！”，他在嘈杂声中对安妮大喊。 “然后坐蓝线到塔克斯克尼亚！ 然后我们在最后一站下车，再坐电车去阿兹特克体育场！”，

“Easy!” yelled Annie. “简单！”，安妮喊道。

Really? thought Jack. 真的吗？杰克心想。

A train was rolling in. “Red Line?” Annie shouted to a woman. 一列火车正驶来。 “红线？”安妮朝一位女士喊道。

As the woman nodded, the metro train came to a stop. The doors opened. As passengers spilled off the train, people on the platform surged forward. 女士点头时，地铁列车停了下来，车门打开了。 当乘客们从列车上下来时，站台上的人群向前涌去。

Jack grabbed Annie’s hand and they moved with the crowd. But before they could even get close to the train, a bell rang and the doors slammed shut. The people left on the platform jumped back as the train pulled away. 杰克抓住安妮的手，他们跟着人群一起移动。 但就在他们还没靠近列车时，突然响起了铃声，车门猛地关上了。 站台上剩下的人急忙后退，列车缓缓驶离了。

“We’ll get the next one!” Jack shouted. “我们坐下一班吧！”杰克喊道。

Soon another train rolled in. Jack grabbed Annie’s hand again, and they surged forward with the crowd. They were pushed and shoved as they tried to get through the doorway. Finally they were inside! 不久后，另一辆列车进站了。 杰克再次抓住安妮的手，他们又跟着人群冲了上去。 他们努力挤过门口时，被人群推搡着。 终于他们进去了！

“Safe!” said Jack, collapsing onto a seat with Annie. But they were nearly squashed as more passengers piled into the metro. More and more kept cramming in, until people were practically sitting on Jack’s and Annie’s laps. “安全了！”杰克说，然后和安妮一起瘫坐在座位上。 但当更多乘客挤进地铁时，他们差点被压扁了。 越来越多的人挤进来，直到人们几乎坐在杰克和安妮的腿上。

The bell rang. The doors closed. The train pulled away from the station. As it rumbled through the dark tunnel, the air in the car was suffocating. Jack could feel sweat trickling down his face and the back of his neck. 铃声响了，车门关上了。 火车离开了车站。 当它在黑暗的隧道中缓缓行驶时，车厢里的空气让人喘不过气来。 杰克能感觉到汗水顺着他的脸和脖子流下来。

“What’s our stop?” Annie yelled to Jack. “我们该在哪个站下车？”安妮朝杰克喊道。

Jack reached into his back pocket. The paper with the directions wasn’t there. He wrestled his backpack off his back and looked inside. The sheet of paper wasn’t there, either. “Do you have our directions?” he shouted at Annie. 杰克伸手摸了摸他背后的口袋。 写着路线的纸不在那儿。 他把背包从背上摘下来，看了看里面。 那张纸也不在那儿。 “你有我们的路线吗？”他朝安妮喊道。

“No! You had them!” said Annie. “没有！ 你有！”安妮说。

“I must have dropped them!” said Jack, panicked. “我一定是把它们弄掉了！”杰克慌张地说。

“Don’t worry,” said Annie. “Excuse me!” she yelled up at a woman standing over them. “How do we get to the Aztec station?” “别担心，”安妮说。 “打扰一下！”她朝站在他们上方的一位女士喊道。 “我们怎么去阿兹特克车站？”

The woman shrugged. Then she asked a man, who asked another woman. But the train stopped and all three people hurried off without answering Annie’s question! 那个女人耸了耸肩。 然后她问了一个男人，那个男人又问了另一个女人。 但火车停了下来，三个人都没回答安妮的问题，就匆匆下了车！

“Should we get off, too?” asked Annie. “我们也该下车吗？”安妮问。

“I don’t know!” said Jack. “We don’t know if this is the right station! They’re not announcing where we are!” “我不知道！”杰克说。 “我们不知道这是不是正确的车站！ 他们根本没有宣布我们现在在哪里！”

New passengers piled into the car, and the aisle became even more crowded. Once again, as the train took off, Jack and Annie were squashed in their seats by the crush of passengers in the aisle. 新的乘客挤进了车厢，过道变得更加拥挤了。 火车再次启动时，杰克和安妮又被过道里拥挤的人群挤在了座位上。

“Where is Aztec Stadium?” Jack yelled to no one in particular. “阿兹特克体育场在哪儿？”杰克朝没人特别喊道。

“Get off at Pino Suaréz!” a man shouted above the roar. The man said more, but Jack couldn’t hear him. Before he could ask the man to repeat his directions, the train stopped—and the man pushed his way out the doors. “在皮诺·苏亚雷斯站下车！”一个男人在轰鸣声中喊道。 男人又说了些什么，但杰克听不见。 还没等他让男人重复方向，火车就停了下来——男人挤着走出了车门。

“Is this Pino Suaréz?” Jack shouted to Annie. He looked around wildly, trying to find a sign. “This is a nightmare!” “这是皮诺·苏亚雷斯吗？”杰克朝安妮喊道。 他四处张望，试图找到一个标识牌。 “这简直是噩梦！”

“Is this Pino Suaréz?” Annie yelled to a teenage girl with a large backpack. “这是皮诺·苏亚雷斯吗？”安妮朝一个背着大背包的少女大喊。

“Sorry, I don’t know,” the girl said. “Check the map!” She pointed to a large map on the far wall of the train. “抱歉，我不知道，”女孩说。 “看看地图！”她指着火车远端墙上的大地图。

“Let’s look!” said Jack. But it was impossible to get to the map, as the aisle had already filled up with new passengers. “我们去看看！”杰克说。 但是不可能拿到地图，因为过道已经被新乘客挤满了。

“Too late,” said Annie. “太晚了，”安妮说。

The metro doors closed, and the train thundered on through the dark tunnel. 地铁门关上了，火车轰隆隆地穿过黑暗的隧道。
