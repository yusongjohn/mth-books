5. High in the Sky 5. 高高在上

W hen the doors opened, Roberto led Jack and Annie off the tram. They walked down a ramp from the station and headed into the huge parking area. 当门打开时，罗伯托带领杰克和安妮走下缆车。 他们从车站走下斜坡，朝巨大的停车场走去。

“Aztec Stadium is named for the people who built an empire in this part of Mexico many hundreds of years ago,” said Roberto. “阿兹特克体育场是以很多很多年前在墨西哥这片地方建立帝国的人命名的，”罗伯托说。

“Really?” said Jack. He’d read about the Aztec Empire. He could imagine the enormous stadium being similar to an Aztec pyramid. “真的吗？”杰克说。 他读过关于阿兹特克帝国的书。 他能想象出这个巨大的体育场和阿兹特克金字塔很像。

Thousands of spectators flooded the parking lot. They wore bright summer clothes and wide-brimmed hats. Vendors walked among the big cars, selling Brazilian, Italian, and Mexican flags. Kids were waving souvenir programs, soccer shirts, and caps for sale. 成千上万的观众涌进了停车场。 他们穿着鲜艳的夏装，戴着宽边帽子。 商贩们在汽车之间走动，卖巴西、意大利和墨西哥的国旗。 孩子们挥舞着纪念品节目单、足球衫和帽子出售。

图中文字：Estadio Azteca 图中文字：Estadio Azteca

Jack and Annie followed Roberto to the nearest entrance, and Annie held out their two tickets. “That way, gate G!” a stadium official said. He pointed toward one of the other entrances. 杰克和安妮跟着罗伯托走向最近的入口，安妮拿出他们的两张票。 “那边，G口！”一位体育场工作人员说。 他指着另一个入口的方向。

When Roberto showed his ticket, the man pointed in the same direction. 当罗伯托出示了他的票时，那个人也指向了同一个方向。

“Hurray! I hope I’m sitting near you,” said Roberto. “欢呼！ 我希望我坐在你附近，”罗伯托说。

“Me too!” said Jack. “我也是！”杰克说。

Jack, Annie, and Roberto followed a crowd through gate G and into the giant stadium. They all looked up at the stands. Dozens of tiers of seats rose above the green soccer field. The rows were so steep, it looked as if spectators in the highest rows might fall straight down onto the field. 杰克、安妮和罗伯托跟着人群穿过G门，走进了巨大的体育场。 他们全都抬头看向看台。 数不清的台阶一层层向上延伸，超过了绿色的足球场。 这些台阶非常陡，看起来好像坐在最高层的观众可能会直接掉到球场上。

“This place is huge!” said Jack. “这个地方真大！”杰克说。

“Where do we go now?” said Annie. “我们现在去哪儿？”安妮说。

“My father said I should ask an official,” said Roberto, looking around. “Over there!” He led Jack and Annie to an usher in uniform standing at the bottom of an aisle. “我爸爸说我要去问一个工作人员，”罗伯托说着，环顾四周。 “在那里！”他带杰克和安妮走到一个穿制服的工作人员面前，那人正站在一条过道的尽头。

“Can you please tell us where our seats are?” Annie asked, showing her ticket to the woman. “请问，能告诉我们我们的座位在哪里吗？”安妮问，她把票递给了那个女人。

When the woman looked at the ticket, her eyes grew wide. “You have some of the best seats in the stadium!” she said. She pointed to the section closest to the field. “Over there—second row, right on the aisle!” 当那个女人看到票的时候，她的眼睛睁得大大的。 “你们在体育场里有最好的座位之一！”她说。 她指着离场地最近的区域。 “在那里——第二排，就在过道旁边！”

“Yay!” said Jack. The guard at the embassy had been right. They did have incredible seats—seats that would help them get close enough to Pelé to learn a secret of greatness from him. “太棒了！”杰克说。 大使馆的警卫说得没错。 他们确实有很棒的座位——这些座位能让他们离贝利足够近，从他那里学到成功的秘密。

Jack turned excitedly to Roberto. “Where’s your seat?” he asked. 杰克兴奋地转向罗伯托。 “你的座位在哪里？”他问。

Roberto showed his ticket to the usher. “Where do I go?” he asked. 罗伯托把票递给了检票员。 “我该去哪儿？”他问。

“Well … you are in the same line as your friends …,” the woman told Roberto. “嗯……你和你的朋友在同一条队伍里……”，那个女人告诉罗伯托。

“Fantastic!” he said with a big grin. “太棒了！”他笑着说。

“But I’m afraid your seat is in a different section,” said the usher. She pointed all the way up. “It’s near the very top, high up in the sky.” “但恐怕你的座位在不同的区域，”检票员说。 她指着远处。 “它就在非常高的地方，靠近天空的顶端。”

The smile left Roberto’s face, but then it returned. “I’d better go,” he said. “It was great to meet you.” 罗伯托脸上的笑容消失了，但很快又回来了。 “我最好走了，”他说。 “见到你们真高兴。”

“You too,” said Jack. “我也很高兴，”杰克说。

“Good-bye, Jack and Annie,” said Roberto, and he started up the steep steps to the top of the stadium. “再见了，杰克和安妮，”罗伯托说，然后他开始沿着体育场陡峭的台阶往上走。

“Roberto, wait!” Annie called, and she hurried up the steps after him. Jack followed her. “Listen—I want to trade seats with you,” she said. She held out her ticket. “罗伯托，等等！”安妮喊道，然后她赶紧跑上台阶追他。 杰克跟了上去。 “听着——我想和你换座位，”她说。 她递出了自己的票。

Really? thought Jack. What about our mission? 真的吗？杰克心想。 我们的任务呢？

“Oh, no! I cannot!” said Roberto. “哦，不！ “我做不到！”罗伯托说。

“Yes, you can!” said Annie. “Please! We have to trade seats.” “你可以的！”安妮说。 “求求你！ 我们必须交换座位。

“But why?” asked Roberto. “但是为什么？”罗伯托问。

“Because it’s your birthday,” said Annie. “And if you have a good seat, you can tell your eight brothers and sisters and your parents all about the game. So it will be like eleven people are getting a good seat. Not just one.” “因为这是你的生日，”安妮说。 “如果你有一个好座位，你就可以告诉你的八个兄弟姐妹和你的父母有关这个游戏的事情。 这样就相当于有十一个人得到了好座位，而不仅仅是一个人。

That actually makes sense, thought Jack. Still, he was amazed by Annie’s generosity. 这其实挺有道理的，杰克心想。 不过，他对安妮的慷慨感到惊讶。

“But no, I cannot—” said Roberto. “但是不行，我不能——”罗伯托说。

“But yes, you can,” said Annie. She grabbed Roberto’s ticket and put her own ticket in his hand. “You guys have fun!” She started up the stairs. “但是可以，你当然可以，”安妮说。 她抓起罗伯托的票，把她的票放在他手里。 “你们玩得开心点！”她说着，开始往上走。

Jack felt a wave of guilt. “Wait, stop. That’s not fair,” he said to Annie. “Maybe I should give up my seat.” 杰克感到一阵内疚。 “等等，别走。 这不公平，”他对安妮说。 “也许我应该让出我的座位。”

“No, it’s okay. I’ll give up mine,” said Annie. “不，没关系。” “我让给你坐我的位置。”安妮说。

Roberto laughed. “You are both too kind,” he said. “I cannot take either of your seats.” Roberto handed Annie her ticket and took his back. 罗贝托笑了。 “你们俩都太客气了，”他说。 “我不能接受你们任何一个位置。”罗贝托把票递给安妮，然后拿回了自己的票。

“No, please, I want—” Annie protested. “不，求你了，我想—”安妮抗议道。

“Wait, I have another idea,” Jack said to Annie. “We can take turns. I’ll switch with Roberto for the first half. And you switch with him for the second half.” “等等，我有个主意，”杰克对安妮说。 “我们可以轮流坐。 我先和罗贝托换座位坐前半段。 你再和他换座位坐后半段。”

“Perfect!” said Annie. “That way, Roberto can get a great view of the whole game. And you and I will each get a great view for half the time.” “太棒了！”安妮说。 “这样，罗伯托就能看到整个比赛的精彩场面了。 你和我各有一半的时间能看到精彩的画面。”

“Right.” Jack pulled the Ring of Truth off his finger. He slipped it into Annie’s hand and spoke softly to her. “The person who’s sitting close to the field should wear this. Be sure to check it if you think you’ve discovered Pelé’s secret.” “对。”杰克把真理之戒从手指上取了下来。 他把它放进安妮的手里，轻声对她说话。 “坐在场地附近的人应该戴上这个戒指。 如果你觉得你发现了贝利的秘密，一定要检查一下。”

“Got it,” Annie said, putting the ring on her finger. “I’ll give it back to you at halftime.” “明白了，”安妮说，把戒指戴在了手指上。 “中场休息时我会把它还给你的。”

“Right,” said Jack. “How long is halftime, Roberto?” “对，”杰克说。 “上半场要多久，罗伯托？”

“Fifteen minutes.” “十五分钟。”

“Plenty of time,” said Jack. He looked at his seat number. “A-26. Okay, I’ll find you at seat A-26, Annie. Wait for me.” Then he swapped tickets with Roberto. “时间很充足，”杰克说。 他看了看自己的座位号。 “A-26。” 好吧，我到A-26座位找你，安妮。 等我一下。”然后他和罗伯托交换了票。

“Thank you a million times, Jack!” said Roberto. “谢谢你，杰克！谢谢你一千万次！”罗伯托说。

“No problem,” said Jack. “See you guys later!” And he started up the stairs. “没问题，”杰克说。 “待会儿见，你们俩！”然后他开始走上楼梯。

“Have fun in the sky!” called Annie. “在天空中玩得开心点！”安妮喊道。

“I will!” Jack felt great as he started up the steps to the top of the stands. He was sure he’d done the right thing, especially since it was Roberto’s birthday. “我会的！”杰克感觉很棒，他开始走上看台的台阶。 他确信自己做对了，尤其是因为这是罗伯托的生日。

But as Jack climbed higher and higher, his spirits fell. The stands were so high, he wondered if he’d be able to see anything. By the time he reached the top rows, he was out of breath. He showed his ticket to an usher, and she pointed toward his seat. 但当杰克爬得越来越高时，他的心情却越来越低落。 看台太高了，他甚至怀疑自己是否能看见什么。 等他终于走到最上面的几排时，他已经气喘吁吁了。 他把票递给一位工作人员，她指了指他的座位。

Jack squeezed past people and collapsed on the bench. From his seat, the field looked a million miles away. How can anyone enjoy the game from up here? he wondered. He wished huge stadium screens had been invented so everyone could see the game well. 杰克挤过人群，瘫坐在长椅上。 从他的座位上看，球场看起来远在千里之外。 这里怎么可能会有人享受比赛呢？他心想。 他希望早就有人发明出巨大的体育场屏幕，这样每个人都能清楚地看到比赛。

The spectators around Jack seemed to be having a good time anyway. A couple and their two children were eating tacos and drinking sodas. Others were waving small multicolored flags. A teenage boy next to Jack was looking through a pair of large binoculars. A man with a bushy mustache held a scratchy-sounding portable radio. 杰克周围的观众看起来玩得很开心。 一对夫妇和他们的两个孩子正在吃玉米饼，喝苏打水。 其他人正在挥舞着五颜六色的小旗子。 杰克旁边的一个少年正用一副大望远镜看着。 一个留着浓密胡子的男士拿着一台发出刺刺声音的便携式收音机。

Jack could hear the sportscaster through the static: “Final World Cup championship! A billion people around the world are watching on television! And over a hundred thousand spectators are here in Aztec Stadium today!” 杰克能透过静电听到体育播音员的声音：‘世界杯决赛开始了！' 全世界有十亿人正在电视上看比赛！ 而且今天有超过十万名观众来到了阿兹特克体育场！'

“Here they come!” shouted the boy looking through binoculars. ‘他们来了！’拿着望远镜的男孩喊道。

Suddenly all the spectators let out a roar. The two soccer teams were running onto the field. 突然，所有观众都大声吼了起来。 两支足球队正在跑上球场。

“Italy’s incredible team has blue shirts and white shorts!” the teenager with binoculars shouted. “Brazil’s Beautiful Team: yellow shirts, blue shorts! Pelé is number ten!” “意大利的神奇队伍穿着蓝色的衬衫和白色的短裤！”，戴望远镜的青少年喊道。 “巴西的美丽队伍：黄色的衬衫，蓝色的短裤！ 贝利是十号！”，

Jack couldn’t see the colors of any uniforms, much less any numbers. 杰克根本看不到任何球衣的颜色，更别说号码了。

“Kickoff! And there Pelé goes!” the commentator on the crackly radio yelled. “ Pelé! There! There!” “开球！ 贝利上场了！”， crackly收音机里的解说员大喊。 “贝利！在那里！ 在那里！

Where? Where? Jack wondered. He really wanted to see Pelé the Great, but from his seat high up in the sky, the players on the field looked no bigger than ants. 哪儿？哪儿？ 杰克想知道。 他真的很想看看传奇的贝利，但从他高高在天空中的座位上看，场上的球员看起来和蚂蚁一样小。

The radio commentator shouted excitedly about the game. Jack could only catch pieces of what he was saying: “What a pass! … Carlos Alberto, captain of Brazil’s team! … Hitting a very long ball! … Dangerous free-kick situation! … Italy, zero, Brazil, zero!” 收音机里的解说员激动地谈论着比赛。 杰克只能听懂他所说的一小部分：‘多漂亮的传球！’ … 卡洛斯·阿尔贝托，巴西队的队长！ … 他踢出了一记非常长的球！ … 危险的任意球机会！ … 意大利，零比零，巴西，零比零！

With every play, everyone in the huge stadium screamed, whistled, cheered, or booed. Then thousands of Brazil fans jumped to their feet, pumping their fists in the air and screaming, “Gooooaaaallll!!!!” Jack jumped to his feet, too. The teenager with the binoculars shouted, “Pelé leaped past the defender! He hit the ball and scored!” 每进行一场比赛，体育场里所有的人都在尖叫、吹口哨、欢呼或者喝倒彩。 然后成千上万的巴西球迷跳了起来，挥舞着拳头，大声喊着：“进——球——啦——！！！”杰克也跳了起来。 拿着望远镜的青少年喊道：“贝利突破了防守队员！ 他击中了球并得分了！

Jack shook his head. He couldn’t believe he’d missed seeing Pelé make a goal! He really wished he had binoculars. He really, really wished he were sitting with Annie and Roberto! 杰克摇了摇头。 他简直不敢相信自己竟然没看到贝利进球！ 他真希望有双筒望远镜。 他真、真希望他能和安妮还有罗伯托坐在一起！

Everyone sat down. Jack listened as carefully as he could to the voice of the radio commentator: “Free kick! … Way, way up! … Beautiful! No wonder Brazil is called the Beautiful Team! … Punching that one away! … Dead ball!” 大家都坐下了。 杰克尽量认真地听着收音机评论员的声音：‘任意球！……飞得高、飞得高！’ ……漂亮！ 难怪巴西被称为美丽的球队！ … 把那个踢开！… 死球！

Nothing the sportscaster said sounded like a secret of greatness to Jack. He just hoped Annie was paying close attention to the game and checking the ring a lot. 杰克觉得体育播音员说的任何话，听起来都不像什么伟大的秘密。 他只是希望安妮在认真看比赛，而且经常检查戒指。

Even though he was envious, Jack was still glad that Roberto had a great view of the game. If he was sitting up here, Jack thought, he’d have nothing to report to his eight brothers and sisters. 尽管他很嫉妒，但杰克还是很高兴罗伯托能有很好的比赛视野。 杰克想，如果他现在坐在这里，他就有 nothing（没什么）可以告诉他的八个兄弟姐妹了。

Suddenly thousands of Italy fans leapt to their feet, screaming, and the older kid with binoculars shouted, “Italy scores! Brazil, one! Italy, one!” 突然，成千上万的意大利球迷跳了起来，大声尖叫，拿着望远镜的那位大孩子喊道：“意大利得分了！巴西一比零！” 意大利，一个！

Jack couldn’t wait to switch seats with Annie and watch the game up close! If the ring hadn’t glowed for her, it would all be up to him. As soon as the radio announcer said, “Halftime!” Jack looked at his watch. It was 12:45. He had fifteen minutes to find Annie and Roberto. Jack squeezed past the people sitting in his row and took off down the mountain of stairs. 杰克迫不及待地想和安妮交换座位，近距离观看比赛！ 如果戒指没有为她发光，那就全靠他了。 广播员一说完，“中场休息！”杰克就看了看手表。时间是12:45。 他还有十五分钟要找到安妮和罗伯托。 杰克挤过坐在他那一排的人，朝那座楼梯冲了下去。
