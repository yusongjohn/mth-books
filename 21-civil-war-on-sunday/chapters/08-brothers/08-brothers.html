8. Brothers 8. 兄弟

Clara Barton had turned the wagon around. She helped Jack and Annie lift the drummer boy into the back. 克拉拉·巴顿已经把马车调转了方向。 她帮杰克和安妮把鼓手男孩抬上车。

“The soldiers said he has heat stroke,” Annie told Clara. “His name is John.” “士兵们说他中暑了，”安妮告诉克拉拉。 “他叫约翰。”

The boy lay down next to the sleeping Confederate soldier. 男孩躺在睡着的南方士兵旁边。

“He does have heat stroke,” Clara said. “The other boy also has a high fever. We must get them to the hospital at once. Can you two stay in the back of the wagon and do as I tell you?” “他确实中暑了，”克拉拉说。 “另一个男孩也在发高烧。 我们必须立刻把他们送到医院。 你们俩能不能留在马车后面，按我说的去做？”

“Sure,” said Jack and Annie. “当然，”杰克和安妮说。

Clara dampened two clean cloths with water from Jack’s canteen. 克拉拉用杰克水壶里的水打湿了两块干净的布。

“Gently press these cloths against their faces to help cool them off,” she said. “轻轻把这些布按在他们脸上，帮他们降降温。”她说。

She gave the cloths to Jack and Annie. 她把布递给杰克和安妮。

Then she went to the front of the wagon and climbed in. The wagon started forward. 然后她走到马车前面，爬了上去。 马车开始往前走。

Jack and Annie gently patted the soldiers’ faces with the damp cloths. Jack looked at the young men lying side by side. The two seemed far more alike than different. 杰克和安妮用湿布轻轻拍着士兵们的脸。 杰克看着这两个并排躺着的年轻人。 这两人看起来相似之处远远多于不同。

In another time and place, they might have been friends, Jack thought. 在另一个时间和地点，他们也许会是朋友，杰克想。

Finally, the wagon arrived at the field hospital. The Confederate soldier was put on a stretcher and carried to a tent. 终于，马车到了野战医院。 南方士兵被抬上担架，送进帐篷。

Two soldiers wearing bandages put the drummer boy on a stretcher. 两个缠着绷带的士兵把鼓手男孩抬上担架。

“Could you stay with John awhile?” Clara Barton asked Jack and Annie. “你们能陪约翰一会儿吗？”克拉拉·巴顿问杰克和安妮。

“Sure,” said Jack. “当然，”杰克说。

“Try to bring down his fever,” Clara said. “A nurse will give you ice packs to press against his skin. Find me when his fever is lower.” “试着把他的烧退下来。”克拉拉说。 “护士会给你们冰袋按在他皮肤上。 等他烧退了再来找我。”

The drummer boy was carried into an empty tent. Jack and Annie followed. 鼓手男孩被抬进一个空帐篷。 杰克和安妮跟上去。

John was put on a cot. Then a nurse brought some rags and a bucket filled with ice. Jack and Annie were left alone with the boy. 约翰被放在行军床上。 然后一个护士拿来一些破布和一桶冰。 杰克和安妮被留下，单独陪着这个男孩。

Jack wrapped some ice in a rag. He pressed the ice pack against the boy’s head and neck and arms. Annie fanned the air to cool John off and to keep away the flies. 杰克用破布包了一些冰。 他把冰袋按在男孩的头、脖子和胳膊上。 安妮扇着风，好让约翰凉快下来，也把苍蝇赶开。

Jack felt so hot he pressed an ice pack against his own face for a moment. Then he looked up drummerboys in his Civil War book. He read: 杰克觉得自己太热了，就拿冰袋按了一会儿自己的脸。 然后他在内战书里查鼓手男孩。他读道：

The Civil War was the last war to use drummer boys. The drumbeat was used to give orders to soldiers. Different beats told them when to eat, how to march, and even how to fight. On smoky battlefields, the boy’s drumming helped soldiers find one another and keep together. 美国内战是最后一次使用鼓手男孩的战争。鼓声用来给士兵下命令。不同的鼓点告诉他们什么时候吃饭、怎么行军，甚至怎么战斗。在烟雾弥漫的战场上，男孩的鼓声帮助士兵找到彼此并保持在一起。

“Wow,” said Jack. He closed the book, pulled out his notebook, and wrote:



 “哇，”杰克说。 他合上书，拿出笔记本写道：

图中文字：drummer boy—really important job 图中文字：鼓手男孩——一份非常重要的工作

Suddenly, John shouted. Jack looked up from his notebook. The drummer boy was still asleep, but he was waving his arms as if he were having a nightmare. 突然，约翰大喊起来。 杰克从笔记本上抬起头。 鼓手男孩还在睡着，可他挥舞着手臂，好像在做噩梦。

Annie shook the boy’s arm. 安妮摇了摇男孩的胳膊。

“Wake up, John,” she said. “You’re okay. Wake up.” “醒醒，约翰，”她说。 “你没事。醒醒。”

The drummer boy opened his eyes. 鼓手男孩睁开眼睛。

“You were having a bad dream,” said Annie. “You’re safe now. You’ll see your family again soon.” “你在做噩梦，”安妮说。 “你现在安全了。 你很快会再见到家人。”

“No! No!” the boy said. He sounded frantic. “I have to go back to the battlefield.” “不是，那是魔法！一团旋转的光！”安妮说。 不！”男孩说。 他听上去慌乱极了。 “我得回战场去。”

“No, you don’t have to fight anymore,” said Annie. “You can go home and be safe.” “不，你不用再打仗了，”安妮说。 “你可以回家，会很安全。”

“No!” the boy said. “They need me! They need my drum!” He sounded more and more upset. “不！”男孩说。 “他们需要我！ “他们需要我的鼓！”他听上去越来越难过。

Jack thought about their list. 杰克想起了他们的清单。

Put aside your own feelings, he remembered. 把你自己的情绪放到一边，他记起来了。

“Okay, John,” said Jack. “You can go back as soon as you feel better.” “好吧，约翰，”杰克说。 “等你感觉好一点，就可以马上回去。”

“Jack!” said Annie. “He’ll get hurt or killed! I’m afraid for him!” “杰克！”安妮说。 “他会受伤或被打死的！ 我为他担心！”

“Me too,” Jack said softly. “But we have to put our own feelings aside. That’s one of the things on our list.” “我也是。”杰克轻声说。 “可我们得把自已的感情放在一边。 那是我们清单上的一条。”

Annie sighed. 安妮叹了口气。

“Okay,” she said sadly. She looked at John. “If you want to fight again in the Civil War, you can. If that’s what you really want.” “好吧，”她难过地说。 她看着约翰。 “如果你想再参加内战去打仗，你可以。 如果那真是你想要的。”

“Thank you,” the boy whispered. “谢谢你们，”男孩低声说。

“You know, you’re the bravest kid I’ve ever met,” Jack said. “你知道吗，你是我见过的最勇敢的孩子。”杰克说。

The drummer boy smiled at Jack. 鼓手男孩对杰克笑了笑。

“You look just like my little brother,” he said hoarsely. “你长得就像我弟弟。”他声音嘶哑地说。

“You look just like my big brother,” said Jack, “except I don’t have a big brother. I don’t have any brothers.” “你长得就像我哥哥，”杰克说，“不过我没有哥哥。 我一个兄弟也没有。”

The three of them laughed. The drummer boy’s laugh was very soft. 他们三个都笑了。 鼓手男孩的笑声很轻。

The boy laid his head back on his pillow and closed his eyes again. 男孩把头靠回枕头上，又闭上了眼睛。

In a moment, he was sleeping peacefully. A smile was on his lips. 很快，他就安稳地睡着了。 他嘴角带着微笑。

Annie felt his forehead. 安妮摸了摸他的额头。

“His fever’s gone down,” she said. “We should go tell Clara.” “他烧退了，”她说。 “我们该去告诉克拉拉了。”

Annie left the tent. 安妮离开了帐篷。

Jack got up and slowly followed her. 杰克站起来，慢慢跟着她。

When he reached the entrance of the tent, he turned and looked back. The shadows of twilight fell across the boy’s calm, sleeping face. 走到帐篷门口时，他转过身回头看。 暮色的阴影洒落在男孩平静熟睡的脸上。

It was strange. Jack hardly knew the drummer boy. But he felt they could be brothers. 真奇怪。 杰克几乎不认识这个小鼓手。 可他觉得他们或许可以成为兄弟。

Listening to the cannon fire in the distance, Jack was afraid for the boy. 听着远处的炮声，杰克为这个男孩担心。

Will he live to see his family again? Jack wondered. 他还能活着再见家人吗？杰克想。

“Good luck, John,” Jack said softly. “祝你好运，约翰，”杰克轻声说。

With a heavy heart, Jack stepped out into the warm evening air. 杰克心情沉重地走进温暖的晚风里。
