2. Cruel War 2. 残酷的战争

Glaring sunlight filled the tree house. 刺眼的阳光充满了树屋。

“It’s really hot here,” said Jack. “这儿真热，”杰克说。

“Especially in these clothes,” said Annie. “尤其是穿着这些衣服。”安妮说。

Their clothes had magically been changed. Annie wore a long dress. Jack wore scratchy pants and a long-sleeved shirt. His backpack was now a leather knapsack. 他们的衣服已经神奇地变了。 安妮穿着一条长裙。 杰克穿着粗糙的裤子和一件长袖衬衫。 他的背包现在变成了皮制的小背包。

“Where are we?” asked Annie. “我们在哪儿？”安妮问。

They looked out the window together. 他们一起望向窗外。

The tree house had landed in a tree at the edge of a field—the same field on the cover of the book. 树屋落在田边的一棵树上——就是书封面上的那片田。

“It looks so peaceful,” said Jack. “Where’s the Civil War?” “看起来好平静，”杰克说。 “内战在哪儿？”

“There,” whispered Annie with a shiver. She pointed to the woods beyond the field. “在那儿，”安妮打了个寒颤，低声说。 她指向田野尽头的树林。

Jack saw a soldier riding a horse out of the woods. The horse was covered with mud. The soldier’s blue uniform was torn. His arm was bloody. 杰克看见一个士兵骑着马从树林里出来。 那匹马身上沾满了泥。 那个士兵的蓝色军装被撕破了。 他的胳膊在流血。

Another man rode into the field. His blue uniform was in rags, too. His head was bandaged. 另一个人骑马进了田野。 他的蓝色军装也成了破布条。 他的头缠着绷带。

“Oh, man,” whispered Jack. “Who are they?” “哦，天哪，”杰克轻声说。 “他们是谁？”

He opened the Civil War book and found a painting of some soldiers in blue. He read to Annie: 他打开南北战争的书，找到一幅穿着蓝色军装的士兵的画。

1861–1865

The Civil War is also called the “War Between the States,” because it was fought between the Southern and Northern states of the United States. Southerners wore gray uniforms and were called Confederate soldiers. Northerners wore blue uniforms and were called Union soldiers. 1861–1865 美国南北战争也被称为“州际战争”，因为它是美国南方州和北方州之间进行的战争。南方人穿灰色军装，被称为南方军队。北方人穿蓝色军装，被称为北方军队。

“So they’re Union soldiers,” said Jack. He pulled out his notebook and wrote:



 “所以他们是联邦军士兵。”杰克说。 他拿出笔记本写道：

图中文字：$$ \begin{aligned}&Civil~War-1861-1865\\&Blue=North=Union\\&Gray=South=Confederate\end{aligned} $$ 图中文字：$$ \begin{aligned}&南北战争-1861-1865\\&蓝色=北方=联邦\\&灰色=南方=邦联\end{aligned} $$

Jack looked at his book again. He read aloud: 杰克又看了看书。他大声读道：

The Civil War was a cruel and bloody war. More people died in this war than in all of America’s other wars put together. One out of every five young men in the nation died or was wounded. 美国内战是一场残酷而血腥的战争。在这场战争中丧生的人数，比美国其他所有战争加起来还多。全国每五个年轻男子中，就有一人死于战争或受伤。

“That’s so sad,” said Annie. “好伤心，”安妮说。

Jack wrote in his notebook:



 杰克在笔记本上写道：

图中文字：cruel war 图中文字：残酷的战争

“Wow, they keep coming,” Annie said. “哇，他们还在不停地过来。”安妮说。

Jack looked up. More Union soldiers were coming through the field. These didn’t have horses. 杰克抬起头。 更多联邦军士兵正穿过田野。 这些人没有马。

They all looked sad and weary. Some of them limped. Some of them helped others along. One man stumbled and fell down. 他们看上去都既悲伤又疲惫。 他们中有的人一瘸一拐地走着。 有的人搀扶着别人往前走。 一个男人踉跄了一下，摔倒了。

“I have to help—” said Annie. “我得去帮忙——”安妮说。

“Wait!” said Jack. “等等——”杰克喘着气说。

She started down the rope ladder. 她开始顺着绳梯往下爬。

“You can’t help!” said Jack. “A kid can’t help … ” “你帮不了！”杰克说。 “小孩帮不了……”

But Annie kept going. 可安妮还是往下爬。

“Don’t forget—we have to find some special writing for Morgan!” Jack called in a loud whisper. “Something to follow!” “别忘了——我们得给摩根找特别的文字！”杰克压低声音大声喊道。 可以遵循的东西

He packed the Civil War book and his notebook in his knapsack. Then he started down the ladder. 他把内战书和笔记本放进背包。 然后他开始往下爬梯子。

When he stepped onto the ground, Jack saw Annie in the distance. 踩到地上时，杰克看见安妮在远处。

She was holding her hand out to the fallen soldier. She helped him to his feet. 她正向那个倒下的士兵伸出手。 她把他扶了起来。

The soldier slowly started walking again. Annie walked beside him. 那士兵慢慢又开始往前走。 安妮走在他旁边。

“Oh, brother,” said Jack. And he hurried to catch up with Annie. “哎呀，真是的。”杰克说。 他赶紧去追上安妮。

The sun was scorching hot as Jack ran through the dry field. He was sweaty in his scratchy clothes. 杰克跑过干燥的田野时，太阳灼热难当。 他穿着粗糙的衣服，浑身是汗。

He caught up with Annie. Together, they walked silently with the soldiers. 他赶上了安妮。 他们一起静静地和士兵们走着。

At the edge of the field was a steep hill. Everyone stopped and gazed at the sight below them—rows and rows of white tents. 田野边缘是一座陡峭的山。 所有人都停下来，注视着下面的景象——一排排白色帐篷。

“Thank goodness,” said the soldier beside Jack. “We’re saved.” “谢天谢地。”杰克旁边的士兵说。 “我们得救了。”
