7. Helping Hands 7. 援助之手

The firing came to an end. The horses calmed down. The smoky air began to clear. 射击停了下来。 马平静下来了。 烟雾弥漫的空气开始散开。

Clara handed Jack and Annie each a canteen. 克拉拉递给杰克和安妮每人一个行军水壶。

“Fill these quickly,” she said. “We have no time to waste.” “赶快装满，”她说。 “我们没时间可浪费。”

Jack’s legs felt wobbly as he followed Annie to the creek. They filled their canteens, then climbed back into the wagon. 杰克跟着安妮走向溪边时，腿都在发软。 他们装满水壶，然后爬回马车。

“Keep alert,” said Clara. “Look for the wounded as they come off the battlefield.” “保持警觉。”克拉拉说。 “留意那些从战场上下来的伤员。”

She snapped the reins. The horses started off again. 她猛地一拉缰绳。 马匹又出发了。

As they bumped along, Jack looked ahead for wounded soldiers. 他们颠簸前行时，杰克向前张望着寻找伤兵。

“There!” said Annie. “那儿！”安妮说。

She pointed to a man limping toward them and waving his arms. 她指向一个正一瘸一拐朝他们走来、挥着手臂的人。

The man looked very young, more like a teenage boy. His uniform was torn and bloody. It wasn’t a blue uniform, though. It was gray. 那人看起来非常年轻，更像是个十几岁的男孩。 他的制服又破又带血。 不过那不是蓝色制服。是灰色的。

Clara pulled the horses to a stop. 克拉拉勒住马停下。

“But he’s a Confederate soldier,” said Jack. “可他是南方邦联的士兵，”杰克说。

“When someone is hurt, you give them a helping hand, no matter who they are,” said Clara. Her voice got softer. “I have seen courage and kindness on both sides of this war. Sometimes things are not as simple as they seem.” “有人受伤时，你就要伸手帮他，无论他是谁。”克拉拉说。 她的声音变得更柔和。 “我在这场战争的两边都见过勇气和善良。 “有时候事情并不像看上去那么简单。”

Jack was glad they had stopped to help the soldier. 杰克很高兴他们停下来去帮助了那个士兵。

He jumped out of the wagon. 他跳下马车。

“Do you need a helping hand?” he asked the young man. “需要帮忙吗？”他问那年轻人。

“Thank you,” the soldier said softly. “谢谢，”士兵轻声说。

Jack helped him into the back of the wagon. The soldier lay down on a pile of blankets and closed his eyes. 杰克扶他上了马车后面。 那个士兵在一堆毯子上躺下，闭上了眼睛。

Jack climbed back onto the seat beside Clara. She snapped the reins, and they rode on. 杰克爬回驾驶座，坐在克拉拉旁边。 她猛地拉紧缰绳。

They came across more ragged men resting in the shade of an oak tree. These soldiers all wore blue uniforms. 他们又碰到更多衣衫褴褛的人，在橡树荫下休息。 这些士兵都穿着蓝色制服。

Again, Clara stopped the horses. 克拉拉又停下马。

“See if any of those men need a ride to the hospital,” she said to Jack and Annie. “看看那些人有没有谁需要搭车去医院。”她对杰克和安妮说。

Jack glanced at the soldier sleeping in the back of the wagon. 杰克瞥了一眼睡在马车后面的士兵。

“Can a Confederate and a Union soldier be together?” he asked worriedly. “南方士兵和北方士兵能在一起吗？”他担心地问。

Clara nodded. 克拉拉点点头。

“Sometimes men are simply too sick and tired to be enemies anymore,” she said. “Sometimes they even know each other. Many families and friendships have been torn apart by this war.” “有时候人实在病得太重、太累，再也当不成敌人了。”她说。 “有时候他们甚至还互相认识。 许多家庭和友谊都被这场战争拆散了。”

“Let’s go,” said Annie, hopping out of the wagon. Jack followed her. “走吧，”安妮说着跳下马车。 杰克跟在她后面。

They carried their canteens to the men under the oak tree. 他们提着水壶走向橡树下的人。

“Hi!” said Annie. “Does anyone need to go to the hospital?” “嗨！”安妮说。 “有人需要去医院吗？”

“Only John, our drummer boy,” a soldier said. “He’s suffering from heat stroke. But we all need some water, miss.” “只有约翰，我们的小鼓手。”一个士兵说。 “他中暑了。 不过我们都需要点水，小姐。”

Jack saw a young boy lying on the ground. His eyes were closed. 杰克看见一个小男孩躺在地上。 他的眼睛闭着。

“Oh, Jack!” whispered Annie. “He looks just like you.” “哦，杰克！”安妮低声说。 “他看起来真像你。”

The boy did look a lot like Jack—just a few years older. 那男孩看上去的确很像杰克——只是大几岁。

“We better get him to Clara’s ambulance right away,” Annie said. “我们最好马上把他送到克拉拉的救护车上。”安妮说。

She handed her canteen to one of the tired soldiers. Another soldier lifted the drummer boy to his feet. 她把水壶递给一个疲惫的士兵。 另一个士兵把小鼓手扶了起来。

The boy opened his eyes and mumbled a few words. He tried to walk, but he swayed as if he were about to faint. 男孩睁开眼睛，含糊地说了几句话。 他试着走路，可身子摇晃着，好像就要晕倒。

“Wait—” Jack grabbed the boy. “We’ll give you a helping hand,” he said. “等等——”杰克一把抓住了男孩。 “我们来帮你，”他说。

The drummer boy put his arms around Jack’s and Annie’s shoulders. 小鼓手把胳膊搭在杰克和安妮的肩膀上。

“Just a little further, John,” Annie said. “You’re doing great. Just a little further … ” “再走一点点，约翰，”安妮说。 “你做得很好。 再一点点……”

The drummer boy moved as if he were walking in his sleep. His head hung down. His feet shuffled in the dust. 鼓手男孩走得像在梦游。 他的头垂了下来。 他的脚在尘土里拖着走。

“Take good care of him!” one of the men called. “We can’t do without him!” “好好照顾他！”一个男人喊道。 “我们离不开他！”
