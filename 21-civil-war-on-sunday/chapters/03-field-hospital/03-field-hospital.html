3. Field Hospital 3. 野战医院

Jack and Annie walked with the soldiers into the camp. 杰克和安妮跟着士兵们走进营地。

Outside one tent was a long line of men in torn blue uniforms. They looked tired and hurt. Many were bloody and barely able to stand. 一顶帐篷外排着一长队穿着破烂蓝军装的人。 他们看起来又累又受伤。 许多人浑身是血，几乎站不住。

Women wearing dark dresses were giving out food and water to the men in line. 穿着深色裙子的女人们正在给排队的人分发食物和水。

“Where are we?” asked Annie. “我们在哪儿？”安妮问。

“I’ll find out,” said Jack. “我会查清楚的，”杰克说。

He pulled out their book and found a picture of the campsite. He read: 他拿出他们的书，找到一张营地的图画。

During the Civil War, field hospitals were set up quickly near battlefields to treat wounded soldiers. Soldiers stayed in the field hospitals for a short time before going back to fight, or moving to a larger hospital, or being sent home. This field hospital in Virginia took care of over 400 patients. 在内战期间，野战医院很快就在战场附近设立，用来治疗受伤的士兵。士兵们在野战医院待一段时间后，会回去继续战斗，或者被送到更大的医院，或者被送回家。这个位于弗吉尼亚的野战医院照顾了超过400名病人。

“That’s a lot,” said Annie. “好多啊，”安妮说。

“It sure is,” said Jack. “确实如此。”杰克说。

He pulled out his notebook and wrote:



 他拿出笔记本写道：

图中文字：field hospital-set up near battlefield 图中文字：野战医院——设在战场附近

Jack read aloud again from the book: 杰克又从书里大声读道：

More than 3,000 women helped out as nurses during the Civil War. Nursing was a new job for women in America. Before the war, only men had been nurses. 超过3000名女性在内战期间担任护士。护理工作对美国女性来说是一个新职业。战争之前，只有男性担任护士。

“Wow,” said Annie. “Maybe we can be nurses.” “哇。”安妮说。 “也许我们可以当护士。”

“Forget it,” said Jack. “Kids can’t be nurses.” “别想了，”杰克说。 “小孩不能当护士。”

Jack wanted to find the special writing for Morgan and go home. The sad scene at the field hospital was making him feel sick. 杰克想给摩根找到特别的文字，然后回家。 战地医院里悲惨的情景让他感到难受。

“I’ll just ask,” said Annie. “我去问问就好，”安妮说。

She headed over to a nurse cooking over a campfire. 她朝一个在篝火上做饭的护士走去。

“Annie! We’ve got a mission!” Jack called. “安妮！ 我们有任务！”杰克喊道。

But Annie kept going. 可安妮还是往下爬。

Jack heaved a sigh. He tucked his notebook and the Civil War book under his arm and followed her. 杰克叹了一口气。 他把笔记本和内战书夹在胳膊下，跟着她。

Waves of heat rose from a small cooking fire. The young nurse was heating a pot of coffee. 热浪从小小的炊火上升起。 那位年轻护士正在加热一壶咖啡。

Flies buzzed everywhere. 到处都是苍蝇在嗡嗡飞。

“Hello,” said Annie. “你好，”安妮说。

The nurse barely smiled at them. 护士几乎没朝他们笑。

Her face was red and beaded with sweat. Her eyes looked terribly tired. 她的脸红红的，布满了小汗珠。 她的眼睛看起来非常疲惫。

“Where are you from?” she asked. “你们从哪儿来？”她问。

“Frog Creek,” said Annie. “We’d like to volunteer as nurses.” “蛙溪镇，”安妮说。 “我们想自愿当护士。”

The young woman didn’t seem at all surprised. 年轻女人一点也不惊讶。

“We could certainly use some help,” she said, sighing. “Some of us have not slept for days.” “我们确实需要帮手。”她叹着气说。 “我们有些人好几天没睡了。”

“Why not?” asked Jack. “为什么？”杰克问。

“The wounded are coming here from a battle near Richmond,” said the nurse. “More and more keep coming. It never seems to end.” “伤员正从里士满附近的一场战役往这儿送。”护士说。 “伤员越来越多。 好像永远没完。”

“Just tell us what to do,” said Annie. “告诉我们该做什么就行，”安妮说。

“While we feed the new patients, you can go to the first two tents,” the young nurse said, “and give the other soldiers their noon meal.” “我们给新来的病人吃饭时，你们可以去前两顶帐篷，”年轻护士说，“把午饭送给其他士兵。”

She pointed to a basket filled with bread and potatoes. Next to it was a ladle and a bucket filled with water. 她指着一只装满面包和土豆的篮子。 旁边是一把大汤勺和一桶水。

“Anything else?” Annie asked. “还有别的吗？”安妮问。

“Just try to give them comfort,” the nurse said. “只要尽量给他们安慰就行。”护士说。

“How do we do that?” said Annie. “那怎么利用？”安妮说。

“I don’t have time to show you,” the nurse said. “But here’s a list of things that can help.” “我没时间教你们，”护士说。 “不过这儿有一份能帮忙的清单。”

She pulled a piece of paper from her apron pocket and handed it to Annie. 她从围裙口袋里抽出一张纸，递给安妮。

Annie read the list to Jack: 安妮把清单读给杰克听：

Be Cheerful.
Lessen sorrow and give hope.
Be brave.
Put aside your own feelings.
Don’t give up.

“Follow that list,” said the nurse, “and you can’t go wrong.” “照那张清单做，”护士说，“就不会出错。”

The nurse took the pot of coffee from the fire and carried it to the line of men. 护士从火上端起咖啡壶，拿到士兵队列旁。

“Follow … ” said Jack. “Follow that list … ” “跟着……”杰克说。 “照那张清单做，”护士说，“就不会出错。”

“That’s what she said,” said Annie. “她就是那么说的，”安妮说。

Jack took the list from her. 杰克从她手里接过清单。

“Don’t you get it?” he said. “This is it! We found it! The special writing for Morgan’s library! Something to follow.” “你还不明白吗？”他说。 “就是这个！我们找到了！ 给摩根图书馆的特别文字！ 可以遵循的东西

“Yes!” said Annie. “对！”安妮说。

Jack put the paper into his knapsack. 杰克把纸放进背包。

“It was handed right to us!” he said, smiling. “We can go home now!” “它正好交到了我们手里！”他笑着说。 “我们现在可以回家了！”

“Oh, no! Not now!” said Annie. “We have to help as nurses first.” “哦，不！ 现在不行！”安妮说。 “我们得先当护士帮忙。”

“But, Annie—” said Jack. “可是，安妮——”杰克说。

She picked up the food basket. Then she started toward the row of white tents. 她拿起食物篮子。 然后朝那排白色帐篷走去。

“Wait—we’re supposed to leave,” Jack said weakly. “Our mission is over.” “等等——我们本该离开的。”杰克有气无力地说。 “我们的任务结束了。”

The truth was that he didn’t want to help. He didn’t want to be around wounded and suffering soldiers. It was too sad. 事实是，他并不想帮忙。 他不想待在受伤和受苦的士兵身边。太让人难过了。

“Bring the water bucket and the ladle!” Annie shouted. Then she disappeared inside the first tent. “把水桶和长柄勺带来！”安妮喊道。 然后她消失在第一个帐篷里。

Jack groaned. He knew he couldn’t change her mind. 杰克哼了一声。 他知道自己没法改变她的想法。

He pulled out the list and read the first line: Be Cheerful. 他拿出那张清单，读了第一行：要开朗。

“Oh, brother,” he said. “真受不了，”他说。

Jack put the list back into his knapsack. He picked up the heavy bucket. Hurrying clumsily after Annie, he tried to smile. 杰克把清单放回背包。 他提起沉重的水桶。 他笨拙地赶在安妮后面，努力挤出笑容。
