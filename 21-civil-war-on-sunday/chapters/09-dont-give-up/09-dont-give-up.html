9. Don’t Give Up 不要放弃。

“Annie?” said Jack. “安妮？”杰克说。

“Over here!” she called. “在这儿！”她喊道。

Jack saw two figures standing in the twilight. 杰克看见暮色里站着两个人影。

He walked over to Annie and Clara. They all stared in the direction of the distant battlefield. 他走向安妮和克拉拉。 他们都朝远处战场的方向望去。

Bright lights flashed against the dark blue horizon—cannon shells exploding. 明亮的光在深蓝色的地平线上闪烁——那是炮弹在爆炸。

“Every time you see a flash, you know a shell may have taken a life or many lives,” said Clara. “每当你看见一道闪光，就知道一发炮弹可能夺走了一条或许多条生命。”克拉拉说。

“That’s awful,” said Annie. “太可怕了，”安妮说。

“Yes, it is,” said Clara. “A whole world can vanish in that flash—all a young man’s joys and sorrows, all his memories.” “是啊，”克拉拉说。 “整个世界都可能在那闪光里消失——一个年轻人所有的喜怒哀乐，他所有的记忆。”

“This is a cruel war!” said Annie. “这是一场残酷的战争！”安妮说。

“All wars are cruel,” said Clara Barton. “People feel they must fight for causes they believe in. But they soon discover that war is not about glory and fame. It’s about misery and terrible pain and sadness.” “所有战争都很残酷，”克拉拉·巴顿说。 “人们觉得必须为自己所信仰的事业而战。 可他们很快发现，战争无关荣耀和名声。 它关乎苦难、可怕的疼痛和悲伤。”

“It makes me miss my mom and dad,” said Annie. “It makes me really miss them.” “这让我想念爸爸妈妈，”安妮说。 “让我真的好想他们。”

Annie sounded tired and homesick. Finally, she had lost her cheerfulness. 安妮听上去又累又想家。 终于，她那股快活劲儿没了。

“I think it’s time for you two to go home,” said Clara. “我想你们俩该回家了。”克拉拉说。

Jack thought of all the wounded men who needed food and water and kindness and comfort. 杰克想到所有需要食物、水、善良和安慰的伤员。

“No!” Jack said. “We have to stay. We can’t give up. That’s on the list—Don’t give up.” “不！”杰克说。 “我们得留下来。我们不能放弃。 不要放弃。

Jack pulled out their list to show Clara Barton. 杰克抽出清单给克拉拉·巴顿看。

“Oh, yes,” she said, nodding. “I see one of my nurses has written down the things I often say. Let me add one thing more—Do not forget the ones who love you.” “哦，是的，”她点着头说。 “我明白了，我的一个护士把我常说的话写了下来。 让我再加一条——不要忘记爱你们的人。”

Jack heaved a big sigh. He was homesick, too. 杰克深深地叹了一口气。 他也想家了。

“Can we keep the list?” he asked. “这张清单我们可以留下吗？”他问。

“Of course,” said Clara. “You don’t have to work in a hospital to follow my words. They work in all of life no matter where you go.” “当然，”克拉拉说。 “你们不必在医院工作，也能照着我的话去做。 它们在生活的所有地方都有效，无论你走到哪里。”

“Thanks,” said Jack. “谢谢，”杰克说。

“My thanks to both of you,” said Clara Barton. “You were great helpers.” “谢谢你们俩，”克拉拉·巴顿说。 “你们是很棒的帮手。”

“You were a great teacher,” said Annie. “您是很棒的老师，”安妮说。

“Good-bye,” said Clara Barton. “Be very careful going home.” “再见，”克拉拉·巴顿说。 “回家的路上一定要小心。”

“We will,” said Jack and Annie. “Bye.” “我们会的，”杰克和安妮说。 “再见。”

The sun was setting as they walked out of the camp. The boom of cannons sounded in the distance. 他们走出营地时，太阳正在落下。 远处传来隆隆的炮声。

Soldiers sang a song around a campfire.



 士兵们围着营火唱歌。

We’re tenting tonight 今夜我们搭帐篷

on the old camp ground; 在旧营地；

Give us a song to cheer 给我们一首歌来鼓劲

Our weary hearts, 安慰我们疲惫的心。

a song of home, 一首关于家的歌。

And friends we love so dear.



 还有我们深爱的朋友。

Jack and Annie walked through the darkening field. By the time they reached the woods, the stars were out. 杰克和安妮走过渐渐变暗的田野。 等他们走到树林时，星星已经出来了。

They climbed up the rope ladder to the tree house. Annie grabbed the Pennsylvania book. 他们爬上绳梯，进了树屋。 安妮抓起宾夕法尼亚的书。

“Wait,” said Jack. “等等，”杰克说。

He looked out the window. He couldn’t see anything below. But the soldiers’ song still carried through the warm, starry night.



 他向窗外望去。 下面什么也看不见。 但士兵们的歌声仍然穿过温暖、繁星点点的夜空。

Many are the hearts 有多少心灵

that are weary tonight, 今夜疲惫不堪。

Wishing for the war to cease …



盼望战争停止……

As Jack listened, he thought of Clara Barton, the elderly slave, the young Confederate soldier, and John, the drummer boy … 杰克听着歌，想起了克拉拉·巴顿、那位年老的奴隶、年轻的邦联军士兵，还有小鼓手约翰……

“War is not a game,” Jack said softly. “It is definitely not a game.”



 “战争不是游戏，”杰克轻声说。 “那绝对不是一场游戏。”

Many are the hearts 有多少心灵

looking for the right 在寻找正义

To see the dawn of peace.



 去迎接和平的黎明。

The song ended. The cannon fire stopped. The night was quiet, except for the croaking of frogs. 歌结束了。 炮声停了。 夜很安静，只有蛙叫声。

“Ready?” Annie whispered. “准备好了吗？”安妮低声问。

“Ready,” said Jack. “准备好了，”杰克说。

“I really wish we could go home,” Annie said, pointing to the picture of the Frog Creek woods. “我真希望我们能回家，”安妮说着，指着蛙溪树林的那幅画。

The wind started to blow. 风开始刮起来。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 绝对静止。
