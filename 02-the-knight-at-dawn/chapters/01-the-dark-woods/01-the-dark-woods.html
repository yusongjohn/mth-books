1. The Dark Woods 1. 黑暗的树林

Jack couldn’t sleep. 杰克睡不着。

He put his glasses on. He looked at the clock. 5:30. 他把眼镜戴上。 他看了看钟。五点半。

Too early to get up. 起床还太早。

Yesterday so many strange things had happened. Now he was trying to figure them out. 昨天发生了好多奇怪的事。 现在他正想把这些事弄明白。

He turned on the light. He picked up his notebook. He looked at the list he’d made before going to bed. 他打开灯。 拿起笔记本。 看了看睡觉前写的那张清单。

图中文字：found tree house in woods

found lots of books in it

Pointed to Pteranodon picture in book

made a wish

went to time of dinosaurs

Pointed to picture of Frog Creek woods

made a wish

Came home to Frog Creek. 图中文字：在树林里发现了一个树屋，里面有很多书。指着书里的翼龙图片许了个愿，就到了恐龙时代。又指着弗罗格克里克树林的图片许了个愿，就回来了。

Jack pushed his glasses into place. Who was going to believe any of this? 杰克把眼镜往上推了推。 谁会相信这些事啊？

Not his mom. Or his dad. Or his third-grade teacher, Ms. Watkins. Only his seven-year-old sister, Annie. She’d gone with him to the time of the dinosaurs. 妈妈不会。爸爸也不会。 三年级的沃特金斯老师也不会。 只有他七岁的妹妹安妮。 她跟他一起去过恐龙时代。

“Can’t you sleep?” “你睡不着吗？”

It was Annie, standing in his doorway. 是安妮，站在他的门口。

“Nope,” said Jack. “睡不着。”杰克说。

“Me neither,” said Annie. “What are you doing?” “我也是。”安妮说。 “你在干什么？”

She walked over to Jack and looked at his notebook. She read the list. 她走到杰克身边，看着他的笔记本。 她把清单读了一遍。

“Aren’t you going to write about the gold medal?” she asked. “你不打算把金牌的事写上去吗？”她问。

“You mean the gold medallion,” said Jack. “你是说金勋章。”杰克说。

He picked up his pencil and wrote: 他拿起铅笔，写道：

图中文字：found this in dinosaur time 图中文字：在恐龙时代发现了这个

“Aren’t you going to put the letter M on the medal?” said Annie. “你不打算在金牌上写个字母M吗？”安妮说。

“Medallion,” said Jack. “Not medal.” “是勋章。”杰克说。 “不是金牌。”

He added an M: 他又加上一个M：

图中文字：M 图中文字：M

“Aren’t you going to write about the magic person?” said Annie. “你不打算写写那个有魔法的人吗？”安妮说。

“We don’t know for sure if there is a magic person,” said Jack. “我们还不能确定真的有个有魔法的人。”杰克说。

“Well, someone built the tree house in the woods. Someone put the books in it. Someone lost a gold medal in dinosaur time.” “可是，树林里的树屋是有人建的。 里面的书也是有人放进去的。 还有人在恐龙时代弄丢了一块金牌。”

“Medallion!” said Jack for the third time. “And I’m just writing the facts. The stuff we know for sure.” “是勋章！”杰克第三次纠正。 “而且我只写事实。 只写我们确定知道的事。”

“Let’s go back to the tree house right now,” said Annie. “And find out if the magic person is a fact.” “我们现在就回树屋吧。”安妮说。 “看看那个有魔法的人是不是事实。”

“Are you nuts?” said Jack. “The sun’s not even up yet.” “你疯了吗？”杰克说。 “太阳都还没出来呢。”

“Come on,” said Annie. “Maybe we can catch them sleeping.” “走嘛。”安妮说。 “说不定能趁他们睡觉时抓住他们。”

“I don’t think we should,” said Jack. He was worried. What if the “magic person” was mean? What if he or she didn’t want kids to know about the tree house? “我觉得不该去。”杰克说。他有点担心。 要是那个“有魔法的人”很凶怎么办？ 要是那人不想让小孩知道树屋的事怎么办？

“Well, I’m going,” said Annie. “那我自己去。”安妮说。

Jack looked out his window at the dark-gray sky. It was almost dawn. 杰克望向窗外，天是深灰色的。 快天亮了。

He sighed. “Okay. Let’s get dressed. I’ll meet you at the back door. Be quiet.” 他叹了口气。 “好吧。 我们穿衣服。 后门见。小声点。”

“Yay!” whispered Annie. She tiptoed away as quietly as a mouse. “耶！”安妮小声说。 她像小老鼠一样，踮着脚尖走了。

Jack put on jeans, a warm sweatshirt, and sneakers. He tossed his notebook and pencil in his backpack. 杰克穿上牛仔裤、暖和的卫衣和运动鞋。 他把笔记本和铅笔扔进背包。

He crept downstairs. 接着蹑手蹑脚地下了楼。

Annie was waiting by the back door. She shined a flashlight in Jack’s face. “Ta-da! A magic wand!” she said. 安妮已经在后门等着了。 她把手电筒照到杰克脸上。 “嗒哒！ 魔法棒！”她说。

“Shhh! Don’t wake up Mom and Dad,” whispered Jack. “And turn that flashlight off. We don’t want anyone to see us.” “嘘！ 别吵醒爸爸妈妈。”杰克小声说。 “还有，把手电筒关掉。 别让人看见我们。”

Annie nodded and turned it off. Then she clipped it onto her belt. 安妮点点头，把手电筒关了。 然后把它夹在腰带上。

They slipped out the door into the cool early-morning air. Crickets were chirping. The dog next door barked. 他们溜出门，走进清凉的清晨空气里。 蟋蟀在叫。 隔壁的狗叫了起来。

“Quiet, Henry!” whispered Annie. “安静，亨利！”安妮小声说。

Henry stopped barking. Animals always seemed to do what Annie said. 亨利不叫了。 动物好像总听安妮的话。

“Let’s run!” said Jack. “快跑！”杰克说。

They dashed across the dark, wet lawn and didn’t stop until they reached the woods. 他们冲过又黑又湿的草坪，一直跑到树林边才停下来。

“We need the flashlight now,” said Jack. “现在得用手电了。”杰克说。

Annie took it off her belt and switched it on. 安妮从腰带上取下手电筒，打开。

Step by step, she and Jack walked between the trees. Jack held his breath. The dark woods were scary. 她和杰克一步一步，走在树中间。 杰克屏住呼吸。 黑树林好吓人。

“Gotcha!” said Annie, shining the flashlight in Jack’s face. “抓到你了！”安妮说着，把手电筒照到杰克脸上。

Jack jumped back. Then he frowned. 杰克往后一跳，然后皱起眉头。

“Cut it out!” he said. “别闹了！”他说。

“I scared you,” said Annie. “我吓到你了。”安妮说。

Jack glared at her. 杰克瞪着她。

“Stop pretending!” he whispered. “This is serious.” “别装了！”他小声说。 “这可是正经事。”

“Okay, okay.” “好好好。”

Annie shined her flashlight over the tops of the trees. 安妮把手电筒照向树梢。

“Now what are you doing?” said Jack. “你现在又在干什么？”杰克说。

“Looking for the tree house!” “找树屋啊！”

The light stopped moving. 光停住了。

There it was. The mysterious tree house. At the top of the tallest tree in the woods. 就在那儿。 那座神秘的树屋。 在树林里最高的那棵树上。

Annie shined her light at the tree house, and then down the tall ladder. All the way to the ground. 安妮先把手电照向树屋，再照向那架高高的梯子。 一直照到地面。

“I’m going up,” she said. She gripped the flashlight and began to climb. “我上去了。”她说。 她握紧手电筒，开始往上爬。

“Wait!” Jack called. “等一下！”杰克喊。

What if someone was in the tree house? 要是树屋里有人怎么办？

“Annie! Come back!” “安妮！回来！”

But she was gone. The light disappeared. Jack was alone in the dark. 可她已经走了。 灯光也消失了。 杰克一个人待在黑暗里。
