9. The Wonderful Room 9. 奇妙的房间

The songs of early-morning birds filled the woods. 清晨的鸟叫声充满了树林。

Jack opened his eyes and sighed. 杰克睁开眼睛，叹了口气。

They were back in Frog Creek. He could breathe again. His eyes didn’t sting anymore. He was wearing his own clothes, even his sneakers. 他们回到了蛙溪。 他又可以呼吸了。 眼睛不再刺痛了。 他穿着自己的衣服，连运动鞋也回来了。

“I wonder what happened to everyone?” Annie asked anxiously. “Andrew, Peter, and their aunt, and Betty and Fred, and all the other people.” “不知道大家怎么样了？”安妮焦急地问。 “安德鲁、彼得和他们的姨妈，还有贝蒂和弗雷德，还有其他所有人。”

Jack pulled out their research book. He turned to the last chapter. He read aloud: 杰克掏出研究书。 他翻到最后一章。他大声读道：

After the earthquake fires were put out, people from all over the world sent help to San Francisco. The brave citizens of the city never gave up hope. Many even wore badges that said, “Let’s rebuild at once.” In less than ten years, San Francisco was once again one of the loveliest cities in the United States. 地震之后，火灾被扑灭了，世界各地的人们纷纷向旧金山伸出援手。这个城市的勇敢市民从不放弃希望，很多人甚至佩戴着写着“立刻重建”的徽章。不到十年，旧金山再次成为美国最美丽的城市之一。

“Oh, good,” breathed Annie. “Hey, do you have our sign?” “哦，太好了，”安妮松了口气。 “嘿，你带着我们的牌子吗？”

Jack reached again into his pack. He pulled out the sign from Peter and Andrew. 杰克又伸手进背包。 他掏出彼得和安德鲁的牌子。

He placed it on the floor, next to the list from the Civil War, the letter from the Revolutionary War, and the slate from the pioneer schoolhouse. 他把它放在地板上，旁边是南北战争的名单、独立战争的信，还有拓荒者校舍的石板。

“We have all four writings now,” he said. “我们现在有了全部四种文字，”他说。

“So what happens next?” said Annie. “接下来会怎样？”安妮说。

Suddenly, there was a roar. A bright light flashed through the tree house. 忽然，传来一声轰鸣。 一道亮光闪过树屋。

Jack covered his face. When he peeked over the tops of his fingers, he saw Morgan le Fay. 杰克捂住脸。 他从手指缝里偷看时，看见了摩根·勒菲。

“Morgan!” Jack and Annie cried joyfully. “摩根！”杰克和安妮高兴地喊道。

They both hugged her. 他们俩都拥抱了她。

Morgan hugged them back. 摩根也拥抱了他们。

“We found the four special writings for your library!” said Jack. “我们为你的图书馆找到了四种特别文字！”杰克说。

Annie picked up the list and the letter. Jack picked up the slate and the sign. 安妮拿起名单和信。 杰克拿起石板和牌子。

“Here they are!” he said. “都在这儿！”他说。

They started to give everything to Morgan. But she held up her hand. 他们开始把东西交给摩根。 可她举起了手。

“Do not give them to me,” she said. “Someone else needs them more.” “别给我，”她说。 “有人更需要它们。”

Suddenly, a blinding light flashed through the tree house again. There came a great roar, then silence. 忽然，又一道刺眼的光闪过树屋。 传来一声巨大的轰鸣，然后一片寂静。

When Jack and Annie opened their eyes, they were no longer in the magic tree house. 杰克和安妮睁开眼睛时，他们已不在魔法树屋里了。

They were standing in a huge, shadowy room. The room smelled wonderful—like leather, books, and a wood-burning fire. 他们站在一间巨大、幽暗的房间里。 房间闻起来很棒——有皮革、书和烧木头的壁炉的味道。

Flames crackled in a huge stone hearth. Along the walls were rows and rows of tall bookcases filled with books. 火焰在巨大的石壁炉里噼啪作响。 沿墙是一排排高大的书架，装满了书。

“Welcome to my library,” Morgan said softly. “欢迎来到我的图书馆，”摩根轻声说。

“Wow,” whispered Jack. “哇，”杰克小声说。

“Someone here is waiting to meet you,” said Morgan. “I told him that two special messengers would soon arrive.” “有人在这儿等着见你们，”摩根说。 “我告诉过他，两位特别的信使很快就会到来。”

“Where is he?” asked Annie. “他在哪儿？”安妮问。

Morgan pointed to a man dressed in dark blue in a corner of the library. He sat in a chair. His head was bowed. His hair was black streaked with silver. 摩根指着图书馆角落里一个穿着深蓝色衣服的男人。 他坐在椅子上。 他低着头。 他的头发是黑色的，夹着银丝。

“He looks tired,” Annie whispered. “他看起来很累，”安妮小声说。

“Yes, he and his knights have been defeated,” Morgan said quietly. “He has given up all hope for his kingdom.” “是的，他和他的骑士们战败了，”摩根轻声说。 “他对自己的王国放弃了所有希望。”

“How can we help him?” asked Annie. “我们怎么帮他？”安妮问。

Morgan looked down at the special writings that Jack and Annie held. 摩根低头看着杰克和安妮拿着的特别文字。

“Would it help if we showed him these?” Jack asked. “如果我们把这些给他看，会有帮助吗？”杰克问。

Morgan smiled. 摩根笑了。

“Come on, let’s show him!” said Annie. “来，我们给他看！”安妮说。

They crossed the room and knelt before the tired-looking man. 他们穿过房间，跪在那个看起来疲惫的人面前。

“Excuse me,” Annie said. “对不起打扰了，”安妮说。

The man looked up. He had sad gray eyes. 那人抬起头。 他有一双悲伤的灰眼睛。

“We’re Morgan’s messengers. We’ve come to help,” said Jack. “我们是摩根的信使。 我们是来帮忙的，”杰克说。

The man shook his head. 那人摇了摇头。

“I don’t understand,” he said in a deep, tired voice. “You are children. How can you help?” “我不明白，”他用低沉疲惫的声音说。 “你们是孩子。 你们怎么帮忙？”

“Sometimes children can help a lot,” said Annie. “Jack and I have helped a general, and we helped wounded soldiers.” “有时候孩子能帮很多忙，”安妮说。 “杰克和我帮助过一位将军，还帮助过受伤的士兵。”

“We helped people caught in an earthquake and a twister,” Jack added. “我们帮助过困在地震和龙卷风里的人，”杰克补充道。

The man straightened up a little. “That is brave,” he said. 那人稍稍挺直了一些。 “那很勇敢，”他说。

“We want to share how we did it,” said Annie. She held up the list from the Civil War. “我们想分享我们是怎么做到的，”安妮说。 她举起南北战争的名单。

“This tells you how to help wounded people,” she said. She held up the letter from the Revolutionary War. “这告诉你怎样帮助受伤的人，”她说。 她举起独立战争的信。

“And this tells you that even when things look their worst, you shouldn’t give up,” she said. “这告诉你，即使事情看起来最糟，也不该放弃，”她说。

Jack held up the slate from the pioneer schoolhouse. 杰克举起拓荒者校舍的石板。

“This says, ‘If at first you don’t succeed, try, try again,’” he said. Then Jack held up the piece of wood from the San Francisco earthquake. “这上面写着：‘如果一开始不成功，就再试，再试，’”他说。 然后杰克举起旧金山地震的那块木头。

“And this says that when you’ve lost everything, you can still have hope,” he said. “这上面写着，即使失去了一切，你仍可以抱有希望，”他说。

The man studied the four special writings. Then he looked at Jack and Annie with piercing gray eyes. 那人仔细看着这四份特别文字。 然后他用锐利的灰眼睛看着杰克和安妮。

“You were both very kind to find all these things and bring them to me,” he said. “Are you magic?” “你们找到这些东西带给我，真是非常好心，”他说。 “你们会魔法吗？”

“Oh no,” said Annie. “Morgan is magic. We’re just ordinary kids.” “哦不，”安妮说。 “摩根会魔法。 我们只是普通的孩子。”

The man smiled. He seemed less sad and weary now. 那人笑了。 他现在看起来不那么悲伤疲惫了。

“And I am just an ordinary king,” he said softly. “I suppose if two ordinary kids can find courage and hope, then an ordinary king can find it, too.” “而我也只是一位普通的国王，”他轻声说。 “我想，如果两个普通孩子能找到勇气和希望，那么一位普通国王也能找到。”

The man slowly stood up. 那人慢慢站起来。

“I will go now and speak to my knights,” he said. “I will share the wisdom you have brought me.” “我现在要去和我的骑士们说话，”他说。 “我要分享你们带给我的智慧。”

He wrapped his cape around him. He bowed to Jack and Annie. 他把斗篷裹在身上。 他向杰克和安妮鞠躬。

“Thank you,” he said. Then he strode bravely out of Morgan’s library. “谢谢你们，”他说。 然后他勇敢地大步走出摩根的图书馆。

“Yes, thank you,” said Morgan, walking over to Jack and Annie. “是的，谢谢你们，”摩根走过来说。

“You’re welcome,” they said. “不客气，”他们说。

“These are for your library,” said Jack. He and Annie gave Morgan the four special writings. “这些是给您的图书馆的，”杰克说。 他和安妮把四份特别文字交给摩根。

Morgan smiled. 摩根笑了。

“Their wisdom will help many readers who come here,” she said. “它们的智慧会帮助许多来到这里的读者，”她说。

“Great,” said Annie. “太好了，”安妮说。

“It’s time for you to go home now,” said Morgan. “现在你们该回家了，”摩根说。

Jack looked around Morgan’s library. He hated to leave. It was the most wonderful room he’d ever seen. 杰克环顾摩根的图书馆。 他不愿离开。 这是他见过的最奇妙的房间。

“Don’t worry. You can come back,” said Morgan, reading his mind. “You must come back, for you both have helped save Camelot. Good-bye for now.” “别担心。 你们可以回来，”摩根说，像是读懂了他的心思。 “你们必须回来，因为你们俩帮助拯救了卡米洛特。 暂时再见了。”

Before Jack or Annie could speak, there was another blinding flash. 杰克或安妮还没来得及说话，又一道刺眼的闪光。

Then they were home again, in the tree house, in Frog Creek, in the early morning. 然后他们又回家了，在树屋里，在蛙溪，在清晨。
