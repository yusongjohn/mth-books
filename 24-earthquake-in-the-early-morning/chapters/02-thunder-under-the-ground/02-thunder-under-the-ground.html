2. Thunder Under the Ground 2. 地下的雷声

“Nice clothes,” said Annie. “衣服不错，”安妮说。

Jack opened his eyes. 杰克睁开了眼睛。

Annie was wearing a blue-and-white dress with a big sailor collar and white stockings. 安妮穿着蓝白相间的裙子，带着大大的水手领和白色长袜。

Jack was wearing brown knee-length pants, a jacket, a cap, and a tie. His backpack had become a leather bag. He and Annie both wore short lace-up boots. 杰克穿着棕色过膝短裤、外套、帽子和领带。 他的背包变成了皮袋子。 他和安妮都穿着短筒系带靴子。

A church bell started to ring. 教堂的钟开始敲响。

Gong. Gong. Gong. Gong. Gong. 当。当。 当。当。 当。

“It rang five times,” said Jack. “It must be five in the morning.” “敲了五下，”杰克说。 “一定是早上五点。”

“Yeah,” said Annie. She was looking out the window. “对，”安妮说。 她正望着窗外。

Jack looked with her. The early-morning air felt fresh and cool. 杰克和她一起看。 清晨的空气清新凉爽。

The tree house had landed in a tree at the bottom of a hill. Painted wooden houses and gaslights lined a quiet cobblestone street. A trolley car moved along tracks up the street. It rounded the top of the hill and disappeared. 树屋落在山脚的一棵树上。 油漆木屋和煤气灯排列在一条安静的鹅卵石街上。 一辆有轨电车沿着轨道往山上开。 它绕过山顶消失了。

Tall buildings towered farther off. The sun was behind them, making pink streaks in the blue sky. 更远处耸立着高楼。 太阳在他们身后，在蓝天上画出粉色的光带。

“It’s really pretty here,” said Annie. “这儿真漂亮，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. “是啊，”杰克说。

He opened the research book and read: 他打开研究书读道：

On Wednesday, April 18, 1906, San Francisco was the biggest city on the west coast of the United States. It had a population of half a million people. It was also one of the loveliest cities in the country. 1906年4月18日星期三，旧金山是美国西海岸最大的城市。那里有五十万人口，也是全国最美丽的城市之一。

Jack pulled out his notebook. He wrote:



 杰克掏出笔记本写道：

图中文字：San Francisco—April 18, 1906

biggest city in the West

lovely 图中文字：旧金山—1906年4月18日 西部最大的城市 美丽

“Let’s go!” Annie said impatiently. “我们走吧！”安妮不耐烦地说。

Jack looked back at the book. He wanted to learn more. 杰克又低头看书。 他还想多了解一些。

“Now,” said Annie. She took the book and notebook away from him and put them into his leather bag. “Let’s not waste any more time.” “现在就走，”安妮说。 她把书和笔记本从他手里拿走，放进他的皮袋。 “别再浪费时间了。”

Annie left the tree house. 安妮离开了树屋。

“Doing research is not wasting time,” Jack called after her. “做研究可不是浪费时间，”杰克在她身后喊道。

But he slung his bag over his shoulder and followed Annie down the rope ladder. When they had both stepped onto the grass, Jack looked around. 可他还是把袋子甩上肩，跟着安妮下了绳梯。 两人都踩上草地后，杰克环顾四周。

“Where to?” he said. “去哪儿？”他说。

“Anywhere!” said Annie. “Let’s just explore and see the sights. We can be tourists.” “哪儿都行！”安妮说。 “我们就去探险，看看风景。 我们可以当游客。”

“Okay,” said Jack. “But don’t forget we have to find the writing for Morgan’s library.” “好，”杰克说。 “可别忘了我们还得为摩根的图书馆找到文字。”

They started up the cobblestone street. As they walked up the steep hill, the sun rose above the tall buildings. 他们开始沿着鹅卵石街往上走。 他们走上陡峭的山坡时，太阳升到了高楼上方。

The early light turned everything to gold: stones, streetlamps, and the glass windows of the silent houses. 清晨的阳光把一切都染成金色：石头、街灯，还有安静房屋的玻璃窗。

“It’s so quiet and peaceful,” said Annie. “这儿好安静好平和，”安妮说。

“Yeah, everyone must still be sleeping,” said Jack. “是啊，大家一定还在睡觉，”杰克说。

Suddenly, out of the quiet came a deep rumbling noise. 忽然，寂静中传来一阵低沉的隆隆声。

Jack stopped. He grabbed Annie’s arm. 杰克停住了。 他抓住安妮的胳膊。

“What’s that?” he said. “那是什么？”他说。

The noise got louder. It sounded like thunder coming from under the ground. 声音越来越大。 听起来像从地下传来的雷声。

The earth started shaking. 大地开始摇晃。

Church bells clanged wildly. 教堂的钟疯狂地响起来。

The whole street began to move. The cobblestones rolled like waves on the ocean. 整条街开始动了。 鹅卵石像海浪一样翻滚。

“What’s happening?” Annie cried. “怎么回事？”安妮喊道。

Chimneys fell off roofs! 烟囱从屋顶上摔下来！

Gaslights toppled over! 煤气灯倒了！

Bricks crashed to the street! 砖块砸到街上！

“Get down!” shouted Jack. “Cover your head!” “趴下！”杰克喊道。 “护住头！”

Jack and Annie crouched on the ground with their arms over their heads. All around them was rumbling, clanging, crashing, and breaking. 杰克和安妮蹲在地上，胳膊护着头。 他们周围全是隆隆声、叮当声、撞击声和破裂声。

Then the world grew still. The rumbling stopped. 然后世界安静了下来。 隆隆声停了。

Jack and Annie raised their heads. The air was filled with dust. 杰克和安妮抬起头。 空气里满是灰尘。

“It’s over,” said Jack. “结束了，”杰克说。

“That must have been an earthquake!” said Annie. “那一定是地震！”安妮说。

“I think so,” Jack agreed. “我想是的，”杰克同意道。

“I guess I should have let you do a little more research on this place,” Annie said. “我想我该让你对这个地方多做点研究的，”安妮说。

“Yeah, probably,” said Jack. “Except I don’t know exactly what we could have done.” “是啊，大概是，”杰克说。 “只不过我也不知道我们到底能做什么。”

Jack slowly stood up. His legs felt wobbly. As he brushed off his pants, the deep rumbling came again—louder than before. 杰克慢慢站起来。 他的腿发软。 他拍掉裤子上的灰时，低沉的隆隆声又来了——比刚才更响。

Then the terrible shaking started. It was even harder than before. 然后可怕的摇晃又开始了。 比刚才更猛烈。

Jack was hurled to the ground. The earth trembled and quaked. Jack bounced against the hard cobblestones. 杰克被摔到地上。 大地震颤着。 杰克在坚硬的鹅卵石上颠簸。

“Annie!” he cried. “安妮！”他喊道。

He tried to stand, but fell again. Through the dusty air, he saw the tall buildings swaying against the sky! 他想站起来，可又摔倒了。 透过尘土飞扬的空气，他看见高楼在天空中摇晃！

Roofs were caving in! 屋顶塌陷了！

Up and down the street, bricks, glass, and concrete showered down! 整条街上，砖块、玻璃和混凝土如雨点般落下！

It seemed like a long time before the dreadful noise and shaking finally stopped. 似乎过了很久，那可怕的噪音和摇晃才终于停下。
