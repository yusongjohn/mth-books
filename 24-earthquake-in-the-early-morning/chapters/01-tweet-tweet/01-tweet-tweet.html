1. Tweet-tweet 1. 叽叽喳喳

Jack sat up in bed. He stared out his window. 杰克在床上坐了起来。 他盯着窗外看。

The sky was dark gray. The sun would be rising soon. 天空是深灰色的。 太阳很快就要升起了。

“It’s almost time,” he whispered to himself. “快到时间了，”他小声对自己说。

The day before, in the magic tree house, Morgan’s note had said, “Come back tomorrow, in the early morning.” 前一天，在魔法树屋里，摩根的纸条上写着：“明天一大早再回来。”

Jack jumped out of bed. He put on his jeans and T-shirt. Then he grabbed his backpack and crept out into the hall. 杰克跳下床。 他穿上牛仔裤和T恤。 然后抓起背包，悄悄走到走廊。

Jack peeked into Annie’s room. She wasn’t there. He slipped downstairs and out the front door. 杰克朝安妮的房间偷看。她不在。 他溜下楼，走出前门。

Annie was sitting on the porch steps. Jack sat down beside her. 安妮正坐在门廊台阶上。 杰克在她旁边坐下。

“What are you doing?” he asked. “你在干什么？”他问。

“I was waiting for the birds to start singing,” said Annie. “Then I was going to wake you up.” “我在等小鸟开始唱歌，”安妮说。 “然后我就去叫醒你。”

Jack and Annie watched the sky go from dark gray to light gray. Then the birds began their song. 杰克和安妮看着天空从深灰变成浅灰。 然后鸟儿开始歌唱。

“Tweet-tweet,” said Annie. “叽叽喳喳，”安妮说。

Without another word, Jack and Annie left their porch. They headed up their street to the Frog Creek woods. 杰克和安妮没再说话，离开了自家门廊。 他们沿着自家那条街，朝蛙溪树林走去。

It was cool beneath the trees. Jack and Annie hurried through the woods to the rope ladder. It hung from the tallest oak. At the top of the oak was the magic tree house. 树下很凉快。 杰克和安妮匆匆穿过树林，来到绳梯旁。 它挂在最高的那棵橡树上。 橡树顶上就是魔法树屋。

They climbed up into the tree house. It was barely light inside. 他们爬进了树屋。 里面几乎没什么光。

Annie picked up the note lying on the floor. She held it up to the window and read aloud: 安妮捡起地板上的纸条。 她把它举到窗边，大声读道：

Dear Jack and Annie, 亲爱的杰克和安妮：

Camelot is in trouble. To save the kingdom, please find these four special kinds of writing for my library:



 卡米洛特有难了。 为了拯救王国，请为我的图书馆找到这四种特别的文字：

Something to follow 用来遵循的东西

Something to send 用来送出的东西

Something to learn 用来学习的东西

Something to lend



 用来出借的东西

Thank you, 谢谢。

          Morgan

Jack took a deep breath. 杰克深深吸了口气。

“Okay,” he said. “We have our first special writing: a list from the Civil War.” “好，”他说。 “我们有了第一份特别文字：一份来自南北战争的名单。”

“We have the second,” said Annie, “a letter from the Revolutionary War.” “我们有了第二份，”安妮说，“一封来自独立战争的信。”

“We have the third,” said Jack, “a poem from a pioneer schoolhouse.” “我们有了第三份，”杰克说，“一首来自拓荒者校舍的诗。”

“Now we just need one more,” said Annie. “现在我们只要再找一份就行了，”安妮说。

“I wonder why we have to find these special writings for Morgan’s library,” said Jack. “How are they going to save Camelot?” “不知道我们为什么要为摩根的图书馆找这些特别文字，”杰克说。 “它们怎么能拯救卡米洛特？”

“I don’t know,” said Annie. “But let’s get going so we can solve the mystery. Where’s our research book?” “我不知道，”安妮说。 “不过我们快走吧，好解开这个谜。 我们的研究书在哪儿？”

They looked around the tree house. 他们环顾树屋。

Their Pennsylvania book, the book that always brought them home, was lying in the corner. Beside it was another book. Annie picked it up. 那本总能带他们回家的宾夕法尼亚书，躺在角落里。 旁边还有另一本书。 安妮把它捡起来。

“This is it,” she said softly. She showed the book’s cover to Jack. It said: “就是这本，”她轻声说。 她把书的封面给杰克看。上面写着：

SAN FRANCISCO, CALIFORNIA, 1906

“California?” said Jack. “I’ve always wanted to go to California.” “加利福尼亚？”杰克说。 “我一直想去加利福尼亚。”

“Me too,” said Annie. She laughed. “I guess I told Miss Neely the truth after all.” “我也是，”安妮说。她笑了。 “看来我到底还是对尼莉小姐说了实话。”

“Yeah,” said Jack, smiling. “是啊，”杰克笑着说。

The magic tree house had taken them to a pioneer school in their last adventure. There, Annie had told the teacher, Miss Neely, that they were on their way to California. 魔法树屋在上一次冒险中把他们带到了一所拓荒者学校。 在那儿，安妮告诉老师尼莉小姐，他们正要去加利福尼亚。

Annie pointed at the cover of the California book. 安妮指着加利福尼亚书的封面。

“We really wish we could go there,” she said. “我们真的希望能去那里，”她说。

The wind started to blow. 风开始刮起来。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。
