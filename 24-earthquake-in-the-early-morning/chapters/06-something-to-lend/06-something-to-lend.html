6. Something to Lend 6. 用来出借的东西

A woman in a blue bathrobe was sitting on a crumbling stone wall. She was sobbing into her handkerchief. 一个穿着蓝色浴袍的女人坐在一堵坍塌的石墙上。 她正用手帕捂着脸哭。

Two boys with dark hair sat next to the woman. The boys wore dusty, torn pajamas. They were both barefoot. The younger one was watching the older boy write on a rectangular piece of wood with a chunk of coal. 两个黑头发的男孩坐在女人身边。 男孩们穿着满是灰尘、破烂的睡衣。 他们都光着脚。 小一点的那个正看着大一点的男孩用一块煤在一块长方形木头上写字。

Annie pulled Jack over to the family. 安妮拉着杰克走向那一家人。

“Hi, I’m Annie,” she said. “嗨，我是安妮，”她说。

The two boys looked up. 两个男孩抬起头。

“I’m Peter,” said the youngest. “He’s my brother, Andrew. And she’s our aunt Mary.” He pointed to his brother, then to the woman, as he spoke. “我是彼得，”最小的那个说。 “他是我哥哥安德鲁。 她是我们的玛丽姨妈。”他一边说一边指着哥哥，再指着那个女人。

Aunt Mary tried to smile through her tears. 玛丽姨妈透过泪水努力笑了笑。

“Forgive me,” she said. “I’m a bit shaken up.” “请原谅，”她说。 “我还有点吓得发抖。”

“We are, too,” said Annie sympathetically. “我们也是，”安妮同情地说。

“The house caught fire,” Peter said. “房子着火了，”彼得说。

“We escaped,” said Andrew. “But we lost most of our things.” “我们逃出来了，”安德鲁说。 “可我们失去了大部分东西。”

“We’ve lost our shoes,” said Peter. “我们的鞋没了，”彼得说。

Jack and Annie looked at the boys’ bare feet. They were cut and bleeding. 杰克和安妮看着男孩们光着的脚。 脚上有伤口，还在流血。

“Hey, my boots are just like boys’ boots,” Annie said. “One of you can wear them.” “嘿，我的靴子就像男孩穿的，”安妮说。 “你们其中一个可以穿。”

“Take mine, too,” said Jack. He and Annie started unlacing their boots. “也拿我的，”杰克说。 他和安妮开始解开靴子的鞋带。

“We can’t just take your boots,” said Andrew. “我们不能就这么拿走你们的靴子，”安德鲁说。

“Then we’ll lend them to you,” said Jack. “那就借给你们，”杰克说。

He and Annie handed their boots to the two boys. 他和安妮把靴子递给两个男孩。

“Thank you, thank you,” said Aunt Mary. She started crying again. “谢谢，谢谢，”玛丽姨妈说。 她又哭了起来。

The two boys put their new boots on. Then Peter whispered something to Andrew. 两个男孩穿上新靴子。 然后彼得对安德鲁小声说了什么。

Andrew held out the piece of wood to Jack and Annie. 安德鲁把那块木头递给杰克和安妮。

“Here’s something we can lend you,” he said. “这是我们能借给你们的东西，”他说。

Jack and Annie read a poem the brothers had written on the piece of wood: 杰克和安妮读着兄弟俩写在木头上的诗：

图中文字：THERE IS NO WATER

AND STILL LESS SOAP.

WE HAVE NO CITY,

BUT LOTS OF HOPE. 图中文字：这里没有水，更没有肥皂。我们没有城市，但有很多希望。

“Thanks,” said Annie. “谢谢，”安妮说。

“It’s a great gift,” said Jack. “We needed some hope.” “这是一份很棒的礼物，”杰克说。 “我们正需要一点希望。”

“It’s the only thing we can lend you,” said Andrew. “这是我们唯一能借给你们的，”安德鲁说。

“Lend?” said Annie. She looked at Jack. “Oh, wow. They just gave us the special writing—something to lend!” “出借？”安妮说。 她看着杰克。 “哦，哇。 他们刚给了我们特别的文字——用来出借的东西！”

Jack smiled. They could go home now. 杰克笑了。 他们现在可以回家了。

“You should go to Golden Gate Park—that’s what a reporter told us,” he said to the boys and their aunt. “你们该去金门公园——一个记者告诉我们的，”他对男孩们和他们的姨妈说。

“Is that where you’re going?” asked Aunt Mary. She had stopped crying at last and looked stronger. “你们也去那儿吗？”玛丽姨妈问。 她终于不哭了，看起来坚强了一些。

“We have to go home to our parents,” said Annie. “我们得回家找父母，”安妮说。

“Will you be safe?” asked the aunt. “你们会安全吗？”姨妈问。

“Yes. Once we’re home, we’ll be safe,” said Jack. “会的。 一到家我们就安全了，”杰克说。

“Thanks for lending us your boots!” said Peter. “You’re good friends!” “谢谢你们把靴子借给我们！”彼得说。 “你们是好朋友！”

“You’re good friends!” said Annie. “你们也是好朋友！”安妮说。

“You’ve helped us more than you know,” said Jack. “你们帮了我们，比你们知道的还多，”杰克说。

“Be careful,” said Aunt Mary. “小心点，”玛丽姨妈说。

“We will!” said Jack and Annie. “我们会的！”杰克和安妮说。

They waved as their new friends headed off to the park. 他们朝新朋友挥手，看着他们朝公园走去。

Jack sighed. 杰克叹了口气。

“Ready?” he said. “好了吗？”他说。

“Yeah,” said Annie. “I guess we just go back the way we came.” “好了，”安妮说。 “我想我们原路返回就行。”

Jack looked down the street. Smoke billowed up from the bottom of the hill. 杰克朝山下望去。 黑烟从山脚翻涌上来。

“That might not be as easy as it sounds,” he said. “那可能没听起来那么容易，”他说。
