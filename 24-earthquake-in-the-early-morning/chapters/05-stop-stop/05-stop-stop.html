5. Stop! Stop! 5. 停下！停下！

Jack and Annie ran up the street to the book wagon. The man was carefully stacking the books in the back of the wagon. He was covered with dust and his glasses were cracked. 杰克和安妮沿着街跑向运书的马车。 那人正小心地把书叠放在马车后部。 他满身灰尘，眼镜也裂了。

“Hey, what’s the story?” Annie asked the man. “嘿，有什么新闻？”安妮问那人。

Jack couldn’t help smiling. Annie sounded just like the newspaper reporter. 杰克忍不住笑了。 安妮听起来就像那个报纸记者。

“I’m moving all the rare books to the Pavilion,” the man said. “我要把所有珍本书运到展览馆去，”那人说。

“Can we help?” asked Jack. “我们能帮忙吗？”杰克问。

“Sure, there are only a few left by the door,” said the man. “Grab ’em! Hurry! The fires on Market Street will soon be blowing this way.” “当然，门口只剩几本了，”那人说。 “拿上！快点！ 市场街的火很快就会吹到这边来。”

Jack and Annie ran into the building. Near the door were two small stacks of books. 杰克和安妮跑进大楼。 门边有两小堆书。

Jack and Annie each gathered up a stack. The books looked very old and fancy. Some even had sparkling gold on their covers. 杰克和安妮各抱起一堆。 那些书看起来非常古老精美。 有些封面甚至闪着金光。

“Wow,” whispered Jack. “哇，”杰克小声说。

He and Annie carried the books outside. 他和安妮把书搬到外面。

“Careful, please!” said the man. “All these books are treasures—ancient Bibles and hand-painted books.” “请小心！”那人说。 “这些书都是珍宝——古代圣经和手绘本。”

The man carefully took the books from Jack’s and Annie’s arms and put them in the back of the wagon. 那人小心地从杰克和安妮怀里接过书，放进马车后部。

“Thanks,” he said, pushing his hat back. “Run home now! The fires will be here soon!” “谢谢，”他说着，把帽子往后推了推。 “现在快跑回家！ 火很快就到了！”

As the horses started up the hill, Annie waved and shouted, “Good luck!” 马开始往山上走时，安妮挥手喊道：“好运！”

“I bet he was the librarian,” said Jack. “我猜他是图书管理员，”杰克说。

He opened his research book. He looked for a photograph of the building that had the books. 他打开研究书。 他找那座有书的大楼的照片。

“Here it is,” he said. He read aloud: “在这儿，”他说。他大声读道：

People tried to save special things. But they did not always succeed. Rare books from a library were moved to the Pavilion building. When the Pavilion building caught fire, all the books burned. The building that originally held them did not burn at all. 人们试图拯救一些特别的东西。但他们并不总是能成功。图书馆里的珍本书被转移到了 Pavilion 大楼。当 Pavilion 大楼起火时，所有的书都烧毁了。原本存放这些书的大楼却一点都没烧。

“Oh no!” cried Jack. “Stop! Stop!” “哦不！”杰克喊道。 “停下！停下！”

Clutching the research book, he ran after the wagon. Annie ran with him. 他紧抓着研究书，追着马车跑。 安妮和他一起跑。

“Stop! Stop!” they both yelled. They ran as fast as they could over the broken cobblestones and up the steep hill. “停下！ 停下！”他们俩都喊道。 他们以最快的速度跑过破碎的鹅卵石，冲上陡峭的山坡。

Near the top, the driver finally heard them. He brought his wagon to a halt. 快到山顶时，车夫终于听见了他们。 他让马车停住了。

“You can’t go to the Pavilion!” Jack cried. “你们不能去展览馆！”杰克喊道。

“You have to take them back to the building where they were!” said Annie. “你们得把书送回原来那栋楼！”安妮说。

“They won’t burn there!” said Jack, trying to catch his breath. “The building you’re taking them to is going to burn instead!” “那儿不会着火！”杰克喘着气说。 “你们要送去的那栋楼反而会烧掉！”

The driver looked at Jack and Annie as if they were crazy. 车夫看着杰克和安妮，好像他们疯了一样。

“You kids need to worry about yourselves, not these books,” he said. “Go home to your parents. I’ll take care of the library.” “孩子们，你们该担心自己，而不是这些书，”他说。 “回家找父母去。 图书馆的事我来管。”

Then the man snapped his reins and went on over the hilltop. 然后那人甩响缰绳，继续翻过山顶。

“Come back!” Jack cried. “回来！”杰克喊道。

They watched helplessly as the wagon bumped down the street, over the rubble. Smoke billowed up from the bottom of the hill. 他们无助地看着马车颠簸着驶下街道，越过瓦砾。 黑烟从山脚翻涌上来。

“I can’t believe it,” Jack said. He was close to tears. “我简直不敢相信，”杰克说。 他几乎要哭了。

“We tried, but we couldn’t save them,” said Annie. She touched Jack’s shoulder gently. “我们试过了，可救不了它们，”安妮说。 她轻轻拍了拍杰克的肩膀。

“All those books … ” His voice trailed off. “那么多书……”他的声音渐渐弱了。

“Hey,” said Annie. “Someone’s crying over there—someone with two kids. Maybe we can help them.” “嘿，”安妮说。 “那边有人在哭——带着两个孩子。 也许我们能帮他们。”
