4. What’s the Story? 4. 有什么新闻？

“Maybe we should leave,” Jack said in a panic. He wanted to get out of San Francisco before the fires spread. “也许我们该离开，”杰克惊慌地说。 他想在火灾蔓延前离开旧金山。

“We can’t,” said Annie. “We have to find our special writing for Morgan’s library, something to lend.” “我们不能，”安妮说。 “我们还得为摩根的图书馆找到特别文字，用来出借的东西。”

“Let’s find it fast,” said Jack. “我们快点找，”杰克说。

He and Annie started walking through the rubble. They stepped over piles of bricks, chunks of concrete, and broken glass. 他和安妮开始穿过瓦砾走。 他们跨过砖堆、混凝土块和碎玻璃。

They passed fallen lamps and twisted trolley-car tracks. 他们走过倒下的灯柱和扭曲的电车轨道。

They saw houses leaning to one side and people hauling their things out to the street. 他们看见房屋歪向一边，人们正把东西拖到街上。

“We can’t worry about our mission now,” Annie said. “We have to help.” “我们现在不能只顾任务了，”安妮说。 “我们得帮忙。”

“Help? How?” said Jack. He was so shaky, he didn’t think he could be much help to anyone. “帮忙？怎么帮？”杰克说。 他抖得厉害，觉得自己帮不了谁什么忙。

“What about them?” said Annie. “那他们呢？”安妮说。

She pointed to some men frantically dragging bags out of a building and piling them into a horse-drawn wagon. 她指着几个男人，正手忙脚乱地把袋子从楼里拖出来，堆进一辆马拉马车。

Annie ran over to the wagon. 安妮跑向马车。

“What are you doing?” she asked the men. “你们在干什么？”她问那些人。

“We’re trying to get these bank bags down to the harbor,” said the wagon driver. “So a boat can take them out into the bay.” “我们正想把这些银行袋子运到港口，”马车夫说。 “好让船把它们运到海湾里。”

“Why?” asked Jack. “为什么？”杰克问。

“So we can save everyone’s money from the fires!” the man said. “好让我们把大家的钱从火灾里救出来！”那人说。

He pointed at the sky. The cloud of smoke was growing bigger and blacker. 他指着天空。 烟云变得更大更黑。

“Can we help?” asked Annie. “我们能帮忙吗？”安妮问。

“We’re done,” said the driver. “You kids run home to your parents. Then get out of the city.” “我们已经弄完了，”马车夫说。 “孩子们，快回家找父母。 然后离开这座城市。”

Jack wished he and Annie could ride with the driver down to the bay and be safe from the fires, too. But he could see the wagon didn’t have room for them. 杰克真希望自己和安妮也能坐着马车去海湾，躲开火灾。 可他看得出马车上没有他们的位置。

“Good luck!” said Annie. “好运！”安妮说。

“Don’t forget what I told you!” the driver said. Then he and his horses took off. The wagon turned onto the main street and disappeared over the hill. “别忘了我告诉你们的话！”马车夫说。 然后他和马出发了。 马车拐上主街，消失在山那边。

“I wonder who we can help?” said Annie. “不知道我们能帮谁？”安妮说。

Jack took a deep breath. 杰克深深吸了口气。

“I don’t know,” he said. “Maybe I’ll take some notes.” “我不知道，”他说。 “也许我记点笔记。”

Jack pulled out his notebook. In wobbly handwriting, he wrote:



 杰克掏出笔记本。 他用发抖的字迹写道：

图中文字：Bank money-wagon to bay, then boat 图中文字：银行的运钞车停在码头，然后坐船

“Hey, what’s the story?” a woman asked. Her voice sounded urgent. “嘿，有什么新闻？”一个女人问。 她的声音听起来很急切。

Jack looked up. 杰克抬起头。

A man and woman stood in front of them. The woman wore a long dress and carried a notebook. The man wore a suit. He carried a big camera and a three-legged stand. 一男一女站在他们面前。 女人穿着长裙，拿着笔记本。 男人穿着西装。 他拿着一架大相机和一个三脚架。

“What story?” said Annie. “什么新闻？”安妮说。

“The story with the bank. My name’s Betty. I’m a reporter,” said the woman. “银行的新闻。我叫贝蒂。 我是记者，”女人说。

“For television?” asked Annie. “电视台的？”安妮问。

“What’s that?” said Betty. “那是什么？”贝蒂说。

“Never mind,” said Jack. He whispered quickly to Annie, “She’s a newspaper reporter. TV hasn’t been invented yet.” “没什么，”杰克说。 他赶紧小声对安妮说：“她是报纸记者。 电视还没发明呢。”

“Oops,” said Annie. “哎呀，”安妮说。

“So what’s the story with the wagon that just left the bank?” Betty asked Jack and Annie. “那么刚离开银行的那辆马车是怎么回事？”贝蒂问杰克和安妮。

Jack looked down at his notebook. 杰克低头看笔记本。

“They’re going to save the money by taking it to the bay and putting it on a boat,” he said. “他们要把钱运到海湾，放到船上保存，”他说。

“Good reporting work, sonny!” she said. “Get a picture of the bank, Fred.” “报道得好，小家伙！”她说。 “给银行拍张照，弗雷德。”

The photographer set his camera on the stand. He put his head under a black curtain and took a picture. 摄影师把相机架在三脚架上。 他把头伸进黑色布帘下拍了一张。

“Got it,” said Fred. “拍好了，”弗雷德说。

As the photographer packed up his equipment, Betty turned to Jack and Annie. 摄影师收拾设备时，贝蒂转向杰克和安妮。

“Go home and get your parents, kids,” she said. “Fires are burning out of control.” “回家去找父母，孩子们，”她说。 “火灾正在失控蔓延。”

“We know,” said Annie. “By the end of three days, the fires will burn down nearly all of San Francisco.” “我们知道，”安妮说。 “三天之内，大火几乎会烧光整个旧金山。”

Betty looked curious. “How do you know that?” she asked. 贝蒂看起来很好奇。 “你怎么知道？”她问。

“She’s just guessing,” Jack said quickly. “她只是猜的，”杰克连忙说。

“Pretty gloomy guess,” said Betty. “Tell your folks not to catch the ferry. Thousands are crowding into the ferry building. Go to Golden Gate Park.” “猜得可真悲观，”贝蒂说。 “告诉你们父母别去赶渡轮。 成千上万的人正挤进渡轮大楼。 去金门公园。”

“Thanks for the tip,” said Annie. “谢谢提醒，”安妮说。

“Thanks for the story,” said Betty. Then she and Fred hurried away. “谢谢你们的新闻，”贝蒂说。 然后她和弗雷德匆匆离开了。

Jack and Annie looked around. 杰克和安妮环顾四周。

Now many people seemed to be fleeing their homes. Some were going up the hill. Some were going down. 现在许多人似乎正逃离家园。 有的往山上走。 有的往山下走。

An old woman was pushing a wheelbarrow filled with pots and pans. A girl was carrying a suitcase and a cat. A boy was carrying a birdcage and a fishbowl. 一个老妇人推着装满锅碗的独轮车。 一个女孩拎着行李箱和一只猫。 一个男孩提着鸟笼和鱼缸。

“They’re all going in different directions,” said Annie. “他们都往不同的方向走，”安妮说。

“I wonder where Golden Gate Park is,” said Jack. “Maybe we should go there. Let’s see if there’s a map in our book.” “不知道金门公园在哪儿，”杰克说。 “也许我们该去那儿。 看看书里有没有地图。”

Jack looked in their research book. He found a map of San Francisco. 杰克查看研究书。 他找到一张旧金山地图。

“Where are we now?” he said. “我们现在在哪儿？”他说。

As he looked for a street sign, he saw a man carrying an armload of books out of a beautiful building. The man put the books into the back of a horse-drawn wagon. 他找路牌时，看见一个男人正抱着一摞书从一座漂亮的大楼里出来。 那人把书放进一辆马拉马车的后部。

“What’s he doing?” asked Jack. “他在干什么？”杰克问。

“I bet he’s saving those books,” said Annie. “我猜他在救那些书，”安妮说。

“Saving books?” said Jack. He loved books. For a moment, Jack forgot his fears. He forgot about trying to save himself. “救书？”杰克说。他热爱书。 那一刻，杰克忘了恐惧。 他忘了只想救自己。

“We’d better help,” he said. “Come on!” “我们最好去帮忙，”他说。 “快点！”
