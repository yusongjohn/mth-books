3. Mamoon 3. 马穆恩

A nnie stuck her head out the tree house window. “Hey!” she called. 安妮把头从树屋的窗户里探了出来。 “嘿！”她喊道。

“Shhhh!” said Jack, pulling her back in. “Don’t let them see us up here! It’s too hard to explain the tree house. Let’s go down.” “嘘——！”杰克说，把她拉了回来。 “别让他们看见我们在这儿！” “解释树屋太难了。我们下去吧。”

“Good point,” said Annie. She handed Merlin’s letter to Jack and started down the rope ladder. Jack grabbed his shoulder bag. He put the letter inside, then added their research book and rhyme book. He slung the leather bag across his chest and climbed down. “好主意，”安妮说。 她把梅林的信递给杰克，然后开始顺着绳梯往下爬。 杰克抓起了他的肩包。 他把信放了进去，然后又放进了他们的研究书和押韵书。 他把皮质的包跨在胸前，然后爬了下来。

When he stepped onto the ground, Jack twisted the rope ladder behind the tree trunk so it wouldn’t be noticed. “Okay,” he said to Annie. 当他踏上地面时，杰克把绳梯在树干后面扭了一下，这样就不会被发现了。 “好，”他对安妮说。

“Hey!” Annie called again, waving. She and Jack stepped out into the open. “嘿！”安妮又喊了一遍，挥了挥手。 她和杰克走了出来，走进了开阔的地方。

The camel riders headed toward the palm trees. The man in the lead made his camel kneel. As he climbed off, Jack and Annie ran over to him. The man wore a long white robe. He had a black beard and stern, dark eyes. “Who are you?” he asked, unsmiling. “From where do you come?” 骆驼骑手们朝棕榈树方向前进。 领头的男人让他的骆驼跪下。 当他跳下来时，杰克和安妮跑过去见他。 那个男人穿着一件长长的白色长袍。 他有一把黑胡子和严厉的黑眼睛。 “你是谁？”他面无表情地问。 “你从哪里来？”

“I am Annie, and this is my brother, Jack,” said Annie. “Our home is far away in Frog Creek, Pennsylvania.” “我是安妮，这是我的哥哥杰克，”安妮说。 “我们的家在宾夕法尼亚州的蛙溪镇，很远的地方。”

“I have not heard of such a place,” the man said. “How do you come to be here in the desert alone?” “我从来没听说过这么个地方，”那个人说。 “你们怎么会独自来到沙漠里？”

“Uh …” Jack didn’t know what to say. “呃……”杰克不知道该说什么。

“We were riding with our family,” Annie said. “We stopped to rest here. My brother and I took a nap behind these trees. When we woke up, everyone was gone. They left us by mistake. See, we have a really big family. There are many brothers and sisters—” “我们是和家人一起骑马来的，”安妮说。 “我们在这里停下来休息了。 我哥哥我和在这些树后面睡了一觉。 我们醒来时，大家都不见了。 他们是把我们误留下的。 看，我们有一个非常大的家庭。 有很多兄弟姐妹—

“Annie,” said Jack. She was saying too much, he thought. “安妮，”杰克说。 她说得太多了，他想。

The man looked concerned. “Why have they not come back for you?” he said, gazing out at the desert. “I hope they have not been attacked by bandits.” 那个男人看起来很担心。 “他们为什么不回来接你？”他望着沙漠说道。 “我希望他们没有被强盗袭击。”

“Are there bandits around here?” asked Annie. “这里附近有强盗吗？”安妮问道。

“There are many bandits prowling the desert,” said the man. “沙漠里有很多强盗在游荡，”那人说。

Jack looked anxiously around at the vast sandy plain. 杰克焦急地环顾着这片广阔的沙漠平原。

“That is why one must always travel with others,” said the man. “But I hope your family is safe and will return for you soon.” “这就是为什么人们旅行时一定要和别人一起，”那个人说。 “但我希望你的家人平安，并且很快会回来接你。”

“Excuse me,” Annie said politely. “But who are you? How did you happen to come here?” “对不起，”安妮有礼貌地说。 “但是你是谁？ 你怎么会来到这里？”

“I am a merchant,” the man said. “My caravan was traveling from the west, when we were surprised by a sudden sandstorm. It seemed to come from nowhere. But luckily it brought us to this oasis. We will rest and water our animals until the sun goes down. In the cool of the night, we will travel on to Baghdad.” “我是个商人，”那个人说。 “我的商队原本是从西边出发，但突然遭遇了一场沙尘暴。 那场沙尘暴看起来是凭空出现的。 不过幸运的是，它把我们带到了这个绿洲。 我们会在这里休息，给动物们饮水，直到太阳落山。” 在夜深人静的时候，我们要去巴格达。”

The caravan leader walked over to his men and spoke to them. They dismounted and started taking saddlebags off the camels. 商队首领走到他的手下身边，对他们说话。 他们下了骆驼，开始把骆驼背上的行李箱卸下来。

Annie turned to Jack. “See, our rhyme worked!” she whispered. “The sandstorm was magic! It brought them here on their way to Baghdad!” 安妮转向杰克。 “你看，我们的押韵起作用了！”她小声说。 “沙尘暴是魔法！ 它把他们带到了这里，本来他们是去巴格达的！

“But how can we get them to help us?” said Jack. “但是我们怎么才能让他们帮助我们呢？”杰克说。

“Well, Merlin said we should be humble, so let’s offer to help them,” said Annie. She walked over to the caravan leader. He was filling a canvas bucket with water from a small spring. “嗯，梅林说过我们要谦虚，所以让我们主动提出帮助他们，”安妮说。 她走到马车队伍的领头人面前。 他正在用一个小泉水里的水往帆布桶里装水。

“Excuse me,” said Annie, “we wondered if we could help you.” “请问，”安妮说，“我们想知道能不能帮上忙。”

The man gave her a quick smile. “Thank you, yes,” he said. “If you could gather dates, it would be most appreciated. My men are very hungry.” He handed Annie two large baskets. 那人给了她一个快速的微笑。 “谢谢，可以的，”他说。 “如果你们能帮忙摘些枣子，那就太好了。 我的手下们非常饿。”他递给安妮两个大篮子。

“No problem,” said Annie. “We’ll gather dates.” “没问题，”安妮说。 “我们会摘枣子。”

Annie carried the baskets to Jack. “Do you know what a date is?” she whispered. “We’re supposed to gather some.” 安妮把篮子拿给了杰克。 “你知道枣子是什么吗？”她小声问。 “我们该收集一些。”

“I’ll look it up,” said Jack. With his back to the camel riders, he pulled their research book out of his bag and looked up dates. He read: “我去查一下，”杰克说。 他背对着骆驼骑士，从包里拿出他们的研究书，翻到日期那一页。他读道：

Dates are known as the fruit of the desert. They hang in bunches from date palms. People gather dates by shaking the trunk of the tree. Not only are dates an important food, but the wood and leaves of the date palm are used to make— 枣子被称为沙漠的果实。它们成串地挂在枣椰树上。人们通过摇动树干来采摘枣子。枣子不仅是重要的食物，枣椰树的木材和叶子还被用来制作——

“Okay, got it,” interrupted Annie, putting the baskets down. “Let’s start shaking the trees!” “好了，我知道了，”安妮打断了他，把篮子放下。 “我们开始摇树吧！”，

Jack put the book away and looked around. For the first time, he noticed bunches of brown fruit hanging from the trees. He grabbed hold of the nearest tree trunk. Annie grabbed the trunk from the other side. Together they shook the tree until dates began falling to the ground. 杰克把书收起来，环顾四周。 第一次，他注意到树上挂着一串串棕色的果实。 他抓住了最近的树干。 安妮从另一边抓住了树干。 他们一起摇动树干，直到椰枣开始掉落到地上。

In the desert heat, Jack and Annie went from tree to tree, shaking each one and gathering the dates that fell to the ground. By the time they had filled their baskets, the trees were casting long shadows over the oasis. 在炎热的沙漠中，杰克和安妮从一棵树走到另一棵树，摇晃每棵树，捡起掉到地上的椰枣。 等他们装满篮子时，树的影子已经长长的投在绿洲上了。

Tired and sweaty, Jack and Annie carried their heavy baskets back to the caravan leader. He was boiling water over a fire of twigs. “Ah, very good,” he said. “Thank you, Jack and Annie.” 又累又出汗的杰克和安妮，扛着沉重的篮子回到商队首领那里。 他正在用细枝生火煮水。 “啊，非常好，”他说。 “谢谢你们，杰克和安妮。”

“You’re welcome,” said Annie. “What else can we do for you?” “不用谢，”安妮说。 “我们还能帮您做些什么吗？”

“You should rest from the heat now,” said the man. “Would you like to sit and have tea with us?” “现在你们应该休息一下，避开炎热，”那人说。 “你们想和我们一起坐下来喝茶吗？”

“Sure,” said Annie. “By the way, what’s your name?” “当然可以，”安妮说。 “顺便问一下，你叫什么名字？”

“My name is very long,” the man said with a smile. “You may call me Mamoon.” “我的名字很长，”那个人微笑着说道。 “你可以叫我马穆恩。”

While their camels grazed, Mamoon and his men sat on a woolen rug spread over the grass. They shared dates and tea with Jack and Annie. The dark, plump fruit was sweet and chewy. The tea was strong but good. 当他们的骆驼在吃草时，马穆恩和他的手下坐在铺在草地上的一块羊毛地毯上。 他们和杰克、安妮一起分享了干果和茶。 这种又黑又圆的水果又甜又嚼劲十足。 茶味道很浓，但很好喝。

In the fiery red glow of the setting sun, Jack watched the grazing camels. He thought the humped animals looked really funny. They had knobby knees, big clumsy feet, and little ears that twitched. Some camels smacked their droopy lips as they drank water. Others gobbled down whole branches of thornbushes without chewing. 在夕阳火红的光芒中，杰克看着正在吃草的骆驼。 他认为这些驼峰动物看起来真的很滑稽。 他们的膝盖很粗糙，脚又大又笨拙，耳朵小小的，还会一抖一抖的。 有些骆驼在喝水时，用下垂的嘴唇啪啪地拍打。 其他的骆驼直接把带刺的树枝整个吞下去，也不嚼。

“Don’t the thorns hurt the camels’ throats?” Jack asked Mamoon. “这些刺不会伤到骆驼的喉咙吗？”杰克问马穆恩。

“No,” said the caravan leader. “Their mouths are very tough. They can eat anything: sticks, bones—” “不会，”商队的领队说。 “它们的嘴非常坚韧， 它们什么都能吃：木棍、骨头——”

“Even our tents and saddlebags if we let them!” said a young camel rider. “甚至我们的帐篷和马鞍包，如果让它们的话！”一个年轻的骆驼骑手说。

Annie and Jack laughed. “What’s in your saddlebags?” Annie asked. 安妮和杰克笑了。 “你的马鞍包里装了什么？”安妮问。

“Our bags are filled with goods from Greece, Turkey, and Syria,” said Mamoon. “We have many things: jewels, beads, and precious spices, such as cinnamon, pepper, and vanilla. We are taking everything to Baghdad to sell.” “我们的包里装满了来自希腊、土耳其和叙利亚的商品，”马穆恩说。 “我们有很多东西：珠宝、珠子和珍贵的香料，比如肉桂、胡椒和香草。 我们要把所有东西带到巴格达去卖。”

“We have to get to Baghdad, too,” said Annie. “我们也得去巴格达，”安妮说。

“We have to meet with the caliph.” “我们要和哈里发会面。”

The camel riders chuckled as if they thought Annie was making a joke. 骆驼骑手们笑了起来，好像觉得安妮在开玩笑。

Only Mamoon did not laugh. “Your family is to meet with the caliph?” he said. 只有马穆恩没有笑。 “你们家要和哈里发会面？”他说。

“No,” said Annie. “Just Jack and me. We have to help him spread wisdom to the world.” “不是，”安妮说。 “只是杰克和我。” 我们必须帮助他把智慧传播到世界各地。”

“Annie,” warned Jack. “安妮，”杰克警告道。

The camel riders laughed loudly. 骆驼骑手们大声地笑了起来。

“What’s so funny?” Annie asked. “你们笑什么？”安妮问。

“The caliph does not meet with children,” said a young man. “He is the most powerful and important person in the world.” “哈里发不会和小孩子见面，”一个年轻人说。 “他是世界上最强大、最重要的人。”

“Oh,” said Annie, frowning. “哦，”安妮皱着眉头说。

The news worried Jack, too. 这个消息也让杰克感到担心。

Mamoon looked at Annie and Jack with a curious expression. “Night will soon be upon us. Since your family has not yet returned, would you like to travel with us to Baghdad?” he said. “You have journeyed by camel this far. I trust you can ride camels the rest of the way.” 马穆恩用好奇的表情看着安妮和杰克。 “黑夜很快就要到来了。” “你们家人还没回来，要和我们一起去巴格达吗？”他说。 “你们一路都是骑骆驼过来的。 我相信你们能骑骆驼走完剩下的路。”

“Sure we can!” said Annie. “We love camels!” “当然可以！”安妮说。 “我们喜欢骆驼！”

We do? thought Jack. 我们真的喜欢吗？杰克心想。

“Good. We love our ships of the desert, too,” said Mamoon. “We will set sail on them soon.” “很好。 我们也喜欢沙漠里的船，”马穆恩说。 “我们很快就会上船出发。”

“So that’s what ‘ships of the desert’ are!” Annie whispered to Jack. “原来‘沙漠里的船’就是骆驼啊！”安妮小声对杰克说。

Camels, thought Jack. Oh, brother. 杰克心想，骆驼啊，哦，天哪。
