4. Ships of the Desert 4. 沙漠中的船只

T he camel riders all silently watched the sun set over the faraway dunes. As the fiery ball slipped beneath the horizon, the desert was flooded with red light. As soon as the sun disappeared, the air grew much cooler. 骆驼骑手们静静地注视着太阳在远处的沙丘上落下。 当那颗火红的球体滑落到地平线以下时，沙漠被红色的光芒笼罩。 太阳一消失，空气立刻变得凉爽了许多。

Mamoon stood up. “It is time to go,” he said. 马穆恩站了起来。 “该走了，”他说。

The camel riders put out their small fire. In the growing darkness, Mamoon helped them saddle up their animals and load them with baggage. 骆驼骑手们熄灭了他们的小火堆。 在逐渐加深的黑暗中，马穆恩帮他们给骆驼套上鞍具，并把行李装上骆驼。

Then Mamoon came over to Jack and Annie. “You can ride those two sisters,” he said, pointing to two camels kneeling in the sand. “Climb on, then come to the front of the line to ride with me.” 然后，马穆恩走到杰克和安妮面前。 “你可以骑这两只骆驼姐妹，”他指着沙地上跪着的两只骆驼说。 “爬上来，然后到队伍最前面和我一起骑。”

Jack and Annie walked over to the two camel sisters. Each had reins hanging from her neck. Saddles made from colorful cushions were piled high on their humps. 杰克和安妮走到两只骆驼姐妹面前。 每只骆驼的脖子上都挂着缰绳。 色彩鲜艳的垫子做成的鞍具堆在它们的驼峰上。

Annie patted the wiry, tan-colored fur of one of the camels. The camel looked at Annie with big eyes and fluttered her thick eyelashes. “Hey, Cutie,” said Annie. 安妮摸了摸其中一只骆驼细密的浅棕色毛发。 那只骆驼用大眼睛看着安妮，眨着浓密的睫毛。 “嘿，小可爱，”安妮说。

The other camel nuzzled Annie’s neck. “Hey, Beauty,” Annie said to the other. “You want some attention, too?” 另一只骆驼蹭了蹭安妮的脖子。 “嘿，美人，”安妮对另一只骆驼说。 “你也想要关注吗？”

“Cutie and Beauty?” said Jack. He didn’t find either camel particularly cute or beautiful. “小可爱和美人？”杰克说。 他觉得这两只骆驼都不怎么可爱或漂亮。

Annie climbed onto Cutie’s saddle cushion and picked up the reins. “Let’s ride!” she said. 安妮爬到小可爱鞍座的垫子上，拿起缰绳。 “我们出发吧！”她说。

Cutie rose awkwardly up from a kneeling position to a full stand. “Oh, wow!” said Annie, towering over Jack. “She’s really tall.” 小可爱从跪着的姿势笨拙地站起来，站成了直立姿势。 “哦，哇！”安妮说，她站在杰克头顶上。 “她真的好高。”

Jack started to climb onto Beauty. But the camel caught an end of his head cloth and began chewing it. 杰克开始爬到美人身上。 但骆驼抓住了他头巾的一端，开始啃咬它。

“Stop that!” said Jack, pulling the cloth away from her. Beauty opened her mouth wide and flashed rows of sharp teeth. Jack drew back. “住手！”杰克说，把布从她手里拉开。 美美张开嘴，露出一排尖利的牙齿。杰克往后退了一步。

“Don’t be afraid,” said Annie. “别害怕，”安妮说。

“Easy for you to say,” said Jack. “Yours likes you.” “你这么说倒是容易，”杰克说。 “你的那只喜欢你。”

“Don’t worry, Beauty likes you, too,” said Annie. “I can tell.” Annie’s camel began ambling toward the other camels waiting to head off into the desert. “Come on, Jack! It’s really fun once you’re moving!” she called. “别担心，贝蒂也喜欢你，”安妮说。 “我能看出来。”安妮的骆驼开始慢悠悠地走向其他等待进入沙漠的骆驼。 “快点，杰克！ 动起来之后真的很有趣！”她喊道。

“Fun,” muttered Jack. “Right.” He held on to the ends of his head cloth and put his leg over Beauty’s hump. The camel eyed him suspiciously. She swished her tail, slapping his back. “有趣，”杰克咕哝道。 “对。”他抓住头巾的两端，把腿跨过贝美的驼峰。 骆驼怀疑地看着他。 她甩动尾巴，拍打他的后背。

“Hey!” said Jack. “嘿！”杰克说。

Jack tried to get comfortable on the saddle cushion. But Beauty spat at him and made a weird screeching sound. 杰克试着在鞍垫上坐舒服一点。 但贝美朝他吐了一口，发出奇怪的尖叫声。

“Quiet!” said Jack. He hooked his shoulder bag onto a saddle horn. When he was finally settled, Beauty turned her head and started chewing on his leather bag. “安静！”杰克说。 他把肩膀上的背包钩在鞍角上。 当他终于坐稳后，贝美转过头，开始啃他的皮质背包。

“No! Don’t!” yelled Jack. He tried pulling the bag away, but Beauty played tug-of-war. “Come on, let go,” Jack said. “Give it back, stupid!” “不！” “别！”杰克喊道。 他试着把袋子拉开，但贝蒂玩起了拔河游戏。 “快点，松开手，”杰克说。 “把东西还给我，笨蛋！”

“Do you really think she is stupid?” “你真的觉得她是个笨蛋吗？”

Jack jumped. Mamoon had ridden up behind him and was watching as he tried to get his bag back from Beauty. 杰克跳了起来。 马穆恩骑着马从后面赶上来，看着杰克试图从贝蒂手里拿回自己的包。

Jack was embarrassed. “Um, she won’t let go of my stuff,” he said. 杰克感到很尴尬。 “呃，她不让我拿回我的东西，”他说。

Mamoon grabbed the strap of Jack’s bag. He clucked his tongue, and the camel let go. She groaned as Mamoon hooked the leather bag back onto the saddle horn. 马穆恩抓住了杰克包的背带。 他吐了吐舌头，骆驼就松开了。 玛穆恩把皮袋子重新挂在马鞍的角上时，她呻吟了一声。

“For thousands of years, camels like this one have carried people across the desert,” said Mamoon. “She is truly a miracle of nature.” “几千年来，像这样的骆驼一直载着人们穿越沙漠，”玛穆恩说。 “她真是自然的奇迹。”

Some miracle, thought Jack. 真是个奇迹，杰克心想。

“She can drink two barrels of water in ten minutes,” said Mamoon, “and then go for a week without drinking again. She can live many days without food, too.” “她能在十分钟内喝下两桶水，”玛穆恩说，“然后一整周都不用再喝水。 她几天不吃东西也能活下来。”

“Really?” said Jack. “真的吗？”杰克说。

“She is well suited to travel in the desert,” said Mamoon. “Her thick eyebrows protect her eyes from the glare of the sun. Her long eyelashes and the fur around her ears keep out the windblown sand.” “她非常适合在沙漠中旅行，”玛穆恩说。 “她浓密的眉毛能保护她的眼睛免受阳光的刺眼。” “她长长的睫毛和耳朵周围的毛发能挡住被风吹来的沙子。”

“Cool,” said Jack softly. “真凉快，”杰克轻声说。

“Her feet are so tough, they do not feel the heat of the desert,” said Mamoon. “And they are so big that they keep her from sinking down into the loose sand.” “她的脚非常坚硬，感觉不到沙漠的炎热，”马穆恩说。 “而且她的脚这么大，能让她不会陷进松软的沙子里。”

“Hmm,” said Jack. “嗯，”杰克说。

“She can carry five hundred pounds of baggage on her back,” said Mamoon, “and travel one hundred miles in a single day.” “她能背着五百磅的行李，”马穆恩说，“一天还能走一百英里。”

“That’s a lot,” murmured Jack. “太多了，”杰克小声说。

Mamoon tugged on the camel’s reins and clucked his tongue. Beauty breathed heavily as she rose up on her long, powerful legs to her full height. 马穆恩拉了拉骆驼的缰绳，发出咔哒咔哒的声音。 贝蒂用她长长的、有力的腿站起来，高高抬起身子，喘着粗气。

Mamoon looked at Jack. “We must respect her and honor her,” he said. “In many ways, she is superior to us, no?” 马穆恩看着杰克。 “我们必须尊重她，也要敬重她，”他说。 “在很多方面，她都比我们强，不是吗？”

Jack nodded. He thought of the words of Merlin’s letter. To succeed in your mission, you must be humble. He patted the camel. “Good girl, Beauty.” 杰克点了点头。 他想起了梅林信中的话。 要完成你的任务，你必须谦虚。 他拍了拍骆驼。 “好姑娘，贝蒂。”

Mamoon clucked his tongue again to coax the camel forward. Perched high on his saddle, Jack rocked from side to side. He did not feel at all safe, but he stayed calm. Beauty ambled over to Cutie. The two sisters stood together and snorted. 马穆恩又吐了吐舌头，想让骆驼往前走。 杰克高高地坐在马鞍上，左右摇晃着身体。 他一点都不觉得安全，但他还是保持冷静。 美美慢悠悠地走到酷酷身边。 这对姐妹站在一起，一起哼哼起来。

The desert sky was bright with stars. Mamoon called to his men, and the caravan started moving forward. 沙漠的天空亮着闪闪的星星。 马穆恩叫了他的手下，商队开始向前移动了。

The camels walked with a swaying motion. They moved two big feet on one side, then two big feet on the other. Jack gripped the horn of his saddle as his “ship of the desert” rocked from left to right. 骆驼走路时一摇一晃的。 它们一边先迈出两只大脚，另一边再迈出两只大脚。 杰克紧紧抓住马鞍上的角，因为他的“沙漠之船”左右摇晃。

“Isn’t this fun?” said Annie, rocking alongside him. “这不是很有趣吗？”安妮一边摇晃着，一边说道。

“Sort of,” said Jack, shivering. Actually, he wasn’t having any fun at all. He felt seasick and was freezing in the night air. Also, he was worried about their mission. Would the caliph meet with them? If he did, how could they help him “spread wisdom to the world”? And if Baghdad was very far away, how would they ever find their way back to the tree house? “还行吧，”杰克说着，打了个哆嗦。 其实，他一点都不觉得有趣。 他感觉晕船了，夜晚的空气又冷得让人发抖。 他还担心他们的任务。 可汗会和他们见面吗？ 如果他会，他们怎么才能帮助他“把智慧传播到世界各地”呢？ 如果巴格达非常遥远，他们怎么才能找到回去树屋的路呢？

Mamoon slowed his camel until he was riding between Jack and Annie. “When I was a boy, I spent many cold nights in the desert riding with my father on journeys to the west,” he said. “At first, I, too, thought camels were foolish. I always longed for more blankets and for a smoother ride. I wished to be back in Baghdad in my own warm bed.” 马蒙让他的骆驼慢下来，直到他骑在杰克和安妮中间。 “我小时候，曾在沙漠里和父亲一起向西旅行，度过了许多寒冷的夜晚，”他说。 “起初，我也觉得骆驼很傻。 我总是渴望有更多的毯子，还有更平稳的旅程。 我真希望回到巴格达，躺在自己温暖的床上。”

Jack smiled. He liked the caravan leader. 杰克笑了。 他喜欢这支队伍的领队。

“But over time, I have come to love the cold desert nights,” said Mamoon. “Now when I am sleeping in my warm bed in Baghdad, I long to be here instead. I wish to be reading the wind and the stars.” “但随着时间的推移，我渐渐爱上了寒冷沙漠的夜晚，”马穆恩说。 “现在，当我躺在巴格达温暖的床上时，我却希望我在这里。 我希望自己能读懂风和星星。”

“How do you read the stars?” asked Annie. “你怎么读星星呢？”安妮问。

“They have their own language,” said Mamoon. “At this moment, we are heading east, toward the Goat Star.” He pointed at the sky. “它们有自己的语言，”马穆恩说。 “此刻，我们正朝东边前进，朝着山羊星的方向。”他指着天空。

Jack couldn’t tell which star was the Goat Star. But he was filled with wonder. Thousands of tiny lights twinkled in the black dome of night. There were more stars than Jack had ever imagined. Some looked close enough to touch. 杰克分不清哪颗星星是山羊星。 但他感到非常惊奇。 无数小小的光点在夜空的黑色穹顶上闪烁着。 星星的数量比杰克想象的还要多。 有些星星看起来近得可以触摸到。

Mamoon started singing a song. The other camel riders joined in. Jack couldn’t understand the words, but the tune was soothing. The camels seemed to sway to the music. 玛穆恩开始唱起一首歌。 其他的骆驼骑手也跟着唱起来。 杰克听不懂歌词，但旋律让人感到平静。 骆驼似乎随着音乐轻轻摇摆。

Jack stopped worrying about how they would get back to the tree house. And he found he was actually enjoying the fresh desert air. He started to relax. 杰克不再担心怎么才能回到树屋了。 他发现自己竟然开始享受这新鲜的沙漠空气了。 他开始放松下来。

“Jack,” Annie said softly. “Guess what—we just solved the first mystery in Merlin’s letter: Ride a ship of the desert on a cold starry night.” “杰克，”安妮轻声说。 “猜猜看——我们刚刚解开了梅林信中第一个谜题：在寒冷的星光夜晚，骑上沙漠中的船。”

“Yeah,” said Jack happily. “And it’s really fun.” “是啊，”杰克开心地说。 “真的很有趣。”

Suddenly a fierce shout came from the distance. Jack sat up straighter. His heart thumped. 突然，远处传来一声凶猛的喊叫。 杰克坐得更直了。 他的心跳加快了。

“Bandits!” one of the camel drivers shouted. “强盗！”其中一个骆驼司机喊道。
