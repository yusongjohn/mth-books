2. Nowhere 2. 无处

J ack felt hot. He opened his eyes. Burning sunlight was flooding into the tree house. He and Annie were wearing long robes tied with cords. They wore white head cloths and pointy slip-on shoes. Jack’s backpack had turned into a leather shoulder bag. 杰克感觉很热。 他睁开了眼睛。 炽热的阳光照进了树屋。 杰克和安妮穿着用绳子绑着的长袍子。 他们戴着白色的头巾，穿着尖头的拖鞋。 杰克的背包变成了一只皮质的肩包。

“We look like characters in that book Aunt Mary gave us,” said Annie, “Tales from the Arabian Nights.” “我们看起来就像玛丽阿姨给我们的那本书里的角色，”安妮说，“《一千零一夜》的故事。”

“Yeah, like Aladdin and Ali Baba,” said Jack. “对，就像阿拉丁和阿里巴巴，”杰克说。

Shading their eyes from the bright sunlight, Jack and Annie squinted out the window. They had landed in the spiky crown of a palm tree. It was the tallest tree in a clump of palm trees. Thorny shrubs and sparse green grass grew under the trees. A small spring bubbled up from the ground. Surrounding the clump of trees were miles and miles of scorching sand. 他们用手遮住明亮的阳光，眯着眼睛从窗户往外看。 他们降落在一棵棕榈树的尖刺状树冠上。 这是这一丛棕榈树中最高的那棵树。 树下长着带刺的灌木和稀疏的绿草。 地上冒出了一小股泉水。 围绕着这丛树的是数英里数英里炽热的沙地。

“This doesn’t look like a golden age to me,” said Annie. “这看起来不像是黄金时代，”安妮说。

“Yeah, and where’s Baghdad?” asked Jack. He picked up their research book and opened it to the first page. He read aloud: “是啊，巴格达在哪里呢？”杰克问。 他捡起他们的研究书，翻开到第一页。他大声读道：

From 762 AD to 1258 AD, the Arab world had a golden age. During that time, a ruler known as a caliph (say KAY-liff) governed an empire that stretched for thousands of miles. The capital of the Arab empire was the city of Baghdad, an important center for learning and trade. 从公元762年到公元1258年，阿拉伯世界迎来了黄金时代。在那个时期，一位被称为哈里发（发音为KAY-liff）的统治者管理着一个横跨数千英里的帝国。阿拉伯帝国的首都是巴格达，这是一个重要的学习和贸易中心。

Jack looked up. “So the caliph is a ruler,” he said, “and he probably lived in Baghdad.” 杰克抬起头。 “所以哈里发是一个统治者，”他说，“他可能住在巴格达。”

“Yeah, but how do we get there?” asked Annie. “是啊，但我们要怎么去那儿呢？”安妮问。

“Patience,” said Jack. “Remember on our last mission, we learned that we have to do things in order, one thing at a time.” He read the first part of Merlin’s instructions. “要有耐心，”杰克说。 “还记得上次任务吗？我们学到必须按顺序做事，一次做一件事。”他读了梅林指示的第一部分。

Ride a ship of the desert

on a cold starry night.

Ride through the dust

and hot morning light. 在寒冷的星光夜晚，乘坐沙漠的船。穿过尘土和炎热的晨光。

“I wonder what a ‘ship of the desert’ is,” said Jack, looking up. “我想知道‘沙漠之船’是什么，”杰克抬头说。

“Well, whatever it is, I’m sure we’ll find it eventually,” Annie said slowly, as if she were trying to sound patient. “We could just sit here and keep an eye out for a big boat. Or …” “不管它是什么，我相信我们最终会找到的，”安妮慢慢地说，好像她正努力表现得有耐心。 “我们可以坐在这里，一直留意有没有大船出现。或者……”

“Or what?” said Jack. “或者什么？”杰克问。

“Maybe we could use one of Teddy and Kathleen’s magic rhymes.” “也许我们可以用泰迪和凯瑟琳的魔法童谣。”

“Not yet,” said Jack. “Merlin said to use our magic wisely. We just got here. We used two rhymes on our last mission. And we only have eight left to divide between three—” “现在还不行，”杰克说。 “梅林说过要明智地使用魔法。我们才刚到这里。” 我们上次任务用了两个押韵的句子。 我们只剩下八个要分给三个人——

“Okay, okay—” Annie broke in. “We can only use a rhyme when there’s absolutely nothing else to do, right?” “好吧，好吧——”安妮插话道。 “我们只有在真的别无选择的时候才能用押韵的句子，对吧？”

“Right,” said Jack. “对，”杰克说。

“So …,” said Annie. “What do you think we should do?” “所以……，”安妮说。 “你觉得我们该怎么做呢？”

“We could start walking,” said Jack. “我们可以开始走路，”杰克说。

“Walk where?” said Annie. “Which way is Baghdad?” “往哪儿走？”安妮说。 “巴格达在哪个方向？”

Jack looked out the window. Beyond the palm trees there was nothing but sand and sky. In the distance were lonely dunes. The desert was eerily silent. 杰克望向窗外。 棕榈树后面，除了沙子和天空什么也没有。 远处是一片孤独的沙丘。 沙漠里寂静得让人害怕。

“We could, uh …” Jack couldn’t think of anything else they could do. “We could look in the rhyme book,” he said. Jack pulled the book of magic rhymes out of his pack. He and Annie read down the table of contents together. “我们呃……”杰克想不出他们还能做别的事。 “我们可以看看韵律书，”他说。 杰克从背包里拿出那本魔法韵律书。 他和安妮一起读起了目录。

“Make a Stone Come Alive,” read Annie. “We did that on our last mission. We can’t do that again.” “让石头活起来，”安妮读道。 “我们在上次任务中做过这个。” “我们不能再这么做。”

“It wouldn’t help, anyway,” said Jack. He looked at other rhymes. “ Bend Iron,” he read.“We’ve already done that, too.” “反正也没用，”杰克说。 他看了看其他的押韵句子。 “弯曲铁器，”他读道。“我们已经做过这个了。”

“Turn into Ducks,” read Annie. She looked at Jack. “变成鸭子，”安妮读道。 她看了看杰克。

“No,” he said. “不行，”他说。

“Mend What Cannot Be Mended,” read Annie. “修补无法修补之物，”安妮读道。

“Nothing needs mending,” said Jack. “没什么需要修补的，”杰克说。

“How about this one?” said Annie. “ Make Helpers Appear out of Nowhere.” “那这个呢？”安妮说。 “让助手从 nowhere 出现。”

“Well …,” said Jack. “Maybe …” “嗯……，”杰克说。 “也许……”

“Come on, it’s perfect,” said Annie. “That’s where we are—nowhere. And we could sure use some helpers.” “来吧，这太完美了，”安妮说。 “我们就在 nowhere ——这里。 我们真的需要一些助手。”

“Okay,” said Jack. “I’ll read the line Teddy wrote. You read Kathleen’s line in her selkie language.” “好，”杰克说。 “我会读泰迪写的那句话。 你用凯瑟琳的海豹语读她写的那句。”

“Okay,” said Annie. She turned to the page with the rhyme. She held the book out to Jack. “好，”安妮说。 她翻到了有押韵的那一页。 她把书递给了杰克。

Jack read in a loud, clear voice: 杰克用响亮清晰的声音读道：

From far beyond, send helpers here! 从遥远的地方，派帮手来这里！

Then Annie read: 然后安妮接着读：

Hah-ee-by, hah-ee-keer!

The second that Annie finished the rhyme, wind gusted in from the desert, blowing a cloud of sand through the window. The wind shook the palm trees. Sand blew into Annie’s eyes. “Oww!” she said. 安妮刚读完押韵的句子，一阵风从沙漠吹进来，把一团沙子吹进了窗户。 风把棕榈树吹得摇晃起来。 沙子吹进了安妮的眼睛。 “啊！”她喊道。

“Get back!” cried Jack. “退回去！”杰克喊道。

Jack and Annie jumped away from the window. They pressed themselves against the wall and covered their faces. Gritty sand kept blowing into the tree house. 杰克和安妮赶紧跳开了窗户。 他们贴着墙，捂住了脸。 粗糙的沙子不停地吹进树屋里。

“It’s a sandstorm!” said Jack. “这是沙尘暴！”杰克说。

The hot sand piled into drifts all over the floor. Then the wind died down as quickly as it had started. The palm trees stopped shaking. 滚烫的沙子在地板上堆成了一堆堆。 然后，风就像刚才突然出现一样，突然停了下来。 棕榈树也不再摇晃了。

Jack and Annie looked out the window. The air was thick with grainy dust, making it hard to see. But the sand was still. 杰克和安妮从窗户往外看。 空气里充满了灰尘，看都看不清。 但沙子已经不再动了。

“I think it’s over,” said Annie. “我觉得结束了，”安妮说。

“I hope so,” said Jack. “Why did our magic rhyme cause a sandstorm instead of sending us helpers?” “但愿如此，”杰克说。 “为什么我们的魔法咒语引起沙尘暴，而不是召唤来帮手呢？”

“I don’t know,” said Annie. “Maybe we said it wrong.” “我不知道，”安妮说。 “也许我们念错了。”

Jack brushed the sand off their research book and looked up sandstorms in the index. He found the right page and read: 杰克把沙子从他们的研究书上擦掉，然后在目录里查找沙尘暴的信息。 他找到了正确的那一页，开始读起来：

The season of the sandstorms begins in the desert in mid-February and continues all spring. Winds can blow as fast as 40 miles per hour. Sandstorms can easily cause travelers to lose their way in the desert. 沙漠里的沙尘暴季节通常从二月中旬开始，一直持续到整个春天。风速可以达到每小时40英里。沙尘暴很容易让旅行者在沙漠中迷路。

“I don’t understand,” said Jack. “We don’t need to lose our way. We need to find our way.” “我不明白，”杰克说。 “我们不需要迷路。 我们需要找到回家的路。”

Just then the sound of bells came from outside. 就在这时，外面传来了钟声。

Jack and Annie looked out the window. Through the haze, they saw four riders perched high on the humps of camels. The riders wore brightly colored robes. Behind them a dozen more camels were tied head to tail and loaded down with saddlebags. As the camels swung from side to side, bells tinkled from around their necks. 杰克和安妮朝窗外望去。 透过朦胧的雾气，他们看到四名骑手高高骑在骆驼的驼峰上。 骑手们穿着色彩鲜艳的长袍。 他们身后还有十几头骆驼，头尾相连地被拴在一起，背上装满了马鞍包。 骆驼左右摇晃时，脖子上的铃铛叮当作响。

Annie grinned. “Helpers!” she said. 安妮露出了笑容。 “助手们！”她说。
