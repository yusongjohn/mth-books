8. Room of the Tree 8. 树屋的房间

“Nooooo!” cried Jack. “不——！”杰克喊道。

Jack ran to Beauty and yanked hunks of paper from her mouth. Annie dropped to her knees and picked up the torn pieces strewn around the camel’s big feet. 杰克跑到贝蒂身边，从她嘴里扯下一大团纸。 安妮跪下来，捡起散落在骆驼大脚边的撕碎的纸片。

“The treasure’s ruined!” moaned Annie. “宝藏毁了！”安妮呻吟道。

Jack was stunned. “I shouldn’t have left my bag here,” he said. 杰克惊呆了。 “我不应该把我的包留在这里，”他说。

“No, it’s all my fault. I shouldn’t have gone back and bragged to those kids,” wailed Annie. “不，都是我的错。 我不应该回去跟那些孩子炫耀，”安妮哭喊道。

“I should have acted more humble, like Merlin said.” Annie sounded like she was about to cry. “我应该像梅林说的那样更谦虚一些。”安妮听起来快要哭了。

“It’s okay,” said Jack. But he knew it wasn’t. The book was destroyed. They’d failed in their mission. “没关系，”杰克说。 但他是知道的，它没有被修复。 这本书被毁了。 他们没能完成任务。

“Maybe—maybe we can fix it,” said Annie. “也许——也许我们可以修复它，”安妮说。

Jack shook his head. “No, we can’t,” he said. “It’s completely destroyed. It can’t be fixed.” 杰克摇了摇头。 “不，我们不能，”他说。 “它已经被彻底毁掉了。 它无法被修复。”

Annie looked up. “What—what did you just say?” she said. 安妮抬头看着他。 “你——你刚刚说了什么？”她说。

“I said it can’t be fixed,” said Jack. “我说它修不好，”杰克说。

A big grin crossed Annie’s face. “Right! It can’t be fixed!” she said. “Help me gather up all these pieces!” 安妮脸上露出一个大大的笑容。 “对！ 它修不好！”，她说。 “帮我把所有这些碎片都捡起来！”，

“Why?” said Jack. Had Annie gone nuts? “为什么？”杰克说。 安妮是不是疯了？

“Just do it! Quick!” said Annie. “快点做！ 快！”安妮说。

Jack and Annie rushed around the stable, gathering up all the torn pages. 杰克和安妮在马厩周围跑来跑去，把所有撕碎的页面都捡了起来。

“Now pile everything over here on top of the cover and give me the book,” said Annie. “现在把所有东西都堆在这里，盖在书上，然后把书给我，”安妮说。

“What book?” said Jack. “什么书？”杰克说。

“The rhyme book!” said Annie. “押韵书！”安妮说。

“Oh. Oh!” said Jack. “Right!” He reached into his shoulder bag and pulled out Teddy and Kathleen’s book of magic rhymes. “哦。哦！”杰克说。 “对！”他伸手到他的肩包里，拿出泰迪和凯瑟琳的魔法押韵书。

Jack opened the book and read down the list. “Here it is: Mend What Cannot Be Mended,” he said. 杰克打开书，开始往下读。 “找到了：修补无法修补的东西，”他说。

“That’s the one,” said Annie. “就是这本，”安妮说。

Jack flipped through the pages until he came to the rhyme. He held up the book so he and Annie could both see. Standing in a shaft of sunlight, Jack read in a loud, clear voice: 杰克翻阅书页，直到找到那首押韵诗。 他举起书，让杰克和安妮都能看到。 杰克站在一束阳光下，大声、清晰地读着：

Heal what is broken beyond repair!
Annie read the second line:
Avi-bree-all! Fen-a-mare!

The paper bits fluttered. They began to swirl off the floor. They swirled up and around as if they were caught in a small tornado. The tornado captured all the torn pieces in its funnel and twisted them together. The papers swirled into a blur of blinding light. 纸片们飘了起来。 它们开始从地板上旋起来。 它们向上盘旋，好像被卷进了一个小龙卷风里。 龙卷风用它的漏斗状的风把所有撕碎的纸片都卷了进去，把它们拧在一起。 纸片们旋转着变成了一片刺眼的亮光。

Jack shielded his eyes. He heard a loud whoof! When he looked up, the bright tornado had vanished. 杰克用手遮住了眼睛。 他听到了一声巨响！ 当他抬起头时，那个明亮的龙卷风已经消失了。

Lying on the floor of the stable in front of Jack and Annie was the ancient book. 在杰克和安妮面前的马厩地板上，躺着那本古老的书。

Holding his breath, Jack carefully picked up the treasure. He opened the leather cover. “Oh, man,” he whispered. Aristotle’s priceless words of wisdom were neatly written on the yellow pages. There was no sign of a rip or tear anywhere. 屏住呼吸，杰克小心翼翼地捡起了宝藏。 他打开了皮革封面。 “哦，天哪，”他小声说。 亚里士多德珍贵的智慧之言整齐地写在泛黄的书页上。 到处都没有撕裂或破损的痕迹。

“Thank goodness,” breathed Annie. “谢天谢地，”安妮喘着气说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. “是啊，”杰克说。

“So what do we do now?” said Annie. “那我们现在该怎么办呢？”安妮说。

“I don’t know,” said Jack. “What’s the next thing Merlin tells us to do?” He pulled out Merlin’s letter and read: “我不知道，”杰克说。 “梅林接下来让我们做什么呢？”他拿出梅林的信，读道：

Beneath birds who sing

in the Room of the Tree,

greet a friend you once knew

and a new friend to be. 在树屋里唱歌的鸟儿下面，去问候一位你曾经认识的朋友，也去结识一位新朋友。

“What’s that mean?” said Jack. “那是什么意思？”杰克说。

Suddenly Jack heard someone behind them. He and Annie turned around. A girl was standing in the doorway of the stable. She wore a long white gown. Her hair and face were hidden by a veil. 突然，杰克听到有人在他们身后。 他和安妮转过身。 一个女孩正站在马厩的门口。 她穿着一件长长的白色长裙。 她的头发和脸被面纱遮住了。

“Hi,” said Annie. “Who are you?” “你好，”安妮说。 “你是谁？”

“I am a servant of the palace,” the girl whispered. “Come.” “我是宫殿的仆人，”女孩低声说。 “来吧。”

She motioned for them to follow her. 她做了个手势，让他们跟着她走。

“Let’s go,” Annie said to Jack. “我们走吧，”安妮对杰克说。

“Where is she taking us?” asked Jack. “And why is she whispering?” “她要带我们去哪儿？”杰克问。 “她为什么在小声说话？”

“I don’t know,” said Annie. “But I feel like we should follow her.” “我不知道，”安妮说。 “但是我觉得我们应该跟着她。”

“Okay,” said Jack. He put the book of wisdom and the book of magic rhymes into his bag. He slung the bag over his shoulder and followed Annie and the servant girl out of the stable. “好吧，”杰克说。 他把智慧之书和魔法韵律之书放进包里。 他把包甩到肩上，跟着安妮和那个女仆离开了马厩。

The three of them walked through the courtyard and through the palace doors. They walked down a wide candlelit hallway over a thick woven carpet. 他们三个人穿过庭院，走进了宫殿的大门。 他们沿着一条铺着厚地毯的、宽宽的、点着蜡烛的走廊走过去。

At the end of the hallway was a tall, carved door. A boy servant stood by the door. He wore baggy trousers and a long shirt. His face was almost completely hidden by his head cloth. He did not speak or look at them. 走廊尽头是一扇高高的、雕刻精美的门。 一个男孩仆人站在门旁边。 他穿着宽松的裤子和一件长衬衫。 他的脸几乎被头巾完全遮住了。 他没有说话，也没有看他们。

The servant girl stood close to Jack and Annie. In her strange, whispery voice, she said, “In front of the chair, bow to the floor. Do not look up and do not speak until you are spoken to.” 仆女站在杰克和安妮身边。 她用她那奇怪的、低声细气的声音说：“在椅子前面，跪下来，对着地板行礼。 不要抬头，也不要说话，直到有人跟你说话。”

“But what—” said Jack. “但是——”杰克说。

“Go quickly,” whispered the girl. “快点走，”女孩低声说。

The boy servant pulled open the heavy door. The girl servant pushed them into the room. 男孩仆人拉开沉重的门。 女孩仆人把他们推进了房间。

“Wait,” said Jack. “等一下，”杰克说。

But the boy servant closed the door behind them, leaving Jack and Annie alone. 但男孩仆人关上了门，把杰克和安妮独自留在了房间里。

“Jack, look! We’re here!” said Annie. “The Room of the Tree!” “杰克，看！ “我们到了！”安妮说。 “树屋的房间！”

Jack turned around. A giant tree stood in the center of the room. It had silver leaves that fluttered as if blown by the wind. Mechanical golden birds sang “Tweet-tweet!” from the silver branches. 杰克转过身。 一棵巨大的树站在房间的中央。 它有银色的叶子，随风轻轻飘动，就像被风吹过一样。 机械的金色小鸟从银色的树枝上唱着“Tweet-tweet！”。

Beneath the strange tree was an empty black chair. Jewels sparkled in its shiny wood. 奇怪的树下有一把空空的黑色椅子。 它的木头闪闪发亮，上面还闪着珠宝的光芒。

“We’re in the right place,” said Annie. She quoted from Merlin’s letter: “我们到对地方了，”安妮说。 她引用梅林的信上的话：

Beneath birds who sing

in the Room of the Tree,

greet a friend you once knew

and a new friend to be. 在树屋里唱歌的鸟儿下面，去见一位你曾经认识的朋友，也去结识一位新朋友。

“Another mystery solved,” Annie said. “另一个谜题解决了，”安妮说。

“Not really,” said Jack. “Where are the two friends?” “其实还没，”杰克说。 “那两个朋友在哪里？”

“I don’t know, but we better bow in front of the chair like that servant girl told us to,” said Annie. “我不知道，但我觉得我们应该像那个女仆告诉我们的那样，跪在椅子前面，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie knelt in front of the shiny black chair. They bowed their heads to the floor. Jack gripped his bag with the treasure inside. 杰克和安妮跪在那把闪亮的黑色椅子前面。 他们低头鞠躬到地板上。 杰克紧紧握着装有宝藏的袋子。

“Remember what else she said,” Annie reminded Jack. “Don’t look up and don’t speak until we’re spoken to.” “还记得她还说了什么吗，”安妮提醒杰克。 “不要抬头，也不要说话，直到有人跟我们说话。”

“But there’s no one here,” said Jack. He felt foolish, bowing in front of a chair and listening to the chirping of mechanical birds. Why are we doing this? he wondered. “但这里什么人也没有，”杰克说。 他觉得有点傻，对着一把椅子鞠躬，还听着机械鸟的叽叽喳喳声。 我们为什么要做这些呢？他心想。

The door opened with a low creaking sound. Jack squeezed his eyes shut. He heard footsteps moving past them. 门发出低沉的吱呀声打开了。 杰克把眼睛紧紧闭上。 他听到有人从他们身边走过。

“How did you get into the throne room?” a deep, gruff voice asked. “你们是怎么进到王座室的？”一个低沉而粗哑的声音问。

“A servant brought us here,” said Annie. “是一个仆人带我们来的，”安妮说。

“And why have you come?” asked the voice. “你们为什么来这里？”那个声音问。

“We have a priceless treasure to give to the caliph of Baghdad,” said Jack, keeping his head down. “It’s a book of wisdom.” “我们有一件无价之宝要送给巴格达的哈里发，”杰克低头说道。 “这是一本智慧之书。”

Jack fumbled in his bag and pulled out the book. He kept his eyes tightly shut as he held the book up toward the voice. 杰克在袋子里摸索着，拿出那本书。 他紧紧闭着眼睛，把书举向那个声音。

There was a long silence. 房间里一片长久的沉默。

“We hope it will help the caliph spread wisdom to the world,” said Annie. “我们希望它能帮助哈里发把智慧传播到世界各地，”安妮说。

“How did you come to be in possession of this book?” the man asked. “你怎么会拥有这本书？”那人问。

“We were helping a friend,” said Annie. “And we got separated in a sandstorm.” “我们在帮助一个朋友，”安妮说。 “然后我们在沙尘暴中分开了。”

“Ah, then I suppose you brought this book to Baghdad to gain a reward,” said the man. “啊，那么我想你们是带着这本书去巴格达，为了得到奖励，”那人说。

“No! No, we didn’t,” said Jack. “It was our mission to give it to the caliph.” “不！” “不，我们没拿。”杰克说。 “我们的任务就是把它交给哈里发。”

“Surely you must be seeking some payment in exchange for this treasure,” said the man. “Would you not like precious perfumes gathered from the sweetest flowers on earth?” “你一定想要用这个宝藏换取一些报酬吧，”那人说。 “你难道不想用地球上最甜美的花朵制成的珍贵香水吗？”

“No thank you,” said Annie. “不用了，谢谢。”安妮说。

“Rubies as large as hens’ eggs?” said the man. “红宝石有母鸡蛋那么大？”那人说道。

“No thanks,” said Jack. “不用谢，”杰克说道。

“You would not accept the book’s weight in gold?” said the man. “你不会接受用这本书的重量来换黄金吗？”那人说道。

“We don’t need a reward, really,” said Jack. “我们真的不需要奖励，”杰克说道。

“Since he likes books so much, why don’t you just tell the caliph to use his gold and rubies to buy more of them?” said Annie. “既然他这么喜欢书，你为什么不告诉哈里发，让他用他的黄金和红宝石去买更多的书呢？”安妮说道。

Again, there was silence. Then the man cleared his throat. When he spoke, his voice was gentle and familiar-sounding. “Look up at me, Jack and Annie,” he said. 再次，房间里一片沉默。 然后，那人清了清嗓子。 当他说话时，声音温柔又熟悉。 “杰克和安妮，抬头看看我，”他说。

Jack opened his eyes. He slowly lifted his head. First he saw gleaming gold slippers … then a long white robe with gold trim … then a familiar face. 杰克睁开了眼睛。 他慢慢抬起头。 他先看到闪闪发光的金拖鞋……然后是一件长长的白色长袍，上面有金色的装饰……然后是一张熟悉的面孔。

Jack gasped. He couldn’t believe his eyes. The man wasn’t a scary person at all. The man was Mamoon! 杰克惊呼起来。 他简直不敢相信自己的眼睛。 这个人一点都不可怕。 这个人是马穆恩！
