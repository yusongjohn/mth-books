10. Before the Moon Rises 10. 月亮升起之前

Jack and Annie looked around the big room. The scholars and scientists were all deep into their reading. 杰克和安妮环顾着这个大房间。 学者们和科学家们都沉浸在阅读中。

“I can’t believe Mamoon is the caliph,” whispered Jack. “我简直不敢相信马穆恩是哈里发，”杰克小声说。

“Remember that life is full of surprises,” Annie repeated from Merlin’s letter. “记住，生活中充满惊喜，”安妮重复着梅林的信上的话。

“Yeah, and Return to the tree house before the moon rises, ” Jack finished. “没错，而且要在月亮升起之前回到树屋，”杰克补充道。

“I almost forgot that part,” said Annie. “我差点忘了这部分，”安妮说。

“Me too,” said Jack. “我也是，”杰克说。

“Shh!” said one of the scholars and scientists. “嘘——！”一位学者和科学家说道。

“Sorry,” said Annie. “对不起，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie looked up at the open window. The sky was pink. The sun would be setting soon. “We have to get back to the tree house,” Annie whispered, “before the moon rises.” 杰克和安妮抬头看向敞开的窗户。天空是粉红色的。 太阳很快就要落山了。 “我们必须回到树屋，”安妮小声说，“在月亮升起之前。”

“I know,” said Jack, “but how?” He felt a moment of panic. The tree house is really far away, he thought. If we traveled on Beauty and Cutie, it would take a whole day and night to get there. And what about sandstorms? And bandits? Jack looked at Annie. “我知道，”杰克说，“但是怎么回去呢？”他感到一阵恐慌。 他想，树屋真的离这儿很远。 如果他们骑着美美和小可爱去的话，要花整整一天一夜才能到那儿。 那沙尘暴呢？还有强盗呢？ 杰克看着安妮。

She was smiling. Magic, she mouthed. 她正在微笑。 魔法，她小声说。

Jack caught his breath and nodded. They both looked around to see if any of the scholars or scientists were watching. They weren’t. 杰克屏住呼吸，点了点头。 他们环顾四周，看看有没有学者或科学家在看着他们。没人。

Jack quietly slipped Teddy and Kathleen’s book out of his bag. He and Annie turned their backs to the others and opened the book to the table of contents. 杰克悄悄地从包里拿出泰迪和凯瑟琳的书。 他和安妮背对着其他人，翻开书的目录。

Annie pointed to Turn into Ducks. 安妮指着‘变成鸭子’那一页。

Jack gave her a look. 杰克看了她一眼。

Annie pointed to Fly Through the Air. 安妮指着‘飞上天空’那一页。

“Yes!” said Jack. “是的！”杰克说。

“Shh!” said a scholar. “嘘——！”一位学者说。

Jack turned to the right page. He held up the book so they both could see. 杰克翻到了右边的那一页。 他举起书，让他们两个都能看到。

Jack said the first line of the rhyme: 杰克念了这首童谣的第一句：

Sail through the sky where you wish to go! 飞向天空，去你想去的地方！

Annie said the second: 安妮念了第二句：

Sawn-ee-fore, sawn-ee-row! 锯——尼——福，锯——尼——罗！

“You must be quiet, or you will have to leave the library!” one of the scholars said grouchily. “你们必须安静，否则就得离开图书馆！”，一位学者不高兴地说。

“Don’t worry, we’re going,” said Annie. “别担心，我们这就走，”安妮说。

A wind blew through the tall open window, fluttering the pages of the Arabian Nights book. The scholars and scientists grabbed their papers before they blew away. 一阵风吹过高高的窗户，让《一千零一夜》这本书的页面哗哗作响。 学者们和科学家们赶紧抓住他们的纸，以免被吹走。

The wind picked up a corner of the small carpet Jack and Annie were standing on. The carpet rippled. Jack and Annie fell forward. As they tried to stand, the carpet lifted off the floor. 风掀起了一角小地毯，杰克和安妮正站在上面。 地毯开始波动。 杰克和安妮向前摔倒了。 当他们试图站起来时，地毯离开了地面。

“Oh!” cried all the scholars and scientists. “哦！”学者们和科学家们喊道。

The carpet began floating up. It rose above the long tables. It rose above the shelves of books. Everyone jumped out of their chairs and scrambled out of the way, shouting, “Help!”— “Move!”—“Watch out!”—“Impossible!”—“What is happening?” 地毯开始向上飘。 它升到了长长的桌子上方。 它升到了书架上方。 每个人都从椅子上跳起来，慌乱地躲开，喊着：“帮忙！”——“让开！”——“小心！”——“不可能！”——“发生了什么？”

“Bye!” called Annie. “再见！”安妮喊道。

The carpet floated to the tall open window and glided out of the House of Wisdom. 地毯飘到了高高的敞开的窗户前，然后滑出了智慧之屋。

Chilly air blew against Jack and Annie. Their head cloths flapped wildly as they clung to the end of the carpet and sailed through the sky. 寒冷的风吹到了杰克和安妮身上。 他们的头巾在空中疯狂地飘动，他们紧紧抓住地毯的一端，像船一样在天空中航行。

“This is great!” cried Jack. “太棒了！”杰克喊道。

“Really great!” shouted Annie. “真的太棒了！”安妮大叫。

The carpet soared over the House of Wisdom, over the caliph riding back to his palace in his carriage, and over the camel stable. 地毯飞过了智慧之屋，飞过了骑着马车返回宫殿的哈里发，还飞过了骆驼棚。

The carpet zipped over the green dome with the horse on top. It flew over the courtyard, where the boys were still playing ball, over the third wall, and past the green field and the grand avenue. 地毯迅速飞过了顶部有马的绿色圆顶。 它飞过了庭院，那里的男孩们还在玩球，飞过了第三堵墙，飞过了绿色的田野和宽阔的大道。

The carpet flew over the second wall, the houses, the hospitals, and the hundred lions in the zoo. It flew over the first wall, the arched bridge, and the moat. 地毯飞过了第二堵墙、房子、医院，还有动物园里的上百只狮子。 它飞过了第一堵墙、拱形的桥，还有护城河。

The carpet zoomed over the bazaar with the maze of stalls, the shoemakers, potters, and weavers. It sailed high over the road to Baghdad. Below, Jack and Annie saw the men driving donkey carts, the boys leading sheep, and the women carrying pots. 地毯飞过了集市，绕过了摆满摊位的迷宫，绕过了鞋匠、陶匠和织布工。 它高高飞过了通往巴格达的路。 下面，杰克和安妮看到男人们赶着驴车，男孩们牵着羊，女人们扛着水罐。

The carpet flew faster and faster and faster—

over the river, the grazing fields,

and the dunes with the whistling sands,

over the red glowing desert

toward the setting sun

and the small oasis

in the middle of nowhere. 地毯飞得越来越快，越来越快——飞过了河流、放牧的田野、有鸣沙的沙丘，飞过了红色发光的沙漠，朝向落日和荒野中央的小绿洲。

The magic carpet slid smoothly onto the scrubby grass, near the small spring and the thorny shrubs, near the date palms and the rope ladder. 魔法地毯平稳地滑到了长满杂草的草地上，靠近小泉眼和带刺的灌木，靠近椰枣树和绳梯。

The desert looked as if it were on fire with golden red light. Jack felt giddy. “That—that was so fast!” he said. “I can’t believe we made it all the way here.” 沙漠看起来好像着火了一样，金色的红光闪闪发亮。杰克感觉晕晕的。 “那——那也太快了！”他说。 “我简直不敢相信我们竟然一路到这里了。”

“No kidding,” said Annie. “The magic must have helped us stay on.” “可不是嘛，”安妮说。 “一定是魔法帮我们保持在轨道上。”

She and Jack tried to stand. They teetered and fell against each other. 她和杰克努力站稳。 他们摇摇晃晃，撞在了一起。

“Steady,” said Annie, giggling. “You okay?” “稳住，”安妮笑着说。 “你没事吧？”

“Perfect,” said Jack. He put his bag over his shoulder and staggered off the carpet. Then he and Annie headed over to the tallest date palm. Jack pulled the rope ladder from behind the trunk, and they climbed up. “很好，”杰克说。 他把背包挎在肩上，摇摇晃晃地走下了地毯。 然后他和安妮朝最高的枣椰树走去。 杰克从树干后面拿出梯子，他们爬了上去。

When they got inside the tree house, Jack took out Merlin’s letter. He glanced out the window one last time. 他们一进树屋，杰克就拿出了梅林的信。 他最后看了一眼窗外。

The sun was gone. The carpet looked small and ordinary in the shadows beneath the palm tree. The desert looked vast and silent and lonely. A thin crescent moon had appeared in the sky. 太阳不见了。 地毯在棕榈树下的阴影里看起来又小又普通。 沙漠看起来又大又安静又孤单。 天空中出现了一弯细细的月牙。

“Return to the tree house before the moon rises,” said Jack. “在月亮升起之前回到树屋，”杰克说。

“That was our last instruction from Merlin,” said Annie. “We’re all done.” “那是梅林给我们的最后一条指令，”安妮说。 “我们全都做完了。”

Jack looked down at Merlin’s letter. He pointed at the words Jack and Annie of Frog Creek. “ I wish we could go home!” he said. 杰克低头看着梅林的信。 他指着上面写着“杰克和安妮来自蛙溪镇”的字。 “我希望我们能回家！”他说。

The wind started to blow. 风开始吹起来了。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转起来。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。

The Frog Creek woods were chilly in the afternoon air. Jack and Annie were dressed in their jeans and jackets again. Jack’s shoulder bag was a backpack. 下午的空气让蛙溪镇的森林变得有点冷。 杰克和安妮又穿上了他们的牛仔裤和夹克。 杰克的肩膀包是一个背包。

“Good trip,” Jack said simply. “这次旅行真棒，”杰克简单地说。

Annie nodded. “Really cool,” she said. 安妮点了点头。 “真的很酷，”她说。

“I guess we should get home,” said Jack. “I have lots of homework to finish.” “我想我们应该回家了，”杰克说。 “我还有很多作业要完成。”

“Leave the research book. But don’t forget to take Teddy and Kathleen’s rhyme book for safekeeping,” said Annie. “把研究书留下。 但别忘了带上泰迪和凯瑟琳的押韵书，用来保管好，”安妮说。

Jack reached into his backpack. He pulled out the book on the golden age of Baghdad and placed it on the tree house floor. Then he threw his pack over his shoulder and climbed down the ladder. Annie followed. Together they walked through the early-spring woods. 杰克伸手进了他的背包。 他拿出一本关于巴格达黄金时代的书，放在树屋的地板上。 然后他把背包甩到肩上，爬下梯子。安妮跟着下来了。 他们一起穿过早春的树林。

“We passed Merlin’s second test,” said Annie. “We helped spread wisdom to the world…. That’s a pretty big deal.” “我们通过了梅林的第二次测试，”安妮说。 “我们帮助把智慧传播到世界各地……” 这可是个大事情。

“Be humble,” Jack reminded her. “要谦虚，”杰克提醒她。

“Well, I guess Teddy and Kathleen’s book did the hard stuff for us,” Annie said humbly. “嗯，我想泰迪和凯瑟琳的书帮我们做了最难的事，”安妮谦虚地说。

“I miss them,” said Jack. “我好想他们，”杰克说。

“Me too,” said Annie. “But I think they might have been with us in Baghdad.” “我也是，”安妮说。 “但是我觉得他们可能曾经和我们在巴格达一起待过。”

“What do you mean?” said Jack. “你是说啥？”杰克说。

“Remember that servant girl and boy who took us to the caliph?” said Annie. “They sort of came out of nowhere. And we never actually saw their faces, did we?” “还记得那个带我们去见哈里发的仆人女孩和男孩吗？”安妮说。 “他们好像是从 nowhere 出现的。 “我们其实从来没见过他们的脸，对吧？”

“No … ”, said Jack. “You think?” “不……”杰克说。 “你认为？”

Annie shrugged. “Maybe.” 安妮耸了耸肩。 “也许吧。”

Jack smiled and took a deep breath. “Maybe,” he said softly. 杰克微笑着深吸了一口气。 “也许吧，”他轻声说道。

“Two missions and five rhymes left,” said Annie. “I hope Merlin sends for us again soon.” “还有两次任务和五句押韵诗没完成，”安妮说。 “我希望梅林很快会再召唤我们。”

“But not too soon,” said Jack. “I need to do my homework first.” “但不要太快，”杰克说。 “我得先做作业。”

Annie laughed. “Math with Arabic numerals?” she said. 安妮笑了。 “用阿拉伯数字做数学题？”她说。

“Right,” said Jack. “And maybe tomorrow we’ll go to the library and see if they have any books of Aristotle’s wisdom.” “对，”杰克说。 “也许明天我们去图书馆，看看有没有亚里士多德智慧的书。”

“Good idea,” said Annie. “好主意，”安妮说。

A cool spring breeze rustled the trees, and Jack and Annie hurried toward home. 一阵凉爽的春风拂过树梢，杰克和安妮赶紧朝家跑回去。
