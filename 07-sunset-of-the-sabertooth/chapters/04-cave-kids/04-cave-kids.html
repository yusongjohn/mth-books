4. Cave Kids 4. 洞穴孩子

Jack and Annie crept to the cave and peeked inside. 杰克和安妮悄悄走到洞口，往里偷看。

A small flame danced from a bed of glowing coals. 一小簇火焰在一层发着红光的炭火上跳动。

Near the fire were knives, axes, and hollowed-out stones. 火堆旁边有刀子、斧头，还有挖空的石头。

Animal skins were neatly stacked against the wall. 兽皮整整齐齐地靠墙堆着。

“People must live here,” said Annie. “一定有人住在这儿，”安妮说。

“Maybe it’s the home of the Cro-Magnons we saw,” said Jack, looking around. “也许就是我们看见的那些克罗马农人的家，”杰克说，一边环顾四周。

“Let’s go inside and get warm,” said Annie. “我们进去暖和暖和吧，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie moved quickly to the fire and warmed their hands. 杰克和安妮很快走到火边，暖着手。

Their shadows danced on the stone walls. 他们的影子在石壁上跳动。

Jack pulled out his Ice Age book. He found a picture of a cave. He read: 杰克拿出他的冰河时代书。 他找到一幅山洞的图。他读道：

Cro-Magnons made many things from animals, plants, and stone. They made flute-like musical instruments from mammoth bones. They made ropes by braiding plant fibers. They made axes and knives from stone. 克罗马农人用动物、植物和石头做出许多东西。 他们用猛犸象的骨头做成像笛子一样的乐器。 他们把植物纤维编成绳子。 他们用石头做成斧头和刀子。

Jack pulled out his notebook and pencil. He started a list: 杰克拿出笔记本和铅笔。 他开始列清单：

图中文字：Cro. Magnons made:

boneflutes

plant ropes

stone axes and knives 图中文字：克罗马农人制作了：骨笛、植物绳索、石斧和石刀

“Ta-da!” said Annie. “嗒嗒！”安妮说。

Jack looked up. Annie was wearing a coat. 杰克抬起头。 安妮穿着一件外套。

It had a hood and long sleeves. It went all the way down to her sneakers. 它有个兜帽，还有长袖子。 外套一直垂到她的运动鞋上。

“Where did you get that?” said Jack. “你从哪儿弄来的？”杰克说。

“From that pile of furry skins,” said Annie, pointing. “These must be their clothes. Maybe they’re being mended.” “从那堆毛茸茸的皮子里，”安妮说，指着那边。 “这些一定是他们的衣服。 也许是拿来缝补的。

She picked up another coat and handed it to Jack. 她又拿起一件外套递给杰克。

“Try one. It’s really warm,” she said. “试试这件。 真的很暖和，”她说。

Jack put his backpack and towel down on the hard dirt floor. He slipped on the coat. 杰克把背包和毛巾放在硬邦邦的泥地上。 他穿上外套。

It did feel very warm—and soft. 确实感觉很暖和——而且很柔软。

“We look like cave kids,” said Annie. “我们看起来像洞穴孩子，”安妮说。

Squeak. Peanut peeked out of Jack’s pack lying on the floor. 花生从地板上杰克的背包里探出头来。

“You stay in there,” said Annie. “There’s no teeny coat for you.” “你待在里面，”安妮说。 “没有给你的小小外套。”

Peanut vanished back into the pack. 花生又缩回了背包。

“I wonder how they made these coats,” said Jack. “真不知道他们是怎么做这些外套的，”杰克说。

He turned the pages in the book until he found a picture of Cro-Magnon women sewing. He read: 他翻着书页，直到找到一幅克罗马农女人在缝衣服的图。他读道：

Cro-Magnons scraped reindeer skins with flint rocks to make them soft. They used bone needles to sew the skins together for clothing. 克罗马农人用燧石刮驯鹿皮，好让皮子变软。 他们用骨针把兽皮缝在一起做衣服。

Jack added to his list: 杰克在清单上又加了几条：

图中文字：reindeer-skin clothes 图中文字：驯鹿皮衣服

“I hope the cave people won’t mind if we borrow their coats,” said Jack. “希望穴居人不会介意我们借他们的外套，”杰克说。

“Maybe we should give them our towels,” said Annie. “To thank them.” “也许我们应该把毛巾送给他们，”安妮说。 “表示感谢。”

“Good idea.” “好主意。”

“And my goggles, too,” said Annie. “还有我的护目镜，”安妮说。

They left their gifts on top of the rest of the animal skins. 他们把礼物放在其余兽皮的上面。

“Let’s explore the cave before they come home,” said Jack. “趁他们还没回家，我们先去探探这个洞吧，”杰克说。

“It’s too dark in the back,” said Annie. “We won’t be able to see anything.” “后面太黑了，”安妮说。 “我们什么也看不见。”

“I’ll find out how Cro-Magnons saw in the dark,” said Jack. “我查查克罗马农人在黑暗中怎么看东西，”杰克说。

He opened the Ice Age book. He found a picture of cave people holding odd-looking lamps. He read aloud to Annie: 他打开冰河时代书。 他找到一幅画，洞里的人拿着样子古怪的灯。 他大声读给安妮听：

Cro-Magnons made stone lamps. They hollowed out a rock, filled it with animal fat, then burned a wick made from moss. 克罗马农人制作石灯。 他们把石头挖空，填进动物脂肪，再点燃用苔藓做成的灯芯。

“There!” said Annie. She pointed to two stones near the fire. In the hollow of each was gooey white stuff and a pile of moss. “那儿！”安妮说。 她指着火堆旁的两块石头。 每块石头的凹处里都有黏糊糊的白色东西，还有一堆苔藓。

“We have to be careful,” said Jack. “我们得小心，”杰克说。

He picked up one stone. It was smaller than a soup bowl, but much heavier. 他拿起一块石头。 它比汤碗还小，但重多了。

Jack held the stone close to the fire and lit the piece of moss. 杰克把石头凑近火，点燃了那块苔藓。

He lit another lamp and gave it to Annie. 他又点亮一盏灯，递给安妮。

“Carry it with two hands,” he said. “用两只手捧着，”他说。

“I know,” she said. “我知道，”她说。

Jack tucked the book under his arm. He and Annie carried their stone lamps to the back of the cave. 杰克把书夹在胳膊下。 他和安妮捧着石灯走向山洞深处。

“Hey, I wonder where this goes,” said Annie. She held her lamp up to an opening in the wall. “嘿，真不知道这通向哪儿，”安妮说。 她把灯举到墙上的一个开口处。

“I’ll check in the book,” said Jack. “我查查书，”杰克说。

He put down his lamp and flipped through the Ice Age book. 他放下灯，翻着冰河时代书。

“I think it’s a tunnel,” she said. “Be right back.” “我觉得这是一条隧道，”她说。 “马上回来。”

“Wait a second,” said Jack. “等一下，”杰克说。

Too late—she had squeezed into the opening and was gone. 太晚了——她已经挤进开口，不见了。

“Oh brother,” said Jack, sighing. “唉，”杰克叹了口气说。

He closed his book and peeked into the opening. 他合上书，朝开口里张望。

“Come back here!” he said. “回来！”他说。

“No! You come here! ” said Annie. Her voice sounded far away. “You won’t believe this!” “不！你过来！ “那就走吧！趁太阳还没落山！”安妮说。 她的声音听起来很远。 “你不会相信的！”

Jack picked up his lamp and book. He ducked into a small tunnel. 杰克拿起灯和书。 他弯腰钻进一条小隧道。

“Wow!” came Annie’s voice. “哇！”传来安妮的声音。

Jack could see her lamp flickering at the other end. 杰克能看见她的灯在另一头闪烁。

Crouching down, he hurried toward her. At the end of the tunnel was a huge cavern with a high ceiling. 他蹲着身子，朝她赶过去。 隧道尽头是一个顶很高的大洞穴。

Annie held her lamp close to the wall. 安妮把灯凑近洞壁。

“Look,” she said. Her voice echoed. “看，”她说。她的声音回荡着。

Animals were painted on the wall in strokes of red and black and yellow. 墙上用红、黑、黄的笔画画着动物。

There were cave bears and lions, elk and reindeer, bison and woolly rhinos and mammoths. 有洞熊和狮子，麋鹿和驯鹿，野牛、长毛犀牛，还有猛犸象。

In the flickering light, the prehistoric beasts looked alive. 在闪烁的灯光里，那些史前野兽看起来像活的一样。
