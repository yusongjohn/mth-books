2. Bones 2. 骨头

Jack, Annie, and Peanut looked outside. 杰克、安妮和花生向外面望去。

Snow was falling from a gray sky. 雪正从灰蒙蒙的天空中飘落。

The tree house was in the tallest tree in a grove of tall, bare trees. 树屋就在一片高大光秃的树林里，那棵最高的树上。

The grove was on a wide, white plain. Beyond the plain were high, rocky cliffs. 这片树林坐落在一片宽阔的白色平原上。 平原尽头是高高的、布满岩石的悬崖。

“I’m c-cold,” said Annie. Her teeth chattered. She wrapped her towel tightly around her. “我……我好冷，”安妮说。 她的牙齿在打颤。 她把毛巾紧紧裹在身上。

Sq- squeak. Peanut sounded cold, too. 吱——吱。 花生听起来也很冷。

“Poor mouse,” said Annie. “I’ll put you into Jack’s pack. You’ll be warmer there.” “可怜的小老鼠，”安妮说。 “我把你放进杰克的背包里。 那儿会暖和些。”

Annie slipped Peanut into the pocket of the backpack. 安妮把花生塞进背包的口袋里。

“We have to go home,” said Jack. “We need warmer clothes.” “我们得回家，”杰克说。 “我们需要更暖和的衣服。”

“We can’t go home,” said Annie. “We can’t find the Pennsylvania book. Not until our mission is complete. Remember? That’s the way the magic works.” “我们回不了家，”安妮说。 “我们找不到宾夕法尼亚那本书。 要等我们的任务完成才行。记得吗？ 魔法就是这样运作的。”

“Oh … right,” said Jack. He looked around. There was no sign of the Pennsylvania book that always took them home. “哦……对，”杰克说。他环顾四周。 那本总能带他们回家的宾夕法尼亚书，一点儿影子也没有。

Annie peered out the window again. “Where are we, anyway?” she asked. 安妮又朝窗外望去。 “我们到底在哪儿？”她问。

“I’ll find out,” said Jack. He picked up the open book and read the title on the cover. “Life in the Ice Age.” “我会查清楚的，”杰克说。 他拿起那本翻开的书，读了读封面上的书名。 《冰川时代的生活》。

“Ice Age?” said Annie. “No wonder we’re cold.” “冰河时代？”安妮说。 “怪不得我们这么冷。”

“We better find the third M thing soon,” said Jack. “Before we freeze to death.” “我们最好快点找到第三样 M 东西，”杰克说。 “免得我们冻死。”

“Look,” whispered Annie, “people.” She pointed out the window. “看，”安妮低声说，“有人。”她指着窗外。

Jack saw them, too: four figures on a cliff. Two big figures and two little ones—all holding long spears. 杰克也看见了：悬崖上有四个身影。 两个大的，两个小的——全都握着长矛。

“Who are they?” said Annie. “他们是谁？”安妮说。

“I’ll look in the book,” said Jack. “我查查书，”杰克说。

He found a picture of some people. He read the caption to Annie: 他找到了一幅画着一些人的图。 他把图注读给安妮听：

Early modern humans were called Cro-Magnons. During the late Ice Age in Europe, they sometimes lived in caves beneath cliffs. 早期的现代人被称为克罗马农人。 在欧洲冰川时代晚期，他们有时住在悬崖下的山洞里。

“Why are they carrying spears?” said Annie. “他们为什么拿着长矛？”安妮说。

Jack turned the page. He found another picture of the Cro-Magnons. He read aloud: 杰克翻过一页。 他又找到一幅克罗马农人的图。他大声读道：

The Cro-Magnon family often hunted together. They covered deep pits with branches. Then they drove reindeer and mammoths into the traps. 克罗马农人一家经常一起打猎。 他们用树枝盖住深深的坑。 然后他们把驯鹿和猛犸象赶进陷阱。

“Oh, trapping the animals—that’s sad,” said Annie. “哦，设陷阱抓动物——好难过，”安妮说。

“No, it’s not,” said Jack. “They couldn’t live without hunting. They didn’t have supermarkets, you know.” “不，不可悲，”杰克说。 “不打猎他们就没法活。 要知道，那时候可没有超市。

They watched the family disappear over the other side of the cliff. 他们看着那一家人消失在悬崖的另一边。

“Come on, I’m freezing,” said Jack. “Let’s hurry and find the M thing while the Cro-Magnons are hunting.” “快点，我快冻僵了，”杰克说。 “趁克罗马农人在打猎，我们赶紧去找那件 M 东西。”

“But I want to meet them,” said Annie. “可我想见见他们，”安妮说。

“Forget it,” said Jack. “They don’t have books that tell them about us. They’ll think we’re some enemy and hurl their spears.” “别想了，”杰克说。 “他们没有书可以告诉他们关于我们的事。 他们会把我们当成敌人，把长矛扔过来。

“Yikes,” said Annie. “哎呀，”安妮说。

Jack put his book away. 杰克把书收好。

Squeak. Peanut peeked out of the backpack. 花生从背包里探出头来。

“Stay in there,” said Annie. “待在里面，”安妮说。

Jack pulled on his pack and started down the rope ladder. 杰克背上背包，开始顺着绳梯往下爬。

Annie followed. 安妮跟在后面。

On the icy ground, they huddled together. 在结冰的地面上，他们挤成一团。

The wind was biting. Jack put his towel over his head. Snow blew against his glasses. 风很刺骨。 杰克把毛巾盖在头上。 雪花打在他的眼镜上。

“Hey, Jack,” said Annie. “Look at me.” “嘿，杰克，”安妮说。 “看看我。”

Annie had put on her swimming goggles. “Now I can see,” she said. 安妮戴上了她的游泳镜。 “现在我能看见了，”她说。

“Good thinking,” said Jack. “Now cover your head with your towel. Most of your body heat is lost through your head.” “好主意，”杰克说。 “现在用毛巾把你的头也盖上。 大部分体温都是从脑袋散掉的。

Annie wrapped her towel around her head. 安妮用毛巾裹住自己的头。

“We should find a cave or someplace warmer,” said Jack. “我们应该找个山洞，或者暖和一点的地方，”杰克说。

“I bet there are caves in those cliffs,” said Annie. “我打赌那些悬崖里有山洞，”安妮说。

She and Jack started across the white plain. The snow wasn’t deep yet. But the wind was blowing hard. 她和杰克开始穿过那片白色的平原。 雪还不太深。 可风刮得很猛。

“I told you!” Annie pointed to an opening in the rocks—a cave. “我早跟你说过！”安妮指着岩石上的一个缺口——那是个山洞。

They ran to it. 他们朝它跑去。

“Careful,” said Jack. They stepped carefully into the shadowy cave. “小心，”杰克说。 他们小心翼翼地走进阴暗的山洞。

It was only slightly warmer inside. But at least the wind wasn’t blowing. 里面只稍微暖和一点点。 但至少风不刮了。

In the gray light, they stamped the snow off their sneakers. 在灰蒙蒙的光线里，他们把运动鞋上的雪跺掉。

Annie took off her goggles. 安妮摘下护目镜。

“It smells in here,” said Jack. “这里有股味道，”杰克说。

“Yeah, like a wet dog,” said Annie. “是啊，像湿狗的味道，”安妮说。

“Let me see what I can find out,” said Jack. “让我看看能查出什么，”杰克说。

He pulled out the Ice Age book. 他拿出冰河时代那本书。

“I’ll look around,” said Annie. “Maybe the M thing is here. Then we can go home and get warm.” “我到处看看，”安妮说。 “也许那件 M 东西就在这儿。 然后我们就能回家暖和暖和了。”

Jack stood by the entrance so he could read the book. 杰克站在洞口，好能看书。

“This cave is filled with sticks,” Annie said. “这个洞里全是树枝，”安妮说。

“What?” said Jack. He didn’t look up from the book. “什么？”杰克说。 他头也没从书上抬起来。

“No, wait. I think they’re bones,” said Annie. “不，等等。 我觉得那是骨头，”安妮说。

“Bones?” echoed Jack. “骨头？”杰克跟着重复了一遍。

“Yeah. Lots of them back here. All over the floor.” “是啊。 后面有好多。 地板上到处都是。”

Jack turned the pages of his book. He found a picture of a cave filled with bones. 杰克翻着书页。 他找到一幅画着满是骨头的山洞的图。

“I hear something,” said Annie. “我听见什么声音了，”安妮说。

Jack read the writing below the picture of the cave. It said: 杰克读了读山洞图画下面的文字。上面写着：

The great cave bears of the Ice Age were over eight feet tall. These bears were larger and fiercer than today’s grizzlies. Their caves were filled with the bones of their ancestors. 冰川时代的巨洞熊身高超过八英尺。 这些熊比今天的灰熊更大，也更凶猛。 它们的洞穴里堆满了祖先的骨头。

“Annie!” whispered Jack. “Get back here now!” “安妮！”杰克低声说。 “马上回来！”

They were in the cave of a great cave bear! 他们进了一头大洞熊的洞穴！
