2. Sleepyhead 2. 瞌睡虫

Jack opened his eyes. Glaring hot sunlight flooded into the tree house. 杰克睁开眼睛。 刺眼的灼热阳光涌进了树屋。

“Neat hats,” said Annie. “帽子真好看，”安妮说。

She and Jack were both wearing hats. 她和杰克都戴着帽子。

“I think they will protect us from the sun,” said Jack. “我想它们能帮我们挡太阳，”杰克说。

He and Annie looked out the window. Teddy looked out, too. 他和安妮向窗外望去。 泰迪也往外看。

The tree house had landed in a scrubby forest filled with droopy plants and dry brown trees. 树屋落在一片灌木林里，到处是耷拉着的植物和干枯的褐色树木。

“Man, this place needs rain,” said Jack. “天哪，这地方真需要下雨，”杰克说。

He sat back on his heels and looked at the picture of where they had landed in the Australia book. 他蹲坐下来，看着澳大利亚书上他们着陆地点的图片。

He read: 在雨林里，许多生物都会融入周围的环境。

Australia’s forests go through times of drought (say DROWT). A drought is a long period of time without any rain. The same forest can be flooded by heavy rains at other times of the year. 澳大利亚的森林有时会经历干旱（说DROWT）。干旱是指很长一段时间没有下雨。同一片森林在一年的其他时候也可能被大雨淹没。

Jack pulled out his notebook and wrote:



 杰克拿出笔记本写道：

图中文字：drought = no rain 图中文字：干旱=没有下雨

“Hey, Jack,” said Annie. “Doesn’t it smell like a cookout?” “嘿，杰克，”安妮说。 “这味道是不是像在露天烧烤？”

Jack sniffed the air. It did smell like a cookout. 杰克闻了闻空气。 确实有烧烤的味道。

Jack looked out the window. A wisp of smoke floated above some trees in the distance. 杰克往窗外看。 远处一些树上方飘着一缕烟。

“Maybe people are camping over there,” Jack said. “也许那边有人在露营，”杰克说。

“Let’s go see,” said Annie. “咱们去看看，”安妮说。

Jack put his notebook and the Australia book into his backpack. 杰克把笔记本和澳大利亚书放进背包。

“Put Teddy in there, too,” said Annie. “也把泰迪放进去，”安妮说。

Jack slipped the little dog into the pack. Then he followed Annie down the ladder. 杰克把小狗塞进背包。 然后他跟着安妮下了梯子。

When they stepped onto the ground, the hot wind nearly blew their hats off. 他们踩到地上时，热风差点把帽子吹跑。

“The campers must be over there,” said Annie. “露营的人一定在那边，”安妮说。

She pointed at the smoke in the blue sky. They started walking across a sun-baked clearing. 她指着蓝天上的烟。 他们开始走过一片被阳光炙烤的空地。

They passed bushes and scrawny trees. Lizards ran over the dry, cracked ground. 他们走过灌木和瘦骨嶙峋的树木。 蜥蜴在干燥开裂的地面上跑来跑去。

Arf! Arf! Teddy barked from Jack’s pack. 汪！汪！ 泰迪从杰克的背包里叫着。

“Whoa!” said Jack. “哇！”杰克说。

A pair of huge, funny-looking birds walked out from behind a bush. 一对体型巨大、样子滑稽的鸟从灌木后面走了出来。

They were taller than Jack. They had fat bodies, long, skinny legs, and long, skinny necks. 它们比杰克还高。 它们身子肥肥的，却有着又长又细的腿和脖子。

“Who are you? ” Annie asked the strange pair. “你们是谁？ ”安妮问那奇怪的一对。

Jack opened his pack and took out the Australia book. He found a picture of the birds. 杰克打开背包，取出澳大利亚书。 他找到这些鸟的图片。

“They’re emus,” he said. He read aloud: “它们是鸸鹋，”他说。

The emu (say EE-myoo) is a large bird that doesn’t fly. It can run as fast as thirty miles per hour. 鸸鹋（发音为EE-myoo）是一种不会飞的大型鸟类，它奔跑的速度可以达到每小时30英里。

“Wow, that’s fast,” said Annie. “哇，真快，”安妮说。

Arf! Teddy jumped out of Jack’s backpack and barked at the strange birds. 汪！ 泰迪跳出杰克的背包，冲着奇怪的鸟叫。

The emus gave the little dog a haughty look. Then they turned and walked proudly away. 鸸鹋傲慢地看了小狗一眼。 然后它们转过身，骄傲地走开了。

Jack wrote in his notebook: 杰克在笔记本上写道：

图中文字：proud birds

don't fly 图中文字：骄傲的鸟儿不飞

“Look, a live teddy bear!” said Annie. “看，一只活的泰迪熊！”安妮说。

Jack looked up. 杰克抬起头。

Annie ran to a tree at the edge of the clearing. The “live teddy bear” was nestled in the fork of the tree. 安妮跑向空地边缘的一棵树。 那只“活泰迪熊”安卧在树杈上。

“Aww, it’s so cute!” whispered Annie. “啊，好可爱！”安妮小声说。

The creature was fast asleep. He had large round ears, a black nose, and a furry body. His feet had long, curved claws. 那小动物正睡得香。 它有大大的圆耳朵、黑鼻子和毛茸茸的身子。 它的脚上有又长又弯的爪子。

“It’s a koala bear,” said Jack. “是考拉，”杰克说。

“Hi, sleepyhead,” Annie said to the koala. “嗨，瞌睡虫，”安妮对考拉说。

She patted his soft fur. He opened his big eyes and looked calmly at her. 她拍了拍它软软的毛。 它睁开大眼睛，平静地看着她。

Jack found a koala picture in the Australia book. He read: 杰克在澳大利亚书里找到一张考拉图片。他读道：

The koala is actually not a bear at all. It’s a marsupial (say mar-SOUP-ee-ul), like a kangaroo. A marsupial mother carries her babies in a stomach pouch. 树袋熊其实根本不是熊。它是一种有袋动物（发音为mar-SOUP-ee-ul），就像袋鼠一样。有袋动物妈妈会把宝宝放在腹部的育儿袋里。

“That’s neat,” said Annie. “真棒，”安妮说。

Jack kept reading: 杰克继续读：

Koalas mostly eat the leaves of gum trees, so cutting down gum trees to clear land has hurt them. Wildfires are also a threat. Koalas are slow-moving and can’t escape the smoke and flames. 考拉主要吃桉树叶，所以砍掉桉树来清理土地伤害了它们。野火也是它们的威胁。考拉行动缓慢，无法逃离烟雾和火焰。

Jack pulled out his notebook and wrote:



 杰克拿出笔记本写道：

图中文字：wildfires are a threat to koalas 图中文字：野火对考拉来说是个威胁

“What’s wrong, sleepyhead?” Annie asked the koala. “Don’t you feel well?” “怎么了，瞌睡虫？”安妮问考拉。 “你不舒服吗？”

“Don’t worry,” said Jack. “Listen to this—” “别担心，”杰克说。 “听听这个——”

He read more from the book: 他又往下读了一些：

Koalas, like kangaroos, are active at night and sleep during the day, when the sun is hot. The name “koala” means “no drink,” because koalas rarely drink water. They get moisture from the leaves they eat. 考拉和袋鼠一样，晚上活动，白天睡觉，因为太阳很热。考拉的名字意思是“不喝水”，因为考拉很少喝水。它们从吃的食物叶子中获取水分。

Jack licked his lips. His mouth felt dry. 杰克舔了舔嘴唇。 他嘴里干干的。

“Speaking of water,” he said, “I’m thirsty.” “说到水，”他说，“我渴了。”

“Me, too,” said Annie. “我也是，”安妮说。

Teddy was panting, as if he was thirsty, also. 泰迪也在喘气，好像也很渴。

“Let’s find those campers,” said Jack, sighing. “Maybe they can give us some water.” “咱们去找那些露营的人，”杰克叹着气说。 “也许他们能给我们点水。”

Jack put Teddy back into his pack. He tucked the book under his arm, in case he needed to look something up. 杰克把泰迪放回背包。 他把书夹在胳膊下，以备随时查阅。

They began walking again. Suddenly, there was a loud, harsh cackle. 他们又开始往前走。 突然传来一声又大又刺耳的笑声。

“Yikes,” said Annie. “哎呀，”安妮说。

“What was that? ” said Jack. “那是什么？”杰克说。
