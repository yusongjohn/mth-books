8. The Rainbow Serpent 8. 彩虹蛇

The mother kangaroo bounded out of the trees. 袋鼠妈妈从树丛间跳了出来。

She landed ten feet away from Jack, Annie, Teddy, and Joey. 她落在离杰克、安妮、泰迪和乔伊大约十英尺远的地方。

They all were still for a moment, as if they all were holding their breath. 他们全都静止了一会儿，好像都屏住了呼吸。

Then Joey tried to jump out of Jack’s backpack. 然后乔伊想从杰克的背包里跳出来。

“Hold on,” said Jack. “等一下，”杰克说。

He put his pack on the ground. 他把背包放到地上。

The little kangaroo leaped out. 小袋鼠跳了出来。

He leaped again … then again … and dived headfirst into his mother’s pouch! 他又跳……再跳……一头扎进妈妈的育儿袋！

Joey turned himself over inside the pouch. Then he peeked out at Jack and Annie. 乔伊在育儿袋里翻过身来。 然后他朝杰克和安妮偷看。

“Yay!” said Jack and Annie together. They laughed and clapped with relief. “耶！”杰克和安妮一起说。 他们笑着拍手，松了一大口气。

“He looks happy to be home,” said Annie. “他回家看起来好开心，”安妮说。

“His mother looks happy, too,” said Jack. “他妈妈看起来也很开心，”杰克说。

The mother kangaroo was gazing down at her joey. She patted his head with her small paws. 袋鼠妈妈低头看着她的小袋鼠。 她用小爪子拍了拍他的头。

Then she looked at Jack and Annie with soft eyes. 然后她用柔和的眼睛看着杰克和安妮。

“She’s saying thank you to us,” Annie said. “她在向我们道谢，”安妮说。

“You’re welcome,” Jack said. “不客气，”杰克说。

“It was no problem,” Annie told the kangaroo. “You have a great joey.” “不客气，”安妮对袋鼠说。 “你有个很棒的小袋鼠。”

The kangaroo gave a little nod. Then she bent over and used a front paw to pick up a small piece of bark from the wet grass. 袋鼠轻轻点了点头。 然后她弯下身，用前爪从湿草里捡起一小片树皮。

The kangaroo held the piece of bark out to Jack and Annie. 袋鼠把那片树皮递给杰克和安妮。

Jack took it from her. 杰克从她手里接过它。

“Oh, man,” he whispered. “It’s our gift from a kangaroo.” “天哪，”他小声说。 “这是我们来自袋鼠的礼物。”

The kangaroo then sprang into the air. She bounded gracefully away through the charred forest. 袋鼠接着跃向空中。 她优雅地跳着，穿过烧焦的森林离开了。

“Thanks!” called Jack. “谢谢！”杰克喊道。

“Bye!” called Annie. “Good luck!” “再见！”安妮喊道。 “祝你们好运！”

Arf! Arf! Teddy barked. 汪！汪！ 泰迪叫着。

The rain stopped as Jack studied the piece of bark. There was a tiny painting on it. It was just like the snake painting in the cave. 雨停了，杰克仔细端详着那片树皮。 上面有一幅小小的画。 就像洞穴里的蛇画一样。

“I wonder what the snake means,” said Jack. “不知道这条蛇是什么意思，”杰克说。

Jack opened the wet cover of the Australia book. He carefully turned the damp pages. He found a picture of the snake painting. 杰克打开湿漉漉的澳大利亚书面。 他小心翻着潮湿的书页。 他找到蛇画的图片。

“Listen,” said Jack. He read: “听，”杰克说。他读道：

The first people of Australia are called “Aborigines” (say ab-uh-RIJ-uh-neez). They have lived there for 40,000 years. Their myths take place in a time they call “Dreamtime.” In Dreamtime, there is a Rainbow Serpent, who sends life-giving rain. 澳大利亚的第一批人被称为“Aborigines”（发音为ab-uh-RIJ-uh-neez）。他们在那里生活了四万年。他们的神话发生在他们称为“Dreamtime”的一个时代。在Dreamtime，有一条彩虹蛇，它会带来滋养生命的雨水。
Aborigine artists paint the Rainbow Serpent on cave walls or on pieces of bark. In special ceremonies, they sometimes honor the Rainbow Serpent by painting their handprints on the magic snake. 原住民艺术家在洞穴墙壁或树皮上绘制彩虹蛇。在特殊仪式上，他们有时会通过在魔法蛇上画手印来向彩虹蛇致敬。

“See?” said Annie. “That explains everything!” “看见了吧？”安妮说。 “这就解释了一切！”

“Explains what?” said Jack. “解释什么？”杰克说。

“We put our hands on the painting of the Rainbow Serpent,” she said. “It was like a special ceremony. So the Rainbow Serpent sent the rain to put out the wildfire.” “我们把手放在了彩虹蟒的画上，”她说。 “那就像一场特别的仪式。 所以彩虹蛇送来雨水扑灭了野火。”

Arf! Teddy barked. 汪！泰迪叫了一声。

Jack frowned. 杰克皱起眉。

“But it’s not a real creature,” he said. “It’s in Dream time. Not real time.” “可它不是真实存在的生物，”他说。 “那是在梦时。不是真实时间。”

Annie smiled. 安妮笑了。

“Then how do you explain that? ” she said. She pointed at the sky. “那你怎么解释那个？”她说。 她指向天空。

The rain clouds were gone. The sun had come back out. 雨云散了。 太阳又出来了。

A rainbow curved across the blue Australian sky. 一道彩虹弯过蓝色的澳大利亚天空。

“Oh, man,” whispered Jack. Though the air was warm again, he shivered. “哦，天哪，”杰克轻声说。 虽然空气又暖和了，他还是打了个寒颤。

“Teddy led us to the painting,” said Annie. “We should thank him.” “泰迪带我们找到了那幅画，”安妮说。 “我们应该谢谢他。”

“How did he know about the Rainbow Serpent in the cave?” Jack asked. “他怎么知道洞穴里的彩虹蛇？”杰克问。

“I told you,” said Annie. “He has a touch of magic.” “我跟你说过，”安妮说。 “他有一点魔法。”

They looked down at the little dog. Teddy tilted his head and seemed to smile. 他们低头看着小狗。 泰迪歪着头，好像在微笑。

“Hey, we have all four gifts now!” said Annie. “嘿，我们现在四样礼物都齐了！”安妮说。

“Oh, yeah!” said Jack. “哦，对！”杰克说。

“Let’s go home and see if Teddy’s spell is broken!” said Annie. “咱们回家，看看泰迪的咒语有没有解除！”安妮说。

Arf! Arf!

Jack put the bark painting and the Australia book in his pack. Then they all headed through the wet, steamy forest in the direction of the tree house. 杰克把树皮画和澳大利亚书放进背包。 然后他们一起穿过湿漉漉、热气腾腾的森林，朝树屋的方向走去。

“I hope the tree house didn’t get burned!” he said. “希望树屋没被烧掉！”他说。

They went past the clearing, past the gum trees and bushes. 他们经过空地，经过桉树和灌木丛。

The tree house was waiting for them. 树屋在等着他们。

“It’s still here! ” said Annie. “它还在这儿！”安妮说。

She grabbed the rope ladder and started up. 她抓住绳梯，开始往上爬。

Jack put Teddy in his pack and followed. 杰克把泰迪放进背包，跟着上去。

Inside the tree house, Teddy wiggled out of the pack. He pawed the Pennsylvania book. 在树屋里，泰迪扭着身子从背包里钻出来。 它用爪子去挠那本宾夕法尼亚的书。

Arf! Arf! 汪！汪！

“Okay, okay,” said Jack. He pointed at a picture of the Frog Creek woods. “I wish we could go there!” “好了，好了，”杰克说。 他指着蛙溪镇树林的图片。 “我希望我们能去那儿！”

“Over the rainbow!” said Annie. “越过彩虹！”安妮说。

And the wind started to blow. 风开始刮起来。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 绝对静止。
