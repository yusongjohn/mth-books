3. S-O-L-D O-U-T 3. S-O-L-D O-U-T

The boat splashed into the lake and shot under the arched bridge. Then it bounced into the air, splashing water everywhere. Jack closed his eyes and ducked his head. All the teenagers screamed. For a moment the boat seemed to fly! Then it landed back on the lake and steadied itself. 船溅入湖中，从拱形桥下疾驰而过。 然后它弹到空中，溅起四处的水花。 杰克闭上眼睛，低下头。 所有青少年都尖叫起来。 有一瞬间，小船好像在飞！ 然后它又落回湖面，稳稳地停了下来。

Jack opened his eyes and looked around. 杰克睁开眼睛，环顾四周。

Annie was laughing with the other passengers as the boat glided peacefully across the water. Everyone seemed to be having fun. But Jack’s heart was thumping and his stomach felt queasy. 安妮和其他乘客一起笑着，小船平稳地在水面上滑行。 每个人都好像玩得很开心。 但杰克的心跳得很快，胃里感觉有点不舒服。

The boatman docked the boat and helped everyone onto the landing. The passengers scattered in different directions. 船夫把船靠岸，帮大家上了码头。 乘客们朝不同的方向散开了。

“Okay! Time to go to the theater!” said Annie. “好了！ 该去剧院了！”安妮说道。

“Wait, I need to sit for a minute,” said Jack. “等等，我需要坐一会儿，”杰克说。

“I thought you were desperate to get our tickets,” said Annie. “我以为你很急着要我们的票，”安妮说。

“In a minute,” said Jack. He pointed to a terrace that bordered the lake. “How about we sit over there?” “一会儿就好，”杰克说。 他指着湖边的一座露台。 “我们去那边坐怎么样？”

“Sure,” Annie said. “Are you okay?” “当然可以，”安妮说。 “你没事吧？”

“I will be,” said Jack. “Come on.” “我会没事的，”杰克说。 “快点。”

Jack and Annie climbed a couple of steps to the terrace and sat at the end of a crowded bench. All around them, people in costumes were hawking tickets to different rides. 杰克和安妮爬上了几级台阶，来到露台上，坐在一个拥挤长椅的尽头。 他们周围，穿着各种服装的人们正在兜售不同游乐设施的门票。

“Step this way, ladies and gentlemen! Enter the Dragon’s Gorge! Greatest roller coaster in the country!” shouted a man wearing a dragon head. “请这边走，女士们先生们！ 进入龙谷！ 国内最好的过山车！”，一个戴着龙头的人喊道。

“Over here! The Buzzard’s Roost!” called a boy in a bird outfit. “Greatest train ride on earth!” “这边来！ 秃鹫的巢穴！”，一个穿着鸟装的男孩喊道。 地球上最好的火车之旅！

“Come to the Babbling Brook!” yelled a girl dressed as a mermaid. “Greatest water ride in the U.S. of A.!” “来巴布林溪吧！”，一个打扮成美人鱼的女孩喊道。 美国最好的水上项目！

“Funny. Sounds like everything in Coney Island is the greatest,” said Jack. “有趣。” “听起来，科尼岛的一切都是最棒的，”杰克说。

“So?” said Annie. “那又怎样？”安妮说。

“That means nothing’ s really the greatest,” said Jack, “because everything’s the same!” “这意味着其实没有什么真的最棒，”杰克说，“因为所有东西都是一样的！”

“Ha!” A woman sitting on the bench laughed. “哈！”坐在长椅上的女人笑了。

Jack realized they were sitting next to the short couple from the Shoot-the-Chutes ride. Their big hats almost completely hid their faces. 杰克意识到他们正坐在“射水滑车”游乐项目的那对矮小夫妇旁边。 他们戴的大帽子几乎完全遮住了他们的脸。

“Did you hear that, Harry?” the woman said to the man. “This boy’s a genius!” “你听到他说的话了吗，哈里？”女人对男人说。 “这男孩是个天才！”

“Ain’t that the truth, Bess?” Harry said. “亲爱的，别用‘ain’t’这个词。”哈里说。

“Don’t say ain’ t, dear. Say isn’ t,” Bess said kindly. “不要说‘ain’t’，亲爱的。” “说不是，”贝丝亲切地说。

Harry smiled at Jack and Annie. “Isn’ t that the truth?” he said. “See, my Bess is always looking after me.” 哈利对杰克和安妮笑了笑。 “这不是真的吗？”他说。 “你看，我的贝丝总是照顾我。”

Jack and Annie smiled politely. 杰克和安妮礼貌地笑了笑。

“Is this your first time in Coney Island, kiddos?” asked Harry. “孩子们，这是你们第一次来康尼岛吗？”哈利问。

“It is,” said Annie. “Yours, too?” “是的，”安妮说。 “你也是吗？”

“Oh, no!” said Bess. “No, no, no. We first met here at Coney Island fifteen years ago. We got married three weeks later!” “哦，不！”贝丝说。 “不，不，不。” 我们十五年前第一次在这里的康尼岛见面。 我们三周后就结婚了！

“Congratulations,” said Annie. “恭喜，”安妮说。

“It was a long time ago,” said Bess. “But I’m more crazy about him than ever. And he writes me a love note every day, rain or shine.” “那已经是很久以前的事了，”贝丝说。 “但是我现在比以前更喜欢他了。 而且他每天都给我写一张爱心便条，不管下雨还是放晴。”

“That’s so sweet,” said Annie. “这太甜蜜了，”安妮说。

“Are you kiddos having a good time here?” asked Harry. “你们这两个小家伙在这里玩得开心吗？”哈里问。

“I am,” said Annie. “我玩得开心，”安妮说。

“Good,” said Bess. Then she turned to her husband. “Time’s running out, dreamboat,” she said. “We’d better move on.” “很好，”贝丝说。 然后她转向她的丈夫。 “时间不多了，亲爱的，”她说。 “我们最好赶紧出发。”

“Can we get ice cream cones now?” Harry asked. “我们现在可以去吃冰淇淋甜筒吗？”哈利问。

“No! You know the routine—ice cream always comes after the show!” Bess said. “If we want to take the Trip to the Moon, we gotta go now.” “不行！ 你知道规矩——看完表演才能吃冰淇淋！”贝丝说。 “如果我们想乘坐去月球的旅行，现在就得出发。”

Harry looked at Jack and Annie. “She’s always wanted to send me to the moon,” he said. 哈利看着杰克和安妮。 “她一直想把我送到月球上，”他说。

Jack and Annie laughed. 杰克和安妮笑了。

“You kiddos want to join us?” Harry asked. “你们想和我们一起去吗？”哈里问。

“I’m sorry, we can’t,” said Jack. “We have to do something else. Hey, do you know where Henderson’s Music Hall is?” “对不起，我们不能去，”杰克说。 “我们得做别的事情。 嘿，你知道亨德森音乐厅在哪里吗？

“Sure. Why do you want to know?” Bess asked. “当然知道。 你为什么想知道？”贝丝问。

“We’re going to see the Great Houdini,” said Jack. “我们要去看伟大的胡迪尼，”杰克说。

“Ah! What a coincidence!” said Bess. “We’re going there, too, after our Trip to the Moon. I adore that man—the Great Houdini! I wouldn’t miss watching that dreamboat for anything in this world.” 真巧啊！”贝丝说。 “我们看完《月球之旅》后也要去那里。 我太喜欢那个人了——伟大的胡迪尼！ 我这辈子都不会错过看那个英俊的小伙子。”

Harry cleared his throat. “Sweetheart, they want directions,” he said. 哈利清了清嗓子。 “亲爱的，他们想问路，”他说。

“Oh, yes!” said Bess. “So, walk the main road there to the arches, and then exit the park.” She pointed to giant gold-colored arches in the distance. “Then turn right and head down Surf Avenue. When you see a big red building, stop. That’s it.” “哦，是的！”贝丝说。 “所以，沿着主路走到那座拱门，然后离开公园。”她指着远处巨大的金色拱门。 “然后右转，沿着冲浪大道走。 当你看到一座大红色的建筑时，就停下来。那就是。”

“Cool,” said Jack. “Thanks.” “太酷了，”杰克说。 “谢谢。”

“So long, kiddos. Maybe we’ll see you later at the Great Houdini show,” said Harry. “再见了，孩子们。 也许我们之后会在伟大的胡迪尼表演上再见，”哈里说。

“And when you watch the show, remember,” said Bess, “even though everything can’t be the greatest, sometimes what folks call great really is great.” “当你看表演时，记住，”贝丝说，“即使不是每件事都能做到最好，有时候人们说很棒的事物真的真的很棒。”

Harry laughed. He tipped his straw hat to Jack and Annie, and he and Bess walked away. 哈里笑了。 他向杰克和安妮 tipped 了他的草帽，然后他和贝丝走开了。

“I liked them,” said Annie. “我喜欢他们，”安妮说。

“Me too,” said Jack. He stood up. Talking with the cheerful couple had made him feel better. “Okay! Let’s go get our tickets! Finally!” “我也是，”杰克说。他站了起来。 和这对快乐的夫妇聊天让他感觉好些了。 “好！ 我们去拿票吧！终于！”，

Jack and Annie stepped down from the terrace and walked up the broad avenue toward the arches. At the arches, they passed ticket sellers sitting in red chariots. “Hurry back to Luna Park!” one shouted. 杰克和安妮从露台上走下来，沿着宽阔的大道朝拱门走去。 在拱门处，他们看到一些售票员坐在红色的马车里。 “快回月之公园吧！”一个人喊道。

“We will, don’t worry!” said Annie. “我们会的，别担心！”安妮说。

Jack and Annie left Luna Park and stepped onto Surf Avenue. “Bess said to go right,” said Jack. 杰克和安妮离开了月之公园，走上了冲浪大道。 “贝丝说要往右走，”杰克说。

As Jack and Annie started up the crowded sidewalk, they passed horses and buggies and old-timey cars. The warm summer night was filled with the sounds of hooves clomping and horns honking: Ah-OO-ga! Ah-OO-ga! 当杰克和安妮沿着拥挤的人行道走起来时，他们经过了马匹和马车，还有老式的汽车。 温暖的夏夜充满了马蹄声和喇叭声：啊——噢——嘎！啊——噢——嘎！

“There it is!” said Jack. He pointed to a large red building with green trim. A big sign over the awning read HENDERSON’S. “那就是它！”杰克说。 他指着一座大红色的建筑，上面有绿色的装饰。 遮篷上方有一个大招牌，上面写着HENDERSON’S。

A huge crowd of people had lined up on the sidewalk in front of the theater. “Oh, man, look at that line!” said Jack. “This is why I wanted to be here early!” 很多人排着长队站在剧院前面的人行道上。 “天啊，看那条队多长啊！”杰克说。 “这就是为什么我想早点来这里！”

Jack and Annie ran to the end of the line, where some teenagers were joking and pushing each other around. 杰克和安妮跑到队尾，那里有几个青少年在开玩笑，互相推搡。

“Is this the line to buy tickets?” asked Annie. “这是买票的队伍吗？”安妮问。

“Nah, this is for folks who got theirs already. Like us!” said a tough-looking kid. “So scram!” “不，这是给那些已经拿到的人的。” “就像我们！”一个看起来很厉害的男孩说道。 “快滚开！”

“Forget it,” said Jack. He pulled Annie along. “We have to find the box office.” “算了吧，”杰克说。 他拉着安妮走了。 “我们必须找到售票处。”

At the front of the theater was a chalkboard that read: 剧院的前面有一块黑板，上面写着：

图中文字：MAGIC TONIGHT!

8:15: The Bambini Brothers 9:00: The Great Houdini

TICKETS HERE! 图中文字：今晚有魔法！8:15：邦比尼兄弟 9:00：伟大的胡迪尼 门票请到这里！

Jack hurried to a window beside the chalkboard. He was surprised there was no line. “Excuse me!” he said, trying to get the attention of the ticket seller. She was talking to a man inside the office. 杰克赶紧跑到黑板旁边的窗户前。 他惊讶地发现没有人排队。 “请问一下！”他说，试图引起售票员的注意。 她正在办公室里和一个男人说话。

“You’re not listening to me, Mrs. Crenshaw!” the man shouted. “It is the end of the world! I tell you, they’ll destroy the theater!” “你根本没听我说话，克伦肖夫人。 ”那个男人喊道。 “世界末日就要来了！ 我告诉你，他们会毁掉这个剧院！”

“Excuse me!” Jack said again, in a louder voice. “We’d like to buy two—” “对不起！”杰克用更大的声音又说了一遍。 “我们想买两——”

“They’ll start a riot! They’ll tear the seats apart!” the man shouted. “Mercy! Mercy, my heart!” He clutched his chest and nearly collapsed. “他们会引发骚乱的！ 他们会把座位撕烂的！”那人喊道。 “求求你！ 求求你，我的心啊！”他抓住胸口，差点摔倒。

“Should we get help?” Annie asked. “我们应该叫人帮忙吗？”安妮问。

Mrs. Crenshaw looked down at Annie and shook her head. “No, dear. Mr. Dewey will be fine,” she said. She rolled her eyes. 女士。 克伦肖看向安妮，摇了摇头。 “不用，亲爱的。先生。 “德威会没事的，”她说。 她翻了翻白眼。

“Yes! Get help! Get help, please!” Mr. Dewey croaked, wiping his brow with a handkerchief. “Find me a new opening act!” “对！快去叫人帮忙！ 快去叫人帮忙，好吗！”先生。 德威咳嗽着，用手帕擦了擦额头。 “给我找个新的开场表演！

“A what?” said Annie. “什么？”安妮说。

“The Bambini Brothers ran off!” said Mr. Dewey. “I caught them red-handed! Stealing from the box office!” “小宝宝兄弟跑掉了！”德威先生说。 “我当场抓住了他们！ 偷票房的钱！”

“That sounds terrible,” said Annie. “那听起来太糟糕了，”安妮说。

“You have no idea, missy!” cried Mr. Dewey. “They were the opening act for the Great Houdini!” He wiped his sweaty face again. “The hooligans in the crowd will go crazy if the curtain’s late! I should have gone into the shoe business instead of show business!” “你根本不知道，小丫头！”德威先生喊道。 “他们是伟大的胡迪尼的开场表演！”他又一次擦了擦汗流浃背的脸。 “如果幕布晚开的话，人群中那些调皮捣蛋的人会发疯的！ 我真应该去开鞋店，而不是搞演艺事业！

Jack felt sorry for the man, but he really didn’t care about the Bambini Brothers. He just wanted tickets for the Great Houdini. “Uh … excuse me,” he said to Mrs. Crenshaw. “We’d like to buy two tickets for the big show—the one with the Great Houdini.” 杰克对那个男人感到同情，但他真的不关心巴姆比尼兄弟。 他只想买到伟大的胡迪尼的演出票。 “呃……请问一下，”他对克伦肖夫人说。 “我们想买两张大演出的票——就是那个有伟大的胡迪尼的演出。”

“Sorry, kids, didn’t you read that?” Mrs. Crenshaw said. She pointed to a sign in the window. The sign read: SOLD OUT. “抱歉，孩子们，你们没看到告示吗？”克伦肖夫人说。 她指着窗户里的一个牌子。 牌子上写着：售完。

“Oh, no, we didn’t see that,” said Annie. “哦，不，我们没看到那个，”安妮说。

“You’re … sold out?” said Jack. “你们……卖光了？”杰克说。

“Yes!” yelled Mr. Dewey. “We sold out an hour ago! S-O-L-D O-U-T! So it’s a full house with no opening act! Go away now, please! We got problems! Big problems!” And with that, Mr. Dewey slammed the box office window shut. “是的！”德威先生喊道。 “我们一小时前就卖光了！S-O-L-D O-U-T！” 所以这是一场满员的演出，却没有开场节目！ 快走吧，求求你们了！我们有麻烦了！ 大问题！”说完，德威 猛地把票房窗口关上了。
