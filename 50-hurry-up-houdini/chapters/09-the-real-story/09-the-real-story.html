9. The Real Story 9. 真实的故事

After the curtain closed, Jack could hear the joyful crowd leaving the theater. Mr. Dewey, Mr. Wilson, Butch, and Hank rushed from the wings to congratulate the Houdinis. 幕布落下后，杰克听到欢乐的人群正离开剧院。先生。 德威先生，先生。 威尔逊、巴奇和汉克从后台冲出来，祝贺胡迪尼一家。

Mr. Dewey actually went down on his knees to thank them. “You have made my audience very happy!” he exclaimed. “And when my audience is happy, I am happy, too! Great show! Great show!” 先生。 德威伊甚至跪下来感谢他们。 “你让观众非常开心！”他激动地说。 “当我的观众开心的时候，我也很开心！太棒的表演了！ 太棒的表演了！”，

“Man, he’s really lost it,” Jack said. “天啊，他真的彻底疯了，”杰克说。

Annie laughed. “Come on, I want to tell them ‘Great show,’ too,” she said. 安妮笑了。 “来吧，我也想对他们说‘太棒了！’，”她说。

But as Jack and Annie started toward the Houdinis, some teenage boys rushed onstage ahead of them. 但当杰克和安妮朝胡迪尼走去时，一些十几岁的男孩抢先冲上了舞台。

“Sorry, boys,” said Mr. Wilson, stepping between the boys and the Houdinis. “Mr. Houdini is exhausted from his magnificent performance. You’ll have to buy a ticket and come back again!” “对不起，孩子们，”先生说， 威尔逊先生站在男孩们和胡迪尼之间。 “先生 胡迪尼因为精彩的表演累坏了。 你得买张票，然后回来再看一次！

“Come on, Harry, let’s get you two out of here!” Mr. Dewey said. “来吧，哈利，我们把你俩带出去！”德威先生说。

As Mr. Dewey whisked Harry and Bess backstage, Jack and Annie followed them. Mr. Dewey ushered the couple into their dressing room. He hurried in after them and closed the door. 正如德威先生 带着哈利和贝丝走到后台，杰克和安妮跟在他们后面。德威先生 德威带这对夫妇走进了更衣室。 他赶紧跟了进去，然后关上门。

Annie was about to knock, but Jack stopped her. “Don’t bother them yet,” he said. “Let them relax a minute first. We’ll see them when they come out.” 安妮正要敲门，但杰克拦住了她。 “别打扰他们，”他说。 “先让他们放松一会儿。 等他们出来我们再见他们。”

“Good idea,” said Annie. “好主意，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie waited outside the Houdinis’ dressing room door as the crew cleared all the hooligan fans off the stage. 杰克和安妮站在霍迪尼的更衣室门口，等着工作人员把所有捣蛋的粉丝都赶下舞台。

They waited as Hank and Butch took away Houdini’s cabinet, his milk can, and his steamer trunk. They waited as Mrs. Crenshaw collected the bird and rabbit cages and carried them away. They waited as Mr. Wilson swept the floor and turned off the stage lights. 他们等着汉克和巴奇把霍迪尼的柜子、牛奶罐和蒸汽箱都拿走。 他们等着Mrs. 克伦肖收集了鸟笼和兔子笼，然后把它们拿走了。 他们等着先生 威尔逊打扫了地板，关掉了舞台灯。

“They should be relaxed by now,” said Annie. “他们现在应该放松下来了，”安妮说。

“No kidding,” said Jack. He tapped softly on the door. There was no answer. “可不是嘛，”杰克说。 他轻轻敲了敲门。 没有人回答。

Annie knocked louder. “Bess? Harry?” she called. 安妮敲得更响了些。 “贝丝？ 哈利？”她喊道。

Still, no one answered. Annie tried the handle, but the door was locked. 仍然没有人回答。 安妮试着转动门把手，但门是锁着的。

“They’re gone,” said Mr. Wilson, passing by Jack and Annie in the hall. “他们走了，”先生说。 威尔逊先生从走廊经过杰克和安妮时说。

“Gone?” said Annie. “走了？”安妮说。

“Yes, both the Houdinis left a while ago,” the stage manager said. “是的，两位胡迪尼先生一会儿就离开了，”舞台经理说。

“Left? How?” asked Jack. He and Annie had been watching the door every minute! “离开？怎么离开的？”杰克问。 他和安妮一直每分钟都在盯着那扇门！

“Mr. Dewey took them through the secret exit out of the stars’ dressing room,” said Mr. Wilson. “It goes through the basement and out of the theater. Otherwise, they’d be mobbed by all the crazy fans.” “先生。 德威先生带他们从星星们的化妆室的秘密出口出去了，”威尔逊先生说。 “它从地下室穿过，然后从剧院出去。” “否则，他们会被疯狂的粉丝们围住。”

“So they’re really gone?” said Jack. “他们真的走了吗？”杰克说。

“They don’t call Harry the Master of Escape for nothing,” Mr. Wilson said. “You’d better change out of your costumes and get going, too. We’ll be locking up the building soon.” “他们可不是无缘无故叫哈利‘逃脱大师’的，”威尔逊先生说。 “你最好也换下戏服，赶紧出发吧。 我们很快就得锁门了。”

Jack and Annie walked down the hall and into their dressing room. “I can’t believe it!” Annie wailed. “We didn’t even get to tell them ‘Great show.’ ” 杰克和安妮沿着走廊走进了他们的化妆间。 “我简直不敢相信！”安妮哭喊道。 “我们甚至都没来得及对他们说‘演出很棒’。”

“Worse than that, we didn’t get to find out a secret of greatness,” said Jack. He held up his finger with the Ring of Truth. “The ring didn’t glow all night.” “更糟糕的是，我们连伟大的秘密都没来得及发现，”杰克说。 他举起戴着真理戒指的手指。 “戒指整晚都没有发光。”

“Well, I guess all we can do is get dressed and go look for them,” said Annie. “好吧，我想我们只能穿上衣服去寻找它们了，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie quickly changed out of the tuxedos and back into their street clothes. Jack grabbed his bag and put his watch in the pocket of his pants. 杰克和安妮迅速换下晚礼服，又穿回了日常衣服。 杰克拿起他的包，把手表放进裤子的口袋里。

“Come on, hurry!” said Annie. She and Jack rushed out the back door of the theater. They ran down the dark alley, past the row of garbage cans, and out to the street. “快点，快点！”安妮说。 她和杰克从剧院的后门冲了出去。 他们跑下昏暗的小巷，经过一排垃圾桶，然后跑到了街上。

Surf Avenue was still bright with lights and just as busy as before. The sidewalks were still crowded with lots of people. The old-timey cars and horses and buggies filled the cobbled street. 冲浪大道上仍然灯火通明，和之前一样热闹。 人行道上仍然挤满了很多人。 老式的汽车、马车和马车夫填满了鹅卵石铺成的街道。

“They could be anywhere,” said Jack. “They could even have gone home.” “他们可能在任何地方，”杰克说。 “他们甚至可能已经回家了。”

“Let’s try Luna Park,” said Annie. “我们去试试月光乐园吧，”安妮说。

“Why would they go back there?” asked Jack. “Especially after getting trapped on their Trip to the Moon?” “他们为什么要去那里呢？”杰克问。 “尤其是在他们去月球旅行时被困住之后？”

“I don’t know. There’s lots they might want to do there,” said Annie. She gasped. “我不知道。 在那里他们可能有很多事情想做，”安妮说。她惊呼起来。

“Ice cream!” Jack and Annie said together. At the same moment, they’d each remembered Bess saying “Ice cream always comes after the show.” “冰淇淋！”杰克和安妮异口同声地说。 就在同一时刻，他们都想起了贝丝说过的话：“冰淇淋总是在演出之后才来。”

“To the ice cream stand!” said Annie. “去冰淇淋摊！”安妮说。

“And fast!” said Jack. “而且要快！”杰克说。

Jack and Annie took off up the street, dodging people along the way. They ran to the tall arches, where ticket sellers in chariots were still welcoming people into Luna Park. 杰克和安妮沿着街道跑起来，途中躲开了路上的人。 他们跑到高大的拱门那里，那里有坐在战车里的售票员，还在欢迎人们进入月之公园。

“Admission, ten cents each,” one said. “门票，每人10美分，”一个人说。

Jack and Annie dumped the rest of their pennies into the ticket seller’s hands. Then they took off, dashing up the broad avenue, weaving around couples and children and barkers on stilts and unicycles. 杰克和安妮把剩下的硬币都倒进了售票员的手心里。 然后他们又跑了起来，沿着宽阔的大道飞奔，绕过情侣、孩子们、踩着高跷的和骑独轮车的叫卖员。

When they came to the ice cream stand, they found a line of people waiting to buy cones. Panting, Jack looked up and down the line. 当他们来到冰淇淋摊时，发现已经排了一队人等着买甜筒。 喘着气，杰克抬头看了看排队的人群，又低头看了看。

The two Houdinis were nowhere to be seen. 那两个胡迪尼却不见了。

Jack’s spirits fell. But Annie poked him. “What?” Jack said. 杰克的心情一下子低落下来。 但安妮戳了他一下。 “什么？”杰克说。

Annie pointed at the terrace. 安妮指着露台。

Jack looked up. Harry and Bess were sitting on a bench side by side, holding ice cream cones. Their big hats hid their faces. 杰克抬头看了看。 哈利和贝丝并排坐在长椅上，手里拿着冰淇淋甜筒。 他们戴着大帽子，遮住了脸。

Jack laughed. “Now I get it! They wear those hats so fans won’t recognize them,” he said. 杰克笑了。 “我现在明白了！ 他们戴这些帽子是为了不让粉丝认出他们，”他说。

“Well, they can’t fool us,” said Annie. “C’mon.” “好吧，他们骗不了我们，”安妮说。 “来吧。”

Annie and Jack headed over to the bench. When they were close, Annie put her finger to her lips. Jack understood what she meant. Without greeting Harry or Bess, Jack and Annie sat down next to them. 安妮和杰克朝长椅走去。 当他们走近时，安妮把手指放在嘴唇上。 杰克明白了她的意思。 没有跟哈利和贝丝打招呼，杰克和安妮坐到了他们旁边。

“Great ice cream, huh?” said Annie. “冰淇淋很棒，对吧？”安妮说。

“Great,” said Bess, not looking up. “很棒，”贝丝说，没有抬头看。

“Would you call it the greatest in the world?” asked Jack. “你会说是世界上最棒的吗？”杰克问。

Tilting her head, Bess peeked out from under the brim of her hat. She laughed loudly. “Hey, Harry! Look!” she exclaimed. “It’s our little friends!” 她歪着头，从帽子的帽檐下偷偷往外看了一眼。 她大声笑了出来。 “嘿，哈利！ 看！”她喊道。 “是我们的小朋友们！”

Harry raised his hat, then laughed too. “The crazy kiddos!” he said. “So what’s the story? What the heck were you two doing up on that stage tonight?” 哈利摘下帽子，然后也笑了起来。 “这些疯孩子！”他说。 “那故事是什么？ 你们今晚在舞台上到底在干什么？

“We just love to perform,” said Annie with a giggle. “我们只是喜欢表演，”安妮咯咯笑着说。

“We just love to make fools of ourselves,” said Jack. “我们只是喜欢让自己出丑，”杰克说。

“Making a fool of yourself is a brave thing to do,” Harry said, smiling. “让自己出丑是一件勇敢的事，”哈利微笑着说。

“That’s what Aristotle once told us,” said Annie, “more or less.” “这就是亚里士多德曾经告诉我们的，”安妮说，“差不多是这样。”

Jack glanced at the Ring of Truth. It wasn’t glowing. 杰克看了看真相之环。 它没有发光。

“Mr. Houdini!” someone said. A man stood in front of the bench. He started to clap. “先生。 胡迪尼！”，有人喊道。 一个男人站在长椅前。 他开始鼓掌。

“Oh, dear,” murmured Bess. “哦，天哪，”贝丝小声说。

“You were unbelievable tonight!” the man said. “I never saw anything like what you did!” “你今晚真是令人难以置信！”，那人说。 “我从来没看过你做得这么神奇的事情！”

Other people turned to look. They recognized Harry, too, and began gathering in front of the bench, telling him how great he was. Harry finished his ice cream cone. Then he stood up and started shaking hands with his admirers. 其他人都转过头来看。 他们也认出了哈利，开始在长椅前聚集，夸奖他有多棒。 哈利吃完了他的冰淇淋甜筒。 然后他站起来，开始和他的粉丝们握手。

Bess shook her head and sighed. “He’s always nice to people,” she said to Jack and Annie. “No matter how tired he is.” 贝丝摇了摇头，叹了口气。 “他总是对人很好，”她对杰克和安妮说。 “不管他有多累。”

Harry’s fans kept asking questions: “How do you do it?” “How did you escape from those handcuffs?” “How’d you get out of that milk can?” 哈利的粉丝们一直问问题：‘你是怎么做到的？’‘你怎样从手铐里逃出来的？’‘你是怎么从那个牛奶罐里出来的？’

“First of all, let me assure you again that I have no supernatural powers,” said Harry. “It’s all illusion. These skills just happen to come easily to me. Always have. I remember when I was a kid …” “首先，让我再次向你们保证，我没有任何超自然的力量，”哈利说。 “这全是幻觉。” 这些技能对我来说特别容易，一直都很容易。 我记得我小时候……

Jack looked at the Ring of Truth. Nothing. 杰克看着真相之环。什么也没有。

“That’s not the real story,” Bess said quietly to Jack and Annie, her eyes twinkling. “这不是真正的故事，”贝丝轻声对杰克和安妮说，她的眼睛闪着光。

“What is the real story?” Annie asked her. “真正的故事是什么？”安妮问她。

“Harry’s always worked very, very hard,” she said. “He’s worked all his life. When he was a little boy, he sold newspapers and flowers. He performed in a neighborhood children’s circus. A few years later, he hopped trains and tried to get work in Milwaukee, Kansas City, New York. He practiced coin tricks and card tricks for hours every day. He read every book he could find on magic. He practiced and practiced and practiced. Those skills did not come naturally to him. They came from hard work and more practice than most people could endure.” “哈利一直都非常、非常努力工作，”她说。 “他一生都在努力工作。 他小时候卖报纸和花。 他还参加过社区儿童马戏团的表演。 几年后，他搭火车去了密尔沃基、堪萨斯城和纽约，试图找份工作。 他每天花好几个小时练习硬币魔术和纸牌魔术。 他读了能找到的每一本关于魔法的书。 他一遍又一遍地练习。 这些技能对他来说并不容易。 它们来自于辛苦的工作和比大多数人能忍受的更多的练习。

Jack thought about how he and Annie had only done their magic tricks once, with the help of the magic mist. No wonder they’d failed so spectacularly the second time. 杰克想起他和安妮只做过一次魔法表演，还靠了魔法雾的帮助。 难怪他们第二次尝试时失败得那么彻底。

“So his secret is hard work?” said Annie. “所以他的秘密是努力工作？”安妮说。

“You’ve got it, kiddo,” said Bess. “But that’s not such a big secret, is it? Anyone would have to work hard to be truly great at something.” “你猜对了，孩子，”贝丝说。 “但是这并不是什么大秘密，对吧？” 任何人要想在某件事上真正变得很棒，都必须努力工作。

“Yeah, they would,” said Jack. “是的，他们会的，”杰克说。

“Oh, wow,” Annie whispered to Jack. She pointed at his hand. “哦，哇，”安妮小声对杰克说。 她指着他的手。

Jack looked down and smiled. The Ring of Truth was finally glowing. 杰克低头笑了笑。 真相之环终于开始发光了。
