4. Reading Lesson 4. 阅读课

It was warm and dry inside the schoolhouse. Several oil lamps lit the darkness. 校舍里面温暖干燥。 几盏油灯照亮了黑暗。

“Class, meet Annie and Jack,” the young teacher said. “同学们，来认识安妮和杰克，”年轻的老师说。

What class? thought Jack. 什么同学？杰克想。

There were only three kids. 只有三个孩子。

On one bench sat a small boy and a girl. The boy looked about Annie’s age. The girl looked a little younger. On another bench sat a tall boy. He was tough-looking. 一条长凳上坐着一个小男孩和一个小女孩。 男孩看起来和安妮差不多大。 女孩看起来更小一点。 另一条长凳上坐着一个高个子男孩。 他看上去很凶。

“Welcome to our first day of school,” said the young teacher. “欢迎来到我们开学的第一天，”年轻的老师说。

“Today’s your very first day?” said Annie. “今天真是你们的第一天？”安妮说。

“Yes, and our first day in this dugout. The family who lived here left for California a week ago,” said Miss Neely. “是的，也是我们在这个地坑屋里的第一天。 住在这儿的那家人一星期前去了加利福尼亚，”尼莉小姐说。

Jack and Annie peered around the room. The walls were made of dirt. The floor was made of wood. It was covered by a worn rug. 杰克和安妮打量着房间。 墙壁是泥土做的。 地板是木头的。 上面铺着一块破旧的地毯。

Miss Neely’s desk was made from a barrel. A small coal stove was near her desk. A crate held a water jug, chalk, and two small blackboards. 尼莉小姐的桌子是用一只木桶做成的。 她桌子旁有一个小煤炉。 一只木箱里放着水壶、粉笔和两块小黑板。

“It’s a nice school,” Annie said politely. “这是所不错的学校，”安妮礼貌地说。

“Thank you. We’re very grateful for it,” said Miss Neely. “And where do you live?” “谢谢。 我们对此非常感激，”尼莉小姐说。 “你们住在哪儿？”

“Well, we actually … ,” Jack started. Then he stopped—he wasn’t sure what to say. “这个，我们其实……，”杰克开口说。 然后他停住了——他不确定该说什么。

“Actually, we don’t live around here,” Annie said. “We’re passing through.” “其实我们不住在附近，”安妮说。 “我们是路过的。”

“You must be from the wagon train I saw this morning,” Miss Neely said. “你们一定是早上我看见的那支篷车队里的，”尼莉小姐说。

Annie nodded. 安妮点了点头。

Jack smiled. 杰克笑了。

Good work, Annie, he thought. 干得好，安妮，他想。

“We can only stay a little while,” he said. “我们只能待一小会儿，”他说。

“How exciting for you,” said Miss Neely. “Heading west on a wagon train. Where are you going?” “你们真让人兴奋，”尼莉小姐说。 “坐着篷车队往西走。 你们要去哪儿？”

“California,” Annie said. “加利福尼亚，”安妮说。

“California! That’s wonderful! Isn’t it, class?” Miss Neely said to the others. “加利福尼亚！太棒了！ 对不对，同学们？”尼莉小姐对其他人说。

“Yes, ma’am!” said the two younger children. “是的，老师！”两个小一点的孩子说。

The older boy barely nodded. 大一点的男孩几乎没点头。

“Have you ever been to school before?” Miss Neely asked Annie. “你们以前上过学吗？”尼莉小姐问安妮。

“Yes, ma’am,” Annie said. “We both know how to read and write. Jack’s one of the best readers you’ll ever meet.” “上过，老师，”安妮说。 “我们都会读会写。 杰克是你能遇见的最会读书的人之一。”

“My! Isn’t that wonderful, class?” said Miss Neely. “天哪！ 这不是太棒了吗，同学们？”尼莉小姐说。

“Yes, ma’am!” said the younger children. “是的，老师！”小一点的孩子们说。

The older boy gave Jack a scowl. 大一点的男孩朝杰克瞪了一眼。

“Not exactly the best,” Jack said modestly. “也不是最会的，”杰克谦虚地说。

“I love to read,” said Miss Neely. “I’ll read any book I can get my hands on.” “我喜欢读书，”尼莉小姐说。 “只要能弄到手的书，我都读。”

“Me too,” said Jack. “我也是，”杰克说。

“Then perhaps you’d like to start off our first reading lesson of the school year,” said Miss Neely. “那也许你愿意开始我们本学年的第一堂阅读课，”尼莉小姐说。

“Sure,” said Jack. “好啊，”杰克说。

“Sit with Jeb, then,” said Miss Neely, “and Annie, you sit with Kate and her brother, Will.” “那就和杰布坐一起，”尼莉小姐说，“安妮，你和凯特还有她哥哥威尔坐一起。”

Will and Kate quickly made room for Annie on their bench. 威尔和凯特很快给安妮在长凳上腾出地方。

But Jeb didn’t move over for Jack, not even an inch. 可杰布连一寸都不给杰克让。

Jack barely had room to sit. He took a deep breath and sat on the end of the bench. 杰克几乎没地方坐。 他深吸一口气，坐在长凳尽头。

Miss Neely handed Jack a book. 尼莉小姐递给杰克一本书。

“This is our only reader,” she said. “It’s called the McGuffey Reader. Please read the first two lines of the poem on page fifty.” “这是我们唯一的读本，”她说。 “叫《麦加菲读本》。 请读第五十页那首诗的前两行。”

“Oh, um … sure, ma’am,” said Jack. “哦，嗯……好的，老师，”杰克说。

He turned to page fifty. He pushed his glasses into place. Then he read aloud: 他翻到第五十页。 他把眼镜推正。 然后大声读道：

“Twinkle, twinkle, little star. “一闪一闪小星星。

How I wonder what you are.” 我想知道你是什么。”

“Very good!” said Miss Neely. “Now pass the reader to Jeb.” “很好！”尼莉小姐说。 “现在把读本传给杰布。”

Jack handed the book to Jeb. 杰克把书递给杰布。

“Jeb, please read the next two lines,” said Miss Neely. “杰布，请读接下来的两行，”尼莉小姐说。

The older boy cleared his throat and stared at the page. 大男孩清了清嗓子，盯着那一页。

“Maybe Jeb can’t read,” Will said in a kind voice to Miss Neely. “也许杰布不会读，”威尔用温和的声音对尼莉小姐说。

Jeb’s face got red. 杰布的脸红了。

“Shut up, Will,” he muttered. “闭嘴，威尔，”他嘀咕道。

“Oh!” said Miss Neely. She looked confused. “哦！”尼莉小姐说。 她看起来很困惑。

Jack felt sorry for Jeb. He wanted to give him some help. 杰克为杰布感到难过。 他想帮他一把。

Barely moving his lips, Jack whispered, “Up above the world so high, like a diamond—” 杰克几乎不动嘴唇，小声说：“高高挂在天空中，好像钻石——”

Jeb turned on Jack with an angry look. 杰布生气地转向杰克。

“I don’t need your help,” he said. “我不需要你帮忙，”他说。

“Now, Jeb, don’t get mad,” said Miss Neely. “And, Jack, you shouldn’t give people the answers.” “杰布，别生气，”尼莉小姐说。 “还有杰克，你不该把答案告诉别人。”

“I’m sorry,” said Jack. “对不起，”杰克说。

Miss Neely sighed and pulled out her pocket watch. She was starting to look tired. 尼莉小姐叹了口气，掏出怀表。 她开始显得疲倦了。

“Why don’t you all go outside and have your noon meal?” she said. “I’ll stay in and prepare for our next lesson.” “你们都到外面去吃午饭吧？”她说。 “我留在里面准备下一课。”

Miss Neely opened the door of the sod hut. 尼莉小姐打开土屋的门。

Annie, Kate, and Will bounced up from their seats and started cheerfully out of the schoolroom. 安妮、凯特和威尔从座位上蹦起来，兴高采烈地走出教室。

Jack turned to Jeb. 杰克转向杰布。

“Hey, sorry for what happened,” Jack said. “嘿，刚才的事对不起，”杰克说。

Jeb just glared at him and didn’t say anything. 杰布只是瞪着他，什么也没说。

“Come on, Jack!” Annie called outside the hut. “Kate wants us to eat with them!” “快点，杰克！”安妮在屋外喊道。 “凯特想让我们和他们一起吃！”

Jack hurried out the door. He didn’t look back at Jeb. 杰克急忙走出门。 他没有回头看杰布。
