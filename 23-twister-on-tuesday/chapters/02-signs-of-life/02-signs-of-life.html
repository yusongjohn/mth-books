2. Signs of Life 2. 生命的迹象

Jack opened his eyes. 杰克睁开了眼睛。

He was wearing pants with suspenders and a shirt with the sleeves rolled up. In place of his backpack was a leather bag. 他穿着带吊带的裤子，衬衫袖子卷了起来。 代替他背包的是一只皮袋子。

Annie was wearing a long dress and a sunbonnet. 安妮穿着一条长裙，戴着一顶太阳帽。

“I like my hat,” she said. “It’ll keep the sun off my face.” “我喜欢我的帽子，”她说。 “它能挡住阳光，不晒到我的脸。”

“Yeah, except the sun’s not shining,” said Jack. “是啊，只不过现在太阳没出来，”杰克说。

He and Annie looked out the window. 他和安妮望向窗外。

The sky was cloudy. 天空乌云密布。

The tree house had landed in a small grove of trees near a creek. Beyond the trees was a wide, open prairie. Green grass and wildflowers swayed in a chilly wind. 树屋落在小溪边的一小片树丛里。 树林外是广阔开阔的大草原。 绿草和野花在冷风中摇曳。

In the distance, a train puffed across the prairie. Sparks of fire came out of its smokestack. Huge clouds of black smoke billowed into the gray sky. 远处，一列火车喷着气驶过草原。 火星从它的烟囱里喷出来。 大团大团的黑烟涌进灰蒙蒙的天空。

“Wow,” said Jack. “哇，”杰克说。

He looked at the picture of the train in their book and read: 他看着书里火车的图画，读道：

After the Civil War, the U.S. government built railroads to link the eastern and western parts of the country. By the 1870s, steam engines carried people across the Kansas prairie. 美国内战结束后，美国政府修建了铁路，把国家的东西部连接起来。到1870年代，蒸汽火车已经载着人们穿越堪萨斯的大草原。

Jack pulled out his notebook and wrote:



 杰克掏出笔记本写道：

图中文字：1870 s—trains across Kansas 图中文字：1870年代——穿越堪萨斯州的火车

“Let’s get going,” said Annie. “We have to find that special writing for Morgan.” “我们走吧，”安妮说。 “我们得为摩根找到那份特别的文字。”

She started down the ladder. 她开始下梯子。

Jack packed his things in his leather bag and climbed down after her. 杰克把东西装进皮袋，跟着她爬下去。

When he stepped onto the ground, Jack looked toward the west. 踩到地上时，杰克朝西望去。

The train was gone. Only a thin trail of smoke floated across the sky. 火车已经不见了。 只有一道淡淡的烟迹飘过天空。

“That train was cool,” said Jack. “那列火车真棒，”杰克说。

“Yeah, and so is that,” said Annie. She pointed in the other direction. “是啊，那个也很棒，”安妮说。 她指着另一个方向。

Far away, in the distance, a line of covered wagons rolled through the rippling grass. Their white coverings billowed in the breeze. 远远的，一队篷车正穿过起伏的草地。 白色的篷布在微风中鼓起。

Jack pulled out the research book. He found a picture of the wagon train. He read aloud: 杰克掏出研究书。 他找到篷车队的图画。他大声读道：

Wagons were the most common way for families to travel west. They could carry clothes, tools, food, and water. A line of wagons was called a “wagon train.” The white cloth coverings over the wagons also made them look like sailing ships, or schooners. For this reason, covered wagons were sometimes called “prairie schooners.” 马车是家庭向西旅行最常用的方式。它们可以装衣服、工具、食物和水。一列马车被称为“马车队”。马车上的白色布篷让它们看起来像正在航行的船只，或者叫纵帆船。因此，带篷的马车有时也被称为“草原纵帆船。”

Jack looked at the wagons again. They did look like ships sailing across a rippling green sea. 杰克又看了看那些篷车。 它们确实像船只，航行在起伏的绿色大海上。

He wrote in his notebook:



 他在笔记本上写道：

图中文字：covered wagons = prairie schooners 图中文字：带篷的马车=草原快车

“Let’s get a closer look,” said Annie. “我们靠近看看吧，”安妮说。

She took off across the grass. 她拔腿跑过草地。

Jack put away his things and ran after her. As they ran, the wind began to blow harder. The clouds overhead grew darker. 杰克收好东西，跟在她后面跑。 他们跑着跑着，风开始刮得更猛了。 头顶的云越来越暗。

“Wait—wait!” Jack finally called to Annie. “We’ll never catch up to it!” “等等——等等！”杰克终于朝安妮喊道。 “我们永远追不上的！”

They both stopped running. Panting, they watched the wagon train vanish over the horizon. 他们都停住了。 喘着气，他们看着篷车队消失在地平线外。

Jack took a deep breath. 杰克深深吸了口气。

“What now?” he said. “现在怎么办？”他说。

They looked around. 他们环顾四周。

All Jack could see was the distant grove of trees with the tree house. 杰克只能看见远处那片有树屋的树丛。

With the train and wagon train gone, there were no signs of life anywhere—no pioneer cabins, no Native American tepees. 火车和篷车队都走了，到处都没有生命的迹象——没有拓荒者的小屋，也没有印第安人的帐篷。

“How can we find the special writing?” said Jack. “There’s nothing out here.” “我们怎么才能找到那份特别的文字？”杰克说。 “这儿什么都没有。”

“Oh yeah?” said Annie. “What’s that?” “是吗？”安妮说。 “那是什么？”

She pointed to a rusty pipe sticking out from the top of a small hill. 她指着一座小山丘顶上伸出的一根生锈管子。

Streaming from the pipe was a column of black smoke. 管子里冒出一柱黑烟。

“Oh, man,” said Jack, “that’s definitely a sign of life.” “哦，天哪，”杰克说，“那绝对是生命的迹象。”
