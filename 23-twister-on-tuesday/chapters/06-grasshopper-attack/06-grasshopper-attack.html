6. Grasshopper Attack? 6. 蝗虫袭击？

Inside the lamplit hut, it was dry and cozy. 灯光照亮的土屋里干燥又舒适。

Jack sat back on his bench. He didn’t dare look at Jeb. 杰克坐回长凳上。 他不敢看杰布。

“It’s time for our writing lesson now,” Miss Neely said. “I’m going to give you each a slate and a pen.” “现在该上写作课了，”尼莉小姐说。 “我要给你们每人一块石板和一支笔。”

She handed out the slates. They looked like small blackboards set in wooden frames. 她分发石板。 它们看起来像镶在木框里的小黑板。

Next she gave everyone a slate pen. Each pen was a thin piece of chalk. 接着她给每人一支石笔。 每支笔都是一根细细的粉笔。

Miss Neely opened the McGuffey Reader. 尼莉小姐打开《麦加菲读本》。

“While you were eating your noon meal, I copied a poem from the book,” she said. “Now I want you to copy it.” “你们吃午饭时，我从书里抄了一首诗，”她说。 “现在我要你们抄下来。”

Miss Neely held her own slate board up for them all to see:



 尼莉小姐举起自己的石板给大家看：

图中文字：'Tis a lesson you should heed,

Try, try again;

If at first you don't succeed,

Try, try again. 图中文字：这是一条你应该记住的教训，再试一次，再试一次；如果第一次没有成功，再试一次，再试一次。

Jack quickly started copying the words. Out of the corner of his eye, he saw Jeb writing very slowly. It took the older boy a long time just to write the letter T. 杰克很快开始抄写那些字。 他用眼角余光看见杰布写得很慢。 大男孩光是写字母T就花了很长时间。

Jack slowed down, too. He didn’t want Jeb to think he was showing off. 杰克也放慢了速度。 他不想让杰布觉得他在炫耀。

Suddenly, loud thumping sounds came from overhead. It sounded as if someone were throwing stones against the roof. 忽然，头顶传来砰砰的响声。 听起来像有人在朝屋顶扔石头。

“Oh no! Grasshopper attack!” screamed Kate. She covered her head. “哦不！ 蝗虫袭击！”凯特尖叫道。 她抱住头。

“Grasshopper attack!” cried Will. He covered his head, too. “蝗虫袭击！”威尔喊道。 他也抱住头。

“Be calm, everyone!” said Miss Neely. “大家冷静！”尼莉小姐说。

What’s a grasshopper attack? What are they talking about? Jack wondered. 什么是蝗虫袭击？ 他们在说什么？杰克想。

Even Jeb seemed worried. As Miss Neely started toward the door, he said, “Don’t open it! They’ll come in!” 连杰布也显得担心。 尼莉小姐朝门口走去时，他说：“别开门！它们会进来的！”

Has everyone gone crazy? Jack thought. How can grasshoppers hurt anyone? 大家都疯了吗？杰克想。 蝗虫怎么能伤害人呢？

Miss Neely opened the door and looked out. A moment later, she stuck her head back in and closed the door. 尼莉小姐打开门向外看。 过了一会儿，她把头缩回来，关上门。

“It’s all right,” she said. “It’s only hailstones.” “没事了，”她说。 “只是冰雹。”

“What’s that?” said Annie. “那是什么？”安妮说。

“Hailstones are small pieces of frozen rain. Sometimes they fall to earth during a thunderstorm,” said Miss Neely. “冰雹是小块冻结的雨。 有时雷暴时会落到地上，”尼莉小姐说。

“Why did Will and Kate yell, ‘Grasshopper attack!’?” Jack asked. “威尔和凯特为什么喊‘蝗虫袭击’？”杰克问。

“Because last spring, grasshoppers did attack us,” said Miss Neely. “因为去年春天，蝗虫真的袭击过我们，”尼莉小姐说。

“Yes! Millions and millions of them came out of the sky,” said Will. “It looked like a huge, shiny cloud.” “对！ 成百上千万只从天上飞下来，”威尔说。 “看起来像一大片闪亮的云。”

“They covered every inch of ground!” said Kate. “They ate everything!” “它们盖住了每一寸地面！”凯特说。 “它们什么都吃！”

“They ate all our crops,” said Will, “our turnips and fruit trees and watermelons.” “它们吃光了我们的庄稼，”威尔说，“芜菁、果树和西瓜。”

“They even ate our clothes and bedsheets!” said Kate. “它们甚至吃了我们的衣服和床单！”凯特说。

“Yuck,” said Annie. “恶心，”安妮说。

“Oh, man,” said Jack. He’d never heard of a grasshopper attack before. “哦，天哪，”杰克说。 他以前从没听说过蝗虫袭击。

“It was very scary,” said Kate. “那非常可怕，”凯特说。

“But remember how we replanted and everyone helped everyone else?” said Miss Neely. “可还记得我们怎样重新播种，大家怎样互相帮助吗？”尼莉小姐说。

Kate and Will nodded. 凯特和威尔点了点头。

“We must try to hold on to the good memories,” Miss Neely said gently, “and let go of the bad ones.” “我们必须努力留住美好的回忆，”尼莉小姐温和地说，“放下不好的那些。”

“Yes, ma’am,” said Kate. “是的，老师，”凯特说。

Everyone was quiet for a moment. Then the sound of the hailstones died away. 大家安静了一会儿。 然后冰雹的声音渐渐停了。

“Let’s go back to our lesson now,” said Miss Neely. “我们现在继续上课吧，”尼莉小姐说。

They all returned to their writing. 大家又回去写字。

Even working as slowly as he could, Jack finished first. He showed his copy of the poem to Miss Neely. 即使尽量慢慢写，杰克还是第一个写完。 他把抄好的诗拿给尼莉小姐看。

“Good work, Jack,” she said. “We can all learn from these words, can’t we?” “写得好，杰克，”她说。 “我们都能从这些话里学到东西，对不对？”

“Yes, ma’am,” said Jack. “是的，老师，”杰克说。

“Hey, this is it, Jack!” Annie blurted out. “ Something to learn!” “嘿，就是这个，杰克！”安妮脱口而出。 “用来学习的东西！”

Miss Neely looked puzzled. 尼莉小姐看起来很困惑。

But Jack smiled. He knew what Annie was talking about: They had their special writing. They could go home! 可杰克笑了。 他知道安妮在说什么：他们找到了特别的文字。 他们可以回家了！

Jack stood up. 杰克站了起来。

“Excuse me, ma’am, but I’m afraid we have to leave,” he said. “对不起，老师，可我们恐怕得走了，”他说。

“So soon?” said Miss Neely. “这么快？”尼莉小姐说。

“Yes, we have to go back to our parents,” said Annie. “是的，我们得回到父母身边去，”安妮说。

“May I take my slate with us?” Jack asked. “我可以把石板带走吗？”杰克问。

“Please do,” said Miss Neely. “Use it on your trip to California to practice your writing.” “当然可以，”尼莉小姐说。 “去加利福尼亚的路上用它练习写字吧。”

“Thanks!” said Jack with a big smile. He put the slate in his leather bag. “We learned a lot, ma’am.” “谢谢！”杰克灿烂地笑着说。 他把石板放进皮袋。 “我们学到了很多，老师。”

“I’m glad you had a chance to come to school,” said Miss Neely. “Good-bye and good luck.” “我很高兴你们有机会来上学，”尼莉小姐说。 “再见，祝你们好运。”

“Good luck to you, too,” said Annie. “也祝你们好运，”安妮说。

“Bye!” Will and Kate called out. “再见！”威尔和凯特喊道。

“Bye!” said Jack and Annie. “再见！”杰克和安妮说。

As they went out the door, Jack glanced at Jeb. He felt sorry for the older boy. He tried one last time to be friends. 出门时，杰克瞥了杰布一眼。 他为这个大一点的男孩感到难过。 他最后一次试着交朋友。

“Bye, Jeb,” he said. “再见，杰布，”他说。

But the boy wouldn’t even look at him. 可那男孩甚至不肯看他。

Jack gently closed the door to the schoolhouse. 杰克轻轻关上了校舍的门。

He breathed a sigh of relief. He was glad to get away from Jeb’s anger. 他松了一口气。 离开杰布的怒气让他觉得轻松。

“That’s weird,” said Annie. “Look at the sky.” “真奇怪，”安妮说。 “看看天空。”

As Jack turned away from the schoolhouse door, he caught his breath. 杰克从校舍门前转过身，倒吸一口气。

The sky did look weird—really weird. 天空看起来很奇怪——真的很奇怪。
