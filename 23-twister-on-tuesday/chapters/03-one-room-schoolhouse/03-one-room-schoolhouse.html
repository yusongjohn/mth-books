3. One-Room Schoolhouse 3. 一间屋的校舍

“Let’s check it out,” said Annie. “我们去看看，”安妮说。

She and Jack walked up the little hill. At the top, they saw that the rusty pipe was rising out of a wooden roof. 她和杰克走上小山丘。 到了山顶，他们看见那根生锈管子是从木屋顶上伸出来的。

They walked around to the other side of the hill. 他们绕到山丘的另一边。

Beneath the wooden roof was a door. The door seemed to open into the hill itself. 木屋顶下面有一扇门。 那扇门好像直接开进山丘里面。

“What is this?” said Annie. “这是什么？”安妮说。

“Let’s find out,” said Jack. “我们弄清楚吧，”杰克说。

He studied their research book until he found a black-and-white photograph. The photograph showed the same hill with the door. 他翻着研究书，直到找到一张黑白照片。 照片上就是这座有门的小山丘。

Jack read aloud: 杰克大声读道：

Since the prairie did not have many trees, wood was hard to find. So pioneers often made their houses out of sod bricks, which were blocks of earth cut out of the prairie. Sometimes a sod house was dug out of the side of a hill. It was called a “dugout.” 因为草原上树木很少，木材很难找到。所以先驱者们经常用草皮砖来建造他们的房子，草皮砖是把草原上的土块挖出来做成的。有时候，草皮房子是挖在山丘旁边的，这种房子叫做“地窖屋”。

Jack pulled out his notebook. He wrote:



 杰克掏出笔记本写道：

图中文字：dugout = sod house carved out of hillside 图中文字：dugout = 用山坡挖出来的茅草屋

Then Jack read more to Annie: 接着杰克又给安妮读了更多：

Tornados, or twisters, are common on the prairie, so many dugouts had storm cellars. A storm cellar was like a rough basement below the ground. During a twister, a storm cellar is the safest place to be. 龙卷风，也叫旋风，在草原上很常见，所以很多地窖都有防风地下室。防风地下室就像地下的一个粗糙的地下室。龙卷风来的时候，防风地下室是最安全的地方。

“Wow, maybe we’ll see a twister,” said Annie. “哇，也许我们会看见龙卷风，”安妮说。

“I hope not,” said Jack. Then he read on: “我可不希望，”杰克说。然后他继续读：

A pioneer family built this dugout for a home. When they moved, the dugout became a schoolhouse. The schoolhouse had only one room. It also had a storm cellar beneath it. 一个先驱家庭建了这个地窖作为他们的家。当他们搬走后，地窖变成了学校。这所学校只有一个房间，下面还有一个防风暴的地窖。

Jack quickly wrote:



 杰克飞快地写下：

图中文字：some dugouts have storm cellars 图中文字：有些地窖有防风暴的地下室

“Hey! This is the place!” said Annie. “嘿！ 就是这个地方！”安妮说。

Jack looked up from his writing. 杰克从书写中抬起头。

“What place?” he asked. “什么地方？”他问。

“Where’s the best place to find our special writing—something to learn?” asked Annie. “找我们特别文字——用来学习的东西——最好的地方是哪儿？”安妮问。

Jack smiled. 杰克笑了。

“A school,” he said. “学校，”他说。

Annie ran to the wooden door and knocked loudly. 安妮跑到木门前，大声敲门。

A moment later, the door creaked open. A girl peeked out. Her hair was in a tight bun, as if she were a grownup. But she didn’t look more than sixteen or seventeen years old. 过了一会儿，门吱呀一声开了。 一个女孩探出头来。 她的头发紧紧挽成一个发髻，像大人一样。 可她看上去不过十六七岁。

“Hi, I’m Annie,” said Annie. “This is my brother, Jack.” “嗨，我是安妮，”安妮说。 “这是我哥哥杰克。”

The girl opened the door wider. 女孩把门开得更大了。

“Hello, Jack and Annie,” she said. “I’m your teacher, Miss Neely.” “你好，杰克和安妮，”她说。 “我是你们的老师，尼莉小姐。”

“You’re the teacher?” said Jack. Miss Neely seemed way too young to be a teacher. “你是老师？”杰克说。 尼莉小姐看起来实在太年轻，不像老师。

“Yes!” she said, smiling. “Come in. You’re late.” “是的！”她笑着说。 “进来吧。你们迟到了。”
