10. The Knights’ Gifts 10. 骑士们的礼物

The three knights lay in the grass, fighting for breath. 三位骑士躺在草地上，喘着粗气。

“The dance … We must stop … stop dancing,” gasped the older knight. “这支舞……我们必须停下……别再跳了，”年长的骑士喘着气说。

“You have stopped! We pulled you away!” said Annie. “你们已经停下了！ 我们把你们拉出来了！”安妮说。

The knight looked up at her and Jack. He had a rough, craggy face. 骑士抬头看着她和杰克。 他有一张粗糙、布满皱纹的脸。

“Who … who are you?” he asked in a hoarse voice. “谁…谁是你们？”他用沙哑的声音问道。

“Friends!” said Annie. She spoke loudly to be heard over the music. “We come from King Arthur’s castle!” “朋友！”安妮说道。 她大声说话，以便盖过音乐的声音。 “我们来自亚瑟王的城堡！”

“We’re on a quest,” said Jack, “to get the Water of Memory and Imagination.” “我们在执行一项任务，”杰克说，“去取得记忆与想象之水。”

“To save Camelot!” said Annie. “为了拯救卡梅洛特！”安妮说。

“Camelot—” whispered the knight. “We come from Camelot …. I don’t recognize you ….” “卡梅洛特——”骑士低声说道。 “我们来自卡梅洛特……” 我不认识你……

“We’re just visiting,” said Annie. “But we know all about you. You’re Sir Lancelot, aren’t you?” “我们只是来拜访的，”安妮说。 “但我们对你们全都知道。 你是兰斯洛特爵士吗？

“Yes,” breathed the knight. “是的，”骑士低声说。

“And Sir Percival and Sir Galahad,” said Jack. “还有珀西瓦尔爵士和加拉哈德爵士，”杰克说。

“Yes … my son, Galahad … ,” said the knight. “是的……我的儿子，加拉哈德……，”骑士说。

“King Arthur thinks you are lost forever,” said Annie. “亚瑟王认为你们永远都找不到了，”安妮说。

Sir Lancelot closed his eyes. “The dance … ,” he said, “it made us forget.… ” 兰斯洛特爵士闭上了眼睛。 “那场舞蹈……，”他说，“它让我们忘记了……”

“I know,” said Jack. “The dancers must be the Keepers of the Cauldron. You can’t get past them without getting caught up in their dance.” “我知道，”杰克说。 “这些舞者一定是坩埚的守护者。 你不跳他们的舞，就别想过去。”

“Father … we must find … the water.… ” Sir Galahad tried to sit up, but he was too weary. He lay back in the grass. “父亲……我们必须找到……水……”加拉哈德试图坐起来，但他太累了。 他躺回草地上。

“That’s okay, we’re here now,” said Annie. “You should all rest.” “没关系，我们在这里了，”安妮说。 “你们都应该休息。”

Sir Galahad closed his eyes. 加拉哈德闭上了眼睛。

“Yeah, don’t worry,” said Jack. “Annie and I will find the magic water for Camelot.” “是啊，别担心，”杰克说。 “安妮和我会为卡梅洛特找到魔法水。”

“But you … you are just children,” said Sir Percival, the third knight. “You must wait … for us.… ” “但是你……你只是孩子，”第三位骑士珀西瓦尔说道。 “你们必须……等我们……”

“There’s no time to wait,” said Jack. “没时间等了，”杰克说。

“Camelot is dying!” said Annie. “We have to hurry!” “卡梅洛特快要灭亡了！”安妮说。 “我们必须快点！”

“Then you must … take this … ,” said Sir Galahad. He reached into a leather pouch that hung around his shoulder. He took out a silver cup. With a trembling hand, the young knight gave the cup to Annie. “那么你们必须……拿这个……”加拉哈德爵士说。 他伸手从肩上挂着的皮 pouch 里拿出东西。 他拿出一个银杯子。 年轻的骑士颤抖着把手，把杯子给了安妮。

“A cup!” she said. “一个杯子！”她说道。

“Take … this, too,” said Sir Percival. He pulled a small wooden box from a bag that hung from his belt. He handed it to Jack. “拿……这个，也拿上，”爵士珀西瓦尔说。 他从腰带上挂着的袋子里拿出一个小木盒子。 他把它交给了杰克。

Jack opened the lid. In the middle of the box was a pointer with markings all around it. 杰克打开了盖子。 盒子中间放着一个指针，指针周围刻满了标记。

“A compass!” said Jack. “指南针！”，杰克喊道。

“And this … ,” said Sir Lancelot. He took a silk cord from around his neck. A glass key hung from the cord. “还有这个……，”兰斯洛特爵士说。 他从脖子上取下一条丝绸绳子。 一条玻璃钥匙挂在绳子上。

“A key!” whispered Annie. “一把钥匙！”，安妮小声说。

Lancelot handed the key to Annie. She and Jack looked at it closely. Then Annie hung it around her neck. When she turned back, all the knights were fast asleep. 兰斯洛特把钥匙交给了安妮。 她和杰克仔细看了看它。 然后安妮把它挂在了脖子上。 当她转身回去时，所有的骑士都睡着了。

“Sweet dreams,” Annie said gently. “You guys need a long nap.” “祝你做个好梦，”安妮温柔地说。 “你们 guys 需要好好睡一觉。”

Jack and Annie stood up. 杰克和安妮站了起来。

“I think we have all our gifts now,” Jack said. “But I’d better make sure.” “我想我们现在已经拿到了所有礼物，”杰克说。 “但我最好再确认一下。”

He hurried to get his backpack. It was lying in the grass near the knights’ swords. He pulled out his notebook and read the second rhyme: 他赶紧去拿他的背包。 它躺在草地上，靠近骑士们的剑旁边。 他拿出笔记本，读了第二段韵文：

“Four gifts you will need— “你需要四件礼物——

The first from me. 第一件来自我。

Then a cup, a compass, 然后是一只杯子、一个指南针。

And, finally, a key.” 最后是一把钥匙。”

“Great,” said Annie. “We got the cloak from the Christmas Knight and the other three gifts from them. This quest is really easy.” “太好了，”安妮说。 “我们从圣诞骑士那里得到了斗篷，其他三件礼物也是他们给的。 这次任务真的很容易。”

Jack shook his head. 杰克摇了摇头。

“It’s not over yet,” he said. “We still have to find the cauldron with the Water of Memory and Imagination.” “还没结束，”他说。 “我们还得找到装着记忆与想象之水的坩埚。”

“We’ll find it,” said Annie. “Read the third rhyme.” “我们会找到的，”安妮说。 “读第三句咒语。”

Jack looked in his notebook and read the third rhyme aloud: 杰克翻开他的笔记本，大声读出第三句咒语：

“If you survive to complete your quest, “如果你活下来完成你的使命。

The secret door lies to the west.” 秘密的门就在西边。”

“No problem!” said Annie. “We survived the guards and the dance. Now the compass can show us how to go west. And the key will unlock the secret door. And we’ll fill the cup with water from the cauldron! See, it’s all easy!” “没问题！”安妮说。 “我们已经躲过了守卫和舞蹈， 现在指南针可以告诉我们怎么往西走。” 而且钥匙会打开秘密的门。 然后我们会用锅里的水把杯子装满！ 看，这很简单！

Jack still felt worried. A little too easy, he thought. 杰克仍然感到担心。 他想，这似乎太容易了。

“What are we waiting for?” said Annie. “Let’s go.” “我们还在等什么？”安妮说。 “我们走吧。”

Jack looked down at the compass. “Okay … ,” he said. “The pointer’s pointing north. So west must be that way.” He pointed left, toward a thicket of bushes and small trees. 杰克低头看着指南针。 “好吧……”，他说。 “指针指着北方。” “所以，西边应该就是那个方向。”他向左指了指，朝一片灌木和小树丛的方向。

“Great,” said Annie. “Here, carry the cup in your pack.” “太好了，”安妮说。 “来，把杯子放进背包里。”

Jack put his notebook and the silver cup into his pack. Then he and Annie started into the thicket. 杰克把他的笔记本和银杯子放进背包里。 然后他和安妮走进了灌木丛。

They ducked under branches and pushed past bushes. Thorns scraped their hands. Twigs snapped against their faces. 他们钻过树枝，挤过灌木丛。 荆棘划伤了他们的手。 树枝啪地一声打在他们的脸上。

Jack kept checking the compass. Could they really be searching in the right place? he wondered. What kind of door would they find in a tangled thicket? 杰克一直看着指南针。 他们真的在正确的地点寻找吗？他想。 他们在一片杂乱的灌木丛中会找到什么样的门呢？

“Listen,” said Annie. “It’s so quiet now.” “听，”安妮说。 “现在这里安静极了。”

The thicket had grown eerily silent. No birds called from the bushes. No music could be heard in the distance. 灌木丛里变得异常安静。 灌木丛中没有鸟儿的叫声。 远处也听不到任何音乐声。

Jack checked the compass once more. “It says we’re still going west,” he said. “I just hope this thing works.” 杰克再次看了看指南针。 “指南针显示我们还在往西走，”他说。 “我只是希望这东西能正常工作。”

“It works,” Annie said softly. “Look—” Annie was holding back a leafy branch. She pointed to a rocky hillside beyond the thicket. Halfway up the hillside was a ledge. “它能正常工作，”安妮轻声说。 “看——”安妮正用手拨开一片枝叶。 她指着灌木丛后面的一片岩石山坡。 山坡中间有一块突出的岩石平台。

Between two giant boulders on the ledge was a shining glass door. 平台上两块巨大的岩石之间，有一扇闪亮的玻璃门。
