3. The Bear Garden 3. 熊园

Jack and Annie passed under the stone gateway that led onto London Bridge. 杰克和安妮穿过通往伦敦桥的石门。

As they started across, Jack was amazed. The bridge was so noisy and smelly! Wagon wheels rumbled like thunder over the cobblestones. Pots clinked in carts. Horses neighed. Shopkeepers shouted. 刚开始过桥，杰克就愣住了。 桥上又吵又臭！ 马车轮在鹅卵石路上轰隆隆地响，像打雷一样。 车上的锅叮当作响。马儿嘶鸣着。 店主们大声吆喝。

“Good pies!” “好馅饼！”

“Hot peas!” “热豌豆！”

“New pins!” “新别针！”

“Shoes! Soap! Salt!” “鞋子！肥皂！ 盐！”

A shopkeeper caught Jack’s eye. “What lack you, boy?” he shouted. 一个店主引起了杰克的注意。 “你需要什么，孩子？”他喊道。

“Nothing, thank you,” said Jack, and kept walking. “不要，谢谢，”杰克说着继续往前走。

“Watch out!” a cart driver yelled. “当心！”一个马车夫喊道。

Jack grabbed Annie’s hand. He pulled her out of the way. The cart rolled past them over the narrow roadway. 杰克抓住安妮的手。 他把她拉到一边。 马车从狭窄的路面上从他们身边碾过。

“Look!” said Annie. She pointed to a bear in a wooden cage in the back of the cart. The bear had matted brown fur. His head was down. “看！”安妮说。 她指着马车后面木笼子里的一只熊。 那只熊有着乱蓬蓬的棕色毛皮。 它低着头。

As the cart rumbled on, Jack shook his head. “What next?” he said. 马车隆隆驶过时，杰克摇了摇头。 “又怎么了？”他说。

“Them,” said Annie, looking up. “那些，”安妮抬头看着说。

She pointed at huge black birds sitting hunched at the edges of the rooftops. The birds sat still as they stared down at all the carts and animals and people crossing London Bridge. Jack shivered and moved quickly past the gaze of the giant silent birds. 她指着蹲在屋顶边缘的巨大黑鸟。 那些鸟一动不动，盯着下面过桥的马车、牲畜和人群。 杰克打了个寒战，赶快从那些巨大而无声的鸟儿的视线下走过。

Finally he and Annie came to the end of the bridge. They stepped onto the riverbank. There they stopped and looked around. 终于，他和安妮走到了桥的尽头。 他们踏上河岸。 他们停下来四处张望。

“I wonder where those big kids went,” said Annie. “不知道那些大孩子去哪儿了，”安妮说。

Jack studied the crowd heading down the road that led from the bridge. There was no sign of the group of ragged boys. 杰克打量着从桥上下来沿路走去的人群。 那群衣衫褴褛的男孩已经不见踪影。

Jack took out their research book. He found the picture of London Bridge. He read aloud: 杰克拿出资料书。 他找到伦敦桥的图。他大声读道：

London Bridge connected London to the south bank of the river, an area where Londoners went for entertainment. The Bear Garden was a popular spot. 伦敦桥连接着伦敦和泰晤士河南岸，那里是伦敦人娱乐的地方。熊花园是个受欢迎的地方。

“The Bear Garden?” said Annie. “That sounds good. Where’s that?” “熊园？”安妮说。 “听起来不错。在哪儿？”

Jack found a map of the south bank. He pointed to a circle that was labeled BEAR GARDEN. 杰克找到一张南岸的地图。 他指着一个标着“熊园”的圆圈。

“Here,” he said. He looked up. “And … there!” He pointed to a dark, round building in the distance. “在这儿，”他说。他抬起头。 “而且……就在那儿！”他指着远处一座黑乎乎的圆形建筑。

“Great!” said Annie. “I want to see the garden filled with bears.” “太好了！”安妮说。 “我想看看满是熊的花园。”

“Let’s read—” started Jack. “我们先读——”杰克开口道。

“Let’s look!” said Annie. She headed toward the Bear Garden. “我们去看！”安妮说。 她朝熊园走去。

Jack put away their book and followed her. As they got closer, they heard loud shouting and laughter coming from inside the round building. 杰克收起书，跟着她。 走近时，他们听见圆形建筑里传来大声的喊叫和笑声。

Annie stopped. 安妮停下了。

“Wait,” she said. “I’m getting a bad feeling about the Bear Garden. Maybe we should read more about it.” “等等，”她说。 “我对熊园有种不好的预感。

Jack opened their book again. He read aloud: 杰克又打开书。他大声读道：

At an arena called the Bear Garden, people watched bears fight with dogs. Animal fights were a common sport in old England. They are against the law today. 在一个叫熊花园的竞技场里，人们看着熊和狗打架。在过去的英国，动物打架是一种常见的运动。现在这是违法的。

“Bears fight with dogs? Yuck!” said Annie. “I couldn’t stand to watch that!” “熊跟狗打架？ 恶心！”安妮说。 “那种场面我可看不下去！”

“Me neither,” said Jack. “Forget that place.” He started to walk away. “我也不，”杰克说。 “忘了那地方吧。”他开始走开。

“Hey, Jack! Look over there!” said Annie. She pointed to a cart nearby. “That’s the bear that passed us on the bridge!” “嘿，杰克！ 看那边！”安妮说。 她指着附近的一辆马车。 “那就是刚才在桥上经过我们的那只熊！”
