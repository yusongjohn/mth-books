5. Stage Fright 5. 怯场

“Why do you need a boy who’s a brilliant reader?” Annie asked the man. “你为什么需要一个朗读很棒的男孩？”安妮问那人。

“Because I have just lost two fairies!” he said. He pointed at Jack. “You can read both!” “因为我刚失去了两个小仙子！”他说。 他指着杰克。 “你可以两个都读！”

And you are nuts, thought Jack. “Well, bye, see you around,” he said. He nudged Annie to move along. 你简直疯了，杰克想。 “好吧，再见，回头见，”他说。 他轻轻推了推安妮，想让她走开。

“Wait, wait,” she said. She turned to the man. “What do you mean, my brother can read both fairies? Read them where?” “等等，等等，”她说。 她转向那人。 “你说我哥哥可以读两个小仙子是什么意思？在哪儿读？”

“Two boy actors didn’t show up today to play fairies,” said the man. “But your brother reads with such expression! He can save us all!” “今天有两个演小仙子的男演员没来，”那人说。 “可你哥哥读得这么有感情！ 他能救我们大家！”

Jack stared. Was this guy saying what he thought he was saying? 杰克瞪大了眼睛。 这家伙说的，难道是他想的那样？

“You mean you want Jack to be in your play?” said Annie. “你是说想让杰克演你的戏？”安妮说。

“Indeed!” said the man. “There are three thousand people here today, waiting to see the play I have written! We cannot disappoint them, can we?” “正是！”那人说。 “今天这儿有三千人，等着看我写的戏！ 我们总不能让他们失望吧？”

“Three thousand?” said Jack. “三千人？”杰克说。

“Yes!” said the man. “And one of them is the most important person in the world!” “是的！”那人说。 “而且其中一位是世界上最重要的人！”

“No. No way. I can’t do that,” said Jack. He had never liked being onstage. He always got stage fright. “不。绝对不行。 我做不到，”杰克说。 他从来就不喜欢站在舞台上。 他总是会怯场。

“Wait, wait, Jack,” said Annie. She turned to the man. “You need two fairies, right?” “等等，等等，杰克，”安妮说。 她转向那人。 “你需要两个小仙子，对吧？”

“Yes,” said the man. “对，”那人说。

“Well … ” Annie tilted her head. Her voice went up. “I can read, too.” “嗯……”安妮歪了歪头。 她的声音提高了。 “我也会读。”

“Yes! Let Annie do it,” said Jack. “She’s a great reader. She can be both fairies!” “对！ 让安妮演，”杰克说。 “她读得特别好。 她可以演两个小仙子！”

“Ah, but of course Annie cannot go onstage,” the man said kindly. “啊，可安妮当然不能上台，”那人温和地说。

“Why not?” asked Annie. “为什么不行？”安妮问。

The man raised his eyebrows. “Surely you know it’s against the law for girls to go on the stage,” he said. “Boys must play all the girls’ parts.” 那人扬起眉毛。 “你当然知道，女孩上台演戏是违法的，”他说。 “女孩的角色都必须由男孩来演。”

“But that’s not fair!” said Annie. “可那不公平！”安妮说。

“Indeed, ’tis not. But we cannot change the law now,” said the man. He turned to Jack. “So, Jack? Will you join our players?” “的确，不是这样。 可我们现在改不了这条法律，”那人说。 他转向杰克。 “那么，杰克？ 你愿意加入我们的戏班吗？”

“No thanks,” said Jack. He tried to walk off, but Annie grabbed his arm. “不用了，谢谢，”杰克说。 他想走开，可安妮抓住了他的胳膊。

“Wait, I think I know what Jack wants,” she said to the man. “He will only be in your play if I can be in it, too.” “等等，我想我知道杰克想要什么，”她对那人说。 “只有我也演，他才愿意演你们的戏。”

“No, that’s not what I want, Annie,” Jack said under his breath. “不，我才不要那样，安妮，”杰克低声说。

“But, Jack, it would be so much fun,” she whispered. “And there’s nothing to be afraid of. You get to read your part. You don’t have to memorize it.” “可是，杰克，那会很好玩的，”她低声说。 “而且没什么好怕的。 你可以照着念自己的台词。 你不用背下来。”

Jack could tell that Annie really, really wanted to be in the play. And it was definitely a way to keep her mind off the bear. 杰克能看出安妮真的、真的很想演戏。 而且这绝对是个让她别再想那只熊的办法。

“Uh, okay,” he said, sighing. He looked at the man. “I’ll be in your play—if Annie can be in it, too.” “呃，好吧，”他叹着气说。 他看着那人。 “我演你们的戏——如果安妮也能演的话。”

The man looked at Annie. She smiled eagerly at him. 那人看着安妮。 她热切地朝他笑了笑。

The man smiled back. 那人也朝她笑了笑。

“Why not?” he said. “But Annie will have to pretend to be a boy. She can tuck up her hair, and we’ll call her Andy.” “为什么不呢？”他说。 “可安妮得假装成男孩。 她可以把头发塞起来，我们叫她安迪。”

“Yay, thanks!” said Annie with a grin. “耶，谢谢！”安妮咧嘴笑着说。

A trumpet blasted inside the theater. 剧院里响起一阵喇叭声。

“Hark, the play begins!” the man said. He took Jack and Annie by the hand. “听，戏开始了！”那人说。 他拉着杰克和安妮的手。

“My name is Will, by the way,” he said. “Come along, Jack and Andy! Be as swift as shadows!” “对了，我叫威尔，”他说。 “跟我来，杰克和安迪！ 像影子一样快！”
