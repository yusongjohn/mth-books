9. Sweet Sorrow 9. 甜蜜的悲伤

“Tut, tut, you’re a sorry sight, man,” Will said to the bear’s owner. “Trying to sell an old tame bear to the fights. Well, I’ve been planning to write a play with a part for a bear. So take this money for him and be gone.” “啧啧，你可真够可怜的，伙计，”威尔对熊的主人说。 “居然想把一只又老又温顺的熊卖去斗熊。 嗯，我正打算写一出有熊出场的戏。 所以拿上这笔买他的钱，走开吧。”

Will handed some gold coins to the bear’s owner. 威尔把几枚金币递给熊的主人。

The man’s eyes grew wide. He laughed. 那人眼睛睁大了。他笑了。

“You can have him!” the man said. And he took off. “你拿走吧！”那人说。然后他走了。

“Thank you, and good riddance!” called Will. Then he turned to Puck. “Take our new player to the stables. Tell the actors they needn’t be afraid of him. He’s tamer than most of them.” “谢谢，总算摆脱了！”威尔喊道。 然后他转向帕克。 “带我们的新演员去马厩。 告诉演员们不用怕他。 他比他们多数人都温顺。”

“Come this way,” Puck said. He put his arm around the bear and gently led him out from the trees. “You’ll love the stage, old man.” “这边走，”帕克说。 他搂住熊，轻轻把它带出树林。 “你会爱上舞台的，老家伙。”

“Bye, Puck! Bye, Dan!” said Annie. “再见，帕克！ 再见，丹！”安妮说。

Puck smiled and waved. The bear stared for a moment at Jack and Annie. He had a grateful look in his eyes. Then he lumbered off with Puck. 帕克笑着挥手。 熊盯着杰克和安妮看了一会儿。 他眼里带着感激的神情。 然后他和帕克一起慢吞吞地走开了。

“Thanks, Will, for helping Dan,” said Annie. “And for helping us.” “谢谢你，威尔，帮了丹，”安妮说。 “也谢谢你帮了我们。”

“Thank you both for helping me,” said Will. “You saved the day.” “也谢谢你们俩帮了我，”威尔说。 “你挽救了局面。”

“The night, you mean,” said Jack. “你是说夜里，”杰克说。

“Indeed, the night,” said Will. “Oh, here is your bag. You forgot it.” He gave Jack his leather bag. Then he held up their two scrolls with their lines on them. “的确，夜里，”威尔说。 “哦，这是你们的袋子。 你们忘了。”他把皮袋子递给杰克。 然后他举起他们写着台词的两卷卷轴。

“And you can take these,” said Will. He handed them to Jack, and Jack put them in his bag. “这些你们也可以带走，”威尔说。 他把卷轴递给杰克，杰克放进袋子里。

“Where are you off to now?” said Will. “你们现在要出发去哪儿？”威尔说。

“The other side of London Bridge,” said Annie. “伦敦桥的另一边，”安妮说。

“Ah, I can take you there in my boat,” said Will. “Follow me.” “啊，我可以用我的船送你们过去，”威尔说。 “跟我来。”

Will led them along a dusty path toward the river. The last rays of the sun slanted through the trees. Soon they came to a small rowboat docked on the Thames. 威尔领着他们沿着一条尘土飞扬的小路朝河边走去。 最后几缕阳光斜斜地穿过树林。 很快他们来到泰晤士河边一艘停靠着的小船上。

“Climb in,” said Will. “爬上来，”威尔说。

Jack, Annie, and Will climbed in. Will untied the boat from the dock. Then he began rowing across the river. 杰克、安妮和威尔爬了上去。 威尔解开停靠在码头的船。 然后他开始划船过河。

The water reflected the purple and pink in the sky. Only a few white swans now glided over the shimmering ripples. The river smelled as bad as before, but Jack didn’t mind now. He had grown used to it. 水面映出天空的紫色和粉色。 现在只有几只白天鹅在闪闪发光的涟漪上滑行。 河水还是和以前一样难闻，可杰克现在已经不在意了。 他已经习惯了。

Jack pulled out his notebook and pencil. 杰克拿出笔记本和铅笔。

“What are you doing?” Will asked. “你在做什么？”威尔问。

“I wanted to write some of my memories in my notebook,” said Jack. “我想把一些回忆写在笔记本里，”杰克说。

“Ah, and I’ll note you both in my book of memory,” said Will. “啊，我会把你们俩记在我的记忆之书里，”威尔说。

Jack smiled. 杰克笑了。

“I have a question, Will,” said Annie. “Why does Queen Elizabeth have black teeth?” “我有个问题，威尔，”安妮说。 “伊丽莎白女王的牙为什么是黑的？”

“Too much sugar,” said Will. “糖吃太多了，”威尔说。

“I hope it doesn’t make her feel bad—looking that way, I mean,” said Annie. “我希望那不会让她难受——我是说，长成那样，”安妮说。

“Oh, no, she’s not the least hurt,” said Will. “The queen has no idea how she looks. She hasn’t peeked in a good mirror for twenty years.” “哦，不，她一点也没受伤，”威尔说。 “女王根本不知道自己长什么样。 她已经二十年没好好照过镜子了。”

“Is that true?” said Annie. “真的吗？”安妮说。

“ ’Tis,” said Will. “The queen pretends to be young and beautiful. Just as you pretended to be a boy, and the bear pretended to be an actor. You see, all the world’s a stage.” “是真的，”威尔说。 “女王假装自己又年轻又美丽。 就像你假装成男孩，熊假装成演员一样。 你看，整个世界就是一个舞台。”

Jack liked that idea. He wrote in his notebook: 杰克喜欢这个想法。 他在笔记本上写道：

图中文字：All the world's a stage 图中文字：全世界都是舞台

Jack looked up at London Bridge as they passed by. The shops on the bridge were closed now. The theater crowd was thinning out. 他们经过时，杰克抬头看着伦敦桥。 桥上的店铺现在都关门了。 看戏的人群渐渐散了。

The scary black birds were no longer on the rooftops. They had swooped down and were picking at the garbage left on the cobblestones. 那些可怕的黑鸟也不在屋顶上了。 它们已经俯冲下来，在啄鹅卵石路上留下的垃圾。

The show was over. 演出结束了。

By the time they came to the riverbank, night was falling. It was much cooler. Will tied up his rowboat and climbed ashore with Jack and Annie. 等他们到达河岸时，夜幕正在降临。 天气凉快多了。 威尔系好小船，和杰克、安妮一起上岸。

“Thanks a lot,” Jack said to Will. “We can go the rest of the way by ourselves.” “太谢谢了，”杰克对威尔说。 “剩下的路我们自己能走。”

“Where do you live?” said Will. “你们住在哪儿？”威尔说。

“In Frog Creek,” said Annie. “在蛙溪，”安妮说。

“What path do you take?” asked Will. “你们走哪条路？”威尔问。

“You’ll never believe this,” said Annie. “We climb up the rope ladder to a tree house in that tree over there. And we open a book—” “你绝对不会相信，”安妮说。 “我们爬上绳梯，到那边那棵树上的树屋里。 然后打开一本书——”

“Then we make a wish,” said Jack, “and we go to the place in the book.” “然后许愿，”杰克说，“我们就去到书里的地方。”

Will smiled. 威尔笑了。

“Thy life’s a miracle, isn’t it?” he said. “你的生命就是个奇迹，不是吗？”他说。

“Yes!” said Annie. Jack nodded. He liked the way Will looked at things. “是的！”安妮说。杰克点了点头。 他喜欢威尔看事情的方式。

“I have an idea,” said Will. “Why don’t you both stay here instead? You can live and act at the Globe Theater. I’ll ask the queen to exclude you from the law about girls going onstage, Annie—because of your great talent. And I will teach you both to write plays.” “我有个主意，”威尔说。 “你们为什么不留在这儿呢？ 你们可以住在环球剧院，在这儿演戏。 我会请求女王把你排除在女孩不能上台的法律之外，安妮——因为你很有天赋。 我还会教你们俩写剧本。”

“Really?” said Jack and Annie together. “真的？”杰克和安妮一起说。

Jack couldn’t imagine anything more fun. Then he thought of his parents back in Frog Creek. 杰克想不出还有什么比这更有趣了。 接着他想起了蛙溪的爸爸妈妈。

“But our mom and dad—” he said. “可是我们的爸爸妈妈——”他说。

“We would really miss them,” said Annie. “我们会非常想他们的，”安妮说。

Will smiled. 威尔笑了。

“I understand,” he said. “And I would miss you if I were them. ” He put his hand over his heart. “So, good night, good night. Parting is such sweet sorrow.” “我明白，”他说。 “要是我是他们，我也会想你们的。”他把手放在胸口。 “那么，晚安，晚安。 离别是这样甜蜜的悲伤。”

“Yes, ’tis,” said Annie, nodding. “是的，的确，”安妮点着头说。

“Farewell!” said Will. He waved. “再见！”威尔说。他挥了挥手。

Jack and Annie waved back. Then Will turned on his heels and headed back to his rowboat. 杰克和安妮也挥手回应。 然后威尔突然转身，朝他的小船走回去。

Jack and Annie walked to the rope ladder and climbed up to the tree house. When they got inside, they looked out the window. 杰克和安妮走到绳梯旁，爬上树屋。 进到里面后，他们朝窗外望去。

Will was rowing back across the Thames River. A single white swan glided across the ripples beside his boat. A silver moon was rising in the sky. 威尔正划着船穿过泰晤士河往回走。 一只白天鹅在他船边的涟漪上滑过。 一轮银色的月亮正在升起。

At that moment, Jack did feel sweet sorrow. He wanted to stay in merry olde England just a little longer. 就在那一刻，杰克真的感到了甜蜜的悲伤。 他还想在快乐的古老英格兰再多待一会儿。

“Wait, Will!” he shouted. “等等，威尔！”他喊道。

But Annie picked up the Pennsylvania book. 可安妮拿起了宾夕法尼亚的书。

“I wish we could go home,” she said. “我希望我们能回家，”她说。

The wind started to blow. 风开始吹起来。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。
