4. A Midsummer Night’s Dream 4. 《仲夏夜之梦》

Annie and Jack ran over to the cart. In the back of it was a cage. In the cage was a big brown bear. 安妮和杰克朝那辆马车跑过去。 车后面有一个笼子。 笼子里是一只棕色的大熊。

The bear was slumped over, his head still down. The sign on the cart said DAN THE DANCING BEAR. 那只熊耷拉着身子，头还垂着。 马车上的牌子写着：跳舞熊丹。

“Dan?” Annie asked. “Are you going to fight?” “丹？”安妮问。 “你要去打架吗？”

The lonely-looking bear raised his huge head and looked at Annie. His dark eyes were sad. He let out a low moan. 那只看起来很孤单的熊抬起巨大的头，看着安妮。 它黑黑的眼睛很悲伤。 它发出一声低低的呻吟。

“I understand,” Annie said. “You don’t want to fight. You’re asking me to take you away.” Annie reached for the door of the bear’s cage. “我明白了，”安妮说。 “你不想打架。 你是在求我把你带走。”安妮伸手去够熊笼的门。

图中文字：Dan the Dancing Bear 图中文字：跳舞的丹熊

“Away with you!” someone shouted angrily. “That’s my bear!” “走开！”有人愤怒地喊道。 “那是我的熊！”

Jack and Annie whirled around. The cart driver was charging toward them. 杰克和安妮猛地转过身。 马车夫正朝他们冲过来。

“He’s mine! I’m selling him!” the man shouted. “他是我的！ 我要卖掉他！”那人喊道。

“Come on, Annie. Let’s go,” said Jack. He pulled her into the crowd walking down the road. “走吧，安妮。 我们走，”杰克说。 他把她拉进沿路走着的人群里。

“But I have to save Dan!” said Annie, looking over her shoulder. “That guy wants to sell him to the bear fights!” “可我得救丹啊！”安妮一边回头看一边说。 “那家伙要把他卖去斗熊！”

“I know,” said Jack. “But we can’t just steal him. That guy is his owner.” “我知道，”杰克说。 “可我们不能就这么偷走他。 那个人是他的主人。”

Jack looked around. He needed to get Annie’s mind off the bear. He saw the group of older kids from the bridge. They were walking toward a round white building. 杰克四下看了看。 他得让安妮别再想那只熊了。 他看见桥上那群大孩子。 他们正朝一座圆形的白色建筑走去。

“Hey, look, the kids from the bridge!” he said. “Let’s see where they’re going.” “嘿，看，桥上那些孩子！”他说。 “我们去看看他们要去哪儿。”

“What about Dan?” said Annie. “那丹怎么办？”安妮说。

“We can figure that out later,” said Jack. “Let’s follow those kids now.” “我们等会儿再想办法，”杰克说。 “现在先跟着那些孩子。”

He steered Annie toward the white building. When they got closer, Jack read the sign out front: 他领着安妮朝那栋白色建筑走去。 走近时，杰克读了门前的牌子：

A PLAY AT THE GLOBE THEATER!

A MIDSUMMER NIGHT’S DREAM 在环球剧院的一场演出！《仲夏夜之梦》

Great! thought Jack. Annie loved plays. She loved acting in them at school. 太好了！杰克想。 安妮热爱戏剧。 她喜欢在学校里演戏。

A man stood at the door of the theater. He was holding a box. 一个男人站在剧院门口。 他手里拿着一个盒子。

“A penny to stand! A penny to stand!” he shouted. “站着看只要一便士！ 站着看只要一便士！”他喊道。

The older kids dropped coins into the box and went inside. 那些大孩子把硬币投进盒子，走了进去。

“Wow, the play costs only a penny!” said Jack. “That’s cheap!” “哇，看戏只要一便士！”杰克说。 “真便宜！”

“But we don’t have any pennies,” said Annie. “Besides, I want to go back and free the bear.” “可我们一分钱也没有，”安妮说。 “再说，我想回去把熊放出来。”

Jack sighed. 杰克叹了口气。

“What will you do with him if you free him, Annie?” he asked. “你要是放了他，打算拿他怎么办，安妮？”他问。

“I’ll figure something out,” she said. “我会想办法的，”她说。

“Well, figure it out when the owner’s not standing there,” Jack said. “Right now, let’s learn something about this play.” “等主人不在旁边时再想办法吧，”杰克说。 “现在先了解一下这出戏吧。”

He quickly pulled out their research book. He found a picture of the Globe Theater. He wanted Annie to forget about the bear, so he read with lots of feeling: 他很快抽出资料书。 他找到一张环球剧院的图片。 他想让安妮忘掉那只熊，于是读得特别有感情：

The first theaters were built in old England. Because there was no electricity, plays were performed during the day, when it was light. Almost everyone could afford to go. 最早的剧院建在英国。因为那时候没有电，戏剧都是在白天演出，天亮的时候演。几乎每个人都能去看。

“Neat, huh?” said Jack. “真有意思，哈？”杰克说。

Annie sighed. 安妮叹了口气。

Jack kept reading in a loud, dramatic voice: 杰克继续用响亮而夸张的声音读着：

Seating for the audience depended on how much was paid. The people who could afford the higher prices sat in galleries above the stage. Others stood in an area below the— 观众的座位取决于他们付了多少钱。那些能付更高价格的人坐在舞台上方的包厢里。其他人则站在舞台下方的区域—

“Boy!” someone shouted. “小伙子！”有人喊道。

Jack looked up. 杰克抬起头。

A man hurried over to Jack and Annie. He was long-legged, with a trim beard and twinkly eyes. 一个男人快步走向杰克和安妮。 他腿很长，留着整齐的胡子，眼睛亮晶晶的。

“I could hear you from across the way,” the man said. “You read very well!” “我在对面就听见你了，”那人说。 “你读得很好！”

Jack smiled shyly. 杰克不好意思地笑了笑。

“No, you are simply brilliant!” the man said. “And I am in great need of a boy who is a brilliant reader!” “不，你简直太出色了！”那人说。 “而我正急需一个朗读很棒的男孩！”
