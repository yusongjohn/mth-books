4. Moon Rabbits 4. 月球兔子

“Oh, wow!” said Annie. She took a step forward. “哦哇！”安妮说。 她向前迈了一步。

But Jack stood frozen. He wanted to get a good look at everything first. 可杰克站在那儿一动不动。 他想先好好看清楚周围的一切。

He stared at the ground. He was standing in a layer of gray dust as fine as powder. 他盯着地面。 他站在一层细得像粉末一样的灰色尘土里。

Footprints were everywhere. Jack wondered who had made them. 到处都是脚印。 杰克想知道是谁留下的这些脚印。

He reached into his pack for the moon book. To his surprise, it was as light as a feather! 他伸手进背包去拿月球书。 让他吃惊的是，它轻得像羽毛一样！

He found a picture of footprints on the moon. He read: 他找到一幅月球脚印的图。他读道：

The moon has no rain or wind to blow the dust around. So footprints will never wear away naturally, not even in a billion years. 月亮上没有雨，也没有风可以把灰尘吹动。所以，脚印永远不会自然消失，哪怕过了十亿年也不会。

“Oh, man,” Jack said. “天哪，”杰克说。

The moon was the stillest place he had ever, ever been. It was as still as a picture. And its stillness would never, ever end. 月球是他去过的最安静的地方。 它静得像一幅画。 而这种寂静永远、永远不会结束。

Jack stared at the ink-black sky. A lovely blue-and-white ball glowed far away. 杰克盯着墨黑的天空。 一个美丽的蓝白相间的球体在远处发光。

Earth.

For the first time, it really hit Jack. They were in outer space. 杰克第一次真正意识到了这一点。 他们在外太空。

“Look!” Annie cried, laughing. “看！”安妮笑着喊道。

She bounded past Jack—almost flying through the air. She landed on her feet. Then she jumped again. 她从杰克身边跳过去——几乎是在空中飞。 她双脚落地。 然后她又跳了一次。

“I’m a moon rabbit,” she called. “我是月球兔子，”她喊道。

Jack laughed. How does she do that? he wondered. He turned a page and read: 杰克笑了。 她是怎么做到的？他想。 他翻过一页读道：

A person weighs less on the moon because of the moon’s low gravity and lack of air. If you weigh 60 pounds on Earth, you would only weigh 10 pounds on the moon. 人在月球上体重更轻，是因为月球的重力小，而且没有空气。如果你在地球上重60磅，到了月球上就只重10磅。

“Don’t just stand there reading!” said Annie, grabbing the book from Jack’s gloved hand. She tossed it into space. “别光站着看书！”安妮说，从杰克戴着手套的手里抢走书。 她把它扔进了太空。

It flew far away. 书飞得很远。

Jack started after it. 杰克去追它。

He bounded up and down. Boing! Boing! Boing! Now he felt as light as a feather. 他上下蹦着。嘣！ 嘣！嘣！ 现在他觉得自己轻如羽毛。

“Look!” he called to Annie. “I’m a moon rabbit, too.” “看！”他对安妮喊道。 “我也是月球兔子。”

Where Jack’s boots hit the ground, moondust gracefully sprayed into space. 杰克的靴子落到地面上时，月尘优雅地喷向太空。

The book had landed at the edge of a shallow crater. 书落在一个浅坑的边缘。

When Jack reached it, he tried to stop. But his feet slipped. 杰克够到书时，他想停下来。 可他的脚滑了一下。

He fell right over and lay on his side. He tried to stand. But he was off-balance. 他一下子摔倒，侧躺在地上。 他想站住。 可他失去了平衡。

He tried again. But the dust was just too deep. And his spacesuit was too clumsy. 他又试了一次。 可尘土实在太深了。 而且他的太空服太笨重了。

“You okay?” asked Annie. “你没事吧？”安妮问。

“I can’t get up,” said Jack. “我起不来，”杰克说。

“You shouldn’t have been goofing off,” said Annie wisely. “你不该瞎闹的，”安妮一本正经地说。

“You goofed off first,” said Jack. “Now, help me up, please.” “是你先瞎闹的，”杰克说。 “现在，请帮我起来。”

Annie started toward him. 安妮朝他走来。

“Don’t fall, too,” warned Jack. “别你也摔倒了，”杰克警告说。

“I won’t.” Annie moved very slowly. She half floated, half walked. “不会。”安妮走得很慢。 她一半像漂着，一半像走着。

“Give me your hand,” she said. “把手给我，”她说。

Annie grabbed Jack’s hand. She pressed her boot against his and pulled him up. 安妮抓住杰克的手。 她把靴子抵着他的靴子，把他拉了起来。

“Thanks,” he said. “谢谢，”他说。

“No problem,” she said. “It was easy. You were really light.” “不客气，”她说。 “很容易。 你真的很轻。”

“Thank goodness,” said Jack. “It’s impossible to get up alone.” “谢天谢地，”杰克说。 “一个人根本起不来。”

He picked up the moon book. It was covered with dust. He brushed it off. 他捡起月亮书。 它上面蒙满了尘土。 他把它掸掉。

“Oh, wow! Look!” said Annie. She stood at the edge of the crater. 看！”安妮说。 她站在陨石坑边缘。

“What is it?” said Jack. “那是什么？”杰克说。

“A moon buggy!” said Annie. “一辆月球车！”安妮说。

The buggy was parked in the crater. It had four huge wheels. 月球车停在坑里。 它有四个巨大的轮子。

“Let’s go for a ride,” said Annie. “我们去兜风吧，”安妮说。

“We can’t,” said Jack. “We just have two hours of air in our tanks. Remember?” “我们不能，”杰克说。 “氧气罐里只有两小时空气。记得吗？”

“I bet we’ll find the M thing faster if we take the moon buggy!” Annie bounded into the crater. “我敢打赌，坐月球车我们能更快找到那件M宝物！”安妮跳进了坑里。

“But we can’t drive!” said Jack. “可我们不会开车！”杰克说。

“I bet I can drive this, ” said Annie. “It looks easy. Come on!” “我打赌我会开这个，”安妮说。 “看起来很容易。来吧！”

She jumped into the driver’s seat. 她跳进驾驶座。

“But you don’t have a license!” said Jack. “可你没有驾照！”杰克说。

“Who cares?” said Annie. “There aren’t any roads on the moon, or stoplights, or policemen either.” “谁在乎？”安妮说。 “月球上既没有路，也没有交通信号灯，更没有警察。”

She was right, Jack thought. 她说得对，杰克想。

“Well, go slow,” he said. And he climbed in beside her. “好吧，开慢点，”他说。 他爬上去坐到她旁边。

Annie pushed a button labeled ON. 安妮按了一个标着ON的按钮。

The moon buggy lurched backward. 月球车猛地向后一晃。

“Yikes!” said Annie. “哎呀！”安妮说。

“Step on the brake!” said Jack. “踩刹车！”杰克说。

Annie pressed a pedal on the floor. The buggy stopped with a jerk. 安妮踩了踩地板上的踏板。 车子猛地一顿，停了下来。

“Whew,” she said. “呼，”她说。

“It must be in reverse,” said Jack. “Let me study this—” “一定是在倒车挡，”杰克说。 “让我研究一下——”

But before he could study anything, Annie pushed another button. 可他还没来得及研究什么，安妮又按了另一个按钮。

The buggy tilted back. Its front wheels started to rise into the air. 车子向后倾斜。 前轮开始升到空中。

“Let me out of here!” said Jack. “让我下去！”杰克说。

Annie pushed more buttons. 安妮又按了几个按钮。

The buggy’s front wheels landed back on the ground. And the buggy leaped forward. 月面车的前轮落回地面。 月球车猛地向前冲去。

“Slower!” said Jack. “慢一点！”杰克说。

“I can’t,” said Annie. “I don’t know how!” “我做不到，”安妮说。 “我不知道怎么弄！”

Annie steered the buggy over the tracks on the ground. The wide wheels kept it from sinking into the deep dust. 安妮驾驶着月球车沿着地上的车辙走。 宽大的轮子使它没有陷进深深的尘土里。

“Careful!” said Jack. “小心！”杰克说。

The buggy zoomed out of the crater. 月球车飞快地冲出了坑。

Gray clouds of dust rose behind them as they took off across the moon. 他们冲过月球表面时，灰色的尘云在身后扬起。
