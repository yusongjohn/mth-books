1. By Moonlight 1. 月光下

“Jack!” whispered a voice. “杰克！”一个声音轻声说。

Jack opened his eyes. He saw a figure in the moonlight. 杰克睁开眼睛。 他看见月光里有个人影。

“Wake up. Get dressed.” It was his sister, Annie. “醒醒。 穿好衣服。”是他的妹妹安妮。

Jack turned on his lamp. He rubbed his eyes. 杰克打开了灯。 他揉了揉眼睛。

Annie was standing beside his bed. She wore jeans and a sweatshirt. 安妮站在他床边。 她穿着牛仔裤和运动衫。

“Let’s go to the tree house,” she said. “我们去树屋吧，”她说。

“What time is it?” asked Jack. He put on his glasses. “现在几点了？”杰克问。 他戴上眼镜。

“Don’t look at your clock,” said Annie. “别看你的钟，”安妮说。

Jack looked at his clock. “Oh, man,” he said. “It’s midnight. It’s too dark.” 杰克看了看钟。 “天哪，”他说。 “已经午夜了。太黑了。”

“No, it isn’t. The moon makes it bright enough to see,” said Annie. “不，不黑。 月亮够亮，能看清路，”安妮说。

“Wait till morning,” said Jack. “等到早上吧，”杰克说。

“No—now,” said Annie. “We have to find the fourth M thing. I have a feeling that the full moon might help us.” “不——就现在，”安妮说。 “我们得找到第四样 M宝物。 我有种感觉，满月也许能帮上我们。”

“That’s nuts,” said Jack. “I want to sleep.” “那太疯狂了，”杰克说。 “我想睡觉。”

“You can sleep when we come back home,” said Annie. “No time will have passed.” “等我们回家你再睡，”安妮说。 “时间不会过去的。”

Jack sighed. “Oh, brother,” he said. 杰克放心地叹了口气。 “唉，”他说。

But he got out of bed. 可他还是下了床。

“Yay!” whispered Annie. “Meet you at the back door.” She tiptoed out of Jack’s room. “耶！”安妮轻声说。 “后门见。”她踮着脚走出了杰克的房间。

Jack yawned. He pulled on his jeans and sneakers and a sweatshirt. He put his notebook and pencil into his backpack. Then he crept down the stairs. 杰克打了个哈欠。 他迅速穿上牛仔裤、运动鞋和一件运动衫。 他把笔记本和铅笔放进背包。 然后他悄悄走下楼梯。

Annie opened the back door. Quietly, they stepped outside. 安妮打开后门。 他们悄悄走了出去。

“Wait—” said Jack. “We need a flashlight.” “等等——”杰克说。 “我们需要手电筒。”

“No, we don’t. I told you—the moon will light our way,” said Annie. And she took off. “不需要。 我跟你说过——月亮会照亮我们的路，”安妮说。然后她就跑开了。

Jack sighed, then followed her. 杰克叹了口气，跟着她。

Annie was right, thought Jack. The moon was so bright that he could see his shadow. Everything seemed washed with silver. 安妮说得对，杰克想。 月亮亮得他都能看见自己的影子。 一切都像被银色洗过一样。

Soon they left their street. Annie led the way into the Frog Creek woods. It was much darker under the shadows of the trees. 很快他们离开了自己的街道。 安妮领路走进蛙溪树林。 在树荫下，要暗多了。

Jack looked up, searching for the tree house. 杰克抬头寻找树屋。

“There!” said Annie. “那儿！”安妮说。

The magic tree house was shining in the moonlight. 魔法树屋在月光下闪着光。

Annie grabbed the rope ladder and started climbing up. 安妮抓住绳梯开始往上爬。

“Careful—go slowly,” said Jack. “小心——慢点，”杰克说。

He followed her up the ladder and into the tree house. 他跟着她爬上梯子，进了树屋。

Moonlight streamed through the window. 月光从窗户泻进来。

It shone on the letter M that shimmered on the wooden floor. 它照在木地板上闪着微光的字母M上。

It shone on the three M things that rested on the M: a moonstone from the time of the ninjas, a mango from the Amazon rain forest, and a mammoth bone from the Ice Age. 它照着放在字母M上的三件M宝物：一块忍者时代的月光石、一颗亚马孙雨林的芒果，还有一根冰河时代的猛犸骨头。

“We need just one more M thing,” said Annie, “to free Morgan from her spell.” “我们只差最后一件M宝物，”安妮说，“就能把摩根从咒语里救出来。”

Squeak.

“Peanut!” said Annie.

In the dim light, Jack saw a tiny mouse. She sat on an open book. 在昏暗的光线里，杰克看见一只小老鼠。 她坐在一本翻开的书上。

“You didn’t expect to see us this late, did you?” said Annie. “你没想到我们这么晚还会来，对吧？”安妮说。

She picked up Peanut. And Jack picked up the open book. 她抱起花生。 杰克拿起那本翻开的书。

“So where are we going this time?” Annie asked him. “那我们这次要去哪儿？”安妮问他。

Jack held the book up to the moonlight. 杰克把书举到月光下。

“Uh-oh,” he said. “I knew we should have brought a flashlight. I can’t read a thing.” “糟糕，”他说。 “我就知道我们应该带手电筒。 我一个字也看不清。”

He could make out diagrams and shadowy pictures. But he couldn’t read a word. 他勉强能辨认出示意图和模糊的图片。 可他一个字也读不出来。

“Look at the cover,” said Annie. “看看封面，”安妮说。

The letters were bigger on the cover. Jack squinted at them. 封面上的字母更大些。 杰克眯起眼睛看。

“It’s called Hello, Moon, ” he said. “书名叫《你好，月亮》，”他说。

Annie gasped. “We’re going to the moon?” 安妮倒抽一口气。 “我们要去月球？”

“Of course not,” said Jack. “It’s impossible to go to the moon without tons of equipment.” “当然不是，”杰克说。 “没有成吨的设备，根本不可能上月球。”

“Why?”

“There’s no air. We couldn’t breathe. Not only that, we’d boil to death if it was day and freeze to death if it was night.” “那儿没有空气。 我们没法呼吸。 “不仅如此，要是白天我们会被热死，要是晚上又会被冻死。”

“Yikes,” said Annie. “So where do you think we are going?” “哎呀，”安妮说。 “那你觉得我们要去哪儿？”

“Maybe a place where people train to be astronauts,” said Jack. “也许是个训练宇航员的地方，”杰克说。

“That sounds neat,” said Annie. “听起来真棒，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. He’d always wanted to meet astronauts and space scientists. “是啊，”杰克说。 他一直想见见宇航员和太空科学家。

“So say the wish,” said Annie. “那就许愿吧，”安妮说。

Jack opened the book again. He pointed to a picture of a dome-shaped structure. 杰克又打开书。 他指着一张圆顶建筑的图片。

“I wish we could go there,” he said. “我希望我们能去那儿，”他说。

The wind started to blow. 风开始刮起来。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster and faster. 它转得越来越快，越来越快。

Then everything was silent. 然后一切都静了下来。

Absolutely silent. As quiet and still as silence could be. 完全寂静。 安静得不能再安静了。
