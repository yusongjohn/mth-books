5. Hang On! 5. 抓紧！

Annie drove the moon buggy over bumps and hollows. It bucked like a bronco. 安妮开着月球车越过凸起和低洼处。 它像野马一样颠簸起来。

“I’m going through there!” She pointed to an opening between two mountains. “我要从那儿穿过去！”她指着两座山之间的缺口。

Jack held on to the dashboard. 杰克紧紧抓住仪表盘。

The buggy bumped toward the opening and shot through. 车子颠簸着冲向缺口，飞快穿了过去。

On the other side, the ground was even rockier. 另一边，地面更加崎岖。

“Look for the fo-fourth M thing!” said Annie, bouncing up and down. “找第——第四件M宝物！”安妮一边说，一边上下颠着。

Jack groaned. Looking for anything on this wild ride was impossible. 杰克抱怨了一声。 在这种疯狂的颠簸中，想找任何东西都不可能。

“Sl-slow d-down!” he said. “慢——慢点——下——来！”他说。

“How?” “怎么弄？”

“Try pressing on the b-brake pedal. On the f-floor—slowly!” “试着踩刹——刹车踏板。 在地——地板上——慢慢踩！”

Annie pressed on the brake. 安妮踩下刹车。

The buggy slowed down. Jack sighed with relief. The ride was still bumpy. But now, at least, he could take a good look at the moon. 月面车慢了下来。 杰克放心地叹了口气。 路还是颠簸。 但至少现在，他能好好看看月球了。

He had never been to such a colorless, barren place. There was no green, no blue, no red. 他从没到过这么毫无色彩、荒凉贫瘠的地方。 没有绿色，没有蓝色，没有红色。

No water, no trees, no clouds. 没有水，没有树，没有云。

Only giant gray rocks and craters—and an American flag. 只有巨大的灰色岩石和陨石坑——还有一面美国国旗。





“Oh, man,” said Jack. “That’s from the first astronauts who landed on the moon!” “天哪，”杰克说。 “那是第一批登上月球的宇航员留下的！”

“And look—a telescope!” said Annie. “快看——一架望远镜！”安妮说。

She drove near the flag and telescope. Then she put her foot on the brake until the buggy stopped. 她开到国旗和望远镜附近。 然后她踩住刹车，直到月面车停下来。

She pressed a button that said OFF. Then she and Jack hopped out. 她按下标着“关闭”的按钮。 然后她和杰克跳了出来。

They took slow giant steps to the site of the first moon landing. 他们迈着缓慢的大步走向第一次登月的地点。

Beside the flag was a sign. Annie read it aloud: 国旗旁边有一块牌子。 安妮大声读道：

HERE MEN FROM THE PLANET EARTH

FIRST SET FOOT UPON THE MOON,

JULY 1969 A.D.

WE CAME IN PEACE

FOR ALL MANKIND. 公元1969年7月，来自地球的人类第一次登上月球，我们和平而来，为了全人类。

“That’s a good message,” said Jack. “这是个很好的留言，”杰克说。

He handed the moon book to Annie. Then he took out his notebook and pencil to copy the sign. 他把月亮书递给安妮。 然后他拿出笔记本和铅笔，把牌子上的字抄下来。

“Let’s leave our own message,” said Annie. “我们自己也留个言吧，”安妮说。

“What should we say?” said Jack. “我们该写什么？”杰克说。

“The same thing,” said Annie. “But say we are the first kids.” “同样的话，”安妮说。 “但说我们是第一批孩子。”

Jack turned to a new page in his notebook. In big letters he wrote their message. 杰克翻开笔记本新的一页。 他用大写字母写下他们的留言。

“Now we have to sign it,” Annie said. “现在我们得签名，”安妮说。

Jack signed his name. 杰克签上了自己的名字。

Then he passed the notebook and pencil to Annie. She signed her name and passed the notebook back. 然后他把笔记本和铅笔递给安妮。 她签上自己的名字，把笔记本递回来。

Jack tore out the piece of paper. He put it by the flag.



 杰克把那张纸撕了下来。 他把它放在国旗旁边。

图中文字：Today the first kids from the planet Earth came to the Moon. We came in peace for all children.

Jack

ANNIE 图中文字：今天，来自地球的第一批孩子来到了月球。我们是为了所有孩子而来，和平地来到这里。杰克 安妮

No wind would ever blow the message away. No rain would ever fall on it. 永远不会有风把留言吹走。 永远不会有雨落在上面。

It would be there forever, unless someone moved it. 它会永远留在那里，除非有人把它拿走。

Thinking of “forever” made Jack feel dizzy. He shook his head to clear his thoughts. Then he remembered the time. Had two hours passed yet? 想到“永远”，杰克觉得头晕。 他摇摇头，想理清思绪。 然后他想起了时间。 两个小时到了没有？

“I wish I had a watch,” he said, standing up. “We might be running out of time.” “真希望我有手表，”他站起来说。 “我们可能快没时间了。”

“Oh, wow. A moon man!” said Annie. 一个月球人！”安妮说。

“What?” Jack turned to look at her. “什么？”杰克转过身看她。

She was staring through the telescope. 她正透过望远镜看。

Jack walked over to the telescope. Annie stepped aside so he could look, too. 杰克走到望远镜旁边。 安妮挪到一旁，好让他也能看。

Jack gasped. In the distance, something was flying above the ground. 杰克倒抽一口气。 远处，有什么东西正贴着地面飞。

It looked like a giant man in a spacesuit. 那看起来像一个穿着宇航服的巨人。
