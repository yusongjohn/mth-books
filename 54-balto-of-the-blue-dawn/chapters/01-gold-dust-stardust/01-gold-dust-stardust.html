Jack was reading on the front porch when Annie peeked out the door. “Bike ride later?” she asked. 杰克正在门廊上读书，安妮从门里探出头来。“一会儿去骑自行车吗？”她问。

“Sure,” said Jack. “Let’s go now, before it gets too dark. Tell Mom and Dad.” “当然可以，”杰克说，“我们现在就去吧，天快黑了。告诉爸妈一下。”

Annie slipped into the house to tell their parents. Jack closed his book about whales and sharks and stood up and stretched. He was tired from swimming at the lake all afternoon, but he felt good. He loved summer. A bike ride in the warm evening air seemed like the perfect way to end the day. 安妮溜进屋里去告诉爸妈。杰克合上他关于鲸鱼和鲨鱼的书，站起来伸了个懒腰。他整个下午都在湖里游泳，有点累，但感觉很好。他喜欢夏天。在温暖的傍晚空气里骑自行车，似乎是一个结束这一天的完美方式。

“Good to go,” said Annie, coming out of the house with their bike helmets. “准备好了，”安妮拿着他们的自行车头盔从屋里走出来。

Jack and Annie ran down the porch steps and climbed on their bikes. They put on their helmets and pedaled out to the street. As they rode up their block, they waved to a neighbor mowing her lawn and to a couple walking two poodles. 杰克和安妮跑下门廊的台阶，爬上他们的自行车。他们戴上头盔，骑上街道。当他们骑到街区时，他们向正在修剪草坪的邻居和正在遛两只贵宾犬的一对夫妇挥手。

“We need a dog!” Annie called back to Jack. “我们需要一只狗！”安妮回头对杰克喊道。

“What kind do you want?” he said. “你想要什么品种的？”他问。

“Any kind. I like all dogs,” said Annie. “任何种类都可以，我喜欢所有狗狗，”安妮说。

“Big or small?” said Jack. “大的还是小的？”杰克问。

“Well, both. I want a little dog and a big dog,” she said. “And maybe an in-between dog, too.” “嗯，两种都要。我想养一只小狗和一只大狗，”她说，“也许还有一只中等大小的狗狗。”

Jack laughed. “Yeah, I’d like a dog,” he said. “But probably just one.” 杰克笑了。“是啊，我也想要一只狗狗，”他说，“不过可能只想要一只。”

“Whoa!” said Annie, putting on her brakes. “Did you see that?” “哇！”安妮说着，踩下了刹车。“你看到那是什么吗？”

“What?” said Jack, stopping beside her. “什么？”杰克停在她旁边问。

“Above the woods—a bright flash of light,” she said. “在树林上方——一道明亮的闪光，”她说。

“You’re kidding,” he said. “你是在开玩笑吧，”他说。

“Not kidding! I had a feeling, even before we left the house!” said Annie. Without another word, she bumped her bicycle over the sidewalk curb and headed into the Frog Creek woods. “我没开玩笑！甚至在我们离开家之前我就有种感觉！”安妮说。她没再说什么，就骑着自行车冲过了人行道的边缘，朝着蛙溪镇的树林冲了进去。

Jack followed, his bike bouncing over roots and twigs in the shadows beneath the trees. Growing darker by the second, the woods were filled with the sounds of crickets. 杰克跟了上去，他的自行车在树影下跳过树根和树枝。树林越来越暗，到处都是蟋蟀的声音。

“Am I brilliant or what?” cried Annie. She had stopped next to the tallest oak. “我是不是太棒了？”安妮喊道。她停在最高的橡树旁边。

“Brilliant,” said Jack, laughing. “很棒，”杰克笑着说。

The magic tree house was perched high in the oak. Their friend Teddy was grinning at them from the window. “It’s about time!” he called. “I was getting ready to summon a crow or a rabbit to go get you.” 神奇的树屋高高地建在橡树上。他们的朋友泰迪从窗户里朝他们咧嘴笑。他喊道：“你们总算来了！我正准备召唤一只乌鸦或者一只兔子去接你们呢。”

Jack and Annie leaned their bikes against the tree and hurried up the rope ladder. 杰克和安妮把自行车靠在树边，然后赶紧爬上了绳梯。

Inside the tree house, Teddy greeted them with hugs. “We must hurry,” he said. “You are needed right away.” 在树屋里，泰迪热情地拥抱了他们。“我们必须赶快，”他说，“你们马上就要用到。”

“Where? When? Why?” said Annie. “哪里？什么时候？为什么？”安妮问道。

“Where? A seaside town in a vast territory below the Arctic,” said Teddy. “When? February 1925. And why? Because Merlin and Morgan believe you can save lives there.” “哪里？一个位于北极以南广阔领土上的海滨小镇，”泰迪说。“什么时候？1925年2月。为什么？因为梅林和摩根相信你们可以在那里救人的。”

“Whoa,” said Jack. “哇，”杰克说。

“Sounds serious,” said Annie. “听起来很严重，”安妮说。

“And cold,” said Jack. “而且很冷，”杰克说。

“It is quite serious, and it will be quite cold,” said Teddy. “First, you’ll need this.” He handed Annie a small travel guide: “情况很严重，而且会非常冷，”泰迪说。“首先，你需要这个。”他递给安妮一本小旅行指南：

“Alaska? Oh, boy, I’ve always wanted to visit Alaska,” she said. “阿拉斯加？哦，天哪，我一直都想参观阿拉斯加，”她说。

“This guide was published in 1925,” said Teddy. “这本指南是1925年出版的，”泰迪说。

“So it will be a different Alaska than Alaska today,” said Jack. “所以这会是一个和现在不一样的阿拉斯加，”杰克说。

“Precisely,” said Teddy. “And you’ll need this.” He reached into his cloak and took out a small gold box. He handed it to Jack. “没错，”泰迪说。“你还需要这个。”他伸手到他的斗篷里，拿出一个小金盒子。他把它递给杰克。

“What’s this for?” said Jack. He started to take off the lid. “这是用来做什么的？”杰克问。他开始打开盒盖。

“No! Don’t open it now,” said Teddy. “There is a thimbleful of enchanted gold dust inside. It is from Morgan. You can use it only once.” “别打开！现在还不能打开，”泰迪说。“里面有一小撮魔法金粉，是摩根给的。你只能用一次。”

“Use it for what?” asked Jack. “用来做什么？”杰克问。

“In your time of need, you can use it to acquire a great skill that you both will share,” said Teddy. “在你需要的时候，你可以用它来获得一项伟大的技能，你们两个人都会拥有，”泰迪说。

“Cool!” said Annie. “Like the time we used the magic mist of Avalon to give us the skill of great rock climbers.” “太酷了！”安妮说。“就像我们用阿瓦隆的魔法雾气，让自己获得成为优秀攀岩者技能的那次一样。”

“And the time we wished to be great magicians,” said Jack. “And great horse trainers.” “还记得我们想成为伟大魔法师的那次吗，”杰克说。“还有成为伟大马术教练的那次。”

“And great soccer players,” said Annie. “还有成为伟大足球运动员的那次，”安妮说。

“Yes, a bit like all those times,” said Teddy. “If you remember, on those missions, the magic lasted only for an hour. This time, the magic will last for twelve hours.” “是啊，有点像那些时候，”泰迪说。“如果你还记得，在那些任务中，魔法只持续了一个小时。这次，魔法会持续十二个小时。”

“Wow, that’s a lot,” said Annie. “哇，好多啊，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack, smiling. “That’s a really long time to be great at something.” “是啊，”杰克微笑着说。“做某件事伟大的时间真的好长。”

“But there is another difference,” said Teddy. “Your skills on those past missions helped you solve your own problems. On this adventure, your great skill can only be used to save the lives of others.” “但还有一个不同之处，”泰迪说。“过去任务中你们的技能帮助你们解决了自己的问题。这次冒险中，你们伟大的技能只能用来拯救别人的生命。”

“Hmm. So what kind of danger will others in Alaska be facing?” asked Jack. “那其他人会面临什么样的危险呢，”杰克问。“

“Merlin and Morgan didn’t tell me that part,” said Teddy. “I imagine they were quite sure you’d be able to figure it out when you arrived in the Alaskan Territory.” “梅林和摩根没有告诉我这部分，”泰迪说。“我想他们一定确信你们到了阿拉斯加领地后能自己想明白。”

“We’ll try,” said Jack. “我们会尽力的，”杰克说。

“One more thing,” said Teddy. “Merlin wants those you help on this mission to be celebrated as heroes. He does not wish his magic to be part of their story.” “还有一件事，”泰迪说。“梅林希望你们这次任务中帮助的人被当作英雄来庆祝。他不希望自己的魔法成为他们故事的一部分。”

“Okay, but how do we help without anyone knowing that we’re helping?” said Annie. “好吧，但我们要怎么帮忙，又不让别人知道我们在帮忙呢？”安妮说。

Teddy reached into his cloak and brought out a second tiny box. This one was made of shimmering dark blue stone. He handed it to Annie. 泰迪伸手到他的斗篷里，拿出一个小小的盒子。这个盒子是用闪着微光的深蓝色石头做的。他把它交给了安妮。

She cradled the box in her hands. “It’s so beautiful,” she breathed. 她小心翼翼地捧着这个盒子。“太美了，”她轻声说。

“Do not open it until you need what’s inside,” said Teddy. “It holds a bit of stardust, very rare and very precious.” “在你需要里面的东西之前，不要打开它，”泰迪说。“这里面装着一点星尘，非常稀有，也非常珍贵。”

“Ohhh. What’s it for?” whispered Annie. “哦，这是用来做什么的？”安妮小声问。

“If you toss the stardust into the air and make a wish to be forgotten,” said Teddy, “any memory or evidence of your visit will instantly be erased.” “如果你把星尘撒向空中，并许愿希望自己被遗忘，”泰迪说，“那么你这次访问的任何记忆或证据都会立刻消失。”

“Oh,” said Jack. “Okay.” “哦，”杰克说。“好的。”

“Put the magic away now,” said Teddy. “You must be on your way.” “现在把魔法东西收起来，”泰迪说。“你们必须赶快离开了。”

Jack hadn’t brought his backpack, so he put the gold box in the pocket of his jeans. Annie did the same with the dark blue box. 杰克没有带他的背包，所以他把金盒子放进牛仔裤的口袋里。安妮也把深蓝色的盒子放进自己的口袋里。

“Ready,” they said together. “准备好了，”他们一起说。

“Be safe. Be well,” said Teddy. “And do not freeze to death.” “小心点，保重身体，”泰迪说。“千万别被冻死。”

“Gee, thanks,” Jack said. “Good advice.” “哎呀，谢谢，”杰克说。“好建议。”

Annie pointed at the cover of the travel guide. “I wish we could go to the Alaskan Territory!” she said. 安妮指着旅行指南的封面。“我希望我们能去阿拉斯加领地！”她说。

The wind started to blow. 风开始吹起来了。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转起来。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后，一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。
