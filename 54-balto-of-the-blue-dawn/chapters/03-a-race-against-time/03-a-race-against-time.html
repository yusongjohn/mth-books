The Alaskan boy looked at Jack and Annie. “They can’t delay!” he whispered frantically. 阿拉斯加男孩看着杰克和安妮。‘他们不能拖延！’他焦急地小声说。

“It sounds like medicine is on the way,” Jack said, trying to calm him. “It will arrive once the storm passes.” ‘听起来药品很快就到，’杰克说，试图安抚他。‘风暴一过去，它就会到达。’

“But it needs to get here as soon as possible!” said Oki. ‘但它必须尽快到达！’奥基说。

“The doctor said—” started Annie. ‘医生说——’安妮开始说。

“The doctor can do nothing until the medicine arrives,” Oki said. “But there is something I can do.” He headed down the hall and hurried out the front door of the hospital into the dark. As he left, a gust of frigid air blew into the waiting room. ‘医生在药品到达之前什么都做不了，’奥基说。‘但有一件事我可以做。’他朝走廊走去，然后匆忙从医院的正门跑出去，走进了黑暗中。当他离开时，一阵刺骨的寒风吹进了等候室。

“That’s it!” Annie said to Jack. ‘就是它！’安妮对杰克说。

“What?” he said. ‘什么？’他说。

“Our mission! Help save the lives of these sick people!” Annie said. “That’s why Merlin sent us here!” ‘我们的任务！帮助拯救这些生病的人！’安妮说。‘这就是为什么梅林派我们来这里！’

“I think you’re right,” said Jack. “So let’s talk to the doctor and nurse and find out what we can do.” ‘我想你是对的，’杰克说。‘那我们去和医生和护士谈谈，看看我们能做些什么。’

“No, we should go,” said Annie. “不，我们应该走，”安妮说。

“Go where?” asked Jack. “去哪儿？”杰克问。

“Go save Oki’s life first!” Annie said. “先去救奥基的命！”安妮说。

“But he’s not one of the people in danger,” said Jack. “但他不是那些有危险的人之一，”杰克说。

“He will be! He’s going to do something crazy. I can feel it!” said Annie. “Come on!” She took off down the hallway and out the front door. Jack followed her into the icy cold. The moon was full and bright overhead. “他会的！他会做些疯狂的事。我能感觉到！”安妮说。“来吧！”她冲下走廊，跑出前门。杰克跟着她走进冰冷的空气里。天上的月亮又圆又亮。

“There!” said Annie. Before Jack could stop her, she took off after the Alaskan boy. “Oki, wait!” she shouted. “在那里！”安妮说。杰克还没来得及拦住她，她就朝那个阿拉斯加男孩追了过去。“奥基，等一下！”她喊道。

Jack hurried after Annie down the moonlit boardwalk. When they caught up with Oki, they were panting, their breath billowing into the air. 杰克赶紧跟在安妮后面，沿着月光照亮的木板路跑。当他们追上奥基时，他们气喘吁吁，呼出的气在空中飘散。

“Stop!” said Annie. “We want to help you!” “停下！”安妮说。“我们想帮你！”

“How can you help me?” said the boy. He kept walking. “你们怎么帮我？”男孩说。他继续往前走。

Annie and Jack fell into step beside him. “We came to Nome to help people fight the epidemic,” said Annie. 安妮和杰克走到他身边。“我们来到诺姆是为了帮助人们抗击疫情，”安妮说。

“Only the medicine can help,” said Oki. “They might all die soon if they do not get the medicine.” “只有药物才能救他们，”奥基说。“如果他们得不到药物，很快都会死掉。”

“Why can’t they bring the medicine here by boat?” asked Jack. “为什么不能用船把药物运到这里？”杰克问。

“The sea is frozen!” said Oki. “Just look!” “海面结冰了！”奥基说。“你看！”

“Well, what about a plane?” said Annie. “那飞机呢？”安妮说。

“No plane can fly at forty or fifty degrees below zero!” said Oki. “The pilot would freeze.” “零下四十到五十度，没有飞机能飞！”奥基说。“飞行员会被冻死的。”

“Oh, yeah, you’re right,” said Jack. Planes in 1925 must still have open cockpits, he thought. “哦，对啊，你是对的，”杰克说。他心想，1925年的飞机可能还是敞篷驾驶舱的。

“A car?” asked Annie. “那汽车呢？”安妮问。

“No roads in winter,” said Oki. “The only way to Nome is by dogsled, over the mail trail.” “冬天没有路，”奥基说。“去诺姆唯一的办法是狗拉雪橇，沿着邮路走。”

“So what do you plan to do?” asked Jack. “那你打算怎么做？”杰克问。

“I will take a team of dogs and find Gunnar Kaasen,” said Oki. “I will travel through the storm and bring the medicine back myself.” “我会带一队狗，去找冈纳·卡森，”奥基说。“我会穿越暴风雪，亲自把药物带回来。”

“But what about that blizzard?” asked Jack. “The one the mayor was talking about—the huge blizzard sweeping down from the Arctic?” “但是那场暴风雪怎么办？”杰克问。“就是市长说的那场从北极席卷而来的巨大暴风雪？”

“Any good musher with a good team of dogs can handle a blizzard,” said Oki. “任何有经验的狗拉雪橇手，只要有一支好的狗队，都能应对暴风雪，”奥基说。

“Where’s your team?” asked Annie. “你的狗队在哪里？”安妮问。

“My uncle Joe is a mail carrier,” said Oki. “He has a team that runs to Port Safety and Solomon and beyond all the time.” “我叔叔乔是个邮递员，”奥基说。“他有一支狗队，经常跑往安全港和索洛门，甚至更远的地方。”

“So your uncle Joe will go with you?” asked Annie. “那你的叔叔乔会和你一起去吗？”安妮问。

“No. He has a broken leg from a sled accident,” said Oki. “But I am not afraid to go alone.” “不。他因为一次雪橇事故摔断了腿，”奥基说。“但我一个人去也不害怕。”

Jack and Annie hurried alongside Oki, until they came to a shack on a spit of land near the frozen sea. 杰克和安妮赶紧跟在奥基身边，直到他们来到一片靠近结冰大海的陆地上的小屋前。

In the bright moonlight, Jack could see that the shack was made of old driftwood and flattened tin cans. Smoke was rising out of a stovepipe on the roof. 在明亮的月光下，杰克看到小屋是用旧木头和压扁的易拉罐做的。屋顶上的烟囱正冒出烟来。

“This is my uncle’s house,” said Oki. “And those are his huskies.” “这是我叔叔的房子，”奥基说。“那些是他的哈士奇。”

Next to the small house was a dog pen surrounded by a wire fence. A dogsled was inside the pen. Eight huskies were sleeping in the snow, their dark bodies curled up together to keep warm. 小屋旁边是一个用铁丝网围起来的狗舍。一辆雪橇狗车在狗舍里面。八只哈士奇在雪地里睡觉，它们黑色的身体蜷缩在一起取暖。

“Oh, wow,” breathed Annie. “哦，哇，”安妮惊叹道。

The dogs woke up at the sound of voices. They leapt to their feet and began yipping and whining. 狗听到声音醒了过来。它们跳起来，开始吠叫和呜咽。

“Hi, guys!” Annie said. She hurried to the fence to pet them. “嗨，大家！”安妮说。她赶紧跑到围栏边去摸它们。

“Careful!” said Oki. “They are very strong. They can jump up.…” “小心，”奥基说。“它们很强壮，能跳起来……”

But Annie had an instant connection with the dogs. “You’re so beautiful!” she said, reaching over the fence and rubbing different furry heads. “Hey, what’s your name? What’s your name?” 但安妮立刻和这些狗建立了联系。“你们真漂亮！”她说，伸手越过围栏，抚摸着不同的毛茸茸的脑袋。“嘿，你们叫什么名字？你们叫什么名字？”

As Annie spoke, the dogs flattened their ears, and their lips curved into smiles. 安妮说话时，狗们把耳朵压平，嘴唇弯成了微笑的样子。

“They really like her,” Oki said to Jack. He sounded impressed. “它们真的很喜欢她，”奥基对杰克说。他听起来很佩服。

“Yeah, Annie has a special way with animals,” said Jack. “Especially dogs.” “是啊，安妮和动物们有特别的相处方式，”杰克说。“尤其是狗。”

Oki smiled for the first time. “Do you and Annie want to come inside and get warm?” he said. 奥基第一次笑了。“你和安妮想进来暖和一下吗？”他说。

“Sure, thanks,” said Jack. “当然，谢谢，”杰克说。

Jack and Annie followed the Alaskan boy into the shack. Inside, a lantern lit a drafty room that smelled of fish and kerosene. Scratchy band music came from a wooden radio with big knobs. 杰克和安妮跟着阿拉斯加男孩走进小屋。屋里，一盏灯照亮了这个又冷又潮湿的房间，房间里有鱼和煤油的味道。粗糙的乐队音乐从一个木制的收音机里传来，收音机上有很大的旋钮。

“I must speak to my uncle. He is in bed,” said Oki. He opened a door and slipped into another room. “我必须去和我叔叔说话。他在床上，”奥基说。他打开一扇门，溜进了另一个房间。

Jack and Annie looked around. Strips of dried dark pink fish hung from a rack near the stove, which was made from an old metal barrel. Wooden barrels were set as chairs around a wooden table. 杰克和安妮四处张望。一些晒干的深粉色鱼条挂在靠近炉子的架子上，炉子是用一个旧金属桶做的。一些木桶被当作椅子，围绕着一张木桌摆放着。

Voices came from the other room. Jack recognized Oki’s. “But I can go alone!” 另一个房间传来声音。杰克听出了奥基的声音。“但是我可以自己去！”

“No, you cannot!” said a deep, rasping voice. “不行，你不能去！”一个低沉而沙哑的声音说。

Jack wondered if he and Annie should leave. It seemed wrong to overhear a family argument. “Let’s go outside,” he said to Annie. 杰克想知道他和安妮是不是应该离开。偷听一个家庭争吵似乎不太对。“我们去外面吧，”他对安妮说。

“But we have to help fight the epidemic,” she said. “但我们得帮忙抗击疫情，”她说。

“I know,” said Jack. “Maybe it would be better to go back to the hospital and—” “我知道，”杰克说。“也许我们最好回到医院，然后——”

“Listen!” Annie interrupted. She pointed to the radio. “听！”安妮打断了他。她指着收音机。

The music had stopped. A news bulletin had come on: “A desperate drama is unfolding in Alaska! In the town of Nome!” the broadcaster said. 音乐停了。一条新闻广播开始了：“一场绝望的戏剧正在阿拉斯加上演！在诺姆镇！”广播员说。

“Nome!” said Annie. She turned up the volume on the radio. “救命呀！”安妮喊道。她把收音机的音量调大了。

“All eyes are on this small icebound town in the northern territory, where an outbreak of diphtheria has already claimed five lives. Twenty of the best mushers and more than a hundred and sixty dogs have been heroically running a relay across the frozen tundra. They must travel a total of six hundred miles to get medicine from Nenana, Alaska, to the town of Nome.…” “所有人的目光都集中在北部地区这个被冰雪覆盖的小城镇，那里已经爆发了白喉，已经有五个人失去了生命。二十位最优秀的雪橇驾驶员和超过一百六十只狗英勇地在结冰的苔原上接力奔跑。他们必须总共行驶六百英里，从阿拉斯加的纳纳纳把药品送到诺姆镇。……”

“That’s us!” said Oki, hurrying back into the room. “那是我们！”奥基赶紧跑回房间说道。

“But now a blizzard threatens to curtail the efforts to save the residents of Nome,” the announcer said. “The storm is expected to sweep down from the Arctic, with forty-mile-per-hour winds and temperatures as low as thirty degrees below zero.” “但现在一场暴风雪威胁着要阻碍拯救诺姆镇居民的努力，”播音员说道。“这场风暴预计会从北极席卷而来，风速高达每小时四十英里，气温低至零下三十度。”

“Oh, man,” said Jack. “哦，天哪，”杰克说道。

“It is unclear whether the mushers and their teams of heroic dogs will be able to continue,” the announcer said. “The medicine is now in the hands of champion musher Gunnar Kaasen. Will he and his lead dog, Balto, be forced to stop their lifesaving journey?” “目前还不清楚这些雪橇驾驶员和他们英勇的狗队是否能够继续前进，”播音员说道。“药品现在掌握在冠军雪橇驾驶员冈纳·卡森手中。他和他的领头狗巴尔托会被迫停止这次拯救生命的旅程吗？”

“Balto!” said Annie. “Didn’t we see a statue of him in Central Park in New York?” “巴尔托！”安妮说道。“我们不是在纽约中央公园见过他的雕像吗？”

“Yeah, but I can’t remember why he’s so famous,” said Jack. “是啊，但我记不起他为什么这么有名，”杰克说道。

“Me neither,” said Annie. “我也不记得了，”安妮说道。

“Will the blizzard keep the medicine from getting to the sick and dying in Nome?” the announcer said. “It’s a race against time! A race against death!” Then the man’s voice turned cheerful: “And now we return to the music of King Oliver’s Creole Jazz Band!” “这场暴风雪会让药品无法送到诺姆镇那些生病和垂死的人手中吗？”播音员说道。“这是一场与时间的赛跑！一场与死亡的赛跑！”然后，那个人的声音变得愉快起来：“现在我们回到金·奥利弗克里奥尔爵士乐队的音乐中！”

The lively music came back on, and Oki turned the radio off. “I’m going. I’m taking the dogs,” he said. He grabbed a burlap sack hanging near the door and started filling it with dried fish. “I’ll take this to feed them along the way. I’ll find Kaasen. I know—” 欢快的音乐又响起来了，奥基关掉了收音机。“我要走了，我要带狗们走，”他说。他抓起门边挂着的一个麻袋，开始往里面装干鱼。“我要带这些路上喂他们。我会找到卡森。我知道——

“Do not go. Please.” “不要去。求你了。”

Jack and Annie turned to see a native Alaskan man hobbling into the room on a crutch. He had long jet-black hair and wore a fur pullover with a hood. He had a weathered face and dark eyes. He barely glanced at Jack and Annie as he fixed his gaze on Oki. “If Gunnar Kaasen and Balto cannot travel in this weather, neither can you,” he said. 杰克和安妮转过身，看见一位阿拉斯加本地人一拐一拐地走进房间，他拄着拐杖。他有一头长长的黑发，穿着一件带兜帽的毛皮开衫。他的脸布满皱纹，眼睛深邃。他几乎没看杰克和安妮一眼，而是盯着奥基看了起来。“如果冈纳·卡森和巴尔托在这样的天气里无法出行，你也不行，”他说。

“I can at least try, Uncle Joe,” said Oki. “I have to.” He kept throwing fish into the sack. “我至少可以试试，乔叔叔，”奥基说，“我必须去。”他继续把鱼扔进袋子里。

“Do not go out there alone, Oki,” rasped his uncle. “You are not a champion like Seppala or Kaasen. And none of my dogs are as strong as Togo or Balto.” “奥基，不要一个人出去，”他的叔叔沙哑地说，“你不是像塞帕拉或卡森那样的冠军。而且我的狗也没有托戈或巴尔托那么强壮。”

“It doesn’t matter how great Kaasen is,” said Oki, “if he is sitting at the roadhouse in Solomon.” “Kaasen 再厉害也没用，”奥基说，“如果他坐在索洛门的路边小屋。”

“If he waits, he waits for good reason,” said Oki’s uncle. “Any team can be lost in a blizzard.” “如果他要等，那一定有他的理由，”奥基的叔叔说，“任何队伍都可能在暴风雪中迷路。”

“Let me go, please,” Oki said, facing his uncle. “请让我去，”奥基面对着他的叔叔说。

“No!” shouted the man. “不行！”那个男人喊道。

Jack held his breath as he looked back and forth from Oki to his uncle. Annie started to interrupt, but Jack shook his head at her. This isn’t our business, he thought. 杰克屏住呼吸，看着奥基和他叔叔来回看。安妮想插话，但杰克朝她摇了摇头。这不是我们的事，他想。

“I have run the trail many times,” said Oki. “I can do it with my eyes closed.” “我跑这条小路很多次了，”奥基说，“我闭着眼睛都能跑。”

“But I have always been with you,” his uncle said. “You have never gone out alone.” “但我一直和你在一起，”他叔叔说，“你从来没一个人出去过。”

“I have to try!” said Oki. “我必须试试！”奥基说。

“Who will help you in a whiteout?” his uncle asked. “Who will help you if the ice breaks and moves out to sea? You will be gone forever.” “谁会在暴风雪中帮你？”他叔叔问，“如果冰面裂开，冰块漂到海里去，谁会帮你？你就会永远消失。”

“Maybe. But what about my mother?” Oki cried. “And my sister? They can’t breathe! They can’t swallow! They are on fire with fever! I promised them the medicine would arrive tonight. If it doesn’t come soon, they will be gone forever. I have to hurry! You heard the radio! It’s a race against death!” “也许吧。但是我的妈妈呢？”奥基喊道，“还有我的妹妹？她们喘不过气来！吞不下东西！她们被高烧烧得浑身发烫！我答应过她们今晚药会送到。如果药不能尽快送到，她们也会永远消失。我必须赶快！你听收音机了吗！这是和死神赛跑！”

Oki slung the sack of dried fish over his shoulder, picked up the kerosene lantern from the table, and hurried outside. 奥基把装着干鱼的袋子甩到肩上，从桌上拿起煤油灯，然后赶紧跑出去了。
