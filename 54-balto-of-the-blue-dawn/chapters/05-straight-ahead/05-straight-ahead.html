Moonlight cast a silvery glow over the icebound sea. The huskies’ heavy breathing made a cloud over the team as it glided along the coast. The only other sounds in the darkness were the jingling bells of the lead dogs, the creaking of the sled basket, and the swish of the smooth wooden runners over the snow. 月光照在结冰的海面上，泛着银色的光芒。哈士奇们喘着粗气，呼出的白气在队伍上方形成了一片云。队伍沿着海岸滑行时，唯一的声音就是领头狗的铃铛声、雪橇篮子的吱呀声，还有雪橇光滑的木制滑板在雪地上滑行的沙沙声。

As the dogs did their job, Jack magically knew how to do his: He knew how to “pedal” the sled by pushing the ground with his right foot while keeping his left on the runner. He knew how to push in the perfect rhythm to keep the team from running too fast, so they wouldn’t pant and hurt their lungs in the freezing air. He knew how to balance his weight and bend his legs to absorb the shock of bumps on the trail. He knew how to keep all the lines tight and straight, so none of the dogs would get tangled in them. 当狗们在工作时，杰克也神奇地知道该怎么做：他知道如何用右脚踩地推动雪橇，同时左脚放在滑板上，这就是“踩踏”雪橇的方法。他知道如何以完美的节奏推动，让队伍不要跑得太快，这样它们就不会喘不过气来，伤到肺。他知道如何调整自己的体重，弯曲双腿来吸收路上颠簸的冲击。他还知道如何把所有的绳子拉紧拉直，这样狗就不会被缠住。

Jack knew all the musher commands, too. When the team came to a snowdrift, he shouted, “Gee!” to make the wheel dogs curve to the right. 杰克还知道所有狗拉雪橇的人的口令。当队伍来到雪堆时，他喊出“右转！”让轮子边的狗向右转弯。

When the team came to another drift, he shouted, “Haw!” to make the wheel dogs curve to the left. 当队伍来到另一堆雪时，他喊出“左转！”让轮子边的狗向左转弯。

When it was time to straighten out the towline again, Jack shouted, “Straight ahead!” 当需要再次拉直拖绳时，杰克喊出“直行！”

Every time the dogs did what Jack commanded, he said, “Good dogs!” But he hardly said more. He knew the fewer words, the better. The dogs depended on clear commands. 每次狗们按照杰克的命令行动时，他都会说：“好样的！”但他几乎不会说更多的话。他知道，说得越少越好。狗们依赖清晰的口令。

Riding in the sled basket, Annie was silent, too, though several times Jack heard her gasp and say, “Wow!” 坐在雪橇的篮子里，安妮也一声不吭，不过杰克好几次听到她倒抽一口气，说：“哇！”

“Wow!” is right, Jack thought. The windless Alaskan night was beautiful. Maybe the forecast was wrong. Maybe their ride would just be a thrilling race to save lives. “哇！”确实没错，杰克心想。没有风的阿拉斯加夜晚真美。也许天气预报错了。也许他们的旅程只是一场刺激的赛跑，为了拯救生命。

Mile after mile, the huskies ran along the well-worn trail. When the dogs raced down an incline, Jack dragged his heel to keep them from going too fast. When they labored up an incline, he lightened their load by hopping off and running beside the sled, holding on to one of the handles to keep it steady. When the trail was flat again, he jumped back on the runners. 一英里又一英里，雪橇犬沿着熟悉的小路奔跑。当狗们冲下坡时，杰克用脚后跟拖地，防止他们跑得太快。当狗们费力爬上坡时，他跳下雪橇，跑到雪橇旁边，抓着把手，让雪橇保持平稳。当小路变平的时候，他又跳回雪橇上。

“Good driving!” Annie called. “你驾驶得真棒！”安妮喊道。

Jack smiled. He loved being a champion musher. He loved knowing exactly what to do and when to do it. But he especially loved the easy connection he felt with the huskies. Sometimes he even felt like a running dog himself as he pedaled in rhythm to their pace. 杰克笑了。他喜欢当一名雪橇犬驾驶冠军。他喜欢知道自己该做什么，什么时候该做什么。但他特别喜欢和雪橇犬之间那种轻松的联系。有时候，他甚至觉得自己也变成了一只奔跑的狗，随着它们的节奏一起奔跑。

Lost in his thoughts, Jack failed to notice a change in the weather, until he heard Annie call out, “The storm’s starting!” 沉浸在自己的想法中，杰克没有注意到天气发生了变化，直到他听到安妮喊道：“暴风雨要来了！”

Jack realized it had gotten colder and darker. Clouds covered the moon. The wind was blowing, and snow was falling. Snowflakes began to freeze on the lenses of Jack’s glasses. Soon he couldn’t see well enough to give commands to the dogs. Flying blindly down the trail, he tried to wipe his glasses with a fur mitten, but that only made it worse. 杰克意识到天气变得又冷又暗。乌云遮住了月亮。风开始吹起来，雪花也开始飘落。雪花开始在杰克眼镜的镜片上结冰。很快，他看不清东西，无法再给狗下命令了。他盲目地沿着小路飞驰，试图用毛皮手套擦拭眼镜，但这让情况变得更糟了。

As the huskies headed into a sharp curve, Jack lost his balance. He slipped off the runners and tumbled into the snow. The huskies came to a halt and started barking. 当雪橇犬冲进一个急转弯时，杰克失去了平衡。他从雪橇上滑了下来，跌进了雪里。雪橇犬停了下来，开始大声吠叫。

“What happened?” Annie shouted. “怎么了？”安妮喊道。

“My glasses got messed up,” Jack said, picking himself up. “I couldn’t see anything.” “我的眼镜坏了，”杰克说，一边站起来一边说，“我看不见任何东西。”

“Don’t worry, that happens,” said Annie. “别担心，这种情况会发生，”安妮说。

“I guess I was too sure of myself,” said Jack. “我想我太自信了，”杰克说。

“Even champion mushers have problems if they can’t see,” said Annie. “Let’s switch places for a while. I’d love to drive.” “就算是最厉害的雪橇犬驾驶员，如果看不见也会遇到麻烦，”安妮说。“我们暂时换一下位置吧，我想试试开车。”

“Sure, thanks!” said Jack. He climbed into the basket and sat on the boards, while Annie took her position on the runners. “当然可以，谢谢！”杰克说。他爬进篮子里，坐在木板上，而安妮则坐到了雪橇的滑板上。

“Line out!” Annie commanded the dogs. “列队出发！”安妮对狗喊道。

The team straightened out the lines. 狗队整理好了绳索。

“Good dogs!” Annie shouted. “Hike! Straight ahead!” “好样的，狗狗们！”安妮大声喊道。“出发！向前！”

The huskies started running again. As they pulled the sled through the swirling snow, Jack took off a mitten and scraped his glasses with his bare fingers. When his skin started burning with pain from the cold and wind, he quickly put the mitten back on. 哈士奇们又开始奔跑。当它们拉着雪橇穿过飞舞的雪花时，杰克摘下手套，用光秃秃的手指擦了擦眼镜。当他的皮肤因为寒冷和风而开始刺痛时，他赶紧把手套重新戴上。

Annie kept a steady hand and a steady pace as she drove the eight huskies through the snowstorm. The wind blew harder, but she shifted her weight to keep the swaying sled from tipping over. She never let the towline go slack and get tangled with the other lines. Every time she yelled “Gee” or “Haw,” Jack leaned to the right or left to help the sled around the curves. 安妮一边驾驶着八只哈士奇穿越暴风雪，一边保持稳定的手势和节奏。风变得更大了，但她调整了身体重心，让摇晃的雪橇不至于翻倒。她从不让牵引绳松开，以免和其他绳子缠在一起。每当她喊出“右转”或“左转”时，杰克就会向右或向左倾斜身体，帮助雪橇转弯。

When the dogs started up a slope, Jack knew he should lighten their load. “I’ll get off!” he called to Annie. 当狗队开始爬坡时，杰克知道他应该减轻它们的负担。“我要下去！”他朝安妮喊道。

“Easy!” Annie commanded the team. “简单！”安妮对队伍下令。

As the huskies slowed down, Jack jumped out of the basket. “I’ll run awhile!” he yelled. 当雪橇犬慢下来时，杰克从篮子里跳了出来。“我要跑一会儿！”他喊道。

“Okay, but stay close!” said Annie. Then she commanded the dogs to pick up speed again: “Get up!” The team easily dashed up the incline and sped along the frozen shore. “好吧，但要靠近点！”安妮说。然后她命令狗再次加快速度：“快点！”队伍轻松地冲上斜坡，沿着结冰的岸边飞驰。

Jack decided to keep running. It felt good to move in the biting cold. Then suddenly he heard the grinding of the sled runners. The team was running over ice instead of snow! Jack heard a loud cracking sound beneath his boots. His feet had broken through the ice! His fur boots sank into a freezing puddle almost a foot deep. 杰克决定继续跑。在刺骨的寒冷中移动感觉很好。突然，他听到了雪橇滑行的声音。队伍正在冰上而不是雪上奔跑！杰克听到他靴子下传来一声巨响。他的脚破冰了！他的毛皮靴子掉进了一个几乎有一英尺深的冰水里。

The team sailed ahead of Jack, pulling Annie and the sled away from the ice. Left behind in the snowy dark, Jack tried to climb out of the crack, but more ice broke off. His feet were getting soaked! 队伍冲到了杰克前面，拉着安妮和雪橇远离了冰面。被留在雪地的黑暗中，杰克试图从裂缝里爬出来，但更多的冰掉了下来。他的脚越来越湿了！

“Annie!” he shouted. “Annie! Stop!” “安妮！”他喊道。“安妮！停下！”

“Gee!” Annie yelled. Jack could hear her turning the team around. He heard the bells of the lead dogs as they headed toward him. “ Gee！”，安妮喊道。杰克能听到她正在调转队伍的方向。他听到领头狗的铃铛声，它们正朝他这边过来。

“Whoa!” Annie brought the huskies to a stop. “Whoa！”安妮让雪橇犬停了下来。

“Stay back!” Jack shouted. “There’s thin ice here! Keep the dogs back!” If the huskies got their feet wet, their soft pads would quickly stick to the ice and freeze. “退后！”杰克喊道。“这里冰很薄！让狗们退后！”如果雪橇犬的脚湿了，它们柔软的脚垫会很快粘在冰上并冻住。

Jack used all his strength to pull his boots out of the crack. He slipped and slid across the ice and snow as he made his way to the sled. “Come on, let’s go!” he cried, climbing into the basket. “My feet are soaked!” 杰克用尽全力把靴子从裂缝里拉出来。他滑着穿过冰和雪，朝雪橇走去。“快点，我们走吧！”他喊道，爬进篮子里。“我的脚全湿了！”

“Hike!” Annie yelled. “快走！”安妮喊道。

As the dogs took off through the bone-chilling cold, Jack felt his feet burning with pain. Soon they started to grow numb. As an expert musher, he knew that wet feet lost heat twenty-five times faster than dry feet. He had to get out of the cold immediately or he would suffer severe frostbite. 当狗们冲进刺骨的寒风中时，杰克感到脚上火辣辣地疼。很快，他的脚开始变得麻木。作为一名专业的狗拉雪橇手，他知道湿脚比干脚失去热量的速度要快二十五倍。他必须立刻离开寒冷，否则他将遭受严重的冻伤。

Before Jack could think of what to do, the huskies slowed down and yelped with excitement. 杰克还没来得及想该怎么办，哈士奇们放慢了脚步，兴奋地叫了起来。

“Oh, wow!” said Annie as the team came to a stop. “哦，哇！”安妮说道，队伍停了下来。

Jack’s glasses were covered with ice again. “What is it?” he shouted, unable to see. “What’s there?” 杰克的眼镜又被冰覆盖了。“怎么回事？”他喊道，看不清东西。“那里有什么？”

“We’ve come to a house!” yelled Annie. “There’s smoke from a chimney! And there’s a light shining inside!” “我们到了一幢房子！”安妮喊道。“烟囱里冒出了烟！而且屋里还有灯光！”

“Just in time!” said Jack. He climbed painfully out of the basket as Annie set the snow hook in the ground to hold the dogs. “Stay!” she commanded. She helped Jack through the blowing snow to the front door of a log cabin. “太及时了！”杰克说道。他痛苦地从篮子里爬出来，安妮把雪钩插进地面，用来固定住狗。“待着！”她命令道。她帮助杰克穿过呼啸的雪花，来到木屋的前门。
