“Cold…face,” Annie said, her teeth chattering. “冷……脸，”安妮说，她的牙齿在打颤。

“Y-yeah, yeah,” said Jack. His breath billowed white into the sharp air. “嗯……嗯，”杰克说。他的呼吸在寒冷的空气中变成了一团白雾。

“I feel like a fat b-brown bear,” said Annie. “我觉得自己像一只胖乎乎的棕熊，”安妮说。

Jack laughed. He and Annie were covered with fur from head to toe. They wore fur pants, fur mittens, knee-high fur boots, and long fur jackets with fur hoods. 杰克笑了。他和安妮从头到脚都长满了毛。他们穿着毛裤，毛手套，膝盖高的毛靴，还有带毛兜帽的长毛外套。

“Welcome to Alaska,” said Jack. He pulled up his hood and tied it tightly under his chin. Annie did the same. Then they looked out the window together. “欢迎来到阿拉斯加，”杰克说。他拉起兜帽，紧紧系在下巴下面。安妮也做了同样的事。然后他们一起看向窗外。

The tree house had landed in one of a few trees at the edge of a snowy field. Beyond the field was a coastal town. The winter sun was setting over an ice-covered sea. 树屋降落在一片雪地边缘的几棵树中。雪地之外是一座沿海城镇。冬天的太阳正在冰封的海面上落下。

“I wonder why we’re needed to save lives here,” said Jack. “我想知道为什么我们需要在这里拯救生命，”杰克说。

“Do you think something scary is about to happen?” said Annie. “你觉得有什么可怕的事情要发生了吗？”安妮说。

“Scary?” said Jack. “可怕？”杰克说。

“Like an earthquake or a volcano?” said Annie. “比如地震或者火山爆发？”安妮说。

“I don’t know,” Jack said. He picked up their guide to the Alaskan Territory. He pulled off one of his fur mittens, opened the book, and read: “我不知道，”杰克说。他拿起他们的阿拉斯加领地指南。他摘下一只毛手套，打开书，读了起来：

About 12,000 years ago, people and their dogs crossed a land bridge over the Bering Sea from Russia into Alaska. When they reached Alaska, they became the first humans and dogs to live in North America. 大约12000年前，人们和他们的狗通过白令海上的陆桥从俄罗斯进入阿拉斯加。当他们到达阿拉斯加时，他们成为了第一个在北美生活的人类和狗。

“Cool,” said Annie. “I like the dog part.” “酷，”安妮说。“我喜欢狗狗的部分。”

Jack kept reading: 杰克继续读着：

Until Russian explorers came to Alaska a few hundred years ago, only native Alaskan people lived there. For thousands of years, the native people survived by making use of the few resources of the rugged, icebound land. 直到几百年前俄罗斯探险者来到阿拉斯加，只有当地的阿拉斯加原住民住在那里。几千年来，原住民依靠利用这片崎岖、冰封土地上的有限资源生存下来。

“Wow, they must be tough people,” said Annie. “哇，他们一定是很坚强的人，”安妮说。

“No kidding,” said Jack. He read more: “可不是嘛，”杰克说。他继续读下去：

In the late 1800s, the United States purchased Alaska from Russia, and it became a territory of the U. S. 在19世纪晚期，美国从俄罗斯购买了阿拉斯加，它成为了美国的一个属地。

“That’s why this guide is called the Territory of Alaska,” said Jack. “It’s from 1925—that’s before Alaska became a state.” “这就是为什么这本指南叫做《阿拉斯加属地》，”杰克说。“它是1925年出版的——那时候阿拉斯加还不是一个州。”

“Got it,” said Annie. “Okay. Let’s go find some lives to save.” “明白了，”安妮说。“好吧，我们去救一些人吧。”

“Wait,” said Jack. “Where’s our magic?” “等等，”杰克说。“我们的魔法在哪里？”

“Check your pockets,” said Annie. Annie pulled off her mittens, and they both reached into the big side pockets of their parkas. “检查一下口袋，”安妮说。安妮摘下她的手套，他们俩都伸手去摸他们羽绒服的大侧口袋。

“Got it!” said Jack as he pulled out the tiny gold box. “明白了！”杰克一边说，一边拿出那个小小的金盒子。

“Me too!” said Annie. She held up the tiny stone box. “我也是！”安妮说。她举起那个小小的石头盒子。

“Gold dust, stardust,” said Annie. “One to save lives, one to make everyone forget that we saved lives.” “金粉，星尘，”安妮说。“一个用来拯救生命，一个让所有人都忘记我们拯救了生命。”

“Yep,” said Jack. “Let’s not lose them.” They tucked the boxes back into their pockets and put their mittens on. “没错，”杰克说。“我们可不能把它们弄丢了。”他们把盒子重新放回口袋里，然后戴上手套。

As Annie started down the tree house ladder, Jack crammed the Alaska guide into another one of his pockets. Then he clumsily followed Annie down the rope ladder in his bulky clothes. 当安妮开始往下爬树屋的梯子时，杰克把阿拉斯加指南书塞进另一个口袋里。然后他笨手笨脚地穿着厚厚的衣物，跟着安妮沿着绳梯往下爬。

As they started across the snow-covered field, their boots squeaked in the snow. Jack’s throat hurt from breathing the dry, cold air. 当他们开始穿过那片覆盖着雪的田野时，他们的靴子在雪地里发出咯吱咯吱的声音。杰克因为呼吸干燥寒冷的空气，喉咙感到很痛。

In the fading winter light, they crossed a bridge and walked along a frozen creek until they came to a boardwalk that ran along the icebound seashore. A sign said FRONT STREET. The town was empty. There were no old cars or wagons, only piles of ice and snow. 在逐渐消失的冬日余晖中，他们走过一座桥，沿着结冰的小溪走，直到来到一条沿着冰封海岸延伸的木板路。一块牌子上写着FRONT STREET。这个小镇空无一人。没有老旧的汽车或马车，只有堆积的冰和雪。

图中文字：NOME LAUNDRY 图中文字：NOME LAUNDRY

As Jack and Annie tramped through the snow, they passed stores and saloons and restaurants with crooked signs and broken windows. They all looked abandoned. There were only a few shops that looked as if they were closed but still might be in business: Nome Pharmacy, Nome Laundry, and Nome Bakery. 当杰克和安妮踏着雪前进时，他们经过了商店、酒吧和餐馆，这些地方的招牌歪歪扭扭，窗户也破碎了。看起来全都荒废了。只有少数几家店铺看起来像是关着门，但也许还在营业：Nome Pharmacy（诺姆药店）、Nome Laundry（诺姆洗衣店）和Nome Bakery（诺姆面包店）。

“I guess we’re in Nome,” said Annie. “我想我们到了诺姆，”安妮说。

“Good guess,” said Jack. He took off one of his mittens, pulled out their Alaska guide, and found Nome. He read: “猜得对，”杰克说。他摘下手套，拿出他们的阿拉斯加指南书，找到了诺姆。他读道：

Around 1900, gold was discovered in Nome, a remote Alaskan town on the Bering Sea. More than 30,000 gold seekers rushed to the boom town. But within ten years, Nome’s gold rush was over, and the town fell on hard times. 1900年左右，有人在白令海边一个叫诺姆的偏远阿拉斯加小镇发现了金子。超过3万多人涌向这个繁荣的小镇寻找黄金。但不到十年，诺姆的淘金热就结束了，小镇陷入了困难时期。

“Hard times? No kidding,” said Annie. “Nome looks more like a ghost town than a boom town.” “困难时期？可不是开玩笑，”安妮说。“诺姆看起来更像是个鬼城，而不是繁荣的小镇。”

“Let’s keep going,” said Jack. He pulled his mitten back on and put their guide in his pocket. “我们继续走吧，”杰克说。他把手套重新戴上，把他们的向导放进口袋里。

As he and Annie kept walking down the snow-covered boardwalk, they passed the Dream Movie Theater and the Golden Gate Hotel. Both had signs that said: 当杰克和安妮继续沿着铺满雪的木板路走时，他们经过了梦电影院和黄金门酒店。这两个地方的牌子上都写着：

TEMPORARILY CLOSED. 暂时关闭。

Finally, they came to a white two-story wooden building that looked like someone’s house. They could see lights and people inside. The black sign above the front door said: 最后，他们来到一座白色的两层木房子前，看起来像某个人的家。他们能看到里面有人和灯光。门上方的黑色牌子上写着：

MAYNARD-COLUMBUS HOSPITAL 梅纳德-哥伦布医院

“At least there are a few people in town,” said Annie. “至少镇上还有几个人，”安妮说。

“But why is the hospital the only place that seems open?” said Jack. “但为什么只有医院看起来是开着的呢？”杰克问。

“Yeah, weird,” said Annie. They walked down the boardwalk until they came to a schoolhouse with a sign that said: “是啊，真奇怪，”安妮说。他们沿着木板路一直走，直到来到一所挂着牌子的学校：

NO SCHOOL. QUARANTINE. 没有学校，隔离了。

“Uh-oh,” said Jack. “糟了，”杰克说。

“Quarantine?” asked Annie. “隔离？”安妮问。

“It means you’re supposed to stay home so you won’t spread a bad disease that’s going around,” said Jack. “这意味着你必须待在家里，以免传播正在传播的坏疾病，”杰克说。

“Maybe that’s why everything is closed,” said Annie. “I wonder what the disease is.” “也许这就是为什么所有地方都关闭了，”安妮说。“我不知道这种疾病是什么。”

“I don’t know,” said Jack. “But if we go back to the hospital, we can ask someone there.” “我不知道，”杰克说。“但是如果我们回到医院，我们可以问那里的人。”

In the twilight, Jack and Annie hurried up the boardwalk to the entrance of the small hospital. When they stepped through the front door, they found a cold waiting room filled with people bundled in fur parkas and boots. Some seemed quite ill, slumped down in their chairs with their eyes closed. Others looked disappointed when they saw Jack and Annie, as if they were waiting for someone else. 在暮色中，杰克和安妮匆匆沿着木板路走到小医院的入口。当他们走进正门时，他们发现一个寒冷的等候室，里面挤满了穿着毛皮大衣和靴子的人。有些人看起来病得很重，坐在椅子上，眼睛紧闭。其他人看到杰克和安妮时显得很失望，好像他们在等别人。

Only one person stepped forward to greet them. A boy about fourteen or fifteen years old stared at them with dark, questioning eyes. “Who are you?” he asked. 只有一人走上前来迎接他们。一个大约十四或十五岁的男孩用深沉、充满疑问的眼睛盯着他们。“你们是谁？”他问。

“Our names are Jack and Annie,” said Annie. “我们的名字是杰克和安妮，”安妮说。

“Are you sick with diphtheria?” the boy said. “你们得了白喉吗？”男孩问。

Annie shook her head. “No, we just arrived from the United States. Excuse me a second.” She turned away from the boy and whispered to Jack, “What’s diphtheria, exactly?” 安妮摇摇头。“不，我们刚从美国来。请等一下。”她转身离开男孩，小声对杰克说：“白喉到底是什么？”

“It’s a dangerous disease from the past. That must be what the quarantine is for,” Jack whispered. “We can’t catch it. We had vaccines.” “这是一种过去很危险的疾病。那一定就是为什么他们要隔离的原因，”杰克小声说。“我们不会被传染的。我们打过疫苗了。”

Annie turned back to the boy. “Do you have diphtheria?” she asked gently. 安妮转过身面对男孩。“你有白喉吗？”她温柔地问。

“No. My sister and my mother do,” he said. “They are very sick in beds upstairs. I’m waiting for the special medicine to arrive. Dr. Welch says it will save their lives.” “没有。我姐姐和妈妈有，”男孩说。“她们现在躺在床上非常虚弱。我正在等一种特别的药送到。韦尔奇医生说这药能救她们的命。”

Before the boy could say more, Jack heard a man shouting in a room down the hall. “Hello! Hello! Can you hear me?” 还没等男孩说完，杰克就听到走廊尽头的房间里有人在大喊：“喂！喂！你能听到我说话吗？”

The boy left the waiting room and hurried to an open doorway. Jack and Annie followed, and the three of them peeked into a doctor’s office. 男孩离开了等候室，赶紧跑向一扇敞开的门。杰克和安妮跟了上去，三个人偷偷 peek 进了一间医生的办公室。

Two people were anxiously watching a man talk on an old-fashioned black telephone. “Yes! This is Mayor Maynard in Nome!” the man shouted into the receiver. “Has the musher arrived with the medicine package yet?” 两个人焦急地看着一个男人在用一部老式黑色电话讲话。“是的！我是诺姆市的市长梅纳德！”男人对着听筒大喊。“那位狗拉雪橇的司机带着药包到那里了吗？”

As Mayor Maynard listened to the person on the other end of the line, Annie whispered to Jack, “A musher is a person who drives a sled pulled by a team of dogs.” 当市长梅纳德听着电话那头的人说话时，安妮小声对杰克说：“狗拉雪橇的司机叫 musher。”

“I know,” said Jack. “我知道，”杰克说。

“That’s a cool job,” Annie whispered. “那是个很酷的工作，”安妮小声说。

“No, listen to me!” Mayor Maynard yelled into the phone. “If Gunnar Kaasen arrives at the roadhouse in Solomon, keep him there! Do not let him and his team move on! We can’t risk losing the medicine! We’re expecting a huge blizzard tonight, sweeping down from the Arctic!” “不，听我说！”梅纳德市长对着电话大喊，“如果冈纳·卡森到达索洛门的路边小屋，就让他留在那里！不要让他和他的团队继续前进！我们不能冒险失去药品！今晚有一场巨大的暴风雪，从北极席卷而来！”

A huge blizzard sweeping down from the Arctic? That’s not good news, thought Jack. 一场从北极席卷而来的巨大暴风雪？杰克心想，这可不是什么好消息。

“We cannot lose that medicine!” said the mayor. “Five have died already. There are at least twenty more cases. Possibly fifty. The Board of Health believes it is better to delay it than to risk losing it altogether! Tell Kaasen to wait until further word!” “我们不能失去那药品！”市长说，“已经有五个人死了，至少还有二十个病例，可能多达五十个。卫生委员会认为，与其冒险失去药品，不如延迟运送！告诉卡森等人，等进一步消息再说！”

“No! You can’t delay it!” the boy said, barging into the office. “Gunnar Kaasen must keep going! The medicine has to get here soon!” “不！你们不能延迟！”男孩冲进办公室说，“冈纳·卡森必须继续前进！药品必须尽快送到这里！”

“I don’t have a choice, Oki,” said the mayor, hanging up the phone. “It’s what the Board of Health has ordered.” “我别无选择，奥基，”市长说着，挂上了电话，“这是卫生委员会下达的命令。”

The boy turned to a large rosy-cheeked woman. “Nurse Morgan, please don’t let them delay!” he said. He sounded near tears. 男孩转向一位面颊红润的高大女士，“摩根护士，请你别让他们延迟！”他说，声音里带着快要哭出来的语气。

“It’s going to be all right, Oki,” Nurse Morgan said, patting him on his shoulder. “Please go back to the waiting room.” “会没事的，奥基，”摩根护士说，一边拍了拍他的肩膀，“请回等候室去吧。”

“Don’t worry,” said a gray-haired man in a white coat. “I promise you, everyone is working night and day to fight this epidemic.” “别担心，”一位穿着白大褂的灰发男子说，“我向你保证，每个人都在日夜不停地努力抗击这场疫情。”

“Dr. Welch, my sister and mother need the medicine now, or they will die!” said Oki. “Even in a blizzard, I know Gunnar Kaasen can keep running!” “韦尔奇医生，我姐姐和妈妈现在急需药品，否则她们就会死的！”奥基说，“即使在暴风雪中，我也知道冈纳·卡森能继续前进！”

“You must leave us to do our work,” said Dr. Welch. He gently took the boy by the arm and led him back into the hall, where Jack and Annie were waiting. “Everything will be fine. Try to get some rest, son. We don’t want you to get sick, too.” “你必须让我们继续工作，”韦尔奇医生说，他轻轻抓住男孩的手臂，把他带回到大厅，杰克和安妮正在那里等候，“一切都会好起来的。试着休息一下吧，孩子，我们可不希望你也生病。”

Then Dr. Welch stepped into the room with Mayor Maynard and Nurse Morgan and closed the door. 然后韦尔奇博士和梅纳德市长、护士摩根一起走进了房间，然后关上了门。
