Annie banged on the wooden door. 安妮用力敲着木门。

A moment later, it opened. “Gunnar?” a man said, peering into the snowstorm. 片刻之后，门打开了。“古纳尔？”一个男人说道，他朝暴风雪中张望。

“No!” Annie shouted. “The ice cracked and my brother fell through! His feet are soaking wet! Can you help us?” “不是！”安妮喊道。“冰面裂开了，我弟弟掉下去了！他的脚全湿透了！你能帮帮我们吗？”

“Hurry! Get inside!” the man said. “快点！快进来！”那人说。

With Annie’s help, Jack limped into the warm, firelit cabin. He took off his mittens and wiped snow and ice from his glasses. 在安妮的帮助下，杰克一瘸一拐地走进了温暖、有火光的木屋。他摘下毛线手套，擦掉眼镜上的雪和冰。

“Sit near the stove!” the man commanded. “I’ll heat some water!” “坐在炉子旁边！”那人命令道。“我要烧点水！”

Annie helped Jack out of his parka, and he sat on a wooden chair near a crackling woodstove. 安妮帮杰克脱下他的派克大衣，他坐在一个靠近噼啪作响的木炉的木椅上。

“Take off his boots!” the man said. “把他的靴子脱了！”那人说。

Annie helped Jack pull off his fur boots and wool socks, then set them near the stove. The man grabbed a kettle from the top of the woodstove and poured water into a bucket. 安妮帮杰克脱下他的毛皮靴子和羊毛袜子，然后把它们放在炉子旁边。那人从木炉顶部拿起一个水壶，把水倒进桶里。

Jack looked at his wet feet. They were white and numb from the cold. He couldn’t move his toes. 杰克看着他湿漉漉的脚。因为寒冷，他的脚变得又白又麻木，他连脚趾都动不了。

“How are they?” the man said, lugging the bucket to Jack. “怎么样？”那人一边拖着桶走到杰克面前，一边问道。

“N-not bad,” Jack said. He was still trembling from the cold. “不、不坏，”杰克说。他仍然因为寒冷而发抖。

“Not good,” said the man, looking at Jack’s feet. He set the bucket down in front of Jack. “Soak them awhile.” “不，很不好，”那人看着杰克的脚说。他把桶放在杰克面前。“把脚泡一会儿。”

“Thanks,” said Jack. As he put his feet into the warm water, he was overcome with pain. He started to pull them out, but the man stopped him. “谢谢，”杰克说。当他把脚放进温暖的水里时，疼痛让他受不了。他开始把脚抽出来，但那个人拦住了他。

“Keep ’em there, kid,” he said. “别动，小子，”那个人说。

“Okay,” said Jack, gritting his teeth. Listening to the howling wind and the snow pelting the cabin windows, he worried about the huskies. “Can—can we bring our dog team inside?” he asked. “好吧，”杰克咬着牙说。听着呼啸的风声和雪粒打在小屋窗户上的声音，他担心起那些哈士奇来。“可——可以让我们把狗队带进来看看吗？”他问。

“Sure, I’ll unhitch them from their harnesses. You stay here and soak your feet,” the man said. Then he threw on a parka and hurried outside to get the dogs. “当然可以，我会把它们从挽具上解开。你在这里泡着脚，”那个人说。然后他穿上一件防寒服，赶紧跑出去牵狗。

“I’ll help him,” Annie said to Jack. “Don’t move.” And she followed the man back into the storm. “我去帮帮他，”安妮对杰克说。“别动。”然后她跟着那个人又走进了风暴中。

As Jack sat alone in the one-room cabin and soaked his feet, he worried about the time. He looked around for a clock but didn’t see one. How long would it take for his boots to dry? he wondered. Would his feet be okay? Did the dogs have frostbite, too? 杰克独自坐在只有一个房间的小屋里，泡着脚，他担心起时间来。他四处张望想找钟表，但没看到。他的靴子要多久才能干？他想。他的脚会没事吗？狗们也得了冻伤吗？

Jack was glad when the door opened. “Everyone in!” Annie shouted. Hauling the sack of dried fish with her, she led the huskies inside. 当门打开时，杰克感到很高兴。“大家都进来！”安妮喊道。她扛着一袋干鱼，领着哈士奇们走进了屋里。

The man followed the team into the cabin and slammed the door against the storm. “Go! Sit,” he ordered the dogs. He pointed to dry straw spread over the floor. The bedraggled huskies took slow steps across the room and settled down in the straw. 那个人跟着狗队走进小屋，猛地把门关上，挡住了风暴。“去吧，坐下，”他对狗们下令。他指着地上铺开的干草。浑身湿透的哈士奇们慢慢地穿过房间，在干草上躺了下来。

“Feel any better?” the man asked Jack. “感觉好点了吗？”那个人问杰克。

“Yes,” said Jack. His teeth had stopped chattering, and the pain in his feet had lessened. “I’m Jack. She’s my sister, Annie.” “是的，”杰克说。他的牙齿不再打颤，脚上的疼痛也减轻了。“我是杰克。她是我的姐姐，安妮。”

“I know, she told me,” said the man. “I’m Ed.” He grabbed the kettle and poured a little more steamy water into the bucket. “我知道，她告诉过我，”那个人说，“我叫埃德。”他抓起水壶，往桶里倒了一些蒸汽腾腾的水。

“Ed’s a musher, too,” said Annie. “His team is in a shed behind the cabin. He’s a mailman, like Oki’s uncle. He recognized their dogs.” “埃德也是个雪橇犬驾驶员，”安妮说，“他的狗队在小屋后面的一个棚屋里。他像奥基的叔叔一样是个邮差。他认出了他们的狗。”

“Cool,” said Jack. “真酷，”杰克说。

“Yep,” said Ed. “Keep soaking your feet while your sister and I take care of the dogs.” “没错，”埃德说，“在你姐姐和我照顾狗狗的时候，你继续泡着你的脚。”

Jack kept his feet in the bucket as Ed poured drinking water into a large pot and gave it to the team. Then Ed and Annie knelt in the straw and massaged the dogs’ feet, one paw at a time. The huskies panted and looked as if they were smiling. 杰克把脚一直泡在桶里，埃德把饮用水倒进一个大锅里，然后给了狗队。接着，埃德和安妮跪在稻草上，一个爪子一个爪子地给狗狗按摩脚。哈士奇们喘着气，看起来好像在笑。

“Good dogs, good dogs,” Annie kept saying. “好狗狗，好狗狗，”安妮一直说着。

“That’s right, good dogs,” Ed chimed in. “没错，好狗狗，”埃德附和道。

Jack saw that the musher was missing teeth and his beard was scraggly. Even so, his face had dignity and strength, Jack thought. Now that he understood the job of a professional musher, he admired Ed a lot. 杰克注意到这个雪橇犬驾驶员缺了几颗牙齿，胡须也很凌乱。尽管如此，他觉得这个人的脸上有一种尊严和力量。现在他明白了专业雪橇犬驾驶员的工作，他非常佩服埃德。

“Ed lives here, at the Port Safety roadhouse,” Annie said. “That’s a good name for it, huh?” “埃德就住在这里，在波特安全驿站，”安妮说，“这个名字起得真好，对吧？”

Jack nodded. As the wind howled outside, it did feel safe inside the cabin, but he worried that they couldn’t stay. They had only twelve hours of magic. “Do you know what time it is?” he asked. 杰克点了点头。外面的风呼啸着，屋内确实感觉很安全，但他担心他们不能在这里待太久。他们只有十二个小时的魔法时间。“你知道现在几点了吗？”他问。

“Hold on.” Ed pulled out a pocket watch. “Almost midnight,” he said. “抓紧了。”埃德掏出一块怀表。“快到午夜了，”他说。

So almost eight and a half hours left to find Gunnar Kaasen and return to Nome, thought Jack. Suddenly he remembered something. “Ed, why did you call us Gunnar when you opened the door?” 杰克心想，还剩下差不多八又一半小时的时间去找到冈纳·卡森并返回诺姆。突然，他想起来一件事。“埃德，你开门的时候为什么叫我们冈纳？”

“Did you mean Gunnar Kaasen?” asked Annie. “Were you expecting him?” “你是说冈纳·卡森吗？”安妮问。“你是不是在等他？”

“I did mean Gunnar Kaasen,” said Ed. “Earlier today I was told to wait for him. If he gets tired on his journey, I’m supposed to take his package on to Nome. But then I got word from Solomon that Gunnar would be staying there awhile. When I heard a knock, I thought he’d decided to push on.” “我确实是说冈纳·卡森，”埃德说。“今天早些时候我被告知要等他。如果他在旅途中累了，我应该替他把包裹送到诺姆。但后来我从所罗门那里得到消息说，冈纳会在那里待一段时间。当我听到敲门声时，我以为他决定继续前进了。”

“No,” said Annie. “The mayor of Nome told him to wait until the storm ends. At least that’s what someone told us.” “不是，”安妮说。“诺姆的市长告诉他要等到暴风雨结束再出发。至少有人是这么告诉我们的。”

“Good thing,” said Ed. “Only a crazy fool would drive in a storm like this. So why the heck were you two out there?” “太好了，”埃德说。“在这种暴风雨中开车的人，只有疯子才会这么做。那你们两个为什么会在外面？”

“Uh…well, we just went out for a short ride to…uh…,” said Annie. “呃……我们只是出去兜了个小圈，去……呃……”，安妮说。

“To exercise Oki’s uncle’s dogs,” said Jack, “while Oki’s at the hospital.” “去锻炼奥基叔叔的狗，”杰克说，“因为奥基在医院。”

“Right,” said Annie. “And then we got lost.” “对，”安妮说。“然后我们就迷路了。”

“You’re lucky you found this place before you froze to death,” said Ed. “你们运气真好，在被冻死之前就找到了这个地方，”埃德说。

“Yeah. Really lucky,” said Jack. “对，真的很幸运，”杰克说。

“How are your feet?” Ed asked him. “你的脚怎么样？”埃德问他。

Jack pulled his feet out of the water. His skin was pink, and the terrible pain was gone. He wiggled his toes. “I think they’re going to be okay,” he said. 杰克把脚从水里拿出来。他的皮肤变得粉红，可怕的疼痛也消失了。他动了动脚趾，说：“我想它们应该没问题了。”

“Good news,” said Ed. He grabbed a towel and a pair of socks from an old canvas bag and tossed them to Jack. “These socks will be too big, but better big socks than wet ones.” “好消息，”埃德说。他从一个旧帆布袋里拿出一条毛巾和一双袜子，扔给了杰克。“这些袜子可能太大了，但总比湿袜子好。”

“Thanks,” said Jack, drying his feet, then pulling on the warm, woolly socks. “谢谢，”杰克一边擦干脚，一边穿上那双又暖和又毛茸茸的袜子。

“You just dodged a case of frostbite, mister,” said Ed. “I bet you kids will think twice before taking a joyride in a blizzard again.” “你刚刚躲过了一次冻伤，先生，”埃德说。“我打赌你们两个以后再也不敢在暴风雪中开这种玩笑车了。”

“Well, actually, I think we should be heading home soon,” said Jack. “But don’t worry. It’s not far.” “嗯，其实我觉得我们该回家了，”杰克说。“不过别担心，不远。”

“What? Are you two crazy? In this weather?” said Ed. “什么？你们俩疯了吗？在这种天气里？”埃德说。

“Well, we’re, um, champion mushers,” Annie said in a small voice. “嗯，我们……呃……是冠军雪橇犬驾驶员，”安妮小声地说。

“Oh, yeah?” Ed squinted at her. “What races have you won?” “哦，是吗？”埃德眯着眼看着她。“你们赢过哪些比赛？”

“Well…um…they weren’t in Alaska,” Annie said. “We, uh…we won the Frog Creek Winter Relay and—” “嗯……他们不在阿拉斯加，”安妮说。“我们……我们赢了蛙溪镇冬季接力赛，而且——”

“Never heard of it,” said Ed. “Doesn’t matter. I don’t care who you are; you’re not going back out in that storm. Your team needs to rest, if nothing else. And eat! Right, fellas?” “从来没听说过，”埃德说。“没关系。不管你是谁，你都不能再出去面对那场风暴。你们的队伍至少需要休息一下，还要吃点东西！对吧，伙计们？”

Ed grabbed the burlap sack and pulled out flat pieces of dried pink fish. The huskies wiggled and whined as Ed started feeding them. 埃德抓起麻袋，拿出几片干透的粉红色鱼片。埃德开始喂他们时，哈士奇们扭动着身体，发出呜咽声。

Annie looked at Jack. “Maybe we can stay a little bit longer?” she said under her breath. 安妮看着杰克，小声说：“也许我们可以再待一会儿？”

Jack nodded. “Okay. But we have to keep our eye on the time.” 杰克点了点头。“好吧。但我们得注意时间。”

“We will,” said Annie. “我们会的，”安妮说。

The dogs gobbled up every piece of fish and drank from bowls of water. Then the huskies sat down, put back their heads, and began to howl. The wind howled outside as all eight dogs howled inside. 狗们狼吞虎咽地吃掉了所有鱼片，然后从水碗里喝水。接着，哈士奇们坐下来，把头往后仰，开始嚎叫。外面的风也嚎叫着，和里面八只狗的嚎叫声交织在一起。

Jack knew the howling meant the dogs were satisfied. He, Annie, and Ed laughed, until the howling stopped as abruptly as it had begun. 杰克知道，狗的嚎叫意味着它们已经满足了。他、安妮和埃德笑起来，直到嚎叫声像开始时一样突然停止了。

The dogs then began nodding happily off to sleep, one by one. Some lay on their sides, while others curled up in tight balls and covered their noses with their bushy tails. 接着，狗们一个接一个开心地打起瞌睡来。有些躺在一边，有些则蜷成一团，用蓬松的尾巴盖住鼻子。

“All right! Now let’s get you some food and drink!” said Ed. “好了！现在让我们给你准备点食物和饮料吧！”埃德说。

“Can you please tell us what time it is again?” asked Jack. “你们能再告诉我们现在几点吗？”杰克问。

Ed glanced at his watch. “One-fifteen,” he said. 埃德看了看他的手表。‘一点十五分，’他说。

Jack didn’t know what to do. He was hungry, but now they only had seven hours left to find Gunnar and get back to Nome. 杰克不知道该怎么办。他饿了，但现在他们只剩下七个小时去找冈纳，然后回到诺姆了。

“I’ll make some soup,” said Ed. He seemed determined to keep them from going back out into the blizzard. He grabbed a can of tomato soup from a shelf and began opening it with a can opener. ‘我来煮点汤，’埃德说。他似乎决心不让他们再次出去面对暴风雪。他从架子上拿了一罐番茄汤，用开罐器开始打开它。

What about our mission? Jack wondered. How could he make Ed understand that they had to leave? “Excuse me,” he said. “I’m afraid—” 我们的任务怎么办？杰克心想。他怎么才能让埃德明白他们必须离开呢？‘打扰一下，’他说。‘恐怕——’

“You can wash your hands over there!” Ed interrupted. He pointed toward a tin basin on a washstand. ‘你们可以在那里洗洗手！’埃德打断了他。他指着洗漱台上的一只铁盆。

“It’s okay,” Annie whispered to Jack. “We have time.” She put her red chapped hands into the cool water and washed them with soap. Jack reluctantly did the same. “没关系，”安妮低声对杰克说。“我们还有时间。”她把她那双红色的干裂的手放进凉水里，用肥皂洗了洗。杰克不情愿地也照做了。

Ed nodded toward the table. “Sit!” he said, as if giving a command to two huskies. 埃德朝桌子点了点头。‘坐！’他说，就像在给两只哈士奇下命令一样。

Jack and Annie smiled. They dried their hands and sat on two wooden barrels at the table. Ed dropped a box of soda crackers between them and gave them each a glass of milk. While the blizzard raged outside, he filled two bowls with steamy soup from a pot on the stove and put them in front of Jack and Annie. Then he sat in an old armchair near the table. 杰克和安妮笑了。他们擦干手，坐在桌子旁的两个木桶上。埃德在他们中间放了一盒苏打饼干，每人给了一个牛奶杯。暴风雪在外面肆虐，他从炉子上的锅里舀了两碗热腾腾的汤，放在杰克和安妮面前。然后他坐在桌子旁边的旧扶手椅上。

“Thanks, Ed!” said Annie. “谢谢你，埃德！”安妮说。

“Yeah, thanks,” said Jack, sighing. Breathing in the delicious smell of the tomato soup, his worries momentarily faded. “嗯，谢谢，”杰克叹了口气说。他吸了一口番茄汤的香味，烦恼暂时消失了。
