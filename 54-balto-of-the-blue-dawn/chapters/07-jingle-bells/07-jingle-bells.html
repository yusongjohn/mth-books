“Mmm, delicious!” said Annie. She and Jack slurped down the hot soup. As they chewed the salty soda crackers, they watched the dogs breathe peacefully in their sleep. “嗯，真好吃！”安妮说。她和杰克吸溜着热汤。他们嚼着咸咸的苏打饼干，看着狗在睡觉时平静地呼吸。

“Do most Alaskans have sled dogs?” asked Annie. “大多数阿拉斯加人养雪橇犬吗？”安妮问。

“Many do,” said Ed, resting in his armchair near the fire. “But the day is coming when dogs won’t be used much anymore, except for racing. I can see it happening already. Small planes will get better and better and take over the long trips. Cars will improve, and roads, too. Soon enough, we’ll have vehicles that go through deep snow in no time.” “很多都养，”埃德说，他正靠在壁炉旁边的扶手椅上休息。“但很快，雪橇犬就不会被用得那么多了，除了比赛。我已经看到这种情况发生了。小型飞机会变得越来越好，会接管长途旅行。汽车也会改进，道路也会改善。不久之后，我们就会有能迅速穿越深雪的交通工具。”

“That’s good in a way,” said Jack. “It’ll help medicine travel faster.” “这在某种程度上是好事，”杰克说。“这会让药品运输得更快。”

“But it’s sad, too,” said Annie. “但这也让人感到难过，”安妮说。

“I’m with you, missy,” said Ed, looking at the sleeping dogs. “Breaks the heart of an old-timer like me. People have used sled dogs for hundreds of years in this territory. I myself have known many brave dogs during my lifetime.” “我明白你的感受，小姑娘，”埃德看着睡觉的狗说。“这让我这个老家伙心里很难受。人们在这片地区用雪橇犬已经有几百年的历史了。我自己这辈子也认识过很多勇敢的狗。”

“Do you know Balto?” said Jack. “Gunnar Kaasen’s dog? We’ve heard of him.” “你知道巴尔托吗？”杰克问。“加纳尔·卡森的狗？我们听说过他。”

“Sure do,” said Ed. “Balto is the best lead dog in Alaska, along with Togo, Leonhard Seppala’s dog. Both Togo and Balto have stopped here on runs with Seppala and Kaasen.” “当然知道，”埃德说。“巴尔托是阿拉斯加最好的领头犬之一，还有托戈，莱昂哈德·塞帕拉的狗。托戈和巴尔托都曾和塞帕拉、卡森一起跑过长途。”

“What’s Balto like?” asked Annie. “巴尔托是什么样的？”安妮问。

“Ahh, that dog loves to run a trail,” said Ed. “Loves it more than any dog I ever saw. Great strength and lots of courage. Big shaggy black dog. Beautiful to watch because of his spirit.” “啊，那条狗特别喜欢跑山路，”埃德说。“我从来没见过哪条狗比它更喜欢跑山路了。它力气很大，也很勇敢。它是一条又大又毛茸茸的黑狗。看着它奔跑的样子，真是让人着迷，因为它充满精神。”

Annie smiled. “I’d like to meet him.” 安妮微笑着说。“我想见见它。”

“Annie has a special way with dogs,” Jack explained. “安妮和狗特别有缘，”杰克解释道。

“With all animals, really,” said Annie. “其实，和所有动物都有缘，”安妮说。

Ed smiled at her. “Alaskans are real respectful of animals, too,” he said. “We wouldn’t have survived a minute without ’em. People here have learned to use all the gifts this frozen world can give.” 埃德微笑着看着她。“阿拉斯加人也很尊重动物，”他说。“没有它们，我们根本活不下去。这里的人已经学会了利用这个冰天雪地的世界能给予我们的一切礼物。”

“Like what?” asked Jack. “比如什么？”杰克问。

“Well, the socks I just gave you are made of sheep’s wool,” said Ed. “The parkas that are drying over there are made of squirrel fur. We all wear sealskin pants and reindeer boots and fox fur hats. I wear moose skin and caribou skin and rabbit fur. The medicine headed for Nome is wrapped in bear hide. The medicine itself is made from horse serum.” “比如说，我刚刚给你的袜子就是用绵羊的羊毛做的，”埃德说。“那边正在晾干的羽绒服是用松鼠的皮毛做的。我们每个人都穿着海豹皮的裤子，驯鹿皮的靴子，狐狸皮的帽子。我穿的是驼鹿皮和麋鹿皮，还有兔子毛。送往诺姆的药品是用熊皮包裹的。药品本身是用马血清制成的。”

“What’s serum?” said Jack. “血清是什么？”杰克问。

“The blood of a horse,” said Ed. “It has antitoxins to kill diphtheria.” “就是马的血，”埃德说。“它含有能杀死白喉的抗毒素。”

“Wow,” said Annie. “So Alaskans get gifts from”—she held up one finger after another—“sheep, squirrels, seals, reindeer, foxes, moose, caribou, rabbits, bears, horses…” “哇，”安妮说。“所以阿拉斯加人得到的礼物来自——”她一边说一边竖起一根手指又一根手指——“绵羊、松鼠、海豹、驯鹿、狐狸、驼鹿、麋鹿、兔子、熊、马……”

“And dogs,” said Jack, finishing his milk. “还有狗，”杰克说完，喝完了牛奶。

“And dogs, of course,” said Annie. “还有狗，当然，”安妮说。

“And fish and whales and birds of the air. All creatures,” said Ed. “还有鱼、鲸鱼和飞鸟，所有动物，”埃德说。

“So everything’s connected,” said Annie. “所以所有东西都是相连的，”安妮说。

“That’s right,” said Ed, nodding. “Eventually it all becomes one thing…the people, the land, the animals, the sea, and the air—it becomes Alaska.” “没错，”埃德点头说。“最终，所有东西都会变成一个整体——人们、土地、动物、海洋和空气——它会变成阿拉斯加。”

“Cool,” said Jack. “太酷了，”杰克说。

The three of them stared for a long moment at the dogs. Some of the huskies paddled the air with their paws, as if they were dreaming about running over the trail. 他们三个人久久地盯着那些狗看。一些哈士奇用爪子拍打空气，好像在梦到自己在小路上奔跑。

Ed yawned, and his eyes started to close. A moment later, his head drooped forward, and he was snoring. 埃德打了个哈欠，眼睛开始闭上。片刻之后，他的头向前低垂，开始打起呼噜来。

Jack quietly put down his spoon. He tiptoed to the stove and grabbed his fur boots. They were dry, but his socks were still damp. So he pulled the boots over the big pair of socks Ed had given him. “It’s time to head to Solomon,” he whispered to Annie. “We have to finish what we came here to do.” 杰克悄悄地放下勺子。他蹑手蹑脚地走到炉子旁，拿起他的毛皮靴子。靴子是干的，但他的袜子还是湿的。所以他把靴子套在埃德给他的那双大袜子上。“是时候去索洛蒙了，”他对安妮小声说。“我们必须完成我们来这里要做的事情。”

Annie nodded. 安妮点了点头。

Ed snored loudly as Jack and Annie silently pulled on their fur parkas. They grabbed their mittens and slipped over to the dogs and gently woke them. The dogs jumped to their feet. Even though there were whines and yips, Ed kept snoring. 埃德大声打鼾，杰克和安妮悄悄地穿上毛皮外套。他们拿起手套，轻手轻脚地走到狗身边，温柔地把它们叫醒。狗们立刻跳了起来。虽然它们有些呜咽和吠叫，但埃德还在继续打鼾。

“Thanks, Ed,” Jack whispered to the sleeping man. “谢谢你，埃德，”杰克对正在睡觉的男人小声说。

“Yeah, thanks for being so nice to us and the dogs,” whispered Annie. Then, very quietly, she led the team out of the log cabin. Jack followed, closing the door behind them. “是啊，谢谢你对我们和狗狗这么好，”安妮也小声说。然后，她非常安静地带领队伍走出了木屋。杰克跟在后面，关上了门。

Outside, the wind and snow were blowing wildly. Jack and Annie pulled off their mittens long enough to harness the team. They nimbly hooked the dogs to tug lines, neck lines, and the towline. 外面，风和雪疯狂地吹着。杰克和安妮摘下手套，给队伍套上缰绳。他们灵巧地把狗拴在拖绳、颈绳和牵引绳上。

“Ready?” Jack shouted above the wind. “准备好了吗？”杰克在风声中大声喊道。

“I hope Ed doesn’t worry about us when he wakes up!” Annie shouted back. “我希望埃德醒来后不要担心我们！”安妮大声回应。

“Oh, man. I just thought of something,” said Jack. “哦，天哪。我刚想到一件事，”杰克说。

“What?” “什么事？”

“Stardust!” said Jack. “For Ed!” “星尘！”杰克说。“给埃德的！”

“Yes!” said Annie. Leaving the dogs waiting for a moment, she and Jack hurried back to the cabin, slipped inside, and closed the door. “对！”安妮说。她让狗狗们等了一会儿，然后和杰克赶紧跑回小屋，溜了进去，关上了门。

Ed was still snoring. 埃德还在打呼噜。

Annie reached into her pocket and took out the dark blue box. When she opened the lid, the silvery dust shimmered like starlight. She emptied a small amount into her palm and tossed the glittering dust into the air, whispering, “We wish to be forgotten!” 安妮把手伸进口袋，拿出那个深蓝色的盒子。当她打开盒盖时，银色的灰尘像星光一样闪闪发亮。她倒出一点点到手心里，然后把闪闪发光的灰尘撒向空中，小声说：“我们希望被遗忘！”

The stardust flashed through the room, then quickly evaporated. To Jack’s amazement, the bowls, spoons, and cups were clean and stacked neatly on the counter. Jack’s damp socks had vanished. The unopened soup can was back on the shelf. The hay was neatly spread across the floor. 星光尘在房间里闪了一下，然后迅速消失了。让杰克惊讶的是，碗、勺子和杯子都干净了，整齐地摆放在柜台上。杰克湿漉漉的袜子不见了。那罐没打开的汤又回到了架子上。干草也被整齐地铺在了地板上。

“No sign we were here,” said Annie. “我们这里一点痕迹都没有，”安妮说。

“Except Ed will be missing a pair of his socks,” whispered Jack. “除了埃德会少了一双袜子，”杰克小声说。

Annie laughed. “Full speed ahead!” she whispered. Then she and Jack slipped back outside into the storm. They ran through the blowing snow to their waiting team. 安妮笑了。“全速前进！”她小声说。然后她和杰克悄悄溜回外面，走进了暴风雪中。他们跑过呼啸的雪花，来到等待他们的队伍那里。

“I’m happy to drive for a while!” Jack shouted in the wind. “Is that okay with you?” “我很乐意开一会儿车！”杰克在风中喊道。“你介意吗？”

“Sure! I’ll take over whenever you want!” said Annie. She picked up the snow hook and put it into the basket, then climbed in herself. “当然可以！你想休息的时候我就接手！”安妮说。她拿起雪钩放进篮子里，然后自己也爬了进去。

Jack stood on the sled runners, crouching slightly and gripping the handlebars. “Okay, team! Ready to find Gunnar and save lives?” he said. 杰克站在雪橇的滑板上，稍微蹲下身子，握紧了车把。“好了，队伍！准备好去找到冈纳并拯救生命了吗？”他说。

The huskies barked, as if saying YES! YES! YES! 哈士奇们汪汪叫着，好像在说：是的！是的！是的！

“Ready, willing, and eager!” said Annie. “准备好了，愿意去，还很兴奋！”安妮说。

“Line out!” Jack commanded. “拉紧！”杰克命令道。

The team pulled the towline straight out, making it taut. 队伍拉紧了拖绳，把它拉得笔直。

“Hike!” said Jack. “前进！”杰克说。

The eight huskies surged forward. Bells jingling, the team raced away from the roadhouse of Port Safety, pulling the sled through the blizzard. 八只哈士奇向前冲去。铃铛叮当作响，队伍离开安全港的路房子，拉着雪橇在暴风雪中飞驰。

As the basket bounced and bumped over uneven ridges on the trail, Annie almost fell out, but she didn’t complain. Jack could hear her laughing, as if she were enjoying the ride. 当篮子在崎岖的山路上颠簸时，安妮差点掉了出来，但她没有抱怨。杰克能听到她在笑，好像她很享受这次旅程。

The wind from the frozen sea was bitterly cold. As the wind howled and snow whirled through the dark, the dogs kept their heads down and ran on and on. 来自结冰大海的风非常寒冷。当风呼啸着，雪在黑暗中飞舞时，狗们低下头，继续奔跑。

Jack used his skills to move the team around banks of drifting snow. Through gale-force winds, he drove the sled over the rough, frozen path at the edge of the sea. 杰克运用他的技能，让队伍绕过堆积的雪堆。在狂风中，他驾驶雪橇在海边结冰的崎岖小路上前进。

Like sand in a sandstorm, ice crystals beat against his face, stinging his skin. His mittens were nearly frozen to the wooden handles. But he felt protected in his thick fur clothing. He was grateful to all the creatures that had given their gifts to help Alaskans survive in their frozen world. 就像沙尘暴中的沙子一样，冰晶打在了他的脸上，刺痛了他的皮肤。他的手套几乎被冻在了木制的把手上了。但他穿着厚厚的毛皮衣服，感到很安全。他感谢所有给予礼物帮助阿拉斯加人在这个冰天雪地中生存的动物们。

As more and more snow stuck to the lenses of his glasses and turned to ice, Jack knew he shouldn’t keep driving. “We need to switch now!” he called to Annie. 当越来越多的雪粘在了他的眼镜镜片上，变成冰时，杰克知道他不能再继续驾驶了。“我们现在得换人了！”他对安妮喊道。

Before he could give a command to stop the dogs, a fierce gust of wind slammed against the sled, sending it into a bank. Jack fell off the runners and was swallowed up in a pile of snow. 就在他能下令让狗停下来之前，一阵猛烈的风吹向雪橇，把它撞到了雪堆里。杰克从雪橇上摔了下来，被埋在了一堆雪里。

“Jack!” cried Annie. “Where are you?” “杰克！”安妮喊道。“你在哪？”

“Here! Here!” said Jack, floundering as he tried to get up. His heavy fur clothing made it hard for him to stand, but Annie grabbed his hands and helped him to his feet. “在这儿！在这儿！”杰克喊道，他努力想站起来，但厚重的毛皮衣服让他很难站稳。不过安妮抓住了他的手，帮他站了起来。

Together they pulled the sled out from the snowbank and untangled the rigging. 他们一起把雪橇从雪堆里拉出来，解开了绳索。

“I can drive for a while!” said Annie. “我可以暂时驾驶！”安妮说。

“Okay!” said Jack. He sat in the basket. “好！”杰克说。他坐在了篮子里。

Annie stood on the runners. “Line out!” she shouted. 安妮站在雪橇的滑板上。“拉紧绳子！”她喊道。

The dogs straightened their lines. But before Annie commanded them to take off, she yelled, “Jack! Listen!” 狗们拉直了绳子。但就在安妮下令让它们出发之前，她喊道：“杰克！听！”

Jack listened. He heard the shriek of the wind and the whooshing of snow. “Listen to what?” he said. 杰克听了。他听见了风的尖啸和雪的呼啸。“听什么？”他说。

“Bells! I hear bells!” Annie shouted. “Up ahead!” “铃声！我听见铃声！”安妮喊道。“在前面！”

Now Jack could hear the bells, too—jingling bells. “Yes!” he said. “Another dog team must be coming toward us!” 现在杰克也能听到铃铛声了，叮叮当当的铃铛。“对！”他说。“一定是另一支狗队朝我们这边来了！”

“Could it be Gunnar Kaasen and Balto?” shouted Annie. “会不会是冈纳·卡森和巴尔托？”安妮喊道。

“Maybe!” yelled Jack. “可能！”杰克大喊。

The jingling bells grew closer…and closer! 叮叮当当的铃铛声越来越近……越来越近！

“Yay! They’re coming this way for sure!” shouted Annie. “太好了！他们肯定朝我们这边来了！”安妮喊道。

But the sound of the bells stopped suddenly. Then through the storm came the frantic, high-pitched barking of dogs. 但铃铛声突然停了下来。接着，暴风雨中传来了狗们焦急、高声的吠叫。
