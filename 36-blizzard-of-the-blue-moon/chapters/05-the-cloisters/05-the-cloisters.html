5. The Cloisters 5. 修道院

“Do you see Teddy and Kathleen?” asked Jack. “你看到泰迪和凯瑟琳了吗？”杰克问。

The crowd was swarming away from the subway platform toward different exits. Jack couldn’t see anyone who looked like the two young enchanters. 人群正从地铁站台往不同的出口跑去。 杰克看不到任何看起来像那两个年轻女巫的人。

“No,” said Annie. “But if we figured out where to get off, I’m sure they did, too. Let’s hurry and get to the zoo. We can meet up with them there.” “不，”安妮说。 “但是如果我们知道在哪里下车，我敢肯定他们也知道。 我们快点去动物园吧。 我们在那里可以和他们会合。”

Jack and Annie moved with the crowd. They passed a change booth on the way out of the station. “Wait, I’ll ask where to go,” said Annie. She waited in line and then leaned forward to talk to the man in the booth. “Do you know how to walk to the Bronx Zoo from here?” she said. 杰克和安妮跟着人群移动。 他们在出站的路上经过了一个换钱的柜台。 “等一下，我要问问去哪，”安妮说。 她排好队，然后向前倾身和柜台里的男人说话。 “你知道怎么从这儿走到布朗克斯动物园吗？”她说。

“Walk to the Bronx Zoo? Are you out of your mind?” said the man. “This isn’t the stop for the Bronx Zoo! It’s much further north.” “走到布朗克斯动物园？” “你是不是疯了？”那人说。 “这不是去布朗克斯动物园的站！ 它在更北边。”

“You mean we got out at the wrong stop?” said Annie. “你是说我们在错误的站台下车了？”安妮说。

“You did, but it doesn’t matter,” said the man. “You can’t get there now, anyway. The tracks near the zoo are above ground. And I just got word they’re buried in snow.” “是的，但这不重要，”那个人说。 “无论如何，你现在也去不了那里。 动物园附近的轨道是露天的。 而且我刚得到消息，它们被埋在雪里。”

“Oh, no,” said Jack. “哦，不，”杰克说。

“Lousy day for the zoo, anyway,” said the man. “Next!” “不管怎么说，今天对动物园来说真是糟糕的一天，”那人说。 “下一个！”，

Jack and Annie stepped away from the booth and walked out of the subway station. The sidewalk was deserted. “Oh, man, that girl gave us the wrong information,” said Jack. 杰克和安妮走开了展台，走出了地铁站。 人行道上空无一人。 “哦，天哪，那个女孩给我们提供了错误的信息，”杰克说。

“This is terrible,” said Annie. “这太糟糕了，”安妮说。

“No kidding,” said Jack. He looked around, wondering what to do next. “可不是嘛，”杰克说。 他环顾四周，不知道接下来该做什么。

AH-U-GA! AH-U-GA! 啊——咕！啊——咕！

“What’s that?” said Jack. “那是什么？”杰克说。

“It looks like a taxi.” Annie pointed at a big yellow car with a checkerboard stripe and writing on the side. Its horn let out a loud squawk: AH-U-GA! AH-U-GA! “看起来像一辆出租车。”安妮指着一辆又大又黄的车，车身上有棋盘格条纹和一些字。 它的喇叭发出一声响亮的鸣叫：AH-U-GA！AH-U-GA！

The driver stuck his head out the window. He wore a big furry cap that came down over his ears. “Need a taxi?” he called. 司机把头从车窗里伸了出来。 他戴着一顶又大又毛茸茸的帽子，帽子盖住了他的耳朵。 “需要出租车吗？”他喊道。

“Yes!” shouted Annie. “Come on, Jack! He can take us to the zoo!” Jack hurried after her through the snow. “需要！”安妮喊道。 “快点，杰克！ 他可以带我们去动物园！”杰克赶紧跟在她后面穿过雪地。

The taxi driver stepped out of the cab and opened the back door. “Hop in!” he said. His cheerful voice was muffled through a plaid scarf that covered half his face. 出租车司机从车里走出来，打开了后门。 “快上车！”他说。 他愉快的声音被一条格子围巾盖住了，围巾遮住了他一半的脸。

“Thanks!” said Jack. He stepped onto a running board and then climbed inside. “谢谢！”杰克说。 他踩上了一个踏板，然后爬进了车里。

The taxi was very roomy. Jack could stretch his legs out and not even touch the front seat. “Boy, lots more room in old cars than ours,” he said to Annie. 出租车非常宽敞。 杰克可以伸直腿，甚至不会碰到前排座位。 “老式汽车比我们的空间大多了，”他对安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Annie. “And there’s no seat belts.” “是啊，”安妮说。 “而且没有安全带。”

“Oh, yeah,” said Jack. “I guess old cars didn’t have any. I hope this guy is a good driver.” “哦，是啊，”杰克说。 “我想老式汽车没有这个。” 希望这位司机技术好。”

The driver slid open a little window that separated the front seat from the back. “Where to?” he asked. 司机滑开了一扇小窗户，把前排和后排隔开。 “去哪儿？”他问。

“Can you please take us to the Bronx Zoo?” said Annie. “你能带我们去布朗克斯动物园吗？”安妮说。

“We’re in a big hurry to get there,” said Jack. “我们非常着急要赶到那里，”杰克说。

“Sure thing, kiddos,” said the driver. “没问题，孩子们，”司机说。

“Great,” said Jack. “太好了，”杰克说。

“Oh, how much will it cost?” asked Annie. “哦，要多少钱呀？”安妮问。

“About thirty cents,” said the driver. “Can you afford it?” “大约三十美分，”司机说。 “你付得起吗？”

“Sure thing, kiddo,” said Annie. “当然可以，孩子，”安妮说。

The driver laughed and closed the window. 司机笑了，然后关上了车窗。

Then the taxi began moving through the snow. 接着，出租车开始在雪中行驶。

“Everything’s so cheap here,” Annie said to Jack. “这里的东西都好便宜，”安妮对杰克说。

“To us it is,” said Jack. “But it’s the Great Depression. Lots of people don’t even have a nickel.” “对我们来说是这样，”杰克说。 “但这是大萧条时期。 很多人甚至没有一枚镍币。”

The taxi slid over the icy road and bumped over the curb. “Whoa!” said Jack, slipping off the seat. 出租车在结冰的道路上滑行，然后撞上了路沿。 “哇！”杰克说道，从座位上滑了下来。

“Sorry, kiddos!” called the driver. He got the taxi back onto the street. Then he plowed on through the snow, swerving this way and that. “对不起，孩子们！”司机喊道。 他把出租车重新开回了街上。 然后他继续在雪中前进，车子左右摇摆。

The ride seemed dangerous in the bad weather, but Jack didn’t want to get out now. He stared nervously out the window. The streets were empty of people. All the stores were closed and shuttered. Snow was piling up on apartment stoops, fire escapes, and iron balconies. Many of the buildings were shabby and crumbling, with broken windows. 天气这么糟糕，乘车看起来很危险，但杰克现在不想下车。 他紧张地望着窗外。 街上一个人也没有。 所有的商店都关门了，窗户也关上了。 积雪堆在公寓的台阶、消防梯和铁制阳台上。 许多建筑破旧不堪，窗户还碎了。

“Hard times,” Annie said softly. “艰难的时光，”安妮轻声说道。

“Yep,” said Jack. He took a deep breath. “对，”杰克说。 他深吸了一口气。

The taxi climbed up a road lined with tall evergreen trees. Suddenly it skidded and came to a stop. The engine roared, but the taxi’s wheels only spun in the snow. 出租车沿着一条两旁种满高大常绿树的路行驶。 突然，车子打滑停了下来。 引擎轰鸣，但出租车的车轮只在雪地里空转。

“What’s happening?” Jack wondered aloud. He tapped on the glass that separated them from the driver. “怎么了？”杰克大声问道。 他敲了敲将他们和司机隔开的玻璃。

The driver slid the window open. “Too bad, kiddos, looks like I’m stuck,” he said. 司机滑开窗户。 “真糟糕，孩子们，看来我被困住了，”他说。

“Are we near the zoo?” asked Annie. “我们离动物园近吗？”安妮问。

“It’s still a long ways from here, I’m sorry to say,” said the driver. “But this taxi’s not going anywhere. I don’t know what else to tell you.” “这里离那里还有很远呢，我很难过，”司机说。 “但这辆出租车哪儿也去不了。 我不知道还能告诉你什么。”

“Oh. Well, thanks,” said Annie. “How much do we owe you?” “谢谢了，”安妮说。 “我们该付你多少钱？”

“Forget it, kiddo,” said the driver. “Good luck!” “不用谢，孩子，”司机说。 “祝你好运！”

“Good luck yourself, kiddo,” said Annie. She opened the door, and she and Jack climbed out into the cold wind. The taxi motor kept running, but the wheels only spun in place, sending up sprays of dirty snow. “也祝你好运，孩子，”安妮说。 她打开门，她和杰克爬出来，走进寒冷的风中。 出租车的引擎还在运转，但车轮只是原地打转，扬起了脏雪。

“I don’t believe this,” said Jack. “我不相信这是真的，”杰克说。

“Maybe there’s a subway station somewhere near here,” said Annie. “也许附近有个地铁站，”安妮说。

“The man in the booth said the subways aren’t running all the way to the zoo,” said Jack. “售票亭的人说地铁不能一直开到动物园，”杰克说。

“I know, but maybe we can at least get closer,” said Annie. “我知道，但也许我们至少能靠近一点，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie struggled through the storm until they came to a steep cliff. Over the edge of the cliff they saw nothing but a cloud of wind-blown snow. 杰克和安妮在暴风雨中艰难前行，直到来到一处陡峭的悬崖。 他们从悬崖边往下看，只看到一片被风吹起的雪云。

“Where are we?” said Annie. “我们这是在哪里？”安妮说。

“I have no idea,” said Jack. His teeth chattered. His eyes and ears stung with the cold, and his hands and feet felt numb. Isn’t this what frostbite feels like? he thought. Numb? “我一点也不知道，”杰克说。 他的牙齿不停地打颤。 他的眼睛和耳朵被寒冷刺痛，手和脚也感觉麻木了。 这不是冻伤的感觉吗？他想。 麻木了吗？

“Let’s go back and sit in that taxi until we figure out what to do,” said Jack. “At least get out of the wind.” “我们回去坐在那辆出租车里，直到想清楚接下来该怎么做，”杰克说。 “至少能避开风。”

“Okay,” said Annie. “Maybe the driver will get it going again.” “好，”安妮说。 “也许司机能重新启动它。”

Jack and Annie headed back the way they’d come. But the taxi was gone! 杰克和安妮朝他们来的方向走回去。 但出租车不见了！

“Hey, he must’ve got unstuck!” said Annie. “And he left us!” “嘿，他一定是挣脱了！”安妮说。 “他丢下我们了！”

“Oh, man,” said Jack. “We’re having really rotten luck. And I think I’m starting to get frostbite. Seriously.” “哦，天哪，”杰克说。 “我们真的运气糟糕透了。 我觉得我开始冻伤了，真的。”

“It looks like there’s a building over there,” said Annie. “I see a tower.” “看起来那边有个建筑物，”安妮说。 “我看到一座塔。”

“Yeah, me too,” said Jack. “Let’s go there and try to figure out what to do next.” “是啊，我也看到了，”杰克说。 “我们去那边，想办法看看接下来该怎么做。”

Jack and Annie trekked up what looked like a long driveway until they came to the gray stone building. Jack wiped snow from a sign in front. 杰克和安妮沿着看起来像一条长长的车道走了过去，直到他们来到那座灰色石砌的建筑前。 杰克擦去了招牌前的积雪。

CLOISTERS OF THE METROPOLITAN MUSEUM, OPEN TO THE PUBLIC “大都会博物馆修道院，向公众开放”

“A museum!” said Annie. “Let’s go in and ask them how to get to the zoo.” “一座博物馆！”安妮说道。 “我们进去问问他们怎么去动物园。”

“Yeah, and get warm for a minute,” said Jack. “对，再待一会儿，暖和一下，”杰克说。

He and Annie climbed the snow-covered steps of the Cloisters. Annie opened the door, and the wind nearly swept them inside. 他和安妮爬上了覆盖着积雪的克洛伊斯特斯台阶。 安妮打开门，风几乎把他们吹了进去。

They pushed the door shut. 他们把门推 shut 了。

“Ah! My first visitors all day!” a woman chirped. “啊！ 我今天第一个参观者！”一位女士欢快地说。

Jack and Annie turned around. A museum lady sat at a desk in a corner. Dressed in a green uniform, she was tall and thin with a friendly face and short gray hair. 杰克和安妮转过身。 一位博物馆的女士坐在角落的一张桌子旁。 她穿着绿色的制服，又高又瘦，脸上带着友好的表情，头发是短短的灰色的。

“I’m sorry, but we can’t stay long,” said Annie. “We just came here to get warm for a minute and get some information.” “对不起，但我们不能待太久，”安妮说。 “我们只是来这里暖暖身子，顺便打听点信息。”

“What can I tell you?” the woman asked. “你们想了解什么？”那位女士问。

“We’re trying to get to the Bronx Zoo,” said Jack. “Is there a subway near here?” “我们正想办法去布鲁克林动物园，”杰克说。 “这里附近有地铁吗？”

“Well, the A train is close by,” said the museum lady. “But that won’t take you anywhere near the zoo.” “嗯，A线地铁就在附近，”博物馆的女士说。 “但那条线离动物园可远着呢。”

“Oh, darn,” said Annie. “哦，真糟糕，”安妮说。

“Oh, I wouldn’t be so sad,” the woman said brightly. “If I were you, I’d stay right here and enjoy the Cloisters! You won’t regret it, I promise. The Cloisters holds most of the medieval collection of the world-famous Metropolitan Museum of Art.” “哦，你不用这么难过，”那位女士高兴地说。 “如果你是我，我就待在这里，享受修道院！” 你不会后悔的，我保证。 “The Cloisters holds most of the medieval collection of the world-famous Metropolitan Museum of Art.”

“What’s a cloister?” Annie asked. “What’s a cloister?” 安妮问。

“It’s an enclosed garden or courtyard,” said the museum lady. “We have four in all. They beautifully evoke medieval times, from the Romanesque period to the light, airy elegance of the Gothic.” “It’s an enclosed garden or courtyard,” 博物馆的女士说。 “我们一共有四个。” “它们非常完美地展现了中世纪的风貌，从罗马式时期到哥特式轻盈优雅的风格。”

“Cool,” said Jack politely. He had no idea what she was talking about. “真酷，”杰克有礼貌地说。 他完全不知道她在说什么。

“I suppose the gardens are cool most days, my dear,” said the woman. “But today they are freezing! Never mind, you don’t have to spend time in the gardens to enjoy the museum. Inside, we have many beautiful exhibits. The tapestries are especially lovely. They were woven with beautiful yarns by weavers from the Netherlands. For years, they hung in a French castle and managed to escape destruction during the Revolution. Then, for two generations—” “亲爱的，我想花园大多数日子都很凉爽，”那个女人说。 “但是今天它们冷得发抖！” 没关系，你不用花时间在花园里，也能享受博物馆。 里面有许多美丽的展品。 这些挂毯特别漂亮。 它们是由来自荷兰的织工用漂亮的毛线编织而成的。 多年来，它们挂在一座法国城堡里，并在革命期间成功逃脱了被毁的命运。 然后，经历了两代人——

“Excuse me,” said Jack. He was eager to end the woman’s boring lecture so they could be on their way. “请问一下，”杰克说。 他迫不及待地想结束这位女士无聊的讲座，好让他们赶紧出发。

But she kept talking excitedly. “The tapestries were used by peasants to protect potatoes stored in their barns until, eventually, a countess rescued them. They were restored, and in 1922, Mr. John D. Rockefeller, Jr., bought them. Just last year, Mr. Rockefeller gave them as gifts to—” 但她一直在兴奋地讲话。 “这些挂毯曾被农民用来保护储存在谷仓里的土豆，直到最终一位伯爵夫人救了它们。 它们被修复了，1922年，约翰·D先生 洛克菲勒二世买了它们。 就在去年，先生。 洛克菲勒把它们作为礼物送给了——”

“Oh, that is so cool!” interrupted Jack loudly. He pretended to be interested so they could escape. “We’d love to see them! Where are they?” “哦，这太酷了！”杰克大声地打断了。 他假装感兴趣，好让他们能逃走。 “我们很想看看它们！它们在哪里？”

“The tapestry room is just on the other side of the first cloister,” the woman said, pointing. “Turn that corner, go out the door, and then walk through the garden to the door of the tapestry room.” “挂毯房间就在第一个回廊的另一边，”女人指着说。 “转过那个拐角，走出去，然后穿过花园走到挂毯房间的门口。”

“Let’s go, Annie. Quick!” said Jack. He and Annie went around the corner, out the door, and into a snowy garden. “快走吧，安妮。” “快！”杰克说。 他和安妮绕过拐角，走出门，走进了一个下雪的花园。

“Whew!” said Jack. “I didn’t want to be rude. But we’re short on time.” “哇！”杰克说。 “我不想显得不礼貌。 但时间不够了。”

“I know,” said Annie. “我知道，”安妮说。

“What now?” said Jack, shivering in the cold. “那现在怎么办？”杰克在寒冷中发抖着说。

“We need to figure out exactly where we are,” said Annie, “and where the Bronx Zoo is. Maybe we can just walk there from here.” “我们需要弄清楚我们现在到底在哪里，”安妮说，“还有布朗克斯动物园在哪里。 也许我们可以从这里一直走到那里。”

“Come on, let’s get inside the tapestry room and check our research book for a map,” said Jack. “快点，我们去挂毯房间看看研究书里有没有地图，”杰克说。

They walked along the edge of the garden under a covered walkway. When they came to a door, Jack pushed it open, and he and Annie slipped out of the cold into a large, warm room. 他们沿着花园的边缘，在有顶棚的走道上走着。 当他们来到一扇门前，杰克推开门，他和安妮从寒冷中溜进了一个又大又温暖的房间。

Jack closed the door against the storm and unbuckled his briefcase to take out their research book. 杰克把门关上挡住风暴，然后解开公文包，拿出他们的研究书。

“Ohh … wow!” breathed Annie. “哦……哇！”安妮轻声说道。

“What? What?” said Jack. He looked up. “什么？什么？”杰克说。 他抬头看了看。

The walls of the room were covered with cloth hangings—tapestries shimmering with gold and silver. 房间的墙壁上挂满了布料——闪闪发光的挂毯，上面有金线和银线。

“Oh … !” whispered Jack. “哦……！”杰克小声说。

“A unicorn!” said Annie. “独角兽！”安妮说。
