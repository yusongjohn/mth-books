1. The Last Unicorn 1. 最后一只独角兽

T he November sky was gray with clouds. Jack sat reading in front of the living room fire. 杰克坐在客厅的火炉前，正在看书。

“Who wants hot chocolate?” his dad called from the kitchen. “谁想要热可可？”他爸爸从厨房里喊道。

“Me, please!” said Jack. “我想要！”杰克说。

The front door burst open, and with a gust of cold wind, Annie rushed inside. “Jack! Guess what!” she whispered. “It’s back!” 前门猛地被推开，一阵冷风吹进来，安妮冲了进来。 “杰克！ 猜猜看！”她小声说。 “它回来了！”

“How do you know?” said Jack. “你怎么知道的？”杰克说。

“I was walking home from the library”— Annie paused to catch her breath—“and I saw a flash in the sky above the woods. The last time that happened—” “我正从图书馆走路回家”—安妮停下来喘口气—“然后我看到天空中森林上空闪过一道光。” 上次发生那件事的时候——

Before she could finish the sentence, Jack jumped up. “Dad, Annie and I are going to go outside for a while!” he shouted. “Can the hot chocolate wait till we get back?” 她还没说完这句话，杰克就跳了起来。 “爸爸，安妮和我要出去一会儿！”他喊道。 “热可可能等到我们回来再喝吗？

“Sure, have fun!” their dad called from the kitchen. “当然可以，玩得开心点！”他们的爸爸从厨房里喊道。

“I have to get my pack,” Jack said to Annie. “Meet you on the porch.” “我得去拿我的背包，”杰克对安妮说。 “在门廊见。”

“Don’t forget the rhyme book!” said Annie. “别忘了带押韵书！“安妮说。

Annie slipped outside and Jack ran up to his room. He grabbed his backpack. He checked to make sure their book of magic rhymes was inside. Good, there it was. 安妮溜了出去，杰克跑上楼去他的房间。 他抓起了他的背包。 他检查了一下，确保他们的魔法押韵书在里面。 很好，它就在那儿。

Jack charged back downstairs. He pulled on his boots, put on his jacket, tied a scarf around his neck, grabbed his mittens, and headed out the door. 杰克冲下楼去。 他穿上靴子，穿上外套，围着一条围巾，拿起手套，然后走了出去。

“Come on!” said Annie. “快点！“安妮说。

Jack could see his breath in the cold air. “Brrr,” he said. “Let’s hurry!” 杰克在冷空气中看到了自己呼出的白气。 “冷死了，”他说。 “快点走吧！”，

Jack and Annie ran down the street and into the Frog Creek woods. They wove between the trees, their boots crunching through the fallen leaves. 杰克和安妮跑下街道，冲进了蛙溪镇的树林里。 他们穿梭在树之间，靴子踩着落叶发出咔嚓咔嚓的声音。

Jack stopped. The magic tree house was back. High in a tall oak tree, it was silhouetted against the gray November sky. “You were right,” he said to Annie. “Good work.” 杰克停下了。 神奇的树屋又回来了。 在高高的橡树上，它在灰蒙蒙的十一月天空中映出剪影。 “你刚才说得对，”他对安妮说。 “干得好。”

“Thanks,” Annie said. She ran to the rope ladder and started up. Jack followed her. “谢谢，”安妮说。 她跑向绳梯，开始往上爬。 杰克跟在她后面。

When they climbed inside the tree house, Jack and Annie saw a book and a scroll of parchment paper lying on the floor. Annie picked up the scroll, unrolled it, and read aloud. 当他们爬进树屋时，杰克和安妮看到一本书和一卷羊皮纸躺在地板上。 安妮捡起羊皮纸，展开后大声读了出来。

Dear Jack and Annie of Frog Creek,

I am sending you on one more mission to

prove that you can use magic wisely. This

poem will guide you. 亲爱的弗罗格克里克的杰克和安妮，我又要派你们执行一次任务，证明你们能明智地使用魔法。这首诗会指引你们。
                       —M.
The very last unicorn

Is now hidden well

By those who have put him

Under a spell. 最后一只独角兽现在被藏得很好，那些施了魔法的人把他藏起来了。
Four centuries, four decades

From that afternoon,

At the end of November,

Before the blue moon, 四百年，四十年，从那个下午开始，十一月结束前，蓝月亮出现之前，
He will wake once more

And be free to go home

If you call out his name:

Divine Flower of Rome. 只要你说出他的名字：罗马的神圣之花，他就会再次醒来，获得自由，回家去。
You must coax him to stand

Once his name is spoken.

His chain will break

And the spell, too, be broken. 你必须在他名字被说出来后，哄他站起来。他的锁链会断裂，咒语也会被打破。
Then a young girl must love him

And show him the way,

Lest he be trapped forever

On public display. 然后，一个年轻女孩必须爱他，并带他找到出路，否则他将永远被困在公众的展示中。
If he loses this chance

To rise and depart,

All magic will fade

From his horn and his heart. 如果他失去了这次机会，站起来离开，所有的魔法都会从他的角和心里消失。

“A unicorn!” breathed Annie. “I love him already. I’ll show him the way!” “一只独角兽！”，安妮惊叹道。 “我一下子就喜欢上他了。 我会带他找到正确的路！”，

“But this poem is really hard to understand,” said Jack. “What kind of research book did Morgan send us?” “但是这首诗真的很难理解，”杰克说。 “摩根给我们寄的是什么研究书啊？”

He picked up the book that had been left for them by Morgan le Fay, the librarian of Camelot. The cover showed a row of skyscrapers. The title was New York City Guide Book, 1938. 他捡起了摩根·勒·法伊留给他们的一本书，她是卡梅洛特图书馆的管理员。 封面显示着一排摩天大楼。 书名是《1938年纽约市指南》。

“New York City?” said Annie. “I love New York City! Remember the great time we had there with Aunt Mallory?” “纽约市？”安妮说。 “我爱纽约市！ 还记得我们和莫莉阿姨在那里度过的美好时光吗？”

“Yeah, I love it, too,” said Jack. “But why would there be a unicorn in New York City in 1938? A unicorn is an ancient fantasy creature. New York City’s a real place, and 1938 is not even that long ago.” “是啊，我也很喜欢那里，”杰克说。 “但是，为什么1938年的纽约市会有独角兽呢？ 独角兽是一种古老的幻想生物。 纽约市是一个真实的地方，而1938年也并不久远。”

“You’re right,” said Annie. “It sounds like a hard mission. But don’t forget we have Teddy and Kathleen’s magic rhymes to help us.” “你没错，”安妮说。 “这听起来像是一项艰巨的任务。 但别忘了，我们还有泰迪和凯瑟琳的魔法押韵来帮助我们。”

“Yeah,” said Jack. He pulled out the book given to them by their friends Teddy and Kathleen, two young enchanters of Camelot. “The problem is, we can only use each rhyme once, and we’ve already used seven out of the ten.” “对，”杰克说。 他拿出了朋友泰迪和凯瑟琳送给他们的书，泰迪和凯瑟琳是卡梅洛特的两位年轻魔法师。 “问题是，我们只能每句押韵用一次，而我们已经用了十句中的七句了。”

“Which means we still have three left,” said Annie. “What are they?” “这意味着我们还有三句没用，”安妮说。 “它们是什么？”

“Pull a Cloud from the Sky,” said Jack. “从天空中拉出一朵云，”杰克说。

“Cool,” said Annie. “太酷了，”安妮说。

“Yeah, it is,” said Jack. “But I’m not sure it will be much use.” He looked back at the book. “ Find a Treasure You Must Never Lose,” he said. “对，是的，”杰克说。 “但我不确定它会有什么用。”他回头看了看那本书。 “你必须找到一份永远不能丢失的宝藏，”他说。

“Hey, that’s a really good one!” said Annie. “The unicorn’s a treasure. So that rhyme could take care of our whole mission.” “嘿，这真是个好主意！”安妮说。 “独角兽是一份宝藏。 所以那首诗能帮我们完成整个任务。”

“But it only partly fits,” said Jack. “You could call the unicorn a treasure. But once we find him, we have to lose him. He has to go back home.” “但它只符合一部分，”杰克说。 “你可以把独角兽称作宝藏。 但我们找到他之后，又得让他离开。 他必须回家。”

“Oh, right…,” said Annie. “What else?” “哦，对了……，”安妮说。 “还有什么？”

“Your favorite,” said Jack. “ Turn into Ducks.” “你最喜欢的，”杰克说。 “变成鸭子。”

Annie laughed. “I can’t wait to use that one!” she said. 安妮笑了。 “我等不及要使用那个了！”她说。

“I hope we never use that one,” said Jack. He didn’t want to waddle around and quack like a duck. “These leftover rhymes don’t seem very helpful to me.” “希望我们永远用不上那个，”杰克说。 他不想像鸭子一样摇摇晃晃地走路，还发出嘎嘎声。 “这些剩下的押韵词对我来说好像没什么帮助。”

“Well, let’s just wait and see,” said Annie. “But now…” She held up Morgan’s research book and smiled. “那我们先等着瞧吧，”安妮说。 “但是现在……”她举起摩根的研究书，微笑着。

Jack nodded. “New York City, here we come,” he said. He pointed at the book’s cover. “I wish we could go there!” 杰克点了点头。 “我们要去纽约市了，”他说。 他指着书的封面。 “真希望我们能去那里！”
