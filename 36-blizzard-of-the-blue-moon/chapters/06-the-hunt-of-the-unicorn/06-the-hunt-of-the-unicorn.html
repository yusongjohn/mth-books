6. The Hunt of the Unicorn 6. 独角兽的狩猎

There were seven tapestries in all. Each was almost as high as the wooden ceiling. Jack read aloud from a sign on the wall under the first one: 总共有七幅挂毯。 每幅挂毯几乎和木制天花板一样高。 杰克大声读着墙上第一幅挂毯下方的牌子：

The Hunt of the Unicorn

Tapestries woven in the Netherlands

at the end of the 1400s 15世纪末在荷兰编织的独角兽挂毯的狩猎场景

The first tapestry showed hunters and hounds searching for the unicorn. The second showed the unicorn being found. 第一幅挂毯上显示的是猎人和猎犬在寻找独角兽。 第二幅挂毯上显示的是独角兽被发现。

The other tapestries showed the unicorn trying to escape, leaping from a stream, chased by hounds, then captured and slain by the hunters’ spears. 其他挂毯上显示的是独角兽试图逃跑，从溪流中跃出，被猎犬追赶，然后被猎人的长矛捕获并杀死。

Oddly, the last tapestry showed the unicorn alive again. He was sitting in a garden of flowers surrounded by a wooden fence. There was a wide blue and gold collar around his neck, and he was chained to a tree. 奇怪的是，最后一幅挂毯上显示独角兽又活过来了。 他正坐在一个花丛中，周围围着木制围栏。 他的脖子上戴着一条宽宽的蓝金相间的项圈，还被拴在一棵树上。

“That’s him,” Annie said softly. “就是他，”安妮轻声说。

“How can it be him?” said Jack. “He’s a picture in a tapestry.” “怎么会是他？”杰克说。 “他是一幅挂毯上的画。”

“Read Merlin’s poem again,” said Annie. “再读一遍梅林的诗，”安妮说。

Jack unbuckled his briefcase and pulled out the parchment scroll. He read aloud. 杰克解开了公文包的扣子，拿出卷轴。他大声读了起来。

The very last unicorn

Is now hidden well

By those who have put him

Under a spell. 最后一只独角兽现在被藏得很好，那些施了魔法的人把他藏起来了。

“The unicorn is hidden in the tapestry,” said Annie. “The people who wove it must be the ones who put him under a spell.” “织这幅挂毯的人，一定是把他施了魔法的人。”

“Hmm …,” said Jack. He read on. “嗯……，”杰克说。他继续读下去。

Four centuries, four decades

From that afternoon,

At the end of November,

Before the blue moon … 四百年，四十年，从那天下午开始，十一月底，蓝月亮出现之前……

“Stop, do the math,” said Annie. “停一下，算算看，”安妮说。

“Right,” said Jack. He took out his notebook and pencil. “Okay, four centuries is four hundred, and four decades is forty. Add ’em up, you get four hundred forty. Then if you subtract four hundred forty from 1938, you get… 1498.” “对，”杰克说。 他拿出他的笔记本和铅笔。 “好吧，四个世纪就是四百，四个十年就是四十。 加起来的话，就是四百四十。 然后如果你从1938里减去四百四十，你得到……1498。”

“It works!” said Annie. “The sign says the tapestries were woven at the end of the 1400s! And it’s the end of November, and Mr. Perkins said there’s a blue moon tonight!” “成功了！”安妮说。 “牌子上写着这些挂毯是在1400年代末织成的！” 而且现在是十一月底，而且先生。 珀金斯说今晚有蓝月亮！

“Oh, man,” whispered Jack. He kept reading. “哦，天啊，”杰克小声说。他继续读着。

He will wake once more

And be free to go home

If you call out his name:

Divine Flower of Rome. 他会在你呼唤他的名字时再次醒来，然后可以自由回家。他的名字是：罗马的神圣之花。

Annie looked up at the tapestry. “ Divine Flower!” she called. 安妮抬头看着挂毯。 “Divine Flower!”她喊道。

Nothing happened. 什么也没发生。

“Divine Flower of Rome!” Jack called. “罗马的神圣之花！”杰克喊道。

Jack and Annie watched and waited. Nothing changed in the tapestry. It looked exactly the same. 杰克和安妮看着，等着。 挂毯上没有任何变化。 看起来和以前一模一样。

“Maybe he’s not the right unicorn after all,” said Jack. “也许他根本就不是合适的独角兽，”杰克说。

“Maybe it’s just not the right name,” said Annie. “Read the rest.” “也许这不是个合适的名字，”安妮说。 “读完剩下的。”

Jack read more of the poem. 杰克继续读诗。

You must coax him to stand

Once his name is spoken.

His chain will break

And the spell, too, be broken. 你必须在他名字被说出来后，哄他站起来。他的锁链会断裂，咒语也会被打破。

“He is the right unicorn!” said Annie. “See? There’s the chain!” She pointed to the chain in the last tapestry. “看到了吗？ 那里有锁链！”她指着最后一幅挂毯里的锁链。

“Yeah, but why didn’t calling his name work?” said Jack. “Why didn’t he wake up?” “是啊，但是为什么叫他的名字没用呢？”杰克说。 “为什么他没醒过来？”

“I don’t know,” said Annie. “What else does the poem say?” “我不知道，”安妮说。 “这首诗还说了什么？”

Jack read on. 杰克继续读下去。

Then a young girl must love him

And show him the way,

Lest he be trapped forever

On public display. 然后，一个年轻女孩必须爱他，并带他找到出路，否则他将永远被困在公众的展示中。
If he loses this chance

To rise and depart,

All magic will fade

From his horn and his heart. 如果他失去了这次机会，站起来离开，所有的魔法都会从他的角和他的心里消失。

“He is on public display, and I’ m the girl, Jack!” said Annie. “I love him a lot! I’ll show him the way!” “我非常爱他！ 我带他去路！”

“Okay, calm down. First we need to wake him up,” said Jack. “好，冷静点。 我们得先叫醒他，”杰克说。

Voices came from outside. Jack moved to a window. He looked out into the courtyard. Two people were coming through the cloister, their heads down against the flying snow. One wore a dark cape, and the other a tan raincoat. 外面传来声音。 杰克走到窗边。 他朝庭院外望去。 有两个人正穿过回廊，低着头，抵挡着飞舞的雪花。 一个人穿着深色斗篷，另一个人穿着棕色雨衣。

Jack turned back to Annie, a big grin on his face. “You were right! They found us! Teddy and 杰克转身对安妮笑了笑，脸上露出大大的笑容。 “你猜对了！他们找到我们了！ 泰迪和

Kathleen are here!” he said. “ They’ ll know how to break the spell!” 凯瑟琳在这里！”他说道。 “他们会知道如何打破这个咒语！”，

“Of course!” said Annie, beaming. “Quick, let’s hide and surprise them for a change.” “当然！”安妮笑着说。 “快，我们躲起来，这次换他们惊喜一下。”

“In there!” said Jack. He and Annie hurried into a long room off the tapestry room. They heard the door from the cloister open. They felt a rush of cold air. They heard footsteps. “在里面！”杰克说。 他和安妮赶紧走进挂毯房间旁边的一间长房间。 他们听到修道院的门被打开了。 他们感到一阵冷风扑面而来。 他们听到了脚步声。

Jack and Annie grinned at each other. Jack put his finger to his lips. Then a quick, nervous boy’s voice came from the tapestry room: “Grinda, they are not here!” 杰克和安妮互相笑了笑。 杰克把手指放在嘴唇上。 然后，从挂毯房间传来一个又快又紧张的男孩的声音：“格里达，他们不在这里！”，

“I see that, Balor, but look—” “我看到了，巴洛，但你看——”，

“Ahh! Is it him, Grinda?” 是他吗，格里达？

Annie grabbed Jack’s arm. “Balor? Grinda?” she whispered. 安妮抓住了杰克的手臂。 “巴洛？ 格里达？”她小声说。

“Shh,” whispered Jack. “嘘——”，杰克小声说。

“Of course ’tis him!” said the girl. “I told you those Frog Creek brats would lead us to him! Get the rope ready!” “当然是他！”女孩说。 “我告诉过你，那些蛙溪镇的顽童一定会带我们找到他！ 准备好绳子！

“Aye,” said the boy. “啊，”男孩说。

Jack and Annie carefully peeked around the corner. They saw a girl and boy standing with their backs to them, facing the unicorn in the last tapestry. The boy held a thick black rope. 杰克和安妮小心地绕过角落。 他们看到一个女孩和男孩背对着他们，正面对着最后一幅挂毯里的独角兽。 男孩手里拿着一根粗粗的黑绳子。

“Call out his name, Grinda,” said the boy. “喊出他的名字，格里达，”男孩说。

The girl took a step toward the tapestry. She raised her arms in front of the unicorn. “ Dianthus!” she called. 女孩向前走了一步，朝挂毯走去。 她举起双臂，挡在独角兽面前。 “迪安瑟斯！”她喊道。

Wind whistled through the open doorway. The flowers in the tapestry swayed as if the wind were blowing them. The scent of roses wafted through the room. The unicorn moved his head. 风从敞开的门口呼啸而过。 挂毯上的花朵随风摇曳，仿佛真的被风吹动了。 玫瑰的香气弥漫在整个房间里。 独角兽动了动他的头。

“Ohh!” whispered Annie. “哦！”安妮小声说道。

“Get ready, Balor, to take him back to the Master,” said the girl. “巴洛，准备好带他回去见主人吧，”女孩说道。

Annie clutched Jack’s arm. “Who’s the Master?” she whispered. 安妮紧紧抓住杰克的手臂。 “主人是谁？”她小声问。

“I don’t know,” whispered Jack. “But I don’t think he’s a good guy.” “我不知道，”杰克小声回答。 “但我觉得他不是个好人。”

The girl turned back to the tapestry and spoke softly to the unicorn. “Come, come, my lovely Dianthus, stand up now. Come out of that old rug….” 女孩转身面对挂毯，轻声对独角兽说话。 “来吧，来吧，我亲爱的迪安瑟，现在站起来吧。 从那张旧地毯上出来……”

The unicorn turned his head and looked out at the girl. The look in his blue eyes was ancient yet young, wise yet innocent. He lifted his head as if he were about to stand. 独角兽转过头，看着女孩。 他蓝色眼睛里的神情古老却又年轻，睿智却又天真。 他抬起脑袋，好像马上就要站起来了。

The girl nodded to the boy. The boy slowly coiled the black rope into a noose. The girl looked at the unicorn again. “Come to me, Dianthus!” she coaxed. “Don’t be afraid. I’ll love you and show you the way—” 女孩朝男孩点了点头。 男孩慢慢地把黑色的绳子缠成一个套索。 女孩再次看向独角兽。 “到我这儿来，迪安瑟！”她轻声劝道。 “别害怕。” “我会爱你，带你找到路——”

“No, Dianthus!” yelled Annie. “Don’t go to her!” Annie and Jack bolted from their hiding place. “不要去她那儿！”安妮喊道。 “别靠近她！”安妮和杰克从藏身的地方冲了出来。

Balor and Grinda whirled around in surprise. They both had pale faces and pale shining eyes. 巴洛和格里达惊讶地转过身来。 他们俩都有苍白的脸和闪亮的苍白眼睛。

“Leave him alone!” Annie yelled at the strange pair. “You don’t love him! We love him!” “别碰他！”安妮对着那对奇怪的人喊道。 “你们不爱他！我们才爱他！”

At that moment, there was a flash of light, and the unicorn in the tapestry leapt like a deer over the woven fence. Balor and Grinda shrieked and jumped back. Jack covered his head. 就在这时，一道亮光闪过，挂毯上的独角兽像鹿一样跳过了编织的围栏。 巴洛和格里达尖叫着往后跳。 杰克用手捂住了头。

Then there was silence. Jack looked up. The tapestry on the wall was unchanged—it still showed a unicorn woven from yarn chained to a tree. 然后，周围一片安静。杰克抬起头。 墙上那幅挂毯一点都没变——它仍然显示着一只由毛线编织成的独角兽，被锁链拴在一棵树上。

But standing on the floor of the museum was the most beautiful creature Jack had ever seen. 但站在博物馆地板上的，是杰克见过的最美丽的生物。
