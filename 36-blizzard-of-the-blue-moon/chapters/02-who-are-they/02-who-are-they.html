2. Who Are They? 2. 他们是谁？

S now blew into the tree house. 雪花飘进了树屋。

Jack and Annie wore wool coats, hats, and mittens. Jack’s canvas backpack had turned into a leather briefcase with buckles and a shoulder strap. Jack and Annie looked out the window. 杰克和安妮穿上了羊毛外套、帽子和毛线手套。 杰克的帆布背包变成了一个有扣子和肩带的皮质公文包。 杰克和安妮望向窗外。

Below the tree house was a wide, snow-covered field that ended in a wall of evergreen trees. Beyond the trees was a city skyline. 树屋下面是一片又宽又大的雪地，雪地尽头是一堵常青树墙。 树的后面是一片城市的天际线。

“This is definitely New York,” said Annie. “See the Empire State Building? Remember our visit to the top?” She pointed to a faraway building that rose above the others. “This must be Central Park. I remember that big field.” “这肯定是纽约，”安妮说。 “看见帝国大厦了吗？ 还记得我们去顶上玩吗？”她指着远处一座高高耸立的建筑物。 “这一定是中央公园。 我记得那片大草地。”

“Yeah, I do, too,” said Jack. “But we’ve landed in New York in 1938. It was different back then.” He opened their research book and read from the introduction: “是啊，我也记得，”杰克说。 “但我们降落在1938年的纽约。 那时候和现在不一样。”他打开他们的研究书，读起了引言：

The city of New York is the largest city in the Western Hemisphere. It covers an area of 322 square miles. 纽约市是西半球最大的城市，面积有322平方英里。

Jack closed the book. “Whoa. Even in 1938, New York was a huge city,” he said. “This is going to be like looking for a needle in a haystack.” 杰克合上了书。 “哇。 即使在1938年，纽约也是一个很大的城市，”他说。 “这就像在干草堆里找针一样困难。”

“I’ll read our mission poem again,” said Annie. She read the first verse aloud: “我要再读一遍我们的任务诗，”安妮说。 她大声读出第一段：

The very last unicorn

Is now hidden well

By those who have put him

Under a spell. 最后一只独角兽现在被藏得很好，那些施了魔法的人把他藏起来了。

“Okay, so this unicorn was put under a spell,” said Jack, “and he must be hidden somewhere in New York, or Merlin wouldn’t have sent us here.” “好吧，这只独角兽被施了魔法，”杰克说，“它一定藏在纽约的某个地方，否则梅林不会派我们来这里。”

“Right,” said Annie. She read the next verse: “对，”安妮说。 她读了下一段：

Four centuries, four decades

From that afternoon,

At the end of November,

Before the blue moon, 四百年，四十年，从那个下午开始，十一月的最后一天，蓝月亮之前，

“What’s a blue moon?” asked Annie, looking up. “I’ve heard that expression before.” “什么是蓝月亮？”安妮抬头问。 “我以前听过这句话。”

“It’s when you have two full moons in the same month,” said Jack. “It doesn’t happen very often.” “这是指一个月里出现两次满月的时候，”杰克说。 “这种情况不常发生。”

“Oh,” said Annie. She read on: “哦，”安妮说。她继续读下去：

He will wake once more

And be free to go home

If you call out his name:

Divine Flower of Rome. 只要你说出他的名字：罗马的神圣之花，他就会再次醒来，获得自由，回家去。

“Wait, does that mean the unicorn’s name is Divine Flower of Rome?” asked Jack. “等等，这意思是独角兽的名字是‘罗马的神圣之花’吗？”杰克问。

“I guess,” said Annie. She read on: “我想是吧，”安妮说。她继续读下去：

You must coax him to stand

Once his name is spoken.

His chain will break

And the spell, too, be broken. 你必须叫出他的名字，才能让他站起来。一旦他的名字被说出来，他的锁链就会断裂，咒语也会被打破。
Then a young girl must love him

And show him the way,

Lest he be trapped forever

On public display. 然后，一个年轻女孩必须爱他，并带他找到出路，否则他将永远被困在公众的展示中。
If he loses this chance

To rise and depart,

All magic will fade

From his horn and his heart. 如果他失去了这次机会，站起来离开，所有的魔法都会从他的角和心里消失。

“So I’ m the young girl!” said Annie. “And I have to help him get home, or his magic will fade away forever!” “所以我是那个小女孩！”安妮说。 “我必须帮助他回家，否则他的魔法就会永远消失！”

“Right,” said Jack. “Okay. Let’s review: There’s a unicorn on public display somewhere in New York City. He’s under a spell. The spell runs out in late November before a blue moon. But he’ll only wake up when someone calls his name, which is Divine Flower of Rome. Then a young girl— you— must love him and show him the way home.” “对，”杰克说。 “好的。” 让我们复习一下：在纽约市的某个地方，有一只独角兽正在公开展出。 他被施了魔法。 这个魔法会在11月下旬的蓝月之前失效。 但只有当有人叫他的名字——‘罗马的神圣花朵’时，他才会醒来。 然后，一个年轻女孩——你——必须去爱他，并带他回家。

“Great,” said Annie. “Let’s get started.” “太好了，”安妮说。 “我们开始吧。”

“Get started? How?” said Jack. “开始？怎么开始？”杰克说。

“Maybe we should talk to some New Yorkers,” said Annie. “We can ask them if they know anything about a unicorn in New York City.” She looked out the window. “There’re some people in the park right now.” “也许我们应该和一些纽约人谈谈，”安妮说。 “我们可以问问他们是否知道纽约市有独角兽的事。”她看向窗外。 “公园里现在有人。”

Jack looked out. Through the falling snow, he saw girls crossing the field carrying skates. He saw two people standing on top of a small hill. One wore a cape, and the other a long raincoat. 杰克往外看了看。 透过飘落的雪花，他看见一些女孩正穿过田野，手里拿着冰刀。 他看见有两个人站在一个小山丘上。 一个人穿着斗篷，另一个人穿着一件长雨衣。

“If we start asking people about unicorns, they’ll think we’re crazy,” said Jack. “如果我们开始问别人关于独角兽的事情，他们会以为我们疯了，”杰克说。

“Who cares?” said Annie. “Maybe someone will at least know something that can help us. Let’s go down.” She started down the ladder. “谁在乎呢？”安妮说。 “也许有人至少知道一些能帮上我们的事情。 我们下去吧。”她开始往下爬梯子。

Jack quickly packed up their books. He buckled his bag and followed her. When they stepped onto the ground, Jack and Annie looked around. The skaters were gone. The two people on the hill were gone, too. 杰克赶紧收拾他们的书。 他系好背包，跟着她走了。 当他们踏上地面时，杰克和安妮环顾四周。 滑冰的人不见了。 山上的两个人也不见了。

“Where’d everybody go?” said Jack. “大家都去哪儿了？”杰克说。

“I don’t know. But we’ll find someone else. Come on,” said Annie. “我不知道。 但我们一定能找到其他人。 来吧，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie started across the wide field. The snow was falling fast and furiously. The wind blew harder. 杰克和安妮开始穿过广阔的田野。 雪花快速而猛烈地飘落下来。 风刮得更猛了。

“Look, a frozen pond,” said Annie, pointing. “That must be where the skaters were coming from.” “看，结冰的池塘，”安妮指着说。 “那一定是滑冰的人来的方向。”

Wet snow stuck to Jack’s glasses. He wiped them off so he could see. No one was skating on the pond now. Whirlwinds of powdery snow swirled over the ice. 湿雪粘在杰克的眼镜上。 他擦掉眼镜，好让自己看得清楚。 现在池塘上没有人滑冰了。 一团团粉末状的雪在冰面上旋转着飘舞。

“Keep going,” said Jack. “继续走，”杰克说。

They kept trudging through the snow. 他们继续在雪中艰难地走着。

“Hey, look! Remember that?” said Annie. “嘿，快看！ 还记得吗？”安妮说。

“What?” said Jack. He wiped the snow off his glasses again. He saw a merry-go-round. “Oh, yeah.” On their trip to New York with their aunt Mallory, they’d taken a ride on it. But now the riderless painted horses looked sad and lonely. “什么？”杰克说。 他又擦了擦眼镜上的雪。 他看见了一个旋转木马。 “哦，对啊。”在他们和姑姑马洛里去纽约旅行时，他们曾经坐过它。 但现在，那些没有骑手的彩绘马看起来很伤心、很孤独。

“I feel like we’re the last living creatures in Central Park,” said Jack. “我觉得我们好像是中央公园里最后活着的生物，”杰克说。

“Which way do we go now?” asked Annie. “Which way did we come from?” “我们现在该往哪走？”安妮问。 “我们是从哪边来的？”

It was hard to see anything in the white haze of the falling snow. The buildings bordering the park and the skyscrapers in the distance had disappeared. 在飘落的白雪造成的白色雾霭中，很难看清任何东西。 公园边上的建筑物和远处的摩天大楼都消失了。

“Let’s see what our book says,” said Jack. He unbuckled his leather briefcase and pulled out their research book. He found a picture of Central Park and read: “我们来看看书上怎么说，”杰克说。 他解开了皮质公文包的扣子，拿出他们的研究书。 他找到了一张中央公园的图片，然后读道：

Central Park is a vast natural area in the middle of New York City. The park has thirty-two miles of winding footpaths and covers 840 acres of land. It has rock formations, woods, and many bodies of water. It also has— 中央公园是纽约市中间的一个很大的自然区域。公园有三十英里蜿蜒的小路，占地840英亩。那里有岩石、树林和许多水体。它还有—

“Okay, okay, got it,” said Annie. “It’s a big park. Is there a map that shows where we are?” “好了好了，我知道了，”安妮说。 “这是一个很大的公园。 有没有一张地图能告诉我们我们现在在哪里？”

Jack tried to read from the index, but the wind and snow made it impossible. He stuffed the book back into his briefcase. “Forget it,” he said. “Let’s just try to get out of the park.” 杰克试着从目录里找信息，但风雪让这变得不可能。 他把书又塞回了公文包里。 “算了吧，”他说。 “我们还是先想办法离开公园吧。”

Jack and Annie turned away from the lonely merry-go-round and walked across the snow. A gust of wind blew Jack’s cap off. As he turned around to grab it, he saw a couple walking a short distance behind them. 杰克和安妮转身离开那个孤零零的旋转木马，走过雪地。 一阵风吹掉了杰克的帽子。 他转身去捡帽子时，看见一对人正走在他们后面不远的地方。

The two people looked like teenagers. They were walking with their heads bowed against the wind. The girl wore a dark cape with a hood. The boy wore a hat and a tan raincoat with a belt. 那两个人看起来像是青少年。 他们低着头，迎着风走。 女孩穿着一件深色的斗篷，戴着兜帽。 男孩戴着帽子，穿着一件棕色的雨衣，系着腰带。

“Hey, look—” Jack said to Annie. But at that moment, the wind shook the trees, and big clumps of snow fell from the branches. Jack and Annie ducked and covered their heads. When the wind died down, Jack looked around for the couple. “嘿，你看——”杰克对安妮说。 但就在那时，风摇晃了树，一大团雪从树枝上掉了下来。 杰克和安妮低下头，用手护住脑袋。 当风停了，杰克四处寻找那对夫妇。

“They’re gone now,” he said. “他们现在不见了，”他说。

“Who’s gone?” said Annie. “Who did you see?” “谁不见了？”安妮问。 “你看到的是谁？”

“Two teenagers, I think,” said Jack. “A boy and a girl. We saw them earlier from the tree house. I think they might be following us.” “我想是两个年轻人，”杰克说。 “一个男孩，一个女孩。 我们之前从树屋里看到过他们。 我觉得他们可能在跟踪我们。”

“Wait a second,” said Annie. “Two teenagers? A boy and a girl? Following us? Who does that remind you of?” “等一下，”安妮说。 “两个青少年？一个男孩和一个女孩？ 在跟踪我们？ 这让你想起谁？”

A big smile crossed Jack’s face. “Teddy and Kathleen?” he said. 杰克脸上露出一个大大的笑容。 “泰迪和凯瑟琳？”他说。

“Think about it,” said Annie. “On our last three missions, it seemed like Teddy and “想想看，”安妮说。 “在我们上次的三次任务中，似乎泰迪和

Kathleen were always nearby, wearing disguises, ready to help us when we needed it.” 凯瑟琳总是附近，穿着伪装服，准备在我们需要的时候帮助我们。”

“Right,” said Jack. “We could sure use their help now.” He turned around. “Hello!” he shouted. “对，”杰克说。 “我们现在确实需要他们的帮助。”他转过身。 “你好！”他喊道。

“Hello!” Annie yelled. “你好！”安妮大喊。

But the wind answered them with a howl. More clumps of snow blew down on Jack and Annie. 但风用一声嚎叫回应了他们。 更多的雪块被吹向杰克和安妮。

“Let’s get going,” said Annie. “They’ll find us sooner or later. They always do.” “我们得赶快出发了，”安妮说。 “他们迟早会找到我们的。他们总是会的。”
