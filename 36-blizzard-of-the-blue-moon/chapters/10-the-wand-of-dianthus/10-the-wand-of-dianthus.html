10. The Wand of Dianthus 10. 迪安瑟斯的魔杖

Jack, Annie, Teddy, and Kathleen laughed. 杰克、安妮、泰迪和凯瑟琳笑了。

“My,” said Kathleen. “I believe I would much rather ride Dianthus than go with Merlin!” “我的，”凯瑟琳说。 “我想我宁愿骑迪安瑟斯，也不愿和梅林一起去！”，

The white unicorn knelt in the snow. Kathleen and Teddy climbed onto his back. Dianthus stood up. 白色的独角兽在雪中跪下。 凯瑟琳和泰迪爬上了他的背。 迪安瑟斯站了起来。

Teddy smiled down at Jack and Annie. “You know ’tis a very great honor to be given the Wand of Dianthus,” he said. 泰迪朝杰克和安妮微笑着看下来。 “你知道，能被授予迪安瑟斯的权杖是一件非常大的荣誉，”他说。

“I know,” said Jack shyly. “Thanks for getting us to the right place at the right time today.” “我知道，”杰克害羞地说。 “谢谢你们今天在正确的时间把我们带到正确的地方。”

“Hey, were you guys in Venice, looking out for us there, too?” said Annie. “And Baghdad? And Paris?” “嘿，你们之前在威尼斯是不是也在帮我们留意着？”安妮说。 “那巴格达呢？还有巴黎呢？”

The two young enchanters looked at one another. Then they nodded their heads. 两个年轻的魔法师互相看了看。 然后他们点了点头。

“We knew it!” said Annie. “Thanks for helping us!” “我们知道的！”安妮说道。 “谢谢你们帮我们！”

“And thanks for the book of magic rhymes,” said Jack. “还有谢谢你们给的魔法韵文书，”杰克说道。

“You are most welcome,” said Teddy. “And now we must go. We hope to see you again soon.” “你们真是太客气了，”泰迪说道。 “现在我们必须走了。 希望很快能再见到你们。”

“Good-bye, Dianthus,” said Annie. She stroked the unicorn’s neck one last time. “再见了，迪安瑟斯，”安妮说道。 她最后一次抚摸独角兽的脖子。

Dianthus lowered his head and stared at her with his soft blue eyes. They sparkled in the last bit of daylight. Annie stood on her toes and whispered in the unicorn’s ear. Then she stepped back. 迪安瑟低下头，用他柔和的蓝眼睛盯着她。 它们在最后一点 daylight 中闪闪发光。 安妮踮起脚尖，在独角兽的耳边轻声细语。 然后她退后一步。

Dianthus snorted. He raised his head. Then he leapt forward. In a flash of silver, the unicorn and his two riders were gone. 迪安瑟喷出一口气。 他抬起头。 然后他向前一跃。 一道银光闪过，独角兽和它的两个骑手消失了。

Standing in the cold dusk, Annie stared silently into the distance. 站在寒冷的暮色中，安妮默默地望向远方。

“What did you say to him?” asked Jack. “你跟他说了什么？”杰克问。

“I told him he had to go with Teddy and Kathleen,” Annie said. She blinked back tears. “I told him they would show him the way home now.” “我告诉他他必须和泰迪、凯瑟琳一起走，”安妮说。 她强忍住泪水。 “我告诉他，现在他们会带他回家。”

“Oh,” said Jack. He put his hand on Annie’s back. “Don’t worry. We’ll see Dianthus again someday. I just feel it.” “哦，”杰克说。 他把手放在安妮的背上。 “别担心。 总有一天我们会再见到迪安瑟斯。我就是有这种感觉。”

Annie smiled. “You’re starting to sound like me,” she said. 安妮笑了。 “你开始听起来像我了，”她说。

“Uh-oh,” said Jack. He shivered. Night was falling fast. “Ready?” “哎呀，”杰克说。他打了个哆嗦。 天色很快就暗下来了。 “准备好了吗？”，

“Sure, let’s go,” said Annie. She followed Jack to the tree house and up the rope ladder. They climbed inside and looked out the window. “当然，我们走吧，”安妮说。 她跟着杰克来到树屋，然后爬上了绳梯。 他们爬了进去，从窗户往外看。

The lights of New York City were starting to come on. A full moon was rising over the snow-covered park. 纽约市的灯光开始亮起来了。 一个满月正在雪覆盖的公园上空升起。

“Hello, blue moon,” said Jack. “你好，蓝月亮，”杰克说。

“Good-bye, blue moon,” said Annie. “再见，蓝月亮，”安妮说。

Jack picked up the scroll from Merlin. He pointed to the words Frog Creek in Merlin’s note. “I wish we could go home!” he said. 杰克从梅林那里捡起了卷轴。 他指着梅林笔记上写着的‘蛙溪镇’。 “我想回家！”他说。

The wind started to blow. 风开始吹起来了。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。

* * *

A cold wind blew through the Frog Creek woods. A few fat snowflakes drifted into the tree house. Jack and Annie were dressed in their own clothes again. Jack’s briefcase had turned back into a backpack. 一股冷风吹过了蛙溪镇的森林。 几片胖胖的雪花飘进了树屋。 杰克和安妮又穿上了自己的衣服。 杰克的公文包又变回了背包。

Jack quickly opened the pack and looked inside. “Good,” he said. “The Wand of Dianthus is still there.” 杰克赶紧打开背包，往里面看。 “好，”他说。 “迪安瑟斯的魔杖还在里面。”

“Should we take it home with us?” asked Annie. “我们要把它带回家吗？”安妮问。

“I think so,” said Jack. “We can keep it safe until our next mission.” He pulled Teddy and Kathleen’s book of rhymes out of his pack. “我想是的，”杰克说。 “我们可以把它保管好，直到下一次任务。”,他从背包里拿出泰迪和凯瑟琳的押韵书。

“I guess we can keep our book of rhymes as a souvenir,” Jack said. “We won’t be using it anymore since we’ve used up all the rhymes.” He stuffed the book back into his backpack. “我想我们可以把押韵书留作纪念，”杰克说。 “既然我们已经用完了所有的押韵，以后也不会再用它了。”,他把书塞回背包里。

“We haven’t used all of them,” said Annie. “We still have one left, remember? Find a Treasure You Must Never Lose.” “我们还没用完它们，”安妮说。 “我们还有一个没用，还记得吗？ “你必须永远不要丢失的宝藏。”

“Oh, I already used that one,” said Jack. “Come on, let’s go.” He grabbed his pack and started down the rope ladder. “哦，我已经用过那个了，”杰克说。 “快点，我们走吧。”他拿起背包，开始顺着绳梯往下爬。

“What do you mean you already used that one?” Annie said as she followed him down. “When did you use that rhyme?” “你什么意思，你已经用过那个了？”安妮边跟着他往下走边说。 “你什么时候用的那首童谣？”

Jack stepped onto the ground. “How do you think I found you when you got lost in Central Park?” he said. 杰克踩到了地上。 “你觉得我怎么在你迷路到中央公园时找到你的？”他说。

“Wait, you thought I was a treasure?” said Annie. “等等，你认为我是一个宝藏？”安妮说。

Jack shrugged. “I guess,” he said. “At least today I did.” 杰克耸了耸肩。 “我想是吧，”他说。 “至少今天我做到了。”

Annie smiled. “Cool,” she said. “Thanks for finding me when you got lost.” 安妮笑了。 “太酷了，”她说。 “谢谢你在我迷路的时候找到我。”

“Not me, you,” said Jack. “You’re the one who got lost.” “不是我，是你，”杰克说。 “是你迷路了。”

“No, you,” said Annie. “不是你，是我，”安妮说。

“You,” said Jack. “是你，”杰克说。

“Youyouyouyouyou!” said Annie. “你你你你你！”安妮说道。

Jack laughed. “Whatever,” he said. “Let’s go have some of Dad’s hot chocolate.” 杰克笑了。 “随你便，”他说。 “我们去喝点爸爸的热可可吧。”

The snow began to fall harder. As the cold wind rattled the bare trees of the Frog Creek woods, Jack and Annie hurried home. 雪花开始下得更大了。 寒风呼啸着，吹得蛙溪镇森林里的光秃秃的树嘎吱作响，杰克和安妮赶紧往家跑。
