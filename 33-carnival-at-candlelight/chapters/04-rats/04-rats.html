4. Rats! 4. 啊呀！老鼠！

J ack found Annie standing behind a column in a lantern-lit courtyard. The courtyard was quiet and empty. “Everyone in Venice must be at Carnival,” said Jack. “I just hope the ruler is home.” 杰克在灯笼照亮的庭院里发现安妮正躲在一根柱子后面。 庭院里安静又空荡荡的。 “威尼斯的每个人一定都去参加狂欢节了，”杰克说。 “我希望统治者在家。”

“Yeah, we’ll ask him if he knows the Grand Lady of the Lagoon,” said Annie. “And we’ll tell him he has to help us save her from a terrible disaster.” “对，我们会问他是否知道湖畔贵妇人。”安妮说。 “我们得告诉他，他必须帮我们把她从一场可怕的灾难中救出来。”

Jack looked at his map of the palace. Several rooms were labeled Ruler’s Living Chambers. “ I guess that’s where he lives,” said Jack. “We have to go up some stairs called the Giants’ Stairs to get there.” 杰克看着他手里的宫殿地图。 有几个房间标着‘统治者的起居室’。 “我想那就是他住的地方，”杰克说。 “我们要走一段叫‘巨人楼梯’的楼梯才能到那里。”

“The Giants’ Stairs?” said Annie. “巨人楼梯？”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. “Listen to this.” “对，”杰克说。 “听我说。”

These stairs are called the Giants’ Stairs because they are guarded by two large statues of gods from Roman mythology: Mars, the god of war, and Neptune, the god of the sea. 这些台阶叫做巨人台阶，因为它们由两个巨大的雕像守护着，这两个雕像来自罗马神话中的神：战神马尔斯和海神涅普顿。

“Cool,” said Annie. “Let’s go.” “太酷了，”安妮说。 “我们走吧。”

Jack and Annie hurried down the passageway that ran along the courtyard until they came to a wide staircase. On either side of the stairs were giant marble statues of strong-looking men. 杰克和安妮赶紧沿着庭院旁边的通道跑下去，直到他们来到一座宽阔的楼梯前。 楼梯的两边站着巨大的大理石雕像，看起来都是很强壮的男士。

“Mars and Neptune,” said Jack. “This is it. Come on.” “这是火星和海王星，”杰克说。 “就是这里，快走吧。”

Jack and Annie quickly climbed the Giants’ Stairs. At the top, Jack looked at the map again. “Now we turn right and head for the Golden Staircase,” he said. 杰克和安妮迅速爬上了巨人楼梯。 到了顶部，杰克又看了看地图。 “现在我们要向右转，去金色楼梯那里，”他说。

Keeping an eye out for more guards, they crept down a hall until they came to a fancy staircase under a gold ceiling. 他们一边留意着更多的卫兵，一边悄悄沿着一条走廊爬行，直到来到一座金灿灿天花板下的华丽楼梯前。

“There it is,” said Jack. “Let’s climb up.” He and Annie hurried up the Golden Staircase. When they got to the top, they froze. Another guard was slouched against the wall by the stairs. His eyes were closed, and he was snoring softly. “那就是金色楼梯，”杰克说。 “我们上去吧。”他和安妮赶紧爬上了金色楼梯。 他们到了顶部时，一下子停住了。 另一个守卫靠在楼梯旁边的墙上，懒洋洋地坐着。 他眼睛闭着，还轻轻打着鼾。

Jack motioned to Annie, and they tiptoed past the sleeping guard to the entrance of the ruler’s living chambers. Jack glanced at the map. “This is it,” he whispered. 杰克朝安妮做了个手势，他们悄悄地从睡着的守卫身边溜到统治者居住的房间入口处。 杰克看了看地图。 “就是这里，”他小声说。

The door was open. Jack and Annie peeked inside. “Knock, knock?” Annie said in a soft voice. 门是开着的。 杰克和安妮偷偷往里面看了看。 “有人吗？”安妮用轻柔的声音说。

No one answered. 没有人回答。

They stepped through the doorway. A fire blazed on the hearth. Overhead many candles burned brightly. The dancing flames cast shadows on a marble floor and a carved gold ceiling. 他们走进了门。 壁炉里火光熊熊。 头顶上，许多蜡烛明亮地燃烧着。 跳动的火光在大理石地板和雕刻着金色图案的天花板上投下影子。

“I have a feeling the ruler’s not here,” said Annie. “Maybe we should leave.” “我觉得统治者不在这里，”安妮说。 “也许我们应该离开。”

Jack looked at their book. “Wait, the next room is the Map Room,” he said. “Let’s just take a look.” 杰克看了看他们的书。 “等等，下个房间是地图室，”他说。 “我们去看看吧。”

“Okay, but we’d better hurry,” said Annie. “好吧，但我们得快点，”安妮说。

Jack led the way into the Map Room. Colorful maps hung on the walls. In the middle of the floor were two huge globes. Jack sighed. “I love this room,” he said. 杰克带头走进了地图室。 墙上挂着色彩鲜艳的地图。 地板中央放着两个巨大的地球仪。杰克叹了口气。 “我最喜欢这个房间，”他说。

“Look, more lions,” said Annie. She pointed to three paintings of winged lions on one of the walls. “Why are there lions with wings everywhere?” “看，还有更多的狮子，”安妮说。 她指着墙上的一幅画，画上有三只长着翅膀的狮子。 “为什么到处都是长着翅膀的狮子呢？”

Jack looked up winged lions in their book. He turned to the right page and read: 杰克在他们的书里查找有翅膀的狮子。 他翻到右边的那一页，开始读起来：

The winged lion is the symbol of Venice. Represented in paintings and sculptures all over the city, the lions stand for strength on both land and sea. 带翅膀的狮子是威尼斯的象征。在全市的绘画和雕塑中都有它的身影，代表着陆地和海洋上的力量。

As Jack and Annie looked back up at the lion paintings, they heard footsteps. The grouchy guard and the sleepy guard rushed into the room. 当杰克和安妮再次抬头看那些狮子的画时，他们听到了脚步声。 脾气暴躁的守卫和打瞌睡的守卫冲进了房间。

“Hi, we’re looking—” began Annie. “你好，我们在找—”安妮说。

“There they are! The thieves!” the sleepy guard shouted at the grouchy guard. “I told you I heard voices!” “他们在这儿！ “他们是小偷！”打瞌睡的守卫对脾气暴躁的守卫喊道。 “我告诉过你我听到声音了！

“We’re not thieves,” said Annie. “We were just looking for your ruler to ask for his help.” “我们不是小偷，”安妮说。 “我们只是在找你们的国王，想请他帮忙。”

“She’s right,” said Jack. “We have to tell him that—” “她说得对，”杰克说。 “我们必须告诉他—”

“Won’t admit your crime, eh?” said the grouchy guard. “The worst cells are reserved for criminals like you! Move!” “不承认自己的罪行，是吗？”脾气暴躁的守卫说。 “最糟糕的牢房是留给像你这样的罪犯的！走！”

“But we—” started Annie. “但是我们—”安妮开始说。

“Move!” shouted the grouchy guard, raising his rifle and pointing to the door. “走！”脾气暴躁的守卫吼道，举起步枪，指着门。

Jack knew there was no use arguing. He took Annie’s hand and led her out of the ruler’s living chambers. The two guards walked behind them, pointing guns at their backs. 杰克知道争论是没有用的。 他牵起安妮的手，带她走出了统治者的起居室。 两名守卫在他们后面走着，枪口对着他们的背。

“To the end of the hall and down the steps!” growled the grouchy guard. “走到走廊尽头，然后下台阶！”脾气暴躁的守卫咆哮道。

Jack and Annie walked quickly down the hall, then down some steep, narrow steps. They moved through a low stone corridor, the guards close behind them. 杰克和安妮快步沿着走廊走，然后走下一些又陡又窄的台阶。 他们穿过一条低矮的石制走廊，守卫们紧随其后。

“Over the Bridge of Sighs!” shouted the grouchy guard. “And be sure to sigh when you cross it—because you won’t be coming back for a long time!” “走过叹息桥！”脾气暴躁的守卫喊道。 “过桥的时候一定要叹气——因为你们很长时间都不会再回来了！”

Jack gripped Annie’s hand as they crossed a covered footbridge to another building. Once inside, they started down a lantern-lit hallway filled with puddles. Jack’s shoes felt squishy and soggy as he sloshed through the water. 杰克牵着安妮的手，他们走过一座有顶的石桥，来到另一栋建筑前。 一进门，他们就开始沿着一条有灯笼照明的走廊走，走廊里到处都是水坑。 杰克的鞋子被水泡得又湿又软，他踩着水一步一步地走。

“Halt!” shouted the grouchy guard. “站住！”脾气不好的守卫喊道。

Jack and Annie stopped in front of a heavy wooden door. The grouchy guard opened the door and pushed them into a dark, damp cell. 杰克和安妮停在了一扇沉重的木门前。 脾气不好的守卫打开了门，把他们推进了一个又黑又潮湿的牢房里。

The door slammed shut. Jack heard a heavy metal bolt clank into place. Then he heard the guards splash away down the hall, arguing with each other. 门砰地一声关上了。 杰克听到一个沉重的金属锁扣咔嗒一声锁上了。 然后他听到守卫们踩着水朝走廊的另一头走去了，他们一边走一边争吵。

The prison was eerily quiet. It was hard to breathe in the stale cell. It was hard to see, too. Only the dim light of the hallway shone faintly through the barred window. Under the window was a wooden bench. 监狱里安静得让人害怕。 牢房里的空气又闷又难闻。 连看都很难看清楚。 只有走廊里昏暗的灯光，透过铁栏窗透进来一点点。 窗子下面有一条木头长椅。

“What now?” Annie asked in a small voice. “现在怎么办？”安妮小声地问。

For a moment, Jack couldn’t answer. He was stunned. Minutes ago they’d been at the bright Carnival. Now they were locked in a dingy prison cell. “I—I’ll look in the book,” he said. 杰克一时说不出话来，他惊呆了。 几分钟前，他们还在热闹的游乐园里。 现在却关在一间又脏又暗的牢房里。 “我……我去书里找找，”他说。

Jack felt shaky as he opened their research book. He moved close to the barred window to read by the dim light. He looked up prison in the index. He found it and read aloud: 杰克打开他们的研究书时，感觉有点发抖。 他走近被栏杆挡住的窗户，在昏暗的光线下读书。 他在目录里查了‘监狱’这个词。 他找到了，然后大声读出来：

The prison cells at ground level in the palace were called the pozzi, meaning “wells” or “pits.” They were dank, airless, and filled with rats. Even the government eventually decided they were too cruel. 宫殿地面上的监狱牢房叫做pozzi，意思是“井”或“坑”。那里又湿又闷，到处都是老鼠。就连政府后来也觉得这里太残忍了。

Jack heard a squeak from a dark corner. He stopped reading and looked up. He heard the squeak again. The hair went up on his neck. Was that a rat? he wondered. 杰克听到黑暗角落里传来一声吱呀声。 他停下阅读，抬起头。 他又听到了一声吱呀声。 他的脖子后面毛都竖起来了。那是老鼠吗？ 他想。

“Was that a rat?” said Annie. “那是老鼠吗？”安妮说。

The squeak came again from the dark corner. Then a squeak came from another corner. Jack heard rustling along the walls and more squeaking. 声音又从黑暗的角落传来了。 接着，另一个角落也传来了声音。 杰克听到墙边有窸窸窣窣的声音，还有更多的吱吱声。

“Oh, man,” he breathed. There were rats everywhere. “天啊，”他低声说。 到处都是老鼠。

“I think it’s time for magic,” said Annie. “我觉得是时候施魔法了，”安妮说。

“Yep,” said Jack, “definitely.” He kept his eye on the dark corners while Annie reached into his backpack and pulled out Teddy and Kathleen’s book. “对，”杰克说，“ definitely（肯定）。”他一直盯着黑暗的角落，而安妮从他的背包里拿出泰迪和凯瑟琳的书。

Annie read from the table of contents: “Make a Stone Come Alive. Make Metal Soft. Turn into Ducks.” Annie looked up. “Are rats afraid of ducks?” she asked. 安妮从目录中读到：“让石头活起来。让金属变柔软。” 变成鸭子。”安妮抬头看了看。 “老鼠害怕鸭子吗？”她问。

“Forget ducks!” said Jack. “Go back to Make Metal Soft—that’s what we need to do! You read the rhyme, and I’ll try to pull the bars apart.” “忘了鸭子吧！”杰克说。 “回去做软金属的事吧，这才是我们需要做的！ 你读那首诗，我来试着把栏杆拉开。”

“Okay, good,” said Annie. “好，很好，”安妮说。

Jack jumped onto the wooden bench under the barred window. The squeaking grew louder. 杰克跳到了有栏杆的窗户下面的木凳上。 吱吱声变得更大了。

Jack reached up and felt the iron bars. They were cold and hard and very solid. Jack couldn’t imagine bending them. 杰克伸手向上，摸到了铁栏杆。 它们又冷又硬，非常结实。 杰克根本想象不出它们能被弯折。

The squeaks were getting louder. Jack gripped two bars in the middle of the window and took a deep breath. “Read the rhyme!” he said. 吱吱声越来越响了。 杰克抓住窗户中间的两根栏杆，深吸了一口气。 “念出这首韵文！”他说。

Annie read aloud: 安妮大声读道：

Iron or copper, brass or steel, 铁或铜，黄铜或钢。

Bree-on-saw! Bro-on-beel! 布里-翁-萨！布罗-翁-比尔！

As Annie finished the rhyme, the bars began to glow. They grew warmer in Jack’s hands. “I think it’s working!” he cried. 当安妮念完这首韵文时，栏杆开始发光。 它们在杰克手中变得越来越热。 “我觉得它起作用了！”他喊道。

Holding the bars tightly Jack pulled in opposite directions. Slowly the glowing bars began to stretch and bend. Jack pulled till there was an opening large enough for Annie and him to fit through. 杰克紧紧抓住栏杆，朝相反的方向用力拉。 发光的栏杆慢慢地开始伸长和弯曲。 杰克使劲拉，直到出现一个足够大、让安妮和他都能钻过去的开口。

“We did it!” he cried. “我们成功了！”他喊道。

“Great! Hurry, hurry! The rats are coming!” cried Annie as she jumped on the bench. “太好了！快点，快点！ “老鼠来了！”安妮一边喊，一边跳上了长凳。

Jack heard a chorus of squeaks from all sides of the cell. He looked down. He saw the shadowy shapes of dozens of rats. They seemed to be sniffing the air below the window. 杰克听到牢房四周传来一串串老鼠的吱吱声。他低头看去。 他看见了成群老鼠的模糊身影。 它们似乎正在窗子下方嗅着空气。

“Go! Go!” Jack cried to Annie. “走！ 走！”杰克对安妮喊道。

Annie squeezed between the bars and jumped down into the hallway. Jack followed her. He hit the wet floor and scrambled to his feet. “Come on!” he cried. 安妮挤过栏杆，跳进了走廊。 杰克跟在她后面。 他摔在湿漉漉的地板上，赶紧爬起来。 “快点！”他喊道。

Jack and Annie sloshed down the watery hallway. At the end of it, they nearly bumped right into the two guards. Jack and Annie kept running. 杰克和安妮踩着水，冲下走廊。 走到走廊尽头时，他们差点撞上两个守卫。 杰克和安妮继续往前跑。

“Hey!” the grouchy guard shouted, running after them. He reached for Jack. The other guard tried to catch Annie. “站住！”脾气不好的守卫喊道，追了上来。 他伸手去抓杰克。 另一个守卫试图抓住安妮。

Jack and Annie dodged away from them. The guards crashed into each other, falling to the floor. Jack and Annie kept running. They dashed across the Bridge of Sighs. They ran through the corridor and up the steep stone steps. 杰克和安妮躲开了他们。 守卫们撞在一起，摔倒在了地上。 杰克和安妮继续跑着。 他们冲过了叹息桥。 他们跑过走廊，爬上了陡峭的石阶。

“This way!” cried Jack. He and Annie tore down the hall, heading for the Golden Staircase. “这边！”杰克喊道。 他和安妮冲下走廊，朝金色楼梯跑去。

“Hey! Hey!” the guards yelled from far behind. “嘿！ 嘿！”远处的守卫们喊道。

Jack and Annie bounded down the Golden Staircase two steps at a time. They flew down the hall and down the Giants’ Stairs. They ran past the statues of Mars and Neptune and charged down the long, open passageway. Finally, they dashed through the entrance of the palace and escaped into Saint Mark’s Square. 杰克和安妮每次跳下两级台阶，飞快地冲下金色楼梯。 他们飞快地跑过走廊，冲下巨人的台阶。 他们跑过了火星和海王的雕像，冲进了长长的走廊。 最后，他们冲进宫殿的入口，逃到了圣马可广场。
