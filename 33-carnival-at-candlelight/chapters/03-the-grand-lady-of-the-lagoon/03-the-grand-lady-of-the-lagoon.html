3. The Grand Lady of the Lagoon 3. 湖泊的高贵女士

The gondola glided toward a landing at the edge of the canal. As the gondolier tied up the boat, water was sloshing from the canal onto a wide walkway filled with Carnival-goers. 小船滑行着，朝运河边缘的码头驶去。 当船夫把船系好时，运河里的水溅到了一条宽阔的步道上，步道上挤满了参加嘉年华的人。

The gondolier reached a gloved hand toward Annie and helped her out of the boat. He then held out a hand to Jack. When Jack grabbed it, he was surprised that the gondolier’s hand felt small under the white glove, like a kid’s hand. 船夫戴着戴着手套的手，朝安妮伸过去，帮她从船上下来。 然后，他又伸出一只手给杰克。 当杰克抓住他的手时，他惊讶地发现，船夫戴着手套的手感觉很小，就像一个小孩的手。

As soon as Jack had stepped out of the boat, the gondolier untied the rope, pushed off from the landing, and began rowing away. 杰克刚一下船，船夫就解开了绳子，划着船离开码头，开始划远了。

“Bye! Thanks!” called Annie. “再见！ 谢谢！”安妮喊道。

The two strangers in the white bird masks waved. 戴着白色鸟面具的两个陌生人挥了挥手。

Jack and Annie watched the gondola disappear over the choppy waters. Then they looked back at the shore. The Carnival crowd was parading up and down the wide walkway along the canal. 杰克和安妮看着游船消失在波浪起伏的水面上。 然后他们又看向岸边。 嘉年华的人群沿着运河边的宽广步道来回游行。

“Hey look,” said Annie. “There’s a bunch of people dressed like us! And like the two people in our gondola!” “嘿，快看，”安妮说。 “有一群人穿着和我们一样的衣服！ 还有和我们游船上那两个人一样的装扮！”，

Jack saw lots of black cloaks, bird masks, funny hats, and ruffled collars. He also saw people dressed as chickens, pirates, and knights. None of the costumed people seemed to mind the water spilling over the edge of the canal, soaking their boots and slippers. 杰克看到很多人穿着黑色斗篷、戴着鸟面具、戴着滑稽的帽子，还有穿着波浪形领子的衣服。 他还看到有人穿着鸡的服装、海盗装和骑士装。 那些穿着服装的人似乎并不在意运河边的水溢出来，把他们的靴子和拖鞋都弄湿了。

As Jack and Annie stared at the crowd, a distant bell began to ring. The bell rang nine times. “I guess that means it’s nine o’clock,” said Jack. 杰克和安妮看着人群时，远处的钟声开始响起来。 钟声响了九下。 “我想这意味着九点整，”杰克说。

Just then another bell began to chime. Jack counted again. This time, ten chimes rang out. “Ten?” said Jack, puzzled. “So what time is it? Nine o’clock or ten o’clock?” 就在这时，另一座钟开始发出钟声。 杰克又数了一遍。 这次响了十下。 “十下？”杰克困惑地说。 “那现在是什么时间呢？ 是九点还是十点？”

“Don’t worry about that now,” said Annie. “I think I see the Grand Lady of the Lagoon!” “现在别担心这个，”安妮说。 “我觉得我看到湖畔的贵夫人了！”

“Where?” said Jack. “在哪？”杰克说。

Annie pointed to a tall woman at the edge of the parade. She was wearing a black mask. She wore piles of jewelry, a white wig, and a wide hoop skirt. 安妮指着游行队伍边缘的一位高个子女士。 她戴着一副黑色面具。 她戴着一堆珠宝，一头白发，还穿着一条宽大的圆裙。

Jack and Annie moved toward the lady. “Excuse me,” said Annie. “Hi.” 杰克和安妮朝那位女士走去。 “请问一下，”安妮说。 “你好。”

The lady looked at Annie. “Hello,” she said in a deep man’s voice. 那位女士看了看安妮。 “你好，”她用一个男人低沉的声音说道。

“Whoa,” said Jack, stepping back. “哇，”杰克往后退了一步，说道。

Annie laughed. “You’re a man!” she said. 安妮笑了。 “你是个男人！”她说。

“Of course,” said the man. “But for Carnival, I am a very beautiful lady, no?” “当然，”那个男人说。 “不过在嘉年华上，我可是个非常漂亮的女士，对吧？”

“We’re looking for someone called the Grand Lady of—” started Annie. But before she could finish, a giant chicken grabbed the man’s hand and pulled him away into the crowd. “我们在找一个叫‘伟大的女士’的人——”安妮开始说道。 但还没等她说完，一只巨大的鸡抓住了那个男人的手，把他拉进了人群里。

“Oh, brother,” said Jack, looking around. Lots of people were dressed as grand ladies! “How are we supposed to know who to visit?” “哦，天哪，”杰克环顾四周，说道。 有很多人都打扮成了伟大的女士！ “我们怎么知道该去拜访谁呢？”

“Maybe it’s time to use one of Kathleen and Teddy’s rhymes,” said Annie. “也许现在该用凯瑟琳和泰迪的押韵诗了，”安妮说。

“No, we should save our rhymes,” said Jack. “不，我们应该留着我们的押韵诗，”杰克说。

“Then let’s just skip visiting the Grand Lady of the Lagoon for now,” said Annie. “What’s the next thing Merlin tells us to do?” “那我们现在就先不去拜访沼泽湖的贵夫人吧，”安妮说。 “梅林接下来让我们做什么？”

Jack looked at Merlin’s directions and read aloud: 杰克看了看梅林的指示，大声读出来：

To find out where you need to go, 去找到你需要去的地方。

Seek out a painter named Tiepolo. 去找一位名叫提埃波洛的画家。

“Good, that’s clear,” said Jack. “Let’s see if Tiepolo is in our research book.” “很好，这很清楚，”杰克说。 “我们看看提埃波洛是否在我们的研究书里。”

As the noisy crowd celebrated around them, Jack pulled out the book. He stood near a lantern and looked in the index. “He’s here!” said Jack. He turned to the right page and read: 当他们周围嘈杂的人群庆祝时，杰克拿出那本书。 他站在一盏灯旁边，看着目录。 “他在这里！”杰克说。 他翻到右边的那一页，读道：

Tiepolo (say tee-EP-uh-lo) was one of the greatest painters of Venice in the 1700s. He painted bright, beautiful oil paintings for palaces and villas. 提埃波洛（发音为 tee-EP-uh-lo）是1700年代威尼斯最伟大的画家之一。他为宫殿和别墅画了许多明亮而美丽的油画。

“If the guy’s so famous, people must know where he lives,” said Annie. “Excuse me!” she called to a clown walking by. “Can you tell us where the painter Tiepolo lives?” “如果这个人这么有名，人们一定知道他住在哪里，”安妮说。 “请问一下！”她对路过的一个小丑喊道。 “你能告诉我们提埃波洛画家住在哪里吗？”，

“Near the Church of San Felice,” the clown said. “在圣费利切教堂附近，”小丑说。

“Thanks!” said Annie. “谢谢！”安妮说。

“But you won’t find him home now,” the clown called over his shoulder. “He’s in Milan, painting.” “不过你现在找不到他，”小丑回头喊道。 “他正在米兰画画呢。”

“Where’s that?” shouted Jack. “那在哪儿？”杰克喊道。

“Over a day’s ride on horseback,” said the clown. Then he disappeared into the crowd. “骑马一天的路程，”小丑说道。 然后他消失在人群中。

“Hmmm …,” said Annie. “Do you think Merlin wants us to go to Milan?” “嗯……，”安妮说道。 “你觉得梅林是让我们去米兰吗？”

“We don’t have time,” said Jack. “I think we have to skip Tiepolo, too.” “我们没时间了，”杰克说。 “我想我们还得跳过提埃波洛。”

“Yeah,” said Annie. “Hey, maybe we should just skip all this stuff and try to find the Ruler of the Seas on our own. Our letter says he’s the only one who can help us save the Grand Lady.” “对，”安妮说。 “嘿，也许我们应该直接跳过所有这些内容，自己去寻找海洋之主。 我们的信上说，他是唯一能帮助我们拯救高贵女士的人。”

“I don’t know …,” said Jack. “In his letter, Merlin tells us to be patient—” “我不知道……”杰克说。 “在信里，梅林告诉我们要有耐心——”

But Annie was already calling to a pirate passing by. “Sir, do you know where we can find the Ruler of the Seas?” 但安妮已经朝路过的海盗喊了起来。 “先生，你知道我们在哪里能找到海之统治者吗？”

“What?” shouted the pirate. “什么？”海盗喊道。

“The ruler! Do you know where he lives?” yelled Annie. “统治者！ 你知道他住在哪儿吗？”安妮大喊。

“In the palace on Saint Mark’s Square!” said the pirate. “在圣马可广场的宫殿里！”海盗说。

“Where’s that?” Annie called. But the pirate had disappeared into the crowd. “那是在哪儿？”安妮喊道。 但海盗已经消失在人群中。

“I’ll look up Saint Mark’s Square,” said Jack. He turned the pages of their book until he came to a map of Venice. “Oh, cool,” he said. Jack loved maps. “我要查一下圣马可广场，”杰克说。 他翻着他们的书，直到翻到一张威尼斯的地图。 “哦，真酷，”他说。杰克很喜欢地图。

“Okay, let’s see,” Jack said. “We’re here.” He pointed to the walkway along the canal on the map. “And we want to go to Saint Mark’s Square.” He pointed to another place on the map. “It’s really close.” “好吧，我们来看看，”杰克说。 “我们在这里。”他指着地图上运河边的小路。 “我们要去圣马可广场。”他指着地图上的另一个地方。 “真的很近。”

“Yeah, and it looks like everyone else is headed there, too,” said Annie. “Come on.” “是啊，而且看起来大家也都是要去那里，”安妮说。 “快点。”

“So, if we go this way …,” said Jack. He traced their route with his finger. “所以，如果我们走这条路……”杰克说。 他用手指沿着路线比划着。

“Come on, Jack!” called Annie. “快点，杰克！”安妮喊道。

Jack looked up from the map. Annie was already walking with the crowd. Jack closed the book and hurried after her. Soon they came to a huge, open square. 杰克从地图上抬起头。 安妮已经和人群一起走了。 杰克合上书，赶紧追了上去。 不久后，他们来到一个宽敞的大广场。

“Wow,” Jack said breathlessly. Saint Mark’s Square was filled with candlelight and musicians. Acrobats balanced on tightropes. Boxers boxed in a ring. Knights fenced with swords. Clowns walked on tall stilts, raced wheelbarrows, and tried to catch live eels in their mouths. All around the square were candlelit buildings. “哇，”杰克喘着气说。 圣马可广场上充满了烛光和音乐家。 杂技演员在绳索上保持平衡。 拳击手在一个环形场地里比赛。 骑士们用剑比武。 小丑们踩着高跷走路，推着独轮车比赛，还试着用嘴抓住活鳗鱼。 广场周围都是点着蜡烛的建筑物。

“Venice is beautiful,” said Annie. “威尼斯真漂亮，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. He looked back at their book. He found an illustration of Saint Mark’s Square. Jack read the descriptions of the buildings: “是啊，”杰克说。 他回头看了看他们的书。 他找到了一张圣马可广场的插图。 杰克读着这些建筑物的描述：

The watchtower of Saint Mark’s Square is the tallest building in Venice. The weather vane on top once helped sailors by showing them which way the wind was blowing. 圣马可广场的钟楼是威尼斯最高的建筑。顶部的风向标曾经帮助水手们判断风的方向。

Jack looked up. “I can barely see the weather vane up there,” he said. “But I think it’s pointing north. So the wind must be blowing from the south.” 杰克抬头看了看。 “我几乎看不见上面那个风向标了，”他说。 “但是我觉得它是指向北边。” 所以风一定是从南方吹来的。”

“What about the ruler’s palace? Where’s that?” asked Annie. “那国王的宫殿呢？ “那在哪儿？”安妮问。

Jack read on: 杰克继续读：

The clock tower is one of the most beautiful towers in the world. On top of the tower, a bell is struck every hour by— 钟楼是世界上最漂亮的塔之一。塔顶的钟每小时都会被敲响——

“Jack, skip to the ruler’s palace!” interrupted Annie. “Please!” “杰克，跳到国王的宫殿那儿！”安妮打断了他。 “请别这样！”

“Okay, okay,” said Jack. He read about the palace: “好吧，好吧，”杰克说。 他读到关于宫殿的内容：

The palace of Venice’s ruler is one of the most splendid structures ever built. It has a great hall where as many as 2,000 nobles once met to discuss city matters. It also holds the city’s grim prison cells. Above the palace door is a sculpture of Saint Mark showing a book to a winged lion— 威尼斯统治者的宫殿是世界上最宏伟的建筑之一。里面有一个大厅，曾经有2000多名贵族在那里开会讨论城市事务。它还关押着城市的囚犯。宫殿门上方有一座雕塑，描绘的是圣马可将一本书递给一只长着翅膀的狮子——

“There’s the palace door!” said Annie. “那里是宫殿的大门！”安妮说。

Jack looked up from the map. Annie was heading toward a huge door with the sculpture of a man and a winged lion above it. Jack closed the book and hurried to catch up to her. 杰克从地图上抬起头来。 安妮正朝一扇巨大的门走去，门上方雕刻着一个人和一只长着翅膀的狮子。 杰克合上书，赶紧去追她。

A guard was standing by the palace door. The guard wore a uniform and held a rifle. “Wait,” Jack whispered. “Is he a real guard? Or is that just a costume?” 一名卫兵正站在宫殿的门前。 卫兵穿着制服，手里拿着一把步枪。 “等等，”杰克小声说。 “他真的是卫兵吗？ 还是只是穿了套戏服？”

“I’ll find out,” said Annie. She walked over to the guard. “Excuse me, sir. Is the ruler of Venice inside the palace now?” “我去找找看，”安妮说。 她走到卫兵面前。 “请问，先生。” 威尼斯的统治者现在在宫殿里吗？“

“Begone, clown!” the guard said gruffly. “滚开，小丑！”卫兵粗声粗气地说。

“But it’s important,” said Annie. “We need to talk to him about something.” “但这是很重要的事情，”安妮说。 “我们需要和他谈谈。”

“I said begone!” the guard growled. “I am tired of clowns wasting my time!” “我叫你滚开！”卫兵咆哮道。 “我受够了小丑浪费我的时间！”

“She’s not really a clown,” said Jack, coming forward. “We’re on a mission. We—” “她其实不是小丑，”杰克走上前说。 “我们正在执行任务。我们—”

“Go! Both of you! Or else!” roared the guard. He held up his rifle. He’s definitely not wearing a costume, thought Jack. “走开！你们两个！ “不然的话！”守卫吼道。 他举起了他的步枪。 杰克心想，他肯定不是在穿戏服。

“Okay, sorry, sorry,” said Jack. He and Annie moved away from the palace entrance. “好吧，对不起，对不起，”杰克说道。 他和安妮离开了宫殿的入口。

“What a grouchy guy,” said Annie. “真是个脾气坏透了的人，”安妮说。

“He’ll never let us in,” said Jack. “他永远不会让我们进去的，”杰克说。

“Maybe it’s time to use one of Teddy and Kathleen’s rhymes,” said Annie. “Maybe we should turn into ducks. The guard wouldn’t mind if a couple of ducks—” “也许现在该用泰迪和凯瑟琳的押韵诗了，”安妮说。 “也许我们应该变成鸭子。 守卫不会介意两只鸭子——

“No,” said Jack. “We have to save our rhymes.” “不，”杰克说。 “我们必须救出我们的押韵词。”

“Well, how will we get inside?” Annie asked. “那我们怎么进去呢？”安妮问。

“Patience,” said Jack. “Remember—” “耐心点，”杰克说。 “记住——”

Before he could finish, Annie broke in, “Hey, look!” 他还没说完，安妮就插话道，“嘿，快看！”

Two clowns on stilts were dancing around the guard. One grabbed the guard’s rifle and tossed it to the other. 两个踩着高跷的小丑在哨兵周围跳舞。 其中一个抓起哨兵的步枪，扔给了另一个。

“Hey!” the guard yelled. “Give that back!” “嘿！”哨兵喊道。 “把那还给我！”

“Now’s our chance!” said Annie. “Quick!” She ran to the entrance and slipped through the door. “现在是我们的机会！”安妮说。 “快！”她跑到入口处，从门里溜了进去。

“Oh, no—oh, man!” said Jack. While the guard chased after the two clowns on stilts, Jack rushed to the doorway of the palace and slipped inside, too. “哦，不——天啊！”杰克说。 当卫兵去追那两个踩着高跷的小丑时，杰克冲到宫殿的门口，也溜了进去。
