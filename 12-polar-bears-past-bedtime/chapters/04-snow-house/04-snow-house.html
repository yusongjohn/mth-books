4. Snow House 4. 雪屋

The dogsled skimmed silently over the frozen tundra. The seal hunter ran alongside it. Sometimes he cracked his whip against the ice. 狗拉雪橇无声地掠过结冰的苔原。 海豹猎人在雪橇旁边跑着。 有时他朝冰面甩一下鞭子。

The snowdrifts looked like giant white sculptures as the sun slipped behind the frozen hills. Then a full orange moon rose in the sky. 太阳滑到冰冻的山丘后面时，雪堆看起来像巨大的白色雕塑。 接着一轮橙红色的满月升上天空。

The moonlight lit a small, rounded igloo in front of them. The dogs slowed, then stopped. 月光把他们前方一座小小的圆顶冰屋照亮了。 狗慢了下来，然后停住了。

Jack stepped off the sled. Annie went to help unhitch the dogs. Jack took his book out and read about igloos: 杰克走下雪橇。 安妮去帮忙解开狗。 杰克拿出书，读到关于冰屋的内容：

The word “igloo” means “house” in the language of native Arctic people. The house is built with blocks of snow. Dry snow is good wall material because it keeps in the heat. The temperature inside an igloo can be 65 degrees warmer than the temperature outside. “igloo”这个词在北极原住民的语言中意思是“房子”。这个房子是用雪块建造的。干燥的雪是很好的建筑材料，因为它能保持热量。在雪屋里的温度可能比外面高65度。

Jack took out his notebook. He pulled off his mitten just long enough to write: 杰克拿出笔记本。 他只脱掉手套写了一会儿：

图中文字：igloo means house 图中文字：igloo 是指房子

“Come on, Jack!” said Annie. “快点，杰克！”安妮说。

She and the seal hunter were waiting for him in front of the igloo. The dogs were leashed together outside. 她和海豹猎人正等在冰屋前面。 那些狗被拴在外面。

Jack hurried to join them. The hunter pushed aside animal skins covering the entrance. They stepped inside. 杰克赶紧过去和他们会合。 猎人把挡住入口的兽皮推开。 他们走了进去。

A fat candle burned brightly. Shadows danced on walls of ice and snow. 一支粗蜡烛燃烧得很亮。 影子在冰雪砌成的墙上跳动。

Jack and Annie sat on a fur-covered platform. They watched as the seal hunter moved about. 杰克和安妮坐在铺着毛皮的平台上。 他们看着海豹猎人在屋里走动。

First he lit a small stove. Then he slipped outside. He came back with a snowball and chunks of frozen meat. 他先点起一个小炉子。 然后溜到外面去了。 他拿着一个雪球和几大块冻肉回来了。

He put the snowball in a pot over the stove. Then he added the meat. 他把雪球放进炉子上的锅里。 然后把肉加了进去。

“What’s he making?” asked Annie. “他在做什么？”安妮问。

Jack pulled out his book and found a picture of the hunter cooking. He and Annie read the words silently: 杰克拿出书，找到猎人做饭的图片。 他和安妮默默地读着上面的字：

There was a time when nearly all of the Arctic people’s food and clothing and tools came from Arctic animals, especially the seal. Nearly every part of the seal could be eaten. Lamps were fueled with seal fat. Clothing was made from sealskin. And knives and needles were carved from seal bones. 曾经有一段时间，北极人的食物、衣服和工具几乎都来自北极的动物，尤其是海豹。海豹的几乎每一部分都可以吃。灯是用海豹脂肪做燃料的。衣服是用海豹皮做的。刀子和针是用海豹骨头雕刻的。

“He must be boiling seal meat,” said Jack. “他一定是在煮海豹肉。”杰克说。

“The poor seals,” said Annie. “可怜的海豹，”安妮说。

The seal hunter looked up. 海豹猎人抬起头。

“They are not poor,” he said. “They help us because they know we would die without them.” “它们不可怜，”他说。 “它们帮助我们，因为它们知道没有它们我们就会死。”

“Oh,” said Annie. “哦，”安妮说。

“In return, we always thank the animal spirits,” said the seal hunter. “作为回报，我们总是感谢动物的灵魂。”海豹猎人说。

“How do you do that?” said Jack. “你们怎么感谢？”杰克说。

“We have many special ceremonies,” said the seal hunter. “我们有许多特别的仪式。”海豹猎人说。

He reached under the fur-covered platform and took out two wooden masks. 他伸手到铺着毛皮的平台下面，拿出两副木头面具。

“Soon there will be a ceremony to honor the spirit of the polar bear,” he said. “I carved these masks for the ceremony.” “很快就会有一场向北极熊灵魂致敬的仪式。”他说。 “我为仪式刻了这些面具。”

“Polar bears?” said Annie. “北极熊？”安妮说。

“Yes,” said the hunter. “Just as the seal has given us many gifts, so has the polar bear.” “是的，”猎人说。 “就像海豹给了我们许多礼物一样，北极熊也是。”

“Like what?” said Jack. “比如什么？”杰克说。

“Long ago the polar bear taught us how to live in the ice and snow,” said the seal hunter. “很久以前，北极熊教我们如何在冰雪中生活。”海豹猎人说。

“Taught you?” said Jack. “I mean, can you give us some facts?” “教你们？”杰克说。 “我是说，你能给我们讲些事实吗？”

The seal hunter smiled. 海豹猎人笑了。

“Yes,” he said. “A polar bear catches a seal when the seal comes up to breathe through a hole in the ice. The oldest seal hunters watched the polar bear and learned. This is how my father taught me to hunt seal, as his father taught him.” “可以，”他说。 “海豹从冰洞上来透气时，北极熊就会抓住它。 最早的海豹猎人观察北极熊，学会了这一招。 我父亲就是这样教我猎海豹的，就像他父亲教他一样。”

“That’s a good fact,” said Jack. “这是个好事实，”杰克说。

“The very first of my people learned to make igloos from polar bears,” said the hunter. “Polar bears build snow houses by digging caves in the drifts.” “我们族人最早就是向北极熊学会造冰屋的。”猎人说。 “北极熊在雪堆里挖洞，给自己造雪屋。”

“Another good fact,” said Jack. “又一个好事实，”杰克说。

“Sometimes the polar bear can even teach people to fly,” said the seal hunter. “有时候，北极熊甚至能教人飞，”海豹猎人说。

“That’s an amazing fact,” said Annie. “这可真是惊人的事实，”安妮说。

Jack smiled. “The rest sounded like true facts,” he said. “But I know that’s pretend.” 杰克笑了。 “其余的听起来都像是真事。”他说。 “但我知道那是编的。”

The hunter just laughed, then turned back to his cooking. 猎人只是笑了笑，又转回去做饭。

That’s why he wasn’t surprised to hear about the tree house, Jack thought. If he believes polar bears can fly, he probably would believe anything. 杰克心想，难怪他听到树屋的事一点也不惊讶。 要是他相信北极熊会飞，那他大概什么都肯信。

The seal hunter lifted the chunks of boiled seal out of his pot. He dropped them into a wooden bucket and gave it to Annie. 海豹猎人把煮好的海豹肉块从锅里捞出来。 他把它们放进一个木桶，递给安妮。

“Let’s feed the dogs,” he said. “我们去喂狗吧。”他说。

“Oh, boy!” said Annie. She followed the hunter outside, swinging the bucket. “太好了！”安妮说。 她晃着木桶，跟着猎人走了出去。

Jack quickly threw his notebook and the Arctic book into his pack. He started to follow them. Then his gaze fell on the two bear masks. 杰克赶紧把笔记本和北极书扔进背包。 他开始跟着他们出去。 这时他的目光落在那两副熊面具上。

He picked them up to get a better look. 他拿起来想看得更清楚些。

Each was carved in the shape of a polar bear’s face with a blunt nose and roundish ears. There were two holes for eyes and a strap to hold it on your head. 每一副都雕成北极熊的脸，鼻子钝钝的，耳朵圆圆的。 上面有两个眼洞，还有一条带子可以戴在头上。

Suddenly howls split the air. The dogs were barking and growling. Annie squealed. 忽然，嚎叫声划破空气。 狗又叫又吼。安妮尖叫起来。

Are the dogs attacking her? Jack wondered. 狗在攻击她吗？杰克心里想。

“Annie!” “安妮！”

Still holding the bear masks, Jack charged out of the igloo. 杰克还拿着熊面具，冲出了冰屋。
