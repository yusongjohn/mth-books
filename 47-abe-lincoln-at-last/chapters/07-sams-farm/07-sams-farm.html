CHAPTER SEVEN

Sam’s Farm

“I don’t know,” said Jack. This was one of the worst things that had ever happened. He pressed his finger against Sam’s wrist to feel for his pulse, like he’d seen on TV and in movies. “我不知道，”杰克说。 这是发生过最糟糕的事情之一。 他用手指按在萨姆的手腕上，感觉他的脉搏，就像他在电视和电影里看到的那样。

Sam’s eyes opened. “Giddyup,” he said weakly. 萨姆的眼睛睁开了。 “驾，”他虚弱地说。

Jack laughed with relief. “Whew, we were afraid you were dead!” he said. 杰克松了口气，笑了起来。 “哇，我们还以为你死了呢！”，他说。

“Ain’t dead yet,” Sam whispered, blinking, “but I am seeing stars and my ears are ringing.” “我还没死呢，”萨姆低声说，眨了眨眼，“但我现在看到星星了，耳朵还在嗡嗡响。”

“Does your head hurt?” asked Annie. “你的头疼吗？”，安妮问。

“Yes, bad,” Sam said quietly, his eyes squinting with pain. “是的，很糟糕，”萨姆轻声说，他的眼睛因疼痛而眯了起来。

“You might have a concussion,” Jack said. “Is there a doctor nearby?” “你可能有脑震荡，”杰克说。 “附近有医生吗？

“Thirty-five …,” said Sam. “三十五……，”萨姆说。

“Minutes?” asked Jack. “分钟？”，杰克问。

“Miles,” Sam whispered. “迈尔斯，”萨姆小声说。

“Whoa, that’s really far,” said Annie. “哇，真的好远，”安妮说。

“I have to go home … to our farm,” said Sam. He struggled to sit up. “我得回家……去我们的农场，”萨姆说。 他努力坐起来。

“Careful,” said Jack. He couldn’t remember what to do if someone had a concussion. “小心点，”杰克说。 他想不起如果有人脑震荡了该怎么办。

With Jack and Annie’s help, Sam managed to get on his feet. “Thanks,” the boy said. He staggered toward his horse, then swayed and collapsed onto the ground again. 在杰克和安妮的帮助下，萨姆终于站了起来。 “谢谢，”男孩说。 他摇摇晃晃地走向他的马，然后又摇晃了一下，又倒在地上。

“Sam!” said Annie. She and Jack gently helped him back up to a standing position. “萨姆！”安妮喊道。 她和杰克轻轻地把他扶起来，让他重新站好。

“Dizzy … just dizzy,” whispered Sam. “头晕……只是头晕，”萨姆小声说。

“We’ll help you get home,” said Annie. “You can’t do it by yourself. Right, Jack?” “我们会帮你回家，”安妮说。 “你不能自己一个人做。对吧，杰克？”

“Right,” said Jack. He knew it was the right thing to do. But as soon as we get him home to his parents, we have to find Abraham Lincoln, he thought. “对，”杰克说。 他知道这是正确的做法。 但他一想到要把他送回家见到父母，就必须找到亚伯拉罕·林肯，他心想。

“Sam can sit in front of me and I’ll hold on to him,” Annie said to Jack. “You can take the reins and walk alongside us.” “萨姆可以坐在我前面，我会紧紧抓住他，”安妮对杰克说。 “你可以拿着缰绳，和我们一起走。”

“Okay.” Jack kept holding Sam, while Annie unhitched the straps, freeing the horse. “好的。”杰克继续抱着萨姆，而安妮则解开了缰绳，让马自由活动。

The wind had died down. The horse was calm as Annie coaxed her to a tree stump. She climbed onto the stump and then onto the horse’s back. 风停了。 安妮轻声安抚着马，马也安静下来，走到一棵树桩旁。 她先爬到树桩上，然后又爬到马背上。

“Your turn, Sam,” said Jack. “轮到你了，萨姆，”杰克说。

Jack held Sam’s elbow as the gangly boy climbed onto the stump. Then Sam hauled himself onto the horse in front of Annie. He started to slump forward. Before he could slide off, Annie grabbed him and held him up. 杰克扶着这个个子高高的男孩的胳膊，帮他爬到树桩上。 然后萨姆爬到安妮前面的马上。 他开始向前倾倒。 在他滑下来之前，安妮抓住了他，把他扶起来。

“Got him?” said Jack. “抓住他了吗？”杰克问。

“My cornmeal,” Sam whispered. “我的玉米粉，”萨姆小声说。

“I’ll get it,” said Jack. He found a panel in the bottom of the grinder and opened it. Then he grabbed the empty sack and scooped the ground corn inside. “我来拿，”杰克说。 他在磨粉机底部找到了一个面板，把它打开了。 然后他拿起空袋子，把磨好的玉米粉倒了进去。

Jack slung the sack over his shoulder. Then he picked up the reins and turned the horse around. Annie held Sam as Jack led the horse along the lonely road back the way they had come. 杰克把袋子甩到肩膀上。 然后他拿起缰绳，让马转过身来。 安妮抱着萨姆，杰克牵着马沿着那条孤独的小路往他们来的方向走回去。

This isn’t the way things are supposed to happen, Jack thought. He knew they were supposed to trust the magic. But now they were helping the person who was supposed to help them. 这不是事情应该发生的办法，杰克心想。 他知道他们应该相信魔法。 但现在他们却在帮助那个本应该帮助他们的人。

“Where is your farm, Sam?” Jack asked after a while. “你的农场在哪里，萨姆？”过了一会儿，杰克问。

The boy didn’t answer. 男孩没有回答。

“Sam!” said Annie, giving him a little shake. “Your farm? Where is it?” “萨姆！”安妮说道，轻轻摇晃了他一下。 “你的农场？在哪里？”

Sam opened his eyes. “Here,” he said. 萨姆睁开了眼睛。 “在这里，”他说。

Jack didn’t see any sign of a farm. The only things up ahead were a small, windowless log cabin and a shed. A curl of smoke rose into the white sky. 杰克没有看到任何农场的迹象。 前方只有一个小木屋和一个棚屋，没有窗户。 一缕烟从白色的天空中升起。

“Here where? ” asked Jack. “这里是什么地方？”杰克问。

Sam pointed to the cabin and shed. 萨姆指着那间小木屋和棚屋。

That’s it? Jack thought. Sam’s family must be really poor. 就是这里吗？杰克心想。 萨姆家的人一定真的很穷。

The cabin and shed were in a scrubby clearing. The clearing was dotted with piles of stones and stumps where trees had been chopped down. 小木屋和棚屋位于一片荒芜的空地上。 空地上散落着石头堆和树桩，那是树木被砍倒的地方。

Not much of a farm, Jack thought. But at least they hadn’t wasted a lot of time getting Sam home. 这根本算不上一个农场，杰克心想。 但至少他们没有花太多时间把山姆送回家。

Jack led the horse toward Sam’s farm. The cabin not only had no windows—it didn’t even have a door! A black bearskin hung over the entrance. The horse stopped near the lean-to shed. The sound of a cow mooing came from inside. 杰克牵着马朝山姆的农场走去。 小屋不仅没有窗户，甚至连一扇门都没有！ 入口上方挂着一张黑熊的皮毛。 马停在了棚屋旁边。 里面传来一头牛的哞哞声。

“I’ll help you, Sam,” Jack said, dropping the sack of cornmeal to the ground. “Careful, careful.” “我会帮你的，山姆，”杰克说，把一袋玉米粉倒在地上。 “小心点，小心点。”

Sam lowered himself down from the horse. When his feet touched the ground, Jack grabbed him. “Lean on me,” he said. He put Sam’s arm around his shoulders. 山姆从马上慢慢下来。 当他脚一落地，杰克立刻抓住了他。 “靠在我身上，”他说。 他把萨姆的胳膊搭在自己肩膀上。

“Got him?” said Annie. “抓住他了吗？”安妮问。

“Yep,” said Jack. “抓住了，”杰克说。

As Jack and Sam stumbled toward the cabin, Annie slid off the horse and tied her to a fence post beside the shed. Then Annie grabbed the sack of cornmeal. She ran to the cabin and pushed aside the bearskin, so Jack could help Sam inside. 当杰克和萨姆跌跌撞撞朝小屋走去时，安妮从马上滑下来，把马拴在小屋旁边的篱笆柱上。 然后安妮抓起了一袋玉米粉。 她跑到小屋前，把熊皮毯子掀开，好让杰克帮助萨姆进去。

No one was home. The only light in the one-room cabin came from daylight streaming through big cracks between the logs of the walls. A low fire burned in a fireplace, but the air was cold and damp. The floor was made of dirt, and the crude furniture was made of planks of wood and tree stumps. 家里没人。 这个只有一间屋子的小屋里，唯一的光亮是白天从墙壁木头之间的大裂缝透进来的阳光。 壁炉里有一堆小火在燃烧，但空气又冷又潮湿。 地板是泥土做的，粗糙的家具是用木板和树桩做的。

“Thank—thank you, Jack,” Sam said, breathing heavily. “You can just leave me right here.” He took his arm from Jack’s shoulders and crumpled onto the dirt floor. He curled up and lay shivering on his side. “谢——谢谢你，杰克，”萨姆说，喘着粗气。 “你们可以把我留在这里。”他把胳膊从杰克的肩膀上拿开，一下子瘫倒在泥土地上。 他蜷缩起来，侧着身发抖。

This is not good, thought Jack. 这可不好，杰克心想。

“You can’t lie on the dirt, Sam,” said Annie. “Don’t you have a bed?” “你不能直接躺在泥地上，萨姆，”安妮说。 “你难道没有床吗？”

Sam pointed to a loft. 萨姆指着上面的阁楼。

“We’ll help you,” said Jack. “我们会帮你的，”杰克说。

Jack and Annie pulled Sam up from the floor. He put his arms around their shoulders, and they brought him to a row of wooden pegs that led to the loft. Sam managed to pull himself up the row of pegs. When he reached the top, he disappeared. 杰克和安妮把萨姆从地上拉了起来。 他环抱着他们的肩膀，带他们来到一排木制的木钉前，这些木钉通往阁楼。 萨姆努力爬上了那排木钉。 当他到达顶部时，消失了。

“Now what?” Jack whispered to Annie. “现在怎么办？”杰克低声对安妮说。

Sam moaned from the loft above. 萨姆从上面的阁楼里呻吟起来。

“Poor kid,” Annie murmured to Jack. “There’s no one here to take care of him.” “这孩子真可怜，”安妮对杰克轻声说。 “这里没有人能照顾他。”

Jack didn’t know what to do. He wanted to help Sam, but they still had to find Abraham Lincoln in the countryside before he returned to the White House. And he wasn’t sure how long the magic would work. 杰克不知道该怎么办。 他想帮助萨姆，但他们还得在乡下找到亚伯拉罕·林肯，否则他就要回到白宫了。 而且他不确定魔法还能持续多久。

Another moan came from the loft. 又传来一声呻吟，是从阁楼上传来的。

“We have to help Sam,” Annie said decisively. She climbed up the wooden pegs. Jack followed. As he crawled into the loft, he had to be careful not to bump his head on the ceiling. “我们必须帮助萨姆，”安妮坚定地说。 她爬上了木桩。杰克跟了上去。 当他爬进阁楼时，得小心别撞到天花板。

Light and cold air came through the cracks between the logs. Sam was lying on a bed of corn husks and dried leaves. His fingers were pressed against his head. 光线和冷空气从木头之间的裂缝透进来。 萨姆正躺在一堆玉米壳和干叶子上。 他的手指按在头上。

“Does your head still hurt?” asked Annie. “你的头还疼吗？”安妮问。

“Bad,” said Sam. He kept pressing his forehead, as if trying to push away the pain. “疼，”萨姆说。 他一直按着额头，好像在努力把疼痛赶走。

“Where are your parents, Sam?” asked Jack. “你父母在哪里，萨姆？”杰克问。

“Pa’s gone,” Sam said hoarsely. His eyes were squeezed shut. “Went to Kentucky last month.” “爸爸走了，”萨姆沙哑地说。 他的眼睛紧紧闭着。 “上个月去肯塔基了。”

“Where’s your mother?” asked Annie. “你妈妈在哪里？”安妮问。

Sam just shook his head. 萨姆只是摇了摇头。

“Can you tell us where your mother is?” Annie asked. “你能告诉我们你妈妈在哪里吗？”安妮问。

“Dead. She’s dead. She died last year,” said Sam. He covered his eyes with his arm. “死了。她死了。 她去年去世了，”萨姆说。 他用胳膊遮住了眼睛。

“Oh, no,” said Annie. “哦，不，”安妮说。

“Is there anyone who can take care of you?” Jack asked. He couldn’t imagine being so alone. “有没有人能照顾你？”杰克问。 他无法想象自己会如此孤单。

“My sister, Sarah,” Sam said in a muffled voice. “我姐姐，莎拉，”萨姆用闷闷的声音说。

“Where’s Sarah now?” asked Jack. “莎拉现在在哪里？”杰克问。

“School,” said Sam. “学校，”萨姆说。

“When does she get home?” asked Jack. “她什么时候回家？”杰克问。

“After dark,” said Sam. “天黑之后，”萨姆说。

“After dark?” said Annie. “天黑之后？”安妮说。

“Short days in December,” said Sam. “十二月的日子很短，”萨姆说。

December? thought Jack. When they’d landed at the White House, it had been March. Maybe Sam’s head injury had confused him. 十二月？杰克心想。 当他们降落在白宫时，那是三月。 也许山姆头部受伤让他搞混了。

“We’re not leaving you, Sam,” said Annie, “not until Sarah comes home.” “我们不会离开你，山姆，”安妮说，“直到莎拉回家之前都不会。”

“Don’t … have to stay,” said Sam, wincing with pain. “你们……不用留下，”山姆说，疼得皱起了眉头。

“We know we don’t have to,” said Jack. “But we want to.” “我们知道不用留下，”杰克说。 “但我们想留下。”

And he meant it. 他说的是真心话。
