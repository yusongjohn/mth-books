CHAPTER SIX

Trust the Magic

6. Trust the Magic 6. 相信魔法

Clouds hid the sun. Jack and Annie sat in a clump of dead weeds beside a dirt road in the countryside. A chilly wind blew the creaky limbs of bare trees. 乌云遮住了太阳。 杰克和安妮坐在乡间一条土路旁边的一堆枯草丛中。 一阵寒冷的风吹过光秃秃的树干，发出吱呀声。

“You okay?” asked Annie. “你没事吧？”安妮问。

“I think so,” said Jack. “Where are we?” “我想是吧，”杰克说。 “我们在哪儿？”

“Looks like we’re somewhere in the country,” said Annie. “看起来我们好像在乡下某个地方，”安妮说。

“No kidding, but where? Why?” said Jack. “真没想到，但到底在哪？为什么？”杰克说。

“Wait, wait,” said Annie. “Mr. Nicolay said if the president had a free moment, he’d take a ride in the country. I’ll bet we’ve come to a spot where we can catch Abraham Lincoln on his ride! Alone!” “等一下，等一下，”安妮说。 “先生。 尼科莱说，如果总统有空的话，他会去乡下兜风。 我敢打赌，我们来到一个可以碰到亚伯拉罕·林肯去乡下兜风的地方！而且他一个人！

“Oh, wow … cool,” said Jack. “哦，哇……太酷了，”杰克说。

“Look!” said Annie. “Someone’s coming this way now! On a horse!” “看！”安妮说。 “有人现在朝这边来了！骑着一匹马！”

A slim figure on a horse was coming down the dirt road. Jack and Annie jumped to their feet. When the rider on the bony white horse got closer, Jack sighed. “It’s not the president,” he said. “It’s just some kid on an old horse.” 一个瘦瘦的人骑着马正沿着泥路走下来。 杰克和安妮立刻跳了起来。 当骑着瘦骨嶙峋的白马的骑手走近时，杰克叹了口气。 “这不是总统，”他说。 “只是个骑着老马的小孩而已。”

“Maybe this kid is supposed to help us somehow,” said Annie. “Remember, trust the magic.” “也许这个小孩能以某种方式帮上忙，”安妮说。 “记住，要相信魔法。”

Jack nodded, but he couldn’t imagine the boy would be much help. He looked to be ten or maybe eleven years old. His matted black hair stuck out from under a coonskin cap. His thin face was dirty, and his buckskin pants and moccasins were stained and torn. A frayed burlap sack hung from his shoulder. 杰克点了点头，但他很难想象那个男孩能帮上什么忙。 他看起来十岁或者可能十一岁。 他乱糟糟的黑头发从浣熊皮帽下露出来。 他瘦瘦的脸很脏，鹿皮裤和鹿皮鞋也又脏又破。 一个破旧的麻布袋挂在了他的肩膀上。

Annie stepped into the road and waved. “Hello!” she called. 安妮走进了马路，挥了挥手。 “你好！”她喊道。

The boy pulled the old horse to a halt. He took off his cap and bowed his head. Then he put his cap back on and looked at them with tired gray eyes. “How do?” he said without a smile. 男孩把老马停了下来。 他摘下帽子，低头鞠了一躬。 然后他又把帽子戴回去，用疲惫的灰色眼睛看着他们。 “怎么了？”他说，脸上没有笑容。

“We do good,” said Annie. “We’re wondering if you can help us. We’re looking for Abraham Lincoln. Does he ride his horse around here? Have you ever seen him?” “我们做的是好事，”安妮说。 “我们在想，你能不能帮我们，” 我们在找亚伯拉罕·林肯。 他在这里骑马吗？ 你见过他吗？”，

The boy’s eyes brightened. “You’re looking for Abraham Lincoln?” he asked. 男孩的眼睛亮了起来。 “你们在找亚伯拉罕·林肯？”，他问。

“Yes, we are,” said Jack. “是的，我们在找他，”杰克说。

“Why?” the boy asked. “为什么？”，男孩问。

“Um … well, we just want to say hi to him,” said Jack. “Do you know if he goes riding in this area?” “嗯……好吧，我们只是想跟他打个招呼，”杰克说。 “你知道他是不是在这个地方骑马吗？”

The boy nodded. “He does,” he said. “In fact, he is in this area as we speak.” 男孩点了点头。 “是的，”他说。 “事实上，他现在就在我们说话的这个区域。”

“Really?” said Annie. She smiled at Jack, as if to say, See! The magic’s working! “真的吗？”安妮说。 她对杰克笑了笑，好像在说，看到了吧！ 魔法起作用了！

Jack couldn’t help smiling back. “So, can you tell us where we can find him?” he asked the boy. 杰克忍不住也笑了。 “那么，你能告诉我们在哪里可以找到他吗？”他问那个男孩。

“Yes,” said the boy, nodding. “But I think it’s better if I take you to him myself. I just have to grind some corn at the mill first.” “可以，”男孩点点头说。 “不过我觉得还是我亲自带你去见他比较好。” 我得先去磨坊磨点玉米。”

How long will that take? Jack wondered. How long will the president be riding in the countryside? 这要花多长时间？杰克想知道。 总统在乡下骑马要花多长时间？

“Maybe you could just tell us where we could find him,” said Jack. “We don’t have much time.” “也许你可以直接告诉我们在哪里能找到他，”杰克说。 “我们没多少时间了。”

“Wait,” said Annie. She whispered to Jack, “We have to trust the magic.” “等等，”安妮说。 她低声对杰克说：“我们必须相信魔法。”

Jack sighed. He looked back at the boy. “Okay, we’ll go to the mill with you,” he said, “but it would be good if we could hurry, so we don’t miss finding Abraham Lincoln.” 杰克叹了口气。 他回头看了看那个男孩。 “好吧，我们和你一起去磨坊，”他说，“但如果我们能快点的话，就不会错过找到亚伯拉罕·林肯了。”

“You won’t miss him. I give you my word,” said the boy. “Come along. The grinder’s around the bend. Giddyup, girl.” He shook his reins, and the old horse started plodding down the road again. “你们不会错过他的。 我向你们保证，”男孩说。 “快点走吧。 磨坊就在拐弯处。 快点，姑娘。”他摇晃着缰绳，老马又开始沿着路慢慢走起来。

Jack and Annie walked after the slow-moving horse. “Our names are Jack and Annie,” Annie called. “What’s yours?” 杰克和安妮跟在缓慢前行的马后面。 “我们的名字是杰克和安妮，”安妮喊道。 “你的名字是什么？”

“You can call me Sam,” the boy said over his shoulder. “你们可以叫我萨姆，”男孩回头说。

“Okay, Sam,” said Annie. “Thanks for helping us.” “好吧，萨姆，”安妮说。 “谢谢你帮我们。”

A gust of wind stirred the branches of the trees. The old horse neighed and stopped. “Keep going, girl,” said Sam. 一阵风吹动了树的枝条。 那匹老马嘶叫了一声，停了下来。 “继续走吧，姑娘，”萨姆说。

But the horse wouldn’t budge. 但那匹马一动也不动。

“She doesn’t hear well. She gets spooked by the wind,” Sam explained to Jack and Annie. “她听不太清楚。 她会被风吓到，”萨姆向杰克和安妮解释道。

The lonely sound of the wind spooked Jack, too. Something felt wrong. This weather was different from the weather at the White House. 风孤独的声音也吓到了杰克。 有什么地方感觉不对劲。 这天气和白宫的天气不一样。

“Giddyup, girl!” said Sam. “快点，姑娘！”，萨姆说。

The horse started plodding down the road again. When they rounded the bend, Jack saw a strange-looking machine in a clearing. It had a barrel-like container with a wooden beam attached to it. Metal rods hung from the end of the beam. 马又开始沿着路慢慢走起来。 当他们转过弯时，杰克看见空地上有一台奇怪的机器。 它有一个像桶一样的容器，上面连接着一根木梁。 木梁的末端挂着一些金属杆。

“What’s that?” said Jack. “那是什么？”，杰克问。

“The grinder,” said Sam. “You ain’t never seen one before?” “那是磨碎机，”，萨姆说。 “你以前从来没看过这种东西吗？”，

“Sure, we have,” said Annie. “当然看过，”，安妮说。

No one was tending the grinder or waiting to use it. Sam dropped his sack to the ground and dismounted. He was tall and skinny. His buckskin pants were too short for him. 没有人正在操作磨面机，也没有人在等着使用它。 萨姆把他的麻袋扔到地上，然后下了马。 他个子高，身体瘦。 他的鹿皮裤对他来说太短了。

“What’s in your bag?” asked Annie. “你的袋子里装的是什么？”安妮问。

“Twenty pounds of corn,” Sam said. “Shelled it all by hand.” “二十磅玉米，”萨姆说。 “都是用手剥出来的。”

“Wow,” said Annie. “哇，”安妮说。

Sam poured the corn kernels into a funnel over the barrel. Then he hitched his old horse to leather straps attached to the metal rods. 萨姆把玉米粒倒进一个漏斗，漏到桶里。 然后他把他的老马拴在固定在金属杆上的皮带上。

Jack and Annie stood to the side and watched Sam walk his horse around in a circle. After a while, Jack grew impatient. The corn grinding seemed to be taking forever. Before he could say anything, though, a gust of wind came up and the horse reared. 杰克和安妮站在一旁，看着萨姆牵着他的马绕着圈走。 过了一会儿，杰克变得不耐烦了。 玉米磨碎的过程似乎永远也结束不了。 就在他还没来得及说话的时候，一阵风吹来，马突然扬起了前蹄。

“Keep moving, girl!” said Sam. “继续走，姑娘！”萨姆说道。

The horse neighed and tossed her head. 马叫了一声，甩了甩头。

“Go on, girl! Giddyup!” said Sam. He slapped her backside. “Giddyup, I said!” “往前走，姑娘！ 驾！”萨姆说道。 他拍了拍马的屁股。 “我说了，驾！”

The horse didn’t budge. 马一动不动。

“These nice folks are waitin’ on us!” said Sam. He pushed the horse from behind. “这些好心人正等着我们呢！”萨姆说。 他从后面推了推马。

The wind picked up, tossing dead leaves into the air. The horse neighed again, then kicked out with her hind foot. Her hoof hit Sam in the head! His coonskin cap flew off as he fell backward and sprawled across the ground. 风起来了，把枯叶吹得满天飞。 马又叫了一声，然后用后腿踢了出去。 她的蹄子踢到了萨姆的头上！ 他倒在地上，帽子飞了出去，整个人摔得四仰八叉。

“Sam!” cried Annie. “萨姆！”安妮喊道。

Annie and Jack knelt in the dirt beside the boy. A trickle of blood ran down the side of his head. His eyes were closed. 安妮和杰克跪在男孩身边，沾满了泥土。 一缕血从他头的一侧流了下来。 他的眼睛闭着。

“Sam?” said Annie. “Can you hear me?” She wiped the blood with her apron. “萨姆？”安妮说。 “你能听到我吗？”她用围裙擦掉血。

Sam didn’t answer or open his eyes. 萨姆没有回答，也没有睁开眼睛。

“Hey, Sam!” Jack said loudly. “Wake up!” “嘿，萨姆！”杰克大声说。 “醒醒！”

But Sam didn’t move. He didn’t even seem to be breathing. 但萨姆没有动。 他甚至看起来好像都不在呼吸。

Jack and Annie looked at each other. 杰克和安妮互相看了看。

“Is he dead?” whispered Annie. “他死了吗？”安妮小声说。
