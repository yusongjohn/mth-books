CHAPTER TEN

Readin’ and Writin’

10. Readin’ and Writin’ 10. 阅读与写作

“We don’t mind,” said Annie. “我们不介意，”安妮说。

“Annie,” said Jack. “安妮，”杰克说。

But Sam opened the speller and handed it to Annie. “Parts of speech,” he said. 但萨姆打开了拼读本，把它递给了安妮。 “词性，”他说。

“Okay,” Annie said. “What is a conjunction?” “好的，”安妮说。 “什么是连词？”

Sam bit his lip. “Let’s see … a conjunction is a part of speech that joins words and sentences together,” he said. “Some conjunctions are and, but, and because.” 萨姆咬了咬嘴唇。 “让我们看看……连词是用来连接词语和句子的语法部分，”他说。 “一些连词是‘和’、‘但是’、‘因为’。”

“Perfect!” said Annie. “太棒了！”安妮说。

“Yes, perfect,” said Jack. “Here’s an example: Jack wants to leave, but Annie is ignoring him.” “是的，太棒了，”杰克说。 “这里有个例子：杰克想离开，但安妮在忽视他。”

“Good example,” said Annie. “What is an interjection?” she asked Sam. “好例子，”安妮说。 “什么是感叹词？”她问萨姆。

“That’s a part of speech that expresses a sudden passion of the mind,” said Sam, “such as ‘Alack!’ or ‘Alas!’ or ‘Fie!’ ” “那是用来表达突然情绪的语法部分，”萨姆说，“比如‘哎呀！’或‘唉！’或‘呸！’。”

“Good,” said Annie, laughing, “except Jack and I don’t use interjections like those. We express a passion of the mind by saying things like ‘Oh, man!’ or ‘Oh, wow!’ or ‘Whoa!’ ” “很好，”安妮笑着说，“不过杰克和我不会用这样的感叹词。 我们通过说‘哦，天哪！’或者‘哦，太棒了！’或者‘哇！’来表达内心的激动。

“Yes, that’s right,” said Jack, glaring at Annie. “Like ‘Oh, man, time is running out!’ Or ‘Oh, wow, the sun is going down!’ Or ‘Whoa, we have a mission to complete!’ ” “对，就是这样，”杰克盯着安妮说。 “比如‘哦，天哪，时间不多了！’或者‘哦，太棒了，太阳下山了！’或者‘哇，我们有任务要完成！’”

Annie laughed again. “Right, that’s how we use our interjections,” she said to Sam. “What else is in your book?” 安妮又笑了起来。 “没错，这就是我们使用感叹词的方式，”她对萨姆说。 “你书里还有什么？”

“Spelling and grammar rules,” said Sam. “And quotes from the Bible and fables.” “拼写和语法规则，”萨姆说。 “还有圣经和寓言里的句子。”

“Cool,” said Annie. “太酷了，”安妮说。

Sam closed his speller. “I only wish I had more books,” he said. “Anyone who’ll give me a book is my best friend. I’ll walk miles to borrow it.” 萨姆合上了他的拼写书。 “我只希望我能有更多书，”他说。 “任何愿意借我书的人，都是我最好的朋友。 我愿意走好几英里去借书。”

“Jack would, too,” said Annie. “And Jack and I both love to write, too. Don’t we?” She looked at Jack. “杰克也会这么做的，”安妮说。 “杰克和我也都超级喜欢写作， 不是吗？”她看着杰克。

“Yes, we do,” said Jack, sighing. “是的，我们确实喜欢，”杰克叹了口气说。

“Oh, I do, too!” said Sam. “Neither my pa nor my ma ever learned to write. But I love it. I write words in the dust or the sand, even in the snow. I write them in the dirt floor with a stick.” Sam laughed. Jack couldn’t help smiling. “Why, I write on wooden shovels with charcoal!” Sam leaned forward and said in a hushed voice, “But the best thing in the world to write with is my quill pen and my blackberry ink!” Sam’s face glowed in the firelight. “哦，我也超级喜欢！”萨姆说。 “我爸爸和妈妈从来没学过写字。但我超级喜欢。 我在灰尘里、沙子里，甚至在雪地里都写单词。” 我用树枝在泥地上写字。”萨姆笑了。 杰克忍不住笑了。 “嘿，我用木铲和木炭写字！”萨姆向前倾身，压低声音说：“但世界上最好用的写字工具是我的羽毛笔和黑莓墨水！”火光映得萨姆的脸闪闪发亮。

“Oh, wow, you do love to write,” said Jack. “So do I.” Jack forgot about Abraham Lincoln for the moment. “I love to make up my own stories.” “哦，哇，你真的很喜欢写字，”杰克说。 “我也是。”杰克暂时把亚伯拉罕·林肯忘了。 “我喜欢编自己的故事。”

Sam smiled. “Me too,” he said. “And now I want to tell you-all a good one. I meant to tell you this before, but I got kicked in the head before I could. I’m kind of famous for playing pranks on folks. But the two of you don’t deserve—” 萨姆笑了。 “我也是，”他说。 “现在我想给你们讲一个好故事。 我本来想早点告诉你们的，但在我能说出来之前，我被踢到了头。 我以捉弄别人而小有名气。 但你们两个不配—”

Suddenly noises came from outside: rumbling and neighing. 突然，外面传来声音：轰隆隆和嘶鸣声。

“What’s that?” said Annie. “那是什么？”安妮说。

Sam froze. Then he turned to Jack and Annie, his eyes wide. “A wagon!” he said. He jumped up and rushed to the entrance of the cabin and pushed aside the bearskin. 萨姆僵住了。 然后他转向杰克和安妮，眼睛睁得大大的。 “是马车！”他说。 他跳起来，冲向小屋的入口，把熊皮毯掀到一边。

“Pa!” Sam shouted, and he disappeared outside. “爸爸！”萨姆喊道，然后他消失在了外面。

“His dad’s back?” said Annie. “他爸爸回来了？”安妮说。

She and Jack hurried to the doorway and peeked out. Four horses were pulling a wagon through the cold dusk. The rickety wagon was filled with kids and furniture. 她和杰克赶紧跑到门口，偷偷往外看。 四匹马拉着一辆马车，穿过寒冷的黄昏。 那辆破旧的马车里装满了孩子和家具。

They watched as Sam ran toward the wagon and the driver pulled the horses to a halt. Sam’s pa jumped down from his bench and threw his arms around Sam. They hugged for a long time. 他们看着萨姆朝马车跑过去，车夫把马停下来了。 萨姆的爸爸从他的座位上跳下来，张开双臂抱住萨姆。 他们紧紧拥抱了好久。

Then a woman stepped down from the driver’s bench. Three children scrambled down from the back. They stood smiling and giggling beside her. 然后，一个女人从车夫的座位上走下来。 三个孩子从车后边慌慌张张地跳下来。 他们站在她旁边，笑着、咯咯地笑着。

“Son, I want you to meet my wife and your new ma from Kentucky,” Sam’s pa said. “And these are her children and your new sisters and brother, Elizabeth, Matilda, and John.” “儿子，我给你介绍一下，这是我的妻子，也是你来自肯塔基州的新妈妈，”萨姆的爸爸说。 “而且这些都是她的孩子，也是你的新姐妹和兄弟，伊丽莎白、玛蒂尔达和约翰。”

Each kid said “howdy” in turn. 每个孩子轮流说：“你好。”

“Howdy, son,” Sam’s new ma said. “I’ve so looked forward to meeting you. Thomas is awful proud of you and your sister. He says you’re a good reader and a good writer.” “你好，儿子，”萨姆的新妈妈说。 “我早就期待着见到你们。 托马斯对你和你姐姐非常自豪。 他说你是个很好的读者，也是一个很好的作家。”

“We hear you’re a good woodchopper, too!” said the boy named John. “我们听说你还是个很棒的伐木工！”名叫约翰的男孩说。

“And you like to tell stories!” said the girl named Elizabeth. “而且你还喜欢讲故事！”名叫伊丽莎白的女孩说。

“And play pranks!” said the girl named Matilda. “还喜欢恶作剧！”名叫玛蒂尔达的女孩说。

“We brought you some books!” John piped up. “我们给你带来了一些书！”约翰插话道。

“And a feather mattress!” said Elizabeth. “还有个羽毛床垫！”伊丽莎白说。

“And a washstand and some soap!” said Matilda. “Come look!” “还有个洗脸架和一些肥皂！”马蒂尔达说。 “快来看！”

The children grabbed Sam. He laughed as they pulled him toward the wagon and started showing him all the things they’d hauled from Kentucky. 孩子们抓住了萨姆。 他笑着，他们把他拉向马车，开始给他看他们从肯塔基州运来的东西。

Jack smiled. Sam wouldn’t be sad or lonely anymore, he thought. It made him feel happy to see such a good thing happen to Sam. 杰克笑了。 他想，萨姆再也不会伤心或孤单了。 看到萨姆发生这么好的事，他感到很高兴。

“Let’s slip outside and hide in the shed,” Jack said to Annie. “So we don’t have to explain where we came from.” “我们溜出去，躲到棚屋里吧，”杰克对安妮说。 “这样我们就不用解释我们是从哪儿来的了。”

Jack and Annie pushed past the bearskin into the shadows of twilight. They crept into the cowshed and peeked out. 杰克和安妮挤过熊皮，走进了黄昏的阴影里。 他们悄悄溜进牛棚，往外张望。

“Pa?” someone shouted. “Paaaa!!!” “爸爸？”有人喊道。 “爸爸！！！”

Across the clearing, a girl came running. She wore a black cape with a hood. 在空地的另一边，一个女孩跑了出来。 她穿着一件带兜帽的黑色斗篷。

“Sarah! My girl!” Sam and Sarah’s father rushed forward and threw his arms around his daughter. “莎拉！ 我的女儿！”萨姆和莎拉的父亲冲了过去，张开双臂拥抱女儿。

Sarah started sobbing. 莎拉开始抽泣。

Her father hugged her. “Don’t cry, girl,” he said. “I brought you a whole new family. We’ll all take good care of each other now. Come on, let’s go in, and you can meet everyone. You’ll love them all, Sarah, I promise. I give you my word.” 她的父亲紧紧地抱着她。 “别哭，孩子，”他说。 “我给你带来了一个全新的家庭。 现在，我们都会好好照顾彼此。 来吧，我们进去，你就能见到大家了。 萨拉，我保证你会喜欢他们的。 我向你发誓。”

As everyone headed into the cabin, Matilda exclaimed, “My goodness, you built this by hand?” 当大家都走进小屋时，玛蒂尔达惊呼道，“天哪，你亲手建了这个房子吗？”

“What a wonderful job you did,” said Sam’s new ma. “你做得太棒了，”萨姆的新妈妈说道。

“It’s going to get better,” said Sam’s pa. “We’re going to make a real door, aren’t we, boys? And we’ll make a real floor with wood, patch the roof, and put mud in the chinks between the logs.” “情况会越来越好的，”萨姆的爸爸说道。 “我们要做一个真正的门，对吧，孩子们？” 而且我们会用木头做真正的地板，修补屋顶，还在木头之间填上泥土。”

“Yes, sir,” said Sam and John together. “是的，先生，”萨姆和约翰一起说道。

Thomas held the bearskin for his wife and all the children. Then he followed them inside. 托马斯为他的妻子和所有孩子拿着熊皮。 然后他跟着他们进去了。

Jack and Annie could hear the sounds of happy conversation coming from the cabin. 杰克和安妮能听到小屋里传来人们愉快交谈的声音。

“Wow, what a day to be with Sam, huh?” Annie said to Jack. “哇，和萨姆一起度过的这一天真棒，对吧？”安妮对杰克说。

“Really,” said Jack. “But what should we do now?” “真的吗，”杰克说。 “但是我们现在应该做什么呢？”

“I don’t know,” said Annie. “I think Sam forgot us in all the excitement.” “我不知道，”安妮说。 “我想萨姆在这么兴奋的场面中把我们给忘了。”

“Like when Tad forgot me under the bed in the White House,” said Jack. “就像塔德在白宫的床上把我忘掉的时候，”杰克说。

“Jack! Annie!” Sam came running out of the cabin, calling to them in the fading light. “杰克！ “安妮！”萨姆从小屋跑出来，朝着他们喊叫，天色渐渐暗了下来。

“He didn’t forget us!” said Annie. “他没有忘记我们！”安妮说。

She and Jack stepped out of the cowshed. 她和杰克从牛棚里走了出来。

“We’re here!” Annie called. “我们在这里！”安妮喊道。

“I want to give you something!” said Sam. He held up a quill pen and a small bottle. “I told you about these. The pen’s made from the feather of a goose, and the ink’s from the roots of a blackberry bush. I want you to have them.” “我想送你们一些东西！”萨姆说。 他举起了一支鹅毛笔和一个小瓶子。 “我告诉过你们这些。 这支笔是用鹅的羽毛做的，墨水是从黑莓灌木的根部提取的。 我想让你拥有它们。”

“Oh, no, Sam,” said Jack. “You keep them. You need them more.” “哦，不，萨姆，”杰克说。 “你留着它们吧。 你更需要它们。”

“Take ’em,” said Sam. “I want to thank you for staying by me when I was feeling poorly, and for trying to do my chores. Your kindness truly helped me.” “拿去吧，”萨姆说。 “我想感谢你在我身体不舒服的时候一直陪在我身边，还努力帮我做家务。 你的善良真的帮助了我。”

“But we didn’t do any chores, not one!” said Annie. “但我们什么家务都没做，一件都没做！”安妮说。

“You tried, though,” said Sam. “And most important, you both love what I love most: readin’ and writin’. Please.” Sam handed Jack the quill pen and the ink bottle. “Use them to write something special.” “不过你尝试了，”萨姆说。 “而且最重要的是，你们两个都和我一样，最喜欢读书和写字。” 请。”萨姆把羽毛笔和墨水瓶递给杰克。 “用它们写点特别的东西。”

“We will,” said Annie. “I can carry them, Jack.” He handed her Sam’s treasures, and she put them into her apron pocket. “Thank you.” “我们会的，”安妮说。 “我可以带它们，杰克。”他把萨姆的珍宝递给她，她把它们放进围裙口袋里。 “谢谢。”

“You’re welcome,” said Sam. “And what I was going to tell you is—” “不用谢，”萨姆说。 “我想告诉你的事情是——”

“Yes—” Jack started. “是——”杰克开始说。

But before Jack could finish, a WHOOSH and a ROAR shook the earth, like a speeding train passing by. The ground opened, and Jack felt as if he were falling through space, 但杰克还没说完，一阵呼啸声和咆哮声震动了大地，就像一列飞驰的火车经过一样。 地面裂开了，杰克感觉好像自己正从空间中掉下去。

through a tunnel,

down through blackness,

into a world of daylight. 穿过隧道，穿过黑暗，来到一个充满阳光的世界。
