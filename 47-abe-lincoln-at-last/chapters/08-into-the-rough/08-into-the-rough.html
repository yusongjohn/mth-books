CHAPTER EIGHT

Into the Rough

Jack and Annie huddled in the loft near Sam. As the wind whistled between the logs, Jack could feel the boy’s sadness. 杰克和安妮在阁楼靠近山姆的地方挤在一起。 风在木头之间呼啸，杰克能感受到男孩的悲伤。

“Thank you,” Sam said. “But I have to get up now—have to do chores—help Sarah.” “谢谢，”萨姆说。 “但现在我得起床了——得干活——要帮莎拉。”

“No, not now,” said Annie. “Maybe Sarah can take care of your chores when she gets home.” “不，现在不行，”安妮说。 “也许莎拉回家后可以帮你做家务。”

“She’ll be too tired,” said Sam. “She has to walk a long way home. With Pa gone, she can’t sleep—hears wolves and wildcats all night.” “她会太累了，”萨姆说。 “她得走很远的路才能回家。 爸爸不在家，她睡不着——整晚都能听到狼和野猫的声音。”

“Really? Are there wolves and wildcats around here?” said Jack. “真的吗？ 这里附近有狼和野猫吗？”杰克问。

“Plenty,” Sam said. “I have to do my chores—” He tried to sit up. “很多，”萨姆说。 “我得做家务——”他试着坐起来。

“Not until you feel better,” Annie said firmly. “You lie here and rest. We’ ll do your chores. Just tell us what to do. We’ll be happy to do it. Won’t we, Jack?” “等你感觉好些再说，”安妮坚定地说。 “你在这里躺着休息。 我们会帮你做家务。 只要告诉我们该做什么。 我们很乐意做。不是吗，杰克？”

“Uh, sure …,” said Jack. “What are your chores, Sam?” “呃，当然……，”杰克说。 “萨姆，你的家务是什么？”

Sam lay back and took a deep breath. “Split wood,” he said, closing his eyes, “milk cow, get water from spring …” 萨姆躺回床上，深吸了一口气。 “劈木头，”他说，闭上眼睛，“挤牛奶，从泉水里取水……”

Jack slipped the pencil and notebook out of his back pocket and wrote: 杰克从他背口袋里拿出铅笔和笔记本，开始写下来：

split wood
milk cow
get water from spring

“Where’s the spring?” asked Jack. “泉水在哪里？”杰克问。

“Just a mile away, through the rough,” said Sam. “就在一英里之外，穿过那片荒地，”萨姆说。

“The rough?” said Jack. “荒地？”杰克说。

“No problem,” said Annie. “Anything else to do?” “没问题，”安妮说。 “还有别的事情要做吗？”，

“Make corn bread, then do homework in speller book …,” said Sam. “做玉米面包，然后在拼读课本上做作业……，”萨姆说。

Jack added to his list: 杰克在他的清单上又加上：

make corn bread
homework in speller book

“That’s it?” said Jack. “就这些？”杰克问。

“Yes,” said Sam. “是的，”萨姆说。

“Good. We can do that!” said Annie. “很好。 我们可以做到！“安妮说。

We can? thought Jack. Milk cow? Make corn bread? And what’s “the rough”? 我们可以？杰克想。 挤牛奶？做玉米面包？ 那“粗糙的地方”是什么？

“Sam, where’s the rough?” asked Jack. “萨姆，‘粗糙的地方’在哪里？”杰克问。

But Sam had fallen asleep. 但萨姆已经睡着了。

“Sam?” said Jack. “萨姆？”杰克说。

“Shhh, let him sleep,” Annie whispered. “嘘，让他睡觉，”安妮小声说。

Jack nodded. He followed Annie down from the loft and across the dirt floor. She pushed aside the bearskin, and they stepped out of the cabin. 杰克点了点头。 他跟着安妮从阁楼下来，穿过泥土地面。 她把熊皮毯子掀开一边，他们走出了小屋。

“Why did you promise to do Sam’s chores?” said Jack. “We don’t know how to do all that stuff.” “你为什么答应帮萨姆干活？”杰克说。 “我们不知道怎么干那些活。”

“It was the only way to keep him from trying to work,” said Annie. “He really needs to rest. Don’t worry. We can figure them out. What’s first?” “那是唯一的方法，才能阻止他干活，”安妮说。 “他真的需要休息。别担心。 我们可以想办法。先做什么？”

Jack looked at their list. 杰克看了看他们的清单。

“Split wood,” he said. “劈柴，”他说。

“How hard could that be?” said Annie. “There’s the woodpile. There’s the ax.” She strode over to a stack of wood in the front yard. An ax was sunk into a fat log. “这能有多难呢？”安妮说。 “木堆在这儿。 那儿有把斧头。”她走到前院的一堆木头上。 一把斧头深深地嵌在一根粗壮的木头里。

Annie rubbed her hands together, then wrapped them around the ax’s long handle. She pulled and pulled, but the ax didn’t budge. 安妮搓了搓手，然后紧紧抓住斧头长长的把手。 她使劲拉啊拉啊，但斧头一动不动。

“Let me try,” said Jack. Annie stepped aside. Jack gripped the handle and pulled as hard as he could. But the ax stayed in the log. “让我来试试，”杰克说。 安妮退到一边。 杰克紧紧握住把手，用尽全力拉。 但斧头还是牢牢地卡在木头里。

“Forget it,” said Jack. “It’s like trying to pull the sword from the stone.” “忘了它吧，”杰克说。 “这就像试图从石头里拔出剑一样。”

Annie laughed. “I guess we’re not meant to be king,” she said. “So, what’s next?” 安妮笑了。 “我想我们本来就不该当国王，”她说。 “那接下来我们做什么？”

“Milk cow,” Jack read from his list. “牛奶牛，”杰克从他的清单上读道。

“All righty,” Annie said cheerfully. She led the way to the shed next to the cabin. “好啊，”安妮开心地说。 她带领大家走向小屋旁边的棚屋。

Inside the shed, a cow was eating hay and swishing her tail. A three-legged stool and a tin pail stood in the corner. 棚屋里，一头牛正在吃干草，甩着尾巴。 一个三条腿的凳子和一个铁桶站在角落里。

“You try first,” said Annie. “你先试试，”安妮说。

“Me?” said Jack. “我？”杰克说。

“I tried the ax first,” said Annie. “我先试了斧头，”安妮说。

Jack put the pail under the cow and moved the stool close to her. Then he sat down. 杰克把桶放在牛下面，然后把凳子搬到牛旁边，接着他坐了下来。

The cow gave Jack a look. Then she whipped him in the face with her tail. 牛看了杰克一眼。 然后她用尾巴抽了杰克的脸一下。

“Oww!” said Jack. He leaned forward and stared at the cow’s udder. “啊！”杰克说。 他向前倾身，盯着牛的乳房看。

Jack looked up at Annie. “I have no idea what to do,” he said. 杰克抬头看向安妮。 “我不知道该怎么做，”他说。

Annie laughed again. “Me neither,” she said. “We’ll come back to this, too. What’s next?” 安妮又笑了。 “我也没见过，”她说。 “我们之后还会回来的。接下来是什么？”

Jack jumped up from the stool and looked at their list. “Get water from the spring,” he said. 杰克从凳子上跳起来，看了看他们的清单。 “从泉眼那儿取水，”他说。

“I saw two jugs by the door,” said Annie. “I’ll get them.” She ran to the cabin and came back a moment later with two brown jugs. “我看到门边有两个水罐，”安妮说。 “我去拿。”她跑到小屋，片刻后拿着两个棕色的水罐回来了。

“Heavy,” Annie warned. She gave one to Jack. “很重，”安妮警告道。 她把一个给了杰克。

The jug was surprisingly heavy. “They’ll be heavier with water,” said Jack. “This isn’t going to be easy, since the spring is a mile away, ‘through the rough.’ Whatever that means.” 水罐出乎意料地很重。 “它们装满水后会更重，”杰克说。 “这不会很容易，因为泉水在一英里外，‘穿过崎岖的地形’。不知道那是什么意思。”

“I’ll bet that’s the rough over there,” said Annie. She pointed to the woods on the other side of the clearing. “我打赌那就是那边的崎岖地形，”安妮说。 她指着空地另一边的树林。

Jack and Annie wound their way through the stumps and stone piles until they came to the woodsy area, thick with underbrush. Wild grapevines twisted through bushes and around bare branches of small trees, binding it all together. 杰克和安妮绕过树桩和石堆，来到一片茂密的树林，灌木丛生。 野葡萄藤缠绕在灌木丛中，绕过小树的光秃枝干，把它们都缠在一起。

“It looks rough all right,” said Jack. “看起来确实很崎岖，”杰克说。

Annie pointed to a narrow path. “I’ll bet that’s how to get to the spring,” she said. “Want to give it a try?” 安妮指着一条狭窄的小路。 “我打赌那就是去泉水的路，”她说。 “想试试看吗？”

“Sure,” said Jack. “Let’s go.” “当然可以，”杰克说。 “我们走吧。”

Carrying the jugs, Jack and Annie started down the path. They pushed aside tangled vines and branches. Crows, sparrows, and woodpeckers swooped overhead. Squirrels ran up and down the small, bare trees. 杰克和安妮拿着水罐，沿着小路往下走。 他们拨开缠绕在一起的藤蔓和树枝。 乌鸦、麻雀和啄木鸟从头顶飞过。 松鼠在那些矮小、光秃秃的树上跑上跑下。

Down the path, the rough got rougher. The path nearly disappeared. The tangle of undergrowth was so thick that Jack began to lose what little hope he had. 沿着小路往下，路变得越来越难走。 小路几乎看不见了。 杂草丛生，密密麻麻的，杰克开始觉得自己那一点点希望也快没了。

“I can’t see pushing our way through this stuff for a whole mile,” he said. “我看不到我们能在这片杂草丛里挤着走一英里，”他说。

“Me neither,” said Annie. “Let’s go just a little further and see if it opens up again.” “我也是，”安妮说。 “我们再往前走一点，看看它会不会再次打开。”

Jack and Annie pushed past more brambles and vines. “This whole journey is leading nowhere,” Jack grumbled. “No spring water, no split wood, no cow’s milk. Worst of all, no Abraham Lincoln. We’ve missed our only chance to use the magic to have a private meeting with him.” 杰克和安妮拨开更多的荆棘和藤蔓。 “这次旅行一点头绪也没有，”杰克抱怨道。 “没有泉水，没有劈开的木头，没有牛奶。 最糟糕的是，没有亚伯拉罕·林肯。 我们错过了唯一一次用魔法和他私下见面的机会。”

“I know,” said Annie. “But we couldn’t just leave Sam to try to do his chores. Offering to help him was the right thing to do.” “我知道，”安妮说。 “但我们不能就这样丢下萨姆，让他一个人做家务。 帮助他做家务是正确的事。”

“I know,” said Jack. “我知道，”杰克说。

“It’s weird,” said Annie. “Even though helping Sam isn’t part of our mission, I feel like doing one good thing is somehow connected to doing another good thing. If we’re helping Sam, we’re also helping Penny.” “这太奇怪了，”安妮说。 “虽然帮助萨姆不是我们的任务，但我感觉做一件好事似乎和做另一件好事有某种联系。 如果我们帮助萨姆，我们也在帮助彭妮。”

“Yeah …,” said Jack. Despite his worries, he agreed with what Annie said. “There’s only one problem: we have to get this feather from—” “是啊……，”杰克说。 尽管他很担心，但他同意安妮说的话。 “只有一个问题：我们必须从——”

“Yikes!” said Annie. “天哪！”，安妮说。

Jack looked back at her. “Yikes, what? You forgot we had to get a feather?” 杰克回头看着她。 “天哪，怎么了？ 你忘记我们要找一根羽毛了吗？

“No. Yikes, did you hear that?” she whispered. “没有。” 天啊，你听到那声音了吗？”，她小声说。

“Hear what?” whispered Jack. He held his breath and listened. “听到什么？”杰克小声问。 他屏住呼吸，仔细听了起来。

“A growl,” whispered Annie. “是低吼声，”安妮小声说。

Jack looked around, his heart pounding. “Like, uh—a wildcat growl? Or a wolf growl?” he asked. 杰克环顾四周，心跳加速。 “比如说——像野猫的低吼？ 还是狼的低吼？”他问。

“Like— that growl,” said Annie. “就是那种低吼，”安妮说。

Jack heard the long, low growl. He heard twigs breaking. The hair went up on the back of his neck. 杰克听到了一声长长的、低沉的咆哮。 他听到树枝断裂的声音。 他的后颈毛发都竖了起来。
