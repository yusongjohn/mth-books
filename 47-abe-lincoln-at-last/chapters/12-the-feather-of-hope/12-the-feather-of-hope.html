CHAPTER TWELVE

The Feather of Hope

12. The Feather of Hope 12. 希望之羽

A spring breeze was whispering through the trees. “We’re home,” said Jack. They were back in Frog Creek, wearing their own clothes again. Jack looked in his backpack and pulled out their book on Abraham Lincoln. 一阵春风在树间轻轻吹拂。 “我们到家了，”杰克说。 他们又回到了蛙溪镇，穿着自己的衣服。 杰克看了看他的背包，拿出了一本关于亚伯拉罕·林肯的书。

Annie reached into one of her jacket pockets. “Good, they’re still here!” she said. She took out Abraham Lincoln’s gifts: the bottle of blackberry ink and the goose-feather pen. 安妮把手伸进她夹克口袋里。 “太好了，它们还在！”，她说。 她拿出了亚伯拉罕·林肯送的礼物：一瓶黑莓墨水和一支鹅毛笔。

“Cool,” said Jack. “真酷，”杰克说。

“Before we go, I want to look in our book and see if there’s a picture of Willie and Tad,” said Annie. She took the book from Jack and checked the index. “Yes!” Then she turned to a page on the Lincoln children. “在我们出发之前，我想在我们的书里看看有没有威利和塔德的照片，”安妮说。 她从杰克手里接过书，翻到了目录部分。 “有！”，然后她翻到一页关于林肯孩子的内容。

Annie read for a moment, then she whispered a sad “Oh, no.” She closed the book and put it down. She looked terribly sad. 安妮读了一会儿，然后小声说了一个悲伤的“哦，不”。她合上书，把它放下了。 她看起来非常难过。

“What’s wrong? What did you read?” said Jack. “怎么了？ “你读了什么？”杰克说。

“I just read that Willie died of typhoid fever in 1862,” said Annie. “我刚读到威利在1862年死于伤寒，”安妮说。

“Oh, no,” said Jack. “That was the year after we met him.” “哦，不，”杰克说。 “那是我们遇见他之后的那一年。”

“Poor Abraham Lincoln,” said Annie. “可怜的亚伯拉罕·林肯，”安妮说。

“Poor Tad,” said Jack. “可怜的塔德，”杰克说。

“Yeah, he really needed Willie,” said Annie. “是啊，他真的很需要威利，”安妮说。

“And then Tad will lose his dad just four years later,” said Jack. “President Lincoln will get assassinated.” “然后四年后，塔德会失去他的爸爸，”杰克说。 “林肯总统会被暗杀。”

“I know,” said Annie softly. “我知道，”安妮轻声说。

Jack didn’t know what to say. He felt like the wind had been knocked out of him. All his annoyance at Tad disappeared. He wished he’d been kinder to him. 杰克不知道该说什么。 他感觉好像被人抽走了风一样。 他对塔德的所有不满都消失了。 他希望当时能对他更友善一些。

“It doesn’t make sense, does it?” said Annie. “这根本不合道理，对吧？”安妮说。

“I guess that’s why hope is such an important thing,” Jack said. “我想这就是为什么希望这么重要，”杰克说。

“What do you mean?” said Annie. “你是什么意思？”安妮说。

“We can’t explain why sad things happen,” Jack said. “All we can do is hope they make sense someday.” “我们无法解释为什么悲伤的事情会发生，”杰克说。 “我们唯一能做的就是希望有一天它们能变得有意义。”

“Like when?” said Annie. “When will they make sense?” “什么时候呢？”安妮说。 “他们什么时候才能明白呢？”

“I don’t know when,” said Jack. “Maybe not even in a person’s lifetime. Maybe in a world beyond this world.” He sighed. “Maybe we just have to accept that it’s a mystery.” “我不知道什么时候，”杰克说。 “也许就连人的一生里都不会发生。” 也许在我们这个世界之外的另一个世界里。” 他叹了口气。 “也许我们只能接受，这只是一个谜。”

Annie nodded, blinking back tears. “Well, we brought back the feather of hope,” she said. 安妮点点头，努力忍住眼泪。 “好吧，我们带回了希望的羽毛，”她说。

“And we gave hope to Abraham Lincoln,” said Jack. “我们还把希望送给了亚伯拉罕·林肯，”杰克说。

“And he did really great things as president, didn’t he?” Annie said. “他当总统的时候真的做了很多好事，对不对？”安妮说。

“Yeah, and he was a great dad, too,” said Jack. “是啊，他还是个很棒的爸爸，”杰克说。

Jack placed the feather of hope in the corner of the tree house next to the glacial buttercup and the emerald rose. 杰克把希望的羽毛放在树屋角落，挨着冰川报春花和翡翠玫瑰。

“There’s one more thing to get to save Penny,” said Annie. “还有一件事要去做才能救出彭妮，”安妮说。

“Tomorrow,” said Jack. “明天，”杰克说。

“Definitely,” said Annie. “Today I just want to live my normal life: have breakfast with Mom and Dad—” “肯定，”安妮说。 “今天我只想过正常的生活：和爸爸妈妈一起吃早饭——”

“And go to school,” added Jack. “然后去上学，”杰克补充道。

“We’re lucky we can go to school,” said Annie. “我们能去上学真幸运，”安妮说道。

“Yeah,” said Jack. “And we’re lucky to have a nice house with heat and running water.” “是啊，”杰克说。 “而且我们很幸运，有一栋有暖气和自来水的房子。”

“And comfortable beds,” said Annie. She started down the rope ladder. “还有舒适的床，”安妮说。 她开始顺着绳梯往下爬。

“And lots of books,” said Jack. He grabbed his backpack and climbed down after her. “还有很多书，”杰克说。 他拿起背包，跟着她爬了下来。

As Jack and Annie headed home between the trees, a breeze shook the wet branches. Sparkling raindrops filled the Frog Creek woods. 当杰克和安妮穿过树林回家时，一阵风吹动了湿漉漉的树枝。 闪闪发光的雨滴洒满了蛙溪镇的森林。
