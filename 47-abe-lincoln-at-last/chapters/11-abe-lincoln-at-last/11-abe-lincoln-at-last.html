CHAPTER ELEVEN Abe Lincoln at Last! 11. 最后见到亚伯拉罕·林肯！

J ack and Annie looked around in a daze. They were under the trees near the tree house, standing in the same spot where they’d sipped the potion. The air was chilly but bright. A fresh breeze rustled the branches. 杰克和安妮一脸茫然地四处张望。 他们站在树屋附近的树下，站在他们喝下魔药时的同一个地方。 空气有点冷，但很明亮。 一阵清新的微风轻轻吹动了树枝。

“The magic ended,” said Jack, stunned. “It ended before we could complete our mission.” “魔法结束了，”杰克惊讶地说。 “它在我们完成任务之前就结束了。”

“I know,” Annie said. “And we didn’t get to say good-bye to Sam.” “我知道，”安妮说。 “我们都没来得及跟萨姆说再见。”

“I didn’t even thank him for the quill pen and the ink,” said Jack. “我甚至都没感谢他送的鹅毛笔和墨水，”杰克说。

“I know. These were his treasures,” said Annie. She held up the goose feather and the ink jar that Sam had given them. “我知道。 这些都是他的宝贝，”安妮说。 她举起了萨姆送给他们的鹅毛和墨水瓶。

“Wait, that’s so weird,” said Jack. “We’re looking for a feather, and Sam gives us a pen made out of a feather—” “等等，这太奇怪了，”杰克说。 “我们在找一根羽毛，而萨姆却给了我们一支用羽毛做的笔——”

Annie gasped. “Look, Jack!” She pointed toward the carriageway. 安妮惊呼起来。 “看，杰克！”她指着马车道说。

A tall man in a dark coat and a high black hat was striding toward the grove of trees. He turned his head, as if he were searching for something. 一个穿着深色外套、戴着高高黑帽子的高个男人正朝树林走去。 他转过头，好像在寻找什么东西。

“At last!” said Jack. “终于！”杰克说道。

“Mr. President!” Annie called. She thrust the quill pen and ink bottle back into her pocket and ran toward Abraham Lincoln. “总统”， 安妮喊道。 她把羽毛笔和墨水瓶塞回口袋，朝亚伯拉罕·林肯跑去。

“Wait!” said Jack, running after her. “What’ll we say?” “等一下！”杰克一边喊一边追了上去。 “我们该说什么？”

“We’ll figure it out!” said Annie. “Mr. President!” “我们会想出来的！”安妮说道。 “总统！”

Abraham Lincoln turned and looked in their direction. He froze and stared at them, as if he were both astonished and afraid. 亚伯拉罕·林肯转过身，朝他们的方向看去。 他愣住了，惊讶又害怕地看着他们。

What’s wrong with him? thought Jack. As they got closer to the president, Jack and Annie slowed to a walk. Abraham Lincoln kept staring at them as if they were ghosts. 他怎么了？杰克心想。 他们越靠近总统，杰克和安妮就走得越慢。 亚伯拉罕·林肯一直盯着他们，好像他们是从鬼魂里出来的。

“Hello, sir,” Annie said shyly. “您好，先生，”安妮害羞地说。

Jack was speechless. 杰克一句话也说不出来。

The creases in the president’s face gave him a deeply worried look. His gray eyes stared at them without blinking. “So it is you,” he said in a hushed voice. “Tad told me your names, but I could not believe it might really be you.” 总统脸上深深的皱纹让他看起来非常担心。 他灰蓝色的眼睛一直盯着他们，一眨都不眨。 “真的是你们，”他低声说。 “塔德告诉我你们的名字，但我一直不敢相信真的会是你们。”

“What do you mean?” Annie asked. “你什么意思？”安妮问。

“You don’t know who I am?” he said. “你不知道我是谁吗？”他说。

“You’re Abraham Lincoln,” said Annie. “President of the United States.” “你就是亚伯拉罕·林肯，”安妮说道。 “美国总统。”

“Yes, but I spent the day with you once long ago,” said the president. “And you vanished, right before my eyes.” “是的，但很久以前我曾经和你度过了一天，”总统说。 “你就在我的眼前消失了。”

“We did?” said Annie. “我们真的做到了？”安妮说。

“Outside our log cabin in Indiana,” said the president. “在印第安纳州我们小木屋的外面，”总统说。

“Indiana?” said Jack. “印第安纳州？”杰克说。

“Yes, it was the day my father brought my stepmother home—and my new sisters and brother.” “是的，那天我父亲把我继母接回家，还接回了我的新妹妹和弟弟。”

“Oh … oh!” said Annie. “哦……哦！”安妮说。

“What?” said Jack. “什么？”杰克说。

“You were Sam!” said Annie. “你就是山姆！”安妮说。

“You were Sam?” said Jack. He couldn’t believe it. “你就是山姆？”杰克说。 他简直不敢相信。

The president nodded. 总统点了点头。

Annie laughed. “So when we told you we were looking for Abraham Lincoln, you played a prank on us and told us your name was Sam!” 安妮笑了。 “所以当我们告诉你我们在寻找亚伯拉罕·林肯时，你骗了我们，说自己叫山姆！”

Abraham Lincoln smiled. “I haven’t seen you since that day so long ago,” he said. “And you haven’t changed at all. I don’t understand. Are you angels? Are you a dream?” 亚伯拉罕·林肯笑了。 “我很久很久以前就见过你了，”他说。 “你一点都没变。” 我不明白。你们是天使吗？ 你是梦吗？

Jack was too stunned to answer. It wasn’t long ago, it was today, he thought. Or—maybe not. Time and magic were confusing things. 杰克太震惊了，说不出话来。 这不是很久以前的事，是今天，他想。或者——可能不是。 时间和魔法都是让人困惑的东西。

“We’re just regular kids, not angels,” said Annie. “But maybe you should think of it all as a dream—a dream with a little magic thrown in.” “我们只是普通的孩子，不是天使，”安妮说。 “但也许你应该把它都当作一个梦——一个有点魔法的梦。”

Abraham Lincoln nodded slowly. Then he smiled. “I remember you tried to do my chores,” he said, “and you thought some wild creature was chasing you. And you told me that your interjections were ‘Oh, wow!’ and ‘Oh, man!’ ” 亚伯拉罕·林肯慢慢地点头。然后他笑了。 “我记得你们曾试图帮我做家务，”他说，“你们还以为有只野兽在追你们。” 而且你告诉我，你的感叹词是“哦，哇！”和“哦，天哪！”

“Right,” said Jack, smiling. “对，”杰克笑着说。

“You also said you loved learning and you loved to read,” said the president. “And you loved to write stories.” “你刚才还说你喜欢学习，也喜欢读书，”总统说。 “你还喜欢写故事。”

“And you said you loved to do that, too,” said Annie. “So you gave us these.” She pulled the ink bottle and the quill pen out of her apron pocket. “These were yours once, remember?” “你当时还说你也喜欢做这些事，”安妮说。 “所以你给了我们这些。”她从围裙口袋里拿出墨水瓶和羽毛笔。 “这些以前是你们的，还记得吗？”

Abraham Lincoln stared at the ink bottle and feather pen. “Yes,” he said. “I made them from blackberry roots and a goose feather.” 亚伯拉罕·林肯盯着墨水瓶和羽毛笔。 “是的，”他说。 “我用黑莓的根和一只鹅的羽毛做的。”

“Oh, man,” whispered Jack. For the first time it fully dawned on him that Sam—who was really Abraham Lincoln—had given them a feather! The rhyme was starting to make perfect sense! “天啊，”杰克小声说。 这是他第一次完全明白，萨姆——其实他是亚伯拉罕·林肯——给了他们一根羽毛！ 这句押韵的诗开始变得完全有意义了！

“Why have you come back?” asked the president. “你们为什么回来？”总统问道。

Now Jack knew exactly what to say. “We have to give you a message of hope,” he answered. He reached for the notebook in his pocket. 现在杰克知道该说什么了。 “我们必须给你带来一条希望的信息，”他回答道。 他伸手从口袋里拿出笔记本。

“Jack’s right,” said Annie. “Just a second.” She opened the ink bottle and dipped the goose-feather pen into the ink. Then she handed the pen to Jack. “What should we say?” she whispered. “杰克说得对，”安妮说。 “等一下。”她打开墨水瓶，把鹅毛笔浸入墨水中。 然后她把笔递给了杰克。 “我们该说什么呢？”她小声说。

“Well, the Civil War is going to have a good ending,” Jack whispered back. “All the country will come together.” “嗯，内战会有个好结局，”杰克小声回答。 “整个国家都会团结在一起。”

“With freedom for everybody,” whispered Annie. “人人享有自由，”安妮小声说。

“I’ll write something about all that,” said Jack. He thought for a second, and using the goose-feather pen, he scratched a message on a page in his notebook: “我要写点关于这些的内容，”杰克说。 他想了想，用鹅毛笔在笔记本的一页上写了一条信息：

Never lose hope. This land will live
peacefully as one nation one day,
with freedom for everyone.

“You told us to use your quill pen and your blackberry ink to write something special,” Jack said. He tore out the page and handed it to the president of the United States. “This is it.” “你告诉我们用你的羽毛笔和黑莓墨水写点特别的东西，”杰克说。 他撕下那页纸，递给了美国总统。 “就是这个。”

Abraham Lincoln read the words on the paper. When he looked at Jack and Annie, the creases in his face had softened. His eyes had grown bright. “Oh, wow,” he said softly. 亚伯拉罕·林肯读了纸上的字。 当他看着杰克和安妮时，他脸上的皱纹变得柔和了。 他的眼睛变得明亮了。 “哦，哇，”他轻声说道。

Jack and Annie laughed. 杰克和安妮笑了。

“Do you really think so?” the president asked. “Do you promise?” “你们真的这么认为吗？”总统问道。 “你能保证吗？”

“Yes. I need to add something,” said Jack. He took the note back from the president and wrote: “能。 我需要加点东西，”杰克说。 他从总统那里拿回纸条，开始写：

We give you our word.
—Jack and Annie

A shout came from the distance: “Pa! Pa!” 远处传来一声喊叫：“爸爸！爸爸！”

It was Tad. He was running up the carriageway, with Willie right behind him. 这是塔德。 他正沿着车道跑，威利紧跟着他。

“Mr. President, we have to leave now,” said Jack. “总统先生， 我们必须现在离开了，”杰克说。

“Really?” said Abraham Lincoln. He looked sad for a moment. Then he looked at his boys running toward him. “Yes, of course, I understand,” he said. “真的吗？”亚伯拉罕·林肯说。 他看起来有一瞬间很伤心。 然后他看着朝他跑来的男孩们。 “是的，当然，我明白，”他说。

“We’ll never forget our times with you, Sam,” said Annie. “我们永远不会忘记和你在一起的时光，萨姆，”安妮说。

“Nor will I forget,” said Abraham Lincoln. “我同样不会忘记，”亚伯拉罕·林肯说。

The boys were getting closer. 男孩们越来越近了。

“Here, sir,” said Jack. He gave the note back to the president. Then he and Annie started moving away. “给您，先生，”杰克说。 他把便条还给了总统。 然后他和安妮开始慢慢走开。

“Good-bye!” they called to Abraham Lincoln. “再见！”他们对亚伯拉罕·林肯喊道。

The president waved and put their note in his pocket. 总统挥了挥手，把他们的便条放进兜里。

Then Jack and Annie quickly climbed up the ladder. Inside the tree house, they looked out the window. They saw Abraham Lincoln hurrying to meet his boys. When he caught up to them, he wrapped his arms around them both. They were all laughing. 接着，杰克和安妮迅速爬上了梯子。 在树屋里，他们从窗户往外看。 他们看到亚伯拉罕·林肯正匆匆忙忙去见他的孩子们。 当他追上他们时，他把双臂环住了他们俩。 他们都在笑。

“Abraham Lincoln’s a good dad,” said Annie. “亚伯拉罕·林肯是个好爸爸，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack, smiling. “Well, we’d better go now. Before Tad tries to take the tree house away from us.” “是啊，”杰克笑着说。 “那我们最好现在就走吧。 不然塔德会试着把树屋从我们这儿拿走。”

Annie laughed. “He’ll be pretty surprised when he discovers it’s disappeared,” she said. She picked up the Pennsylvania book and pointed to a picture of the Frog Creek woods. “I wish we could go home!” she said. 安妮笑了。 “当他发现树屋不见了的时候，肯定会很惊讶，”她说。 她拿起那本宾夕法尼亚的书，指着一张蛙溪镇森林的图片。 “我希望我们能回家！”她说。

The wind began to blow. 风开始吹起来了。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转起来。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后，一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。
