CHAPTER ONE

The Third Thing

A nnie peeked into Jack’s room. “Ready?” she whispered. “准备好了吗？”她小声说。

“Yep,” said Jack. “准备好了。”杰克说。

Even though it was two hours before school started, Jack and Annie were already dressed. Jack put his notebook and pencil into his backpack. Then he picked up the pack and his sneakers and followed Annie into the hall. In their sock feet, Jack and Annie tiptoed past their parents’ bedroom. Then they slipped down the stairs. 虽然离上学还有两个小时，杰克和安妮已经穿好了衣服。 杰克把他的笔记本和铅笔放进书包里。 然后他拿起书包和运动鞋，跟着安妮走进了走廊。 光着脚，杰克和安妮蹑手蹑脚地从他们父母的卧室旁边走过。 然后他们滑下了楼梯。

When they reached the front hall, Jack and Annie put on their jackets and shoes and stepped outside. The early-morning sky was gray. Everything was quiet, except for the sound of a gentle spring rain. 当他们到达前厅时，杰克和安妮穿上外套和鞋子，走出了门外。 清晨的天空是灰色的。 一切都很安静，除了春雨轻柔的声音。

“Should we get our raincoats?” Jack asked. “我们应该拿雨衣吗？”杰克问。

“It’s clearing up,” said Annie. She pointed to blue sky in the distance. “天晴了，”安妮说。 她指着远处的蓝天。

“Oh, good,” said Jack. “Let’s hurry.” “哦，太好了，”杰克说。 “我们快点走吧。”

Jack and Annie stepped off their porch. Then they ran up the sidewalk and crossed the street. By the time they started into the Frog Creek woods, the rain had stopped. Misty rays of sunlight slanted down through the wet trees. 杰克和安妮走下了他们的门廊。 然后他们沿着人行道跑起来，穿过街道。 等他们开始进入蛙溪镇的森林时，雨已经停了。 湿漉漉的树上斜斜地透进几缕迷蒙的阳光。

Jack and Annie kept going until they came to the tallest oak. The leaves at the top of the tree sparkled with raindrops, and the magic tree house was lit by the morning sun. 杰克和安妮一直往前走，直到他们来到最高的橡树前。 树顶的叶子上闪着雨滴，神奇的树屋被早晨的阳光照得亮晶晶的。

“It’s waiting for us,” said Jack. “它在等我们，”杰克说。

“I knew it would be,” said Annie. She grabbed the rope ladder and started up. “我知道它会的，”安妮说。 她抓住绳梯，开始往上爬。

Jack climbed after her. Inside the tree house, they looked around for the two special things they had found on their last missions. 杰克跟在她后面爬了上去。 在树屋里，他们四处张望，寻找上次任务中找到的那两件特别的东西。

“Great, they’re still here,” said Jack, pointing to a green jewel and a white and yellow flower in the corner. “太好了，他们还在那儿，”杰克指着角落里的一颗绿色宝石和一朵白黄相间的花说道。

“And it looks like Teddy and Kathleen sent some stuff for us,” said Annie. “看起来泰迪和凯瑟琳给我们寄了一些东西，”安妮说。

Lying in the shadows was a book. Next to it were a small folded note and a tiny blue bottle. Jack picked up the book. Its cover showed an old black-and-white photograph of a building. It looked like the White House in Washington, D.C. 一本小书躺在阴影里。 它旁边是一张折起来的小纸条和一个小小的蓝色瓶子。 杰克捡起了那本书。 它的封面显示了一张黑白的老照片，拍的是一个建筑物。 看起来像华盛顿特区的白宫。

Jack gasped. “Oh, man! Abraham Lincoln!” 杰克惊呼起来。 “哦，天哪！是亚伯拉罕·林肯！”

图中文字：THE LIFE OF

ABRAHAM LINCOLN 图中文字：亚伯拉罕·林肯的一生

“Wow,” said Annie, looking at the book’s cover. “He was a great president.” “哇，”安妮看着书的封面说道。 “他是一位伟大的总统。”

“No kidding,” said Jack. “Do you think we’re actually going to meet him?” He opened the book to the first page and read aloud: “真的吗？”杰克说。 “你觉得我们真的会见到他吗？”他翻开书，翻到第一页，大声读起来：

Abraham Lincoln served as president of the United States from March 1861 until his assassination in April 1865. He led the country through the terrible crisis of the American Civil War. He preserved the union of states and outlawed slavery. 亚伯拉罕·林肯从1861年3月担任美国总统，直到1865年4月被暗杀。他带领国家度过了美国南北战争这一场可怕的危机。他维护了国家的统一，废除了奴隶制。

“I can’t believe it,” Jack said, closing the book. “Abraham Lincoln! Do you think he’s supposed to help us find the third thing to break the spell that turned Penny into a statue?” “我简直不敢相信，”杰克说，合上了书。 “亚伯拉罕·林肯！ 你觉得他是不是要帮我们找到第三样东西，来打破把彭妮变成雕像的咒语？

“Maybe,” said Annie. “Let’s see what else Teddy and Kathleen left for us.…” She picked up the tiny bottle and the note. She unfolded the note and read aloud: “也许吧，”安妮说。 “让我们看看泰迪和凯瑟琳还给我们留下了什么……”她捡起了那瓶小瓶子和那张纸条。 她展开纸条，大声读起来：

The third thing to break the spell

is a single feather from a hero’s hand.

Use it wisely to give him hope—

the hope he needs to heal his land. 打破咒语的第三件事，是一根英雄手上的羽毛。要明智地使用它，给他带来希望——他治愈这片土地所需要的希望。

“That sounds like a riddle,” said Jack. “听起来像是个谜语，”杰克说。

“I’ll bet Abraham Lincoln is the hero,” said Annie. “And”—she looked at the note again—“we have to get a feather from him. Then we’ll use the feather to give him hope.” “我敢打赌亚伯拉罕·林肯是英雄，”安妮说。 “而且”—她又看了看纸条—“我们必须从他那里得到一根羽毛。 然后我们用这根羽毛给他带来希望。”

“Confusing,” said Jack. “有点难懂，”杰克说。

“A lot of our missions sound confusing at first, don’t you think?” said Annie. “But in the end, they all make sense.” “我们很多任务一开始听起来都很难懂，你不觉得吗？”安妮说。 “但最后，它们都会变得有意义。”

“Yeah, I guess,” said Jack. “But that doesn’t help us right now. There must be a magic potion in the bottle. What does the label say?” “是啊，我想是这样，”杰克说。 “但这对我们现在没帮助。 瓶子里一定有魔法药水。 标签上写着什么？”

Annie held up the small blue bottle and read the tiny writing on its label aloud: 安妮举起那个小小的蓝色瓶子，大声读出标签上的小字：

Take a sip. Make a wish for one thing to help you on your mission. Remember: Trust the magic.

“That sounds kind of general,” said Jack. “Wish for one thing to help us on our mission? That could be anything.” “听起来有点太笼统了，”杰克说。 “许一个愿望，帮我们完成任务？ 那可以是任何东西。”

“And remember to trust the magic,” said Annie. She shrugged. “还有，记住要相信魔法，”安妮说。她耸了耸肩。

“Okay … we’ll try to do that,” said Jack. He took the bottle and the note from Annie and put them into his backpack. “好吧……我们会试着做到的，”杰克说。 他从安妮那里接过瓶子和纸条，把它们放进自己的背包里。

“We’ve got everything we need,” said Annie. “A mission, a research book, and a little bit of magic. Ready to go?” “我们已经准备好了所有需要的东西，”安妮说。 “一个任务，一本研究书，还有一点点魔法。准备出发了吗？”

“Yep,” said Jack. He pointed to the picture of the White House on the cover of their book and said, “I wish we could go there!” “准备好了，”杰克说。 他指着书封上白宫的图片，说：“我希望我们能去那里！”

The wind started to blow. 风开始吹起来了。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。
