CHAPTER NINE

Corn Bread and Molasses 第九章 玉米面包和糖蜜

“Turn around slowly,” Jack said to Annie. “慢慢转过去，”杰克对安妮说。

Clutching the water jugs, Jack and Annie turned around and started back the way they’d come. They tried not to make noise, but sticks and branches cracked and snapped. 紧紧抱着水罐，杰克和安妮转过身，开始往他们来的路上走回去。 他们尽量不发出声音，但树枝和树枝还是咔嚓咔嚓地折断了。

The growl came again. Louder. 咆哮声又来了。更响了。

“Forget slow!” said Jack. “Run!” “别再慢慢来了！”杰克说。 “跑！”

Annie bolted ahead through the brush. Jack ran after her. His heart pounded. Brambles and vines blocked their way. Thorns snagged their clothes. They ran as fast as they could, not knowing if all the breathing and thrashing sounds were coming from them—or from the beast chasing them. 安妮猛地冲了出去，穿过灌木丛。 杰克追了上去。 他的心跳得很快。 荆棘和藤蔓挡住了他们的路。 尖刺勾住了他们的衣服。 他们拼命地跑，不知道那些呼吸和挣扎的声音是来自他们自己——还是来自追赶他们的野兽。

They burst into the clearing. Jack looked back. He didn’t see a wolf or wildcat, but he wasn’t ready to stop yet. “Keep going!” he cried. 他们冲进了空地。杰克回头看了看。 他没有看到狼或野猫，但他还没准备好停下来。 “继续跑！”他喊道。

Jack and Annie tore across the scrubby clearing. Finally they came to the cabin. 杰克和安妮冲过了这片杂草丛生的空地。 终于，他们来到了小木屋前。

Sam was standing by the woodpile, swinging the ax! He gracefully split a log in two. He looked up at Jack and Annie and smiled. “How do?” he said. 萨姆正站在木柴堆旁，挥舞着斧头！ 他优雅地将一根木头劈成两半。 他抬头看着杰克和安妮，微笑着。 “你好吗？”他说。

Jack and Annie laughed as they tried to catch their breath. For some reason, Jack felt safe now, with Sam. “Fine!” he said. “Fine, fine, fine!” 杰克和安妮努力喘着气，笑了起来。 不知为什么，现在有萨姆在身边，杰克感到很安全。 “很好！”他说。 “很好，很好，很好！”

“How do you do?” said Annie. “Why are you splitting wood?” “你好吗？”安妮说。 “你为什么在劈木头？”

“I said to myself, I ain’t going to lie in bed forever,” said Sam. “My headache stopped as soon as I started my chores. I figured the two of you had left.” “我对自己说，我不会再躺在床上永远躺下去了，”萨姆说。 “我开始干活后，头痛就停止了。 我想你们俩已经离开了。”

“Oh, no, we tried to do your chores,” said Jack. “But—” “哦，不，我们试着帮你干活了，”杰克说。 “但是——”

“We were headed to the spring to get water and we heard a growl,” said Annie. “我们正要去泉边打水，结果听到了一声低吼，”安妮说。

“Like a wolf,” said Jack. “像狼一样，”杰克说。

“Or a wildcat,” Annie said. “或者像野猫，”安妮说。

“So we ran,” said Jack. “所以我们逃跑了，”杰克说。

Annie held up a jug. “No water. Sorry.” 安妮举起了一个罐子。 “没有水，对不起。”

“No milk, either,” said Jack. “也没有牛奶，”杰克说。

“No split wood,” said Annie. “没有劈好的木头，”安妮说。

“No corn bread,” said Jack. “也没有玉米面包，”杰克说。

Sam gave them a big grin. “Don’t worry. I took care of milking the cow, and I found water in the rain bucket. The corn bread’s baking now.” 萨姆给了他们一个大大的笑容。 “别担心。 我负责挤牛奶，还在雨桶里找到了水。 玉米面包现在正在烤呢。”

“Wow,” said Annie. “哇，”安妮说。

“That’s amazing,” said Jack. Now that Sam was better, he wondered if he could lead them to the president. “Do you still have time to help us find Abraham Lincoln?” he asked. “太棒了，”杰克说。 现在萨姆感觉好些了，他想知道自己是否能带他们找到总统。 “你还有时间帮我们找到亚伯拉罕·林肯吗？”他问。

“Sure,” said Sam. “I gave you my word.” “当然，”萨姆说。 “我答应过你。”

“Great. Do you think he’s still riding his horse in the country?” said Jack. “太好了。 你觉得他还在乡下骑着他的马吗？”杰克问。

“Nope. He’s not riding anymore,” said Sam. “But I guarantee you he’s around here.” “不， 他不再骑马了，”萨姆说。 “但我保证，他就在附近。”

“Like where?” said Jack. “像哪里呢？”杰克说。

“Don’t worry. I’ll introduce you to him very soon,” said Sam. “Let’s go inside first.” “别担心。 我很快就会给你介绍他，”萨姆说。 “我们先进去吧。”

Sam stuck his ax in a log. He picked up an armload of wood and headed into the cabin. As Jack and Annie followed, Jack glanced at the sky. The sun would be going down soon. 萨姆把斧头插进树桩里。 他抱起一大堆木头，朝小屋走去。 当杰克和安妮跟着进去时，杰克看了看天空。 太阳很快就要落山了。

Inside, Sam put more wood on the fire. Then he lit two oil lamps. “Would you like to have some corn bread with butter and molasses?” he asked. 屋里，萨姆往火堆上又添了些木头。 然后他点燃了两盏油灯。 “你想吃点加了黄油和糖蜜的玉米面包吗？”他问。

“Oh … wow …” Jack didn’t know what to say. He was desperate to look for Abraham Lincoln, but he was also very hungry. “哦……哇……”杰克不知道该说什么。 他非常想找到亚伯拉罕·林肯，但他也非常饿。

“I’d love it!” said Annie. “我非常想吃！”安妮说。

“Me too,” said Jack, relieved. “We’ll eat fast. And then we can look for Abraham Lincoln, okay?” “我也是，”杰克松了口气说。 “我们吃得快一点。 然后我们就可以去找亚伯拉罕·林肯，好吗？”

“Yes indeed. But first, you-all sit down,” said Sam. “当然可以。 但首先，你们都坐下来，”萨姆说。

Jack and Annie sat on small tree stumps that served as stools. Sam lifted the lid on a pot hanging over the fire. The delicious smell of corn bread filled the air. 杰克和安妮坐在小树桩上，那些树桩当作了凳子。 萨姆掀开了挂在火上的锅盖。 烤玉米面包的香味充满了整个空气。

Sam moved the pot to the wood table. Then he sliced pieces of steaming bread and put them on wooden plates. He smeared butter and dark molasses over the bread and ladled milk from the pail into wooden cups. 萨姆把锅移到了木桌旁。 然后他切了几片热腾腾的面包，放在木制的盘子里。 他把黄油和深色的糖浆抹在面包上，又从桶里舀了牛奶倒进木杯里。

Jack sipped the sweet milk and ate the hot, buttery corn bread. “Yum,” he said. He thought it might be the best meal he’d ever had. 杰克喝了一口甜牛奶，吃起了热乎乎的黄油玉米面包。 “真好吃，”他说。 他觉得这可能是他吃过的最好的一顿饭了。

“You really worked hard after we left,” Annie said to Sam. “你离开后真的努力干活了，”安妮对萨姆说。

“I like to make things nice for Sarah for when she gets home from school,” the boy said. “我想为莎拉把事情做得漂亮一点，等她放学回家的时候，”男孩说。

“Do you ever go to school?” asked Jack, his mouth full. “你上学吗？”杰克边说边满嘴塞着东西。

Sam nodded. “Since Pa left, I stay here to watch over things and do chores. But Sarah comes home and shares what she’s learned. I do homework and everything.” 萨姆点了点头。 “自从爸爸离开后，我就一直住在这里，照看东西，做家务。 但萨拉回家时会跟我分享她学到的东西。 我也会做作业，什么都会。”

“Have you lived here a long time?” Jack asked, looking around at the crude cabin. “你在这里住很久了吗？”杰克环顾着简陋的小屋问道。

“A few years,” said Sam. “We came from Kentucky. Pa and I cut our cabin out of the wilderness. We chopped down trees to make a road. We rolled fifty logs to this site and put up these walls. Did it all by hand and all without nails.” “几年了，”萨姆说。 “我们是从肯塔基来的。 我和爸爸在荒野中砍出了一间小屋。 我们还砍了树，铺出了一条路。” 我们滚了五十根木头到这个地方，然后建起了这些墙。 全靠手工完成，一根钉子都没用。

“Whoa,” said Jack. It sounded like work for the strongest men, he thought, but Sam couldn’t have been more than seven or eight at the time. “哇，”杰克说。 他觉得这活儿看起来得最强壮的男人才能干，但当时萨姆最多也就七八岁。

“We did as best we could with the furniture,” Sam said with a laugh. “Someday we’ll do better.” “我们只能尽量用家具来帮忙，”萨姆笑着说。 “总有一天我们会做得更好。”

“It’s not bad,” said Jack. He looked at the cabin with new eyes. It seemed like a miracle now—everything made by hand, without the help of machines or even nails. “这也不算太差，”杰克说。 他用新的眼光看着小屋。 现在看起来简直是个奇迹——所有东西都是手工做的，连机器和钉子都没用。

“You make all your own food, too, don’t you?” asked Annie. “你们是不是也自己种粮食，自己做食物？”安妮问。

“ ’Course,” said Sam. “We have our crops, and Pa hunts for our meat, or he did when he was here.” “当然啦，”萨姆说。 “我们有我们的庄稼，爸爸以前在这里的时候会为我们打猎找肉吃。”

“I wouldn’t be a good hunter,” said Annie. “我可不是个好猎手，”安妮说。

“Me neither,” said Jack. “我也不行，”杰克说。

“Me neither,” said Sam. “I shot a turkey once. Then I took a good hard look at the bird. I was so taken with its beauty, I ain’t pulled a trigger on a wild creature since. That’s why we haven’t had any meat since Pa left.” “我也不行，”萨姆说。 “我曾经射杀过一只火鸡。 然后我好好地看了一眼那只鸟。 我被它的美丽深深吸引，从那以后我就再也没有向任何野生动物开过枪。 这就是为什么自从爸爸离开后，我们就没有肉吃了。”

“Well, you do a great job making corn bread,” said Annie. “嗯，你做的玉米面包可做得很好，”安妮说。

“You sure do,” said Jack. He took his last bite, finished his milk, and wiped his mouth on his sleeve. Okay. Now they had to look for the president. Through the cracks in the cabin, he could see it was getting darker. “你确实做到了，”杰克说。 他吃完了最后一口，喝完了牛奶，然后用袖子擦了擦嘴。好了。 现在他们得去找总统了。 透过小屋的裂缝，他看到天色越来越暗了。

“Did you get all your chores done?” said Annie. “你做完所有家务了吗？”安妮说。

“Nope. I ain’t worked in my Dilworth speller yet,” said Sam. “But I don’t really consider that a chore. It’s my favorite thing. You could say I have a great thirst for learnin’.” “没有。 我还没用我的迪尔沃思拼读课本呢，”萨姆说。 “但我觉得那不算家务。 那是我最喜欢做的事。 你可以说我有很强的学习欲望。”

“So do we,” said Annie. “What’s your homework for today?” “我们也是，”安妮说。 “你今天的家庭作业是什么？”

“Annie,” said Jack, trying to catch her eye. “安妮，”杰克说，试图引起她的注意。

“Hold on, I’ll get the speller that Sarah brought me from school and show you.” Sam crossed the room and scrambled up to the loft. “The lesson I studied this morning is parts of speech,” he called down. “等一下，我去拿莎拉从学校带给我的拼写本给你看。”萨姆穿过房间，爬上了阁楼。 “我早上学的课程是词性，”他喊道。

“We have to go,” Jack whispered to Annie. “我们必须走了，”杰克对安妮低声说。

“We can’t hurt his feelings,” whispered Annie. “Just let him show us the speller.” “我们不能伤害他的感情，”安妮低声说。 “就让他给我们看看拼写本吧。”

“But we have a mission—” Jack started. “但我们有一个任务——”杰克开始说。

“Here it is!” said Sam, climbing down from the loft. He grinned at them and held up a tattered book. “Would you mind giving me a little test?” “在这里！”萨姆喊道，从阁楼爬了下来。 他冲他们咧嘴笑，举起了一本破旧的书。 “你们介意让我考考你们吗？”
