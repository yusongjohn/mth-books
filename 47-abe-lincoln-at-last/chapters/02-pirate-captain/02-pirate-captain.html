CHAPTER TWO

Pirate Captain

2. Pirate Captain 2. 海盗船长

Jack shivered. The air was chilly, but the sun was bright. Bare branches outside the tree house swayed in the wind. Annie was wearing a long dress with an apron. Jack wore a cotton shirt over a red undershirt and a pair of trousers with suspenders. His backpack had turned into a leather bag. 杰克打了个寒战。 空气有点冷，但太阳很明亮。 树屋外面的树枝在风中摇晃。 安妮穿着一条长裙，还系着围裙。 杰克穿着一件棉衬衫，里面是红色的背心，还穿着一条带吊带的裤子。 他的背包变成了一个皮包。

Jack looked into the bag. Inside were his notebook and pencil, the message from Teddy and Kathleen, and the bottle with the magic potion. “Good,” he said, “it’s all here.” 杰克看了看包里面。 里面装着他的笔记本和铅笔，泰迪和凯瑟琳留下的信，还有装着魔法药水的瓶子。 “很好，”他说，“所有东西都在这儿。”

“We’ve worn clothes like these before,” said Annie. “我们以前穿过这样的衣服，”安妮说。

“Yeah, when we ran from that twister on the prairie,” said Jack. “对，就是我们在大草原上逃过龙卷风那次，”杰克说。

“And when we helped Clara Barton in the Civil War,” said Annie. “还有我们帮助克拉拉·巴顿参加美国内战那次，”安妮说。

“Right,” said Jack. “So, did we land at the White House?” “对，”杰克说。 “那，我们是不是降落在白宫了？”

They looked out the window. The tree house had landed in a grove of bare, sunlit trees. Beyond the trees, horse-drawn carriages rumbled over a circular carriageway toward a stately white mansion with tall columns. 他们朝窗外看去。 树屋降落在一片光秃秃的、阳光照耀的树丛中。 树丛后面，马车沿着圆形的车道缓缓驶向一座庄严的白色豪宅，豪宅上有高大的柱子。

“Oh, man,” whispered Jack. “哦，天啊，”杰克小声说。

The White House was breathtaking in the morning air, bathed in sunlight. A crowd was gathered outside the front entrance: men in long black coats and tall hats, and women in hoopskirts and bonnets with big bows. 白宫在清晨的空气中显得令人惊叹，沐浴在阳光下。 一大群人聚集在正门外面：一些男人穿着长长的黑色外套和高高的帽子，一些女人穿着蓬蓬裙和带有大蝴蝶结的帽子。

“Looks like lots of people are visiting Abraham Lincoln today,” said Annie. “看起来今天有很多人来参观亚伯拉罕·林肯，”安妮说。

Jack thumbed through their research book until he found another black-and-white photograph of the White House. He read aloud: 杰克翻阅他们的研究书，直到找到另一张白宫的黑白照片。他大声读道：

When Abraham Lincoln became president in 1861, the White House was considered to belong to all the citizens of the country, as well as to the president and his family. Anyone could walk right in. President Lincoln sometimes found it hard to work in his White House office because of the number of people swarming through the building. 当亚伯拉罕·林肯在1861年成为总统时，白宫被认为是属于全国所有公民，以及总统和他的家人。任何人都可以随意走进去。林肯总统有时很难在白宫办公室工作，因为有很多人挤在大楼里。

“So anyone can just walk right into the White House and look for the president?” said Annie.

“That’s crazy,” said Jack. “这太疯狂了，”杰克说。

“But it’s good for us!” said Annie. “但这对我们来说是好事！”安妮说。

“I guess,” said Jack, “but I don’t want to be one of those people who make it hard for the president to work.” “我想是吧，”杰克说，“但我可不想成为那些让总统工作变得困难的人之一。”

“Don’t forget,” said Annie, “we’re supposed to give him hope.” “别忘了，”安妮说，“我们本来就是应该给他希望的。”

“With a feather that he’ s supposed to give us, ” said Jack. He shook his head, then took out their note from Teddy and Kathleen. “用他应该给我们的羽毛，”杰克说。 他摇了摇头，然后拿出泰迪和凯瑟琳给他们的便条。

The third thing to break the spell

is a single feather from a hero’s hand.

Use it wisely to give him hope—

the hope he needs to heal his land. 打破咒语的第三件事，是一根英雄手上的羽毛。要聪明地使用它，给他希望——他治愈这片土地所需要的希望。

“How can we get a feather from him?” said Jack. “And how can it give him hope?” “我们怎么才能从他那里得到一根羽毛呢？”杰克说。 “而且，它怎么才能给他希望呢？”

“It’s better to do just one thing at a time,” said Annie. “First we have to find the president.” “最好一次只做一件事，”安妮说。 “First we have to find the president.”

“Hey, Willie! Look!” someone shouted from below. “It’s a tree house! See? See?” “嘿，威利！ 看！”，有人从下面喊道。 “这是一个树屋！看？ 看？”

“Oh, no!” whispered Jack. “哦，天哪！”杰克小声说。

Jack and Annie peeked out the window. A boy about seven or eight years old was looking up at the tree house. The boy wore baggy gray trousers with suspenders and a white shirt. He had dark, piercing eyes. 杰克和安妮从窗户往外看。 一个大约七八岁的男孩正抬头看着树屋。 那个男孩穿着宽松的灰色裤子，系着背带，还穿着一件白衬衫。 他有一双深邃的黑眼睛。

“Hello!” the boy shouted when he saw Jack and Annie. “Who are you? Why are you in our tree house?” “你好！”男孩看到杰克和安妮时喊道。 “你们是谁？ 为什么在我们的树屋里？”

“Your tree house?” said Jack. “It’s not your tree house!” “你们的树屋？”杰克说道。 “这不是你们的树屋！”

“Yes, it is!” the boy said confidently. “是的，就是！”男孩自信地说。

“Tad, hush!” An older boy ran to join the younger one. He had a friendly, open smile and looked to be around Jack’s age. “Don’t mind my brother Tad!” he shouted. “塔德，别说话！”一个年纪较大的男孩跑过来加入了较小的那个男孩。 他露出友好的笑容，看起来和杰克年龄相仿。 “别理我弟弟塔德！”他喊道。

“But it’s ours, Willie!” said Tad. “The White House is our house! And the tree house is in our yard!” “但这是我们的，威利！”塔德说道。 “白宫就是我们的家！ 而且树屋就在我们的院子里！

Oh, man, thought Jack. That was what the book said, too: the White House was considered to belong to all the citizens of the country, as well as to the president. 哦，天哪，杰克心想。 书上也这么说：白宫被认为是属于这个国家所有公民的，也属于总统。

“I’m sorry, but this tree house is not like the White House,” Jack called. “It doesn’t belong to the citizens of the country. It’s ours!” “对不起，但这个树屋不像白宫，”杰克喊道。 “它不属于这个国家的居民，是我们的！”

“No, it’s not!” yelled Tad. “I’m coming up!” “不，不是！”塔德喊道。 “我马上就上来！”

“No, you’re not!” Jack yelled back. He reached for the rope ladder to pull it up. But Tad had already started climbing. “不，你不会的！”杰克喊道。 他伸手去抓绳梯，把它拉上来。 但是塔德已经开始爬树了。

“Hide our stuff!” Jack said to Annie. “把东西藏好！”杰克对安妮说。

Jack quickly pushed the Lincoln book into his leather bag. Annie stuck their note and the tiny bottle into her apron pocket. 杰克迅速把林肯的书塞进他的皮质包里。 安妮把他们的便条和小瓶子塞进围裙口袋里。

“Tad, come back!” called Willie. “Leave them alone!” “塔德，回来！”威利喊道。 “别管他们！”

Tad scrambled into the tree house. He grinned at Jack and Annie, his dark eyes gleaming. “I’m a pirate captain, and I’m taking over your ship!” Tad shook his small fists in Jack’s face. “Fight me!” he shouted. 塔德爬进了树屋里。 他对着杰克和安妮露出笑容，黑亮的眼睛闪着光。 “我是个海盗船长，我要接管你们的船！”塔德把小拳头举到杰克面前晃了晃。 “和我战斗吧！”他大喊。

“Cut it out,” said Jack, waving him away. “别闹了，”杰克说，挥手让他走开。

“Tad!” Willie shouted from below. “塔德！”威利从下面喊道。

The boy just laughed like a maniac and danced around the tree house, trying to box with Jack. “This is my ship now, matey!” 那个男孩只是像疯了一样大笑，绕着树屋转圈，试图和杰克打架。 “现在这艘船归我了，伙计！”

“Quit it!” said Jack. “住手！”杰克说。

“Tad!” his brother yelled again. “塔德！”他哥哥又喊了一遍。

“Your brother’s calling you,” Annie said firmly to Tad. “Go! Now!” “你哥哥在叫你，”安妮坚定地对塔德说。 “走！现在就走！”

“Who are you to boss me, missy?” Tad said, jutting out his chin. “你凭什么指挥我，小姐？”塔德说，把下巴翘起来。

Annie laughed. “I’m not a missy, shrimp,” she said. “I’m Annie. And this is my brother, Jack.” 安妮笑了。 “我不是什么小姐，小虾米，”她说。 “我是安妮。 这是我的哥哥，杰克。”

Tad lowered his fists. “ Oh! Hello, Annie, I’m Tad.” He put out his hand, and Annie shook it. “Pleased to meet you,” said Tad, completely dropping his role as pirate captain. “What are you and Jack doing today?” 塔德放下了拳头。 你好，安妮，我是塔德。” 他伸出了手，安妮握了握。 “很高兴认识你，”塔德说，完全放下了海盗船长的角色。 “你们今天和杰克在干什么？

“Actually, we’re hoping to meet with President Lincoln,” Annie said. “其实，我们希望能见到林肯总统，”安妮说。

“Really?” said Tad. “Me and Willie know a secret.…” He gave them a sly grin. “If you come with us, we’ll take you straight to the president. I give you my word.” “真的吗？”塔德说。 “我和威利知道一个秘密。……” 他给了他们一个狡猾的微笑。 “如果你和我们一起去，我们会直接带你去总统那里。” 我向你保证。”

“Thanks, but we can handle it ourselves,” said Jack. The last thing he wanted was for this kid to get in their way. “谢谢，但我们自己能处理，”杰克说。 他最不想看到的就是这个孩子妨碍他们。

“But I want to help you. Come with me,” said Tad. He started down the ladder. “但我想帮助你们。 “跟我来，”塔德说。 他开始往下爬梯子。

“Should we go with him?” Annie whispered. “我们跟他去吗？”安妮小声说。

“No, he’s just making stuff up,” said Jack. “不，他只是在编故事，”杰克说。

“Are you coming down?” called Tad. “Or should I come back up so we can play?” “你下来吗？”塔德喊道。 “或者我应该下去，这样我们就能一起玩了？”

“Darn,” said Jack under his breath. “Let’s go, just to get him away from the tree house.” Jack grabbed his bag. “该死，”杰克小声地说。 “我们走吧，这样就能把他从树屋里赶走。”杰克抓起他的包。

Suddenly Tad poked his head back into the tree house. “Are you coming or not?” he said. 突然，塔德把头又探回了树屋里。 “你来不来？”他说。

“Yes! Go!” said Annie. “来！快走！”安妮说。

“What’s in your bag?” asked Tad. “你的包里有什么？”塔德问。

“Nothing,” said Jack. “Go back down!” He didn’t want Tad to see their Lincoln book. “什么也没有，”杰克说。 “回去！”他不想让塔德看到他们的林肯书。

“Let me see,” said Tad, climbing into the tree house again. “What’s inside?” “让我看看，”塔德说，又爬进了树屋里。 “里面有什么？”

“Nothing, he told you nothing,” said Annie. “什么也没有，他什么都没告诉你，”安妮说。

“Then why is he bringing it with him?” Tad asked her. “那他为什么还要带着它？”塔德问她。

“Fine, I’ll leave it!” Jack said crossly. He dropped the leather bag to the floor. “Happy? Let’s go!” “好吧，那我就把它留下！”杰克不高兴地说。 他把皮袋子丢在了地上。 “开心了吗？我们走吧！”，

“Yes! Let’s go!” said Tad, and he disappeared down the ladder again. “好！”， “我们走吧！”塔德说，然后他又一次顺着梯子下去了。

Jack reached into his bag, grabbed his small notebook and pencil, and stuck them into the back pocket of his pants. “I’ll come back later and get the book,” Jack whispered, “after we get rid of this kid.” 杰克把手伸进他的包里，拿出他的小笔记本和铅笔，把它们塞进了他裤子的后口袋里。 “等会儿我再回来拿这本书，”杰克小声说，“等我们把这孩子赶走之后。”

Annie smiled. “If we can,” she said. Then she and Jack started down the rope ladder. 安妮笑了。 “如果可以的话，”她说。 然后她和杰克开始顺着绳梯往下爬。
