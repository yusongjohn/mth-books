4. Commander-in-Chief 4. 总司令

Jack and Annie ran along the riverbank. The wind whooshed over the cold water. Wet snowflakes hissed in the dark. 杰克和安妮沿着河岸跑。 风呼呼刮过冰冷的河面。 潮湿的雪花在黑暗中发出嘶嘶声。

But then Jack heard other sounds. He heard voices, lots of voices. 可接着杰克听见了别的声音。 他听见说话声，很多说话声。

He and Annie soon came upon hundreds and hundreds of soldiers gathered near the dark river. 他和安妮很快就碰到了成百上千名聚集在黑暗河边的士兵。

Many carried oil lanterns. The lanterns gave an eerie glow to the snowy twilight. 许多人提着油灯。 灯笼给雪中的暮色添上了一层怪异的光。

“The captain and his men must be here somewhere,” said Jack, looking around. “上尉和他的人一定在这儿的某个地方，”杰克四处看着说。

Boats like giant canoes were tied near the river. Men were leading horses and loading cannons onto the boats. 像巨大独木舟一样的船系在河边。 人们正牵着马，把大炮装上船。

“What are they all doing?” said Annie. “他们都在干什么？”安妮说。

Jack pulled out their Revolutionary War book. He read in a whisper: 杰克掏出独立战争的书。 他小声读道：

On Wednesday, December 25, 1776— 1776年12月25日，星期三——

“December 25? That’s Christmas!” said Annie. “Today’s Christmas!” “12月25日？ 那是圣诞节！”安妮说。 “今天就是圣诞节！”

“Cool,” said Jack. He started reading again: “酷，”杰克说。 他又开始读：

On Wednesday, December 25, 1776, the patriots were losing the war. Ragged and weary, many were ready to give up. Then something began to happen that would turn the war around. About 2,400 American patriots gathered on the west bank of the Delaware River in Pennsylvania. They prepared to cross the river to go on a secret mission. 1776年12月25日星期三，爱国者们正在输掉战争。他们衣衫褴褛，筋疲力尽，许多人准备放弃了。然后发生了一件事，让战争的局势开始转变。大约2400名美国爱国者在宾夕法尼亚州特拉华河的西岸聚集在一起。他们准备过河，去执行一项秘密任务。

“A secret mission? Oh, man … ,” said Jack. 哦，天哪……，”杰克说。

He started to pull out his notebook. 他开始掏笔记本。

“Attention, troops! The commander-in-chief!” a soldier shouted. “立正，部队！ 总司令到了！”一名士兵喊道。

Jack and Annie saw a man in a dark cape and a three-cornered hat ride up on a white horse. 杰克和安妮看见一个穿着深色斗篷、戴着三角帽的人，骑着白马来了。

The commander-in-chief loomed above the crowd of soldiers. His cape flapped in the wind. 总司令高高耸立在士兵人群之上。 他的斗篷在风中飘动。

He sat calmly and with dignity on the back of his horse. 他沉着而庄重地坐在马背上。

Even at a distance, Jack thought the commander-in-chief looked familiar, very familiar. But he couldn’t figure out why. 即使隔着一段距离，杰克也觉得这位总司令很面熟，非常面熟。 可他想不出为什么。

“A dangerous mission lies before you all,” the man shouted above the wind. “But I want you to have courage. You must remember the words of Thomas Paine.” “一项危险的任务摆在你们所有人面前，”那人的喊声盖过了风声。 “可我希望你们鼓起勇气。 你们必须记住托马斯·潘恩的话。”

The commander-in-chief held up a piece of paper. He read to his men: 总司令举起一张纸。 他向部下读道：

“ ‘These are the times that try men’s souls. The summer soldier and the sunshine patriot will, in this crisis, shrink from the service of their country. But he that stands it now deserves the love and thanks of man and woman.… ’ ” “‘这是考验人们灵魂的时刻。 夏天的士兵和晴天的爱国者，会在这场危机中逃避为国效力。 但现在能坚持下来的人，值得男人和女人的爱与感谢。……’”

“Wow, that’s great,” whispered Annie. “哇，太棒了，”安妮小声说。

Listening to the powerful words, Jack felt his spirits rise, too. 听着这些有力量的话，杰克也觉得自己振奋起来。

“ ‘The harder the conflict, the more glorious the triumph,’ ” the commander-in-chief read on. “ ‘ What we obtain too cheap, we esteem too lightly. It is dearness only that gives everything its value.’ ” “‘斗争越艰难，胜利就越光荣，’”总司令继续读着。 “‘得来太容易的东西，我们往往看得太轻。 唯有珍贵，才赋予一切价值。’”

There was a silence, as if everyone were thinking about the words the man had read. Then the soldiers started cheering and clapping. They didn’t seem tired at all anymore. Now they seemed eager to set out on their mission. 一阵沉默，仿佛每个人都在思考那人读过的话。 然后士兵们开始欢呼鼓掌。 他们似乎一点也不累了。 现在他们似乎渴望出发去执行任务。

The commander-in-chief saluted his men. He steered his horse toward the river. 总司令向部下敬礼。 他策马朝河边走去。

As the horse moved past them, Jack got a better look at the rider. 马从他们身边经过时，杰克看清了骑马的人。

He gasped. 他倒吸一口气。

Of course! he thought. He’d seen that face before—on dollar bills! 当然了！他想。 他以前见过那张脸——就在美元纸币上！

Jack grabbed Annie’s arm. 杰克抓住安妮的胳膊。

“I know who the commander-in-chief is!” he exclaimed. “He’s George Washington!” “我知道总司令是谁了！”他喊道。 “他是乔治·华盛顿！”
