8. These Are the Times 8. 这是考验的时刻

Jack grabbed Annie’s hand. 杰克抓住安妮的手。

“We’re not spies!” he said. “我们不是间谍！”他说。

He whirled around to face George Washington. 他猛地转过身，面向乔治·华盛顿。

“General Washington, remember those words you said to your men?” Jack said. “You should believe them yourself, sir!” “华盛顿将军，还记得您对部下说的那些话吗？”杰克说。 “您自己也该相信那些话，长官！”

“What are you talking about?” George Washington asked. “你在说什么？”乔治·华盛顿问。

Jack yanked the captain’s letter from his bag. 杰克从包里猛地抽出上尉的信。

By the light of the lamp, he read the words the captain had copied for his children: 借着灯光，他读出上尉抄给孩子们的那些话：

“ ‘These are the times that try men’s souls … ,’ ” Jack read. “ ‘ But he that stands it now deserves the love and thanks of man and woman.… The harder the conflict, the more glorious the triumph.’ ” “‘这是考验人们灵魂的时刻……，’”杰克读道。 “‘但现在能坚持下来的人，值得男人和女人的爱与感谢。……斗争越艰难，胜利就越光荣。’”

Jack looked at George Washington. 杰克看着乔治·华盛顿。

“Even if things look impossible, you should keep going, sir,” he said. “The harder things seem, the greater the triumph, right? That’s what you read to your men. You have to keep going for their sake.” “就算事情看起来不可能，您也该继续下去，长官，”他说。 “事情看起来越艰难，胜利就越伟大，对不对？ 那是您读给部下听的。 看在他们的份上，您必须继续下去。”

“Yes! And you have to keep going for our sake,” said Annie. “For the sake of the future children of America, sir.” “对！ 看在我们的份上，您也必须继续下去，”安妮说。 “为了美国将来的孩子们，长官。”

As the wet snow hissed about them, George Washington stared a long moment at Jack and Annie. 湿雪在他们周围发出嘶嘶声，乔治·华盛顿盯着杰克和安妮看了很久。

Finally, he put a hand on each of their shoulders. 终于，他一只手搭在杰克肩上，一只手搭在安妮肩上。

“I do not know who you are,” he said. “I do not know how you know what you know. But I believe you. For your sake, and for the future children of America, we will march on.” “我不知道你们是谁，”他说。 “我不知道你们怎么知道这些。 可我相信你们。 为了你们，也为了美国将来的孩子们，我们将继续前进。”

“Yay!” cried Annie. “耶！”安妮喊道。

“Yay,” said Jack softly. He sighed with relief and put away the captain’s letter. “耶，”杰克轻声说。 他松了口气，把上尉的信收了起来。

“Now get back in the boat,” said George Washington. “You must leave the fighting to us. To me and my men.” “现在回船上去，”乔治·华盛顿说。 “打仗的事必须交给我们。 交给我和我的士兵。”

Jack felt very grateful to George Washington and his whole army. They were risking their lives for all of America’s children, past and future. He could barely speak. 杰克对乔治·华盛顿和他的整支军队充满感激。 他们正在为美国过去和未来的所有孩子冒生命危险。 他几乎说不出话来。

“Thank you, sir,” said Annie. “谢谢您，长官，”安妮说。

“Thank you both for telling me to listen to my own advice,” said George Washington. He called to the rowers waiting in the boat. “Take good care of these two.” “谢谢你们提醒我听从自己的建议，”乔治·华盛顿说。 他向等在船上的桨手们喊道。 “好好照顾这两个孩子。”

George Washington climbed onto his horse. He looked down at Jack and Annie. 乔治·华盛顿爬上马背。 他低头看着杰克和安妮。

“Merry Christmas,” he said. “圣诞快乐，”他说。

Then the commander-in-chief rode away into the stormy darkness. 然后总司令骑马消失在风暴的黑暗中。
