3. It’s Time 3. 时间到了

Annie lifted up her long dress and ran to a tree close to the river. Jack held on to his hat and ran after her. 安妮提起长裙，跑到河边一棵树旁。 杰克按住帽子，跟在她后面跑。

They peeked out from behind the tree. More snow flurries swirled in the twilight. 他们从树后探出头偷看。 更多的小阵雪在暮色里打着转。

“Redcoats?” whispered Annie. “红衣军？”安妮小声问。

“Can’t tell,” said Jack. “看不清，”杰克说。

In the flickering firelight, the men didn’t seem to be wearing uniforms at all. They wore ragged pants and coats. Some even had their feet wrapped in tattered cloth. 在闪烁的火光里，那些人好像根本没穿制服。 他们穿着破旧的裤子和外套。 有些人甚至用破布裹着脚。

“Come on,” Annie said. “来吧，”安妮说。

She ran to a tree closer to the river. 她跑到更靠近河边的一棵树旁。

Jack followed. 杰克跟了上去。

“This is as close as we should get,” he whispered. “我们不该再靠近了，”他小声说。

“But we still can’t tell what they’re wearing,” said Annie. “可我们还是看不清他们穿什么，”安妮说。

She crept forward and hid behind a scraggly bush. 她悄悄向前，藏到一丛稀疏的灌木后面。

“No closer!” Jack whispered. “别再近了！”杰克小声说。

But Annie took off again. She crouched behind a rock. She was only about ten feet from the campfire. 可安妮又跑开了。 她蹲在一块岩石后面。 她离营火只有大约十英尺了。

Oh, brother, she’s way too close now, Jack thought. 天哪，她现在靠得实在太近了，杰克想。

But he took a deep breath and ran to the rock. 可他还是深吸一口气，跑向那块岩石。

When he joined Annie, she looked at him and grinned. 他和安妮会合时，她看着他笑了。

“This is like hide-and-seek,” she whispered. “这就像捉迷藏，”她小声说。

“It’s not a game, Annie,” Jack whispered back. “It’s war. Be serious.” “这不是游戏，安妮，”杰克小声回道。 “这是战争。严肃点。”

“I am serious!” said Annie, her voice rising. “我很严肃！”安妮说，声音抬高了。

“Shh!” said Jack. “嘘！”杰克说。

But it was too late. One of the men stood up and looked around. 可已经太晚了。 其中一个男人站了起来，四处张望。

“What’s the matter, Captain?” another asked. “怎么了，上尉？”另一个人问。

“I heard something,” said the captain. He held up his musket. “我听见有动静，”上尉说。 他举起了火枪。

Jack stopped breathing. 杰克屏住了呼吸。

“Who’s there?” the captain shouted. “谁在那儿？”上尉喊道。

Jack looked at Annie. She shrugged. 杰克看着安妮。她耸了耸肩。

“We’re caught,” she whispered. “我们被发现了，”她小声说。

“Who’s there?” the captain shouted again. “谁在那儿？”上尉又喊道。

“Just two kids,” Annie answered in a small voice. “只是两个孩子，”安妮用很小的声音回答。

“Come out and show yourselves!” the captain said. “出来，现身！”上尉说。

Jack and Annie stood up from behind the rock. They both held up their hands. 杰克和安妮从岩石后站了起来。 他们都举起了双手。

“We come in peace,” said Annie. “我们为和平而来，”安妮说。

In the shadowy twilight, the captain moved toward them. 在影影绰绰的暮色里，上尉朝他们走来。

“Who are you?” he asked. “你们是谁？”他问。

“We’re Jack and Annie,” said Annie. “我们是杰克和安妮，”安妮说。

“Why were you spying on us?” he asked. “你们为什么要侦察我们？”他问。

“We weren’t spying,” said Jack. “We just wanted to know if you were redcoats or patriots.” “我们不是在侦察，”杰克说。 “我们只是想知道你们是红衣军还是爱国者。”

“Which would you like us to be?” the shadowy figure asked. “你们希望我们是哪边？”那个影子般的人影问。

“Patriots,” said Jack. “爱国者，”杰克说。

“We are patriots,” the captain said. “我们是爱国者，”上尉说。

“Thank goodness!” said Annie. “谢天谢地！”安妮说。

The captain smiled. 上尉笑了。

“Where are you from?” he asked. His voice had softened. “你们从哪儿来？”他问。 他的声音变得温和了。

“We’re visiting relatives nearby,” Jack said. “我们在附近走亲戚，”杰克说。

“Frog Creek, Pennsylvania,” said Annie at the same time. “宾夕法尼亚州的蛙溪镇，”安妮同时说。

“But that’s amazing!” the captain said. “My farm is in Frog Creek. Where is your farm?” “那太巧了！”上尉说。 “我的农场就在蛙溪镇。 你们的农场在哪儿？”

Jack didn’t know how to answer. 杰克不知道该怎么回答。

“It’s near the Frog Creek woods,” said Annie. “在蛙溪树林附近，”安妮说。

“All farms are near woods,” the man said with a laugh. “What—” “所有农场都在树林附近，”那人笑着说。 “什么——”

Just then, someone called from up the river. “It’s time, Captain!” 就在这时，上游有人喊道。 “时间到了，上尉！”

The captain turned to the other men near the campfire. “It’s time!” he repeated. 上尉转向营火旁的其他人。 “时间到了！”他重复道。

The soldiers quickly put out their fire. They stood with their muskets on their shoulders. 士兵们很快扑灭了火。 他们把火枪扛在肩上站好。

“Time for what, Captain?” Annie asked. “去干什么，上尉？”安妮问。

“Time to meet up with our commander-in-chief,” he said. “Return to your family now so your mother and father won’t worry about you.” “该去会合我们的总司令了，”他说。 “现在回家去吧，别让爸爸妈妈担心你们。”

“Yes, sir,” said Jack. “是，长官，”杰克说。

“It is nice to see children,” the captain said. “I was just trying to write a letter to my own son and daughter. I didn’t know what to say.” “看见孩子真好，”上尉说。 “我正想给自己的儿子和女儿写信。 可不知道该写些什么。”

“Tell them that you miss them,” said Annie. “告诉他们你想他们，”安妮说。

The man smiled. 那人笑了。

“And I do, indeed,” he said softly. “我确实很想他们，”他轻声说。

Then he turned and headed up the riverbank. His ragged men followed behind. Soon they all disappeared into the cold mist. 然后他转身沿着河岸往上游走去。 他那些衣衫褴褛的士兵跟在后面。 很快，他们都消失在寒冷的雾里。

Jack looked around. The wind was blowing harder. The snow was sticking to the ground. 杰克环顾四周。 风刮得更猛了。 雪开始粘在地上。

“What now?” he asked. “现在怎么办？”他问。

More than anything, he wanted to go back home. With the soldiers gone, the riverbank felt lonely and scary. 他最想做的事，就是回家。 士兵们走了，河岸显得又孤单又可怕。

“We still have to find something to send, ” said Annie. “我们还得找到要送出的东西，”安妮说。

“I know,” said Jack. “我知道，”杰克说。

“Maybe we should just follow the captain and his men,” said Annie. “They might lead us to something.” “也许我们该跟着上尉和他的人，”安妮说。 “他们也许会带我们找到什么。”

Jack wasn’t sure that was a good idea. But he didn’t have a better one. 杰克不确定这是不是个好主意。 可他也想不出更好的办法。

“Okay. But let’s try not to get caught this time,” he said. “好。 不过这次咱们尽量别被抓住，”他说。

He and Annie took off through the frozen twilight, following the snowy footprints of the American patriots. 他和安妮在冰冷的暮色中出发，沿着美国爱国者留在雪地上的脚印走去。
