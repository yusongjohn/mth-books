10. This Peaceful Place 10. 这个和平的地方

Gray early light spilled into the tree house. 灰蒙蒙的晨光洒进树屋。

Birds sounded in the woods. 树林里响起鸟叫声。

The summer wind felt warm and gentle. Jack and Annie were wearing their nice, dry clothes again. 夏风温暖而轻柔。 杰克和安妮又穿上了干爽舒适的衣服。

“Oh, man,” said Jack. “It’s good to be home.” “哦，天哪，”杰克说。 “回家的感觉真好。”

“Yeah,” said Annie, sighing, “back in this peaceful place.” “是啊，”安妮叹了口气说，“又回到这个和平的地方了。”

Jack pulled the captain’s letter out of his pack. He turned it over. It was addressed to: 杰克从背包里抽出上尉的信。 他把它翻过来。 上面写着收件人是：

Molly and Ben Sanders Apple Tree Farm Frog Creek, Pennsylvania 莫莉和本·桑德斯，苹果树农场，宾夕法尼亚州蛙溪镇

“Molly and Ben lived on a farm near these woods over two hundred years ago,” said Jack. “两百多年前，莫莉和本就住在这片树林附近的农场上，”杰克说。

Annie gently touched the letter. 安妮轻轻摸了摸那封信。

“Your dad is going to make it home, kids. He misses you,” Annie whispered, as if she could send comforting words back through time. “孩子们，你们的爸爸会成功到家的。 他很想你们，”安妮低声说，仿佛能把安慰的话送回过去。

Jack carefully placed the special writing from the Revolutionary War next to the writing from the Civil War. 杰克小心翼翼地把独立战争的这份特别文字，放在南北战争的那份旁边。

“Look,” said Annie. She picked up a note lying in the corner. It said simply: Come back on Tuesday. “看，”安妮说。 她捡起角落里的一张纸条。 上面只写着：星期二再来。

“Another message from Morgan,” said Annie. “又是摩根的信息，”安妮说。

Jack smiled. 杰克笑了。

He pulled on his pack. 他背上背包。

“See you on Tuesday, tree house,” he said. “星期二见，树屋，”他说。

He started down the rope ladder. Annie followed. 他开始爬下绳梯。安妮跟着。

In the early daylight, they ran through the Frog Creek woods. Then they ran down their street. 在清晨的日光里，他们跑过蛙溪树林。 然后他们沿着街道跑下去。

They climbed onto their porch and rested against the railing. They looked out at the dawn sky. 他们爬上门廊，靠着栏杆歇着。 他们望着黎明的天空。

Jack remembered the whoosh of the cold wind on the Delaware. He remembered the hiss of the wet snow and the slosh of the icy waves. 杰克想起特拉华河上呼啸的寒风。 他想起湿雪的嘶嘶声和冰浪的拍打声。

He remembered George Washington reading to his men. 他想起乔治·华盛顿向部下朗读。

“The harder the conflict, the more glorious the triumph,” Jack whispered. “斗争越艰难，胜利就越光荣，”杰克小声说。

“Those words are true,” said Annie. “It was a hard conflict, and I feel kind of glorious right now. Don’t you?” “那些话是真的，”安妮说。 “那是一场艰苦的斗争，我现在觉得挺光荣的。你呢？”

Jack laughed. 杰克笑了。

“Yeah, definitely,” he said. “当然了，”他说。

Then he and Annie slipped into their quiet, peaceful house. 然后他和安妮溜进了安静平和的家。
