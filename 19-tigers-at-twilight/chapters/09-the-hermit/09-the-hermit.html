9. The Hermit 9. 隐士

Kah and Ko smoothed the man’s hair with their little paws and patted his cheeks gently. 卡和科用小爪子抚平那人的头发，又轻轻拍了拍他的脸颊。

The man smiled and whispered to the langurs. His eyes stayed closed. 那人微笑着，对叶猴低声说话。 他的眼睛一直闭着。

Teddy walked up to the man and licked his hands. 泰迪走到那人跟前，舔他的手。

The man still didn’t open his eyes. But he stroked Teddy’s fur. 那人仍然没有睁开眼睛。 可他抚摸着泰迪的毛。

“Knock, knock,” Annie said softly. “敲敲敲。”安妮轻声说。

“Is someone there?” the man asked. “有人在那儿吗？”那人问。

He turned his face toward Jack and Annie. Now his eyes were open, but he did not seem to see them. Jack realized that the man was blind. 他把脸转向杰克和安妮。 现在他的眼睛睁开了，可好像看不见他们。 杰克意识到这个人是盲人。

“Hi, I’m Annie,” said Annie. “嗨，我是安妮，”安妮说。

“And I’m Jack,” said Jack. “我是杰克，”杰克说。

The blind man smiled. 盲人微笑了。

“Good,” he said, nodding. “Would you like to visit with me?” “好，”他点头说。 “你们愿意和我坐坐吗？”

“Sure,” said Annie. “当然，”安妮说。

She and Jack sat down next to the man. 她和杰克在那人旁边坐下。

“Do you live in this forest?” Annie asked. “你住在这片森林里吗？”安妮问。

“Yes,” he said. “是的，”他说。

“Are you a hermit?” Jack asked. “你是隐士吗？”杰克问。

“Yes,” the blind man said. “是的，”盲人说。

“What’s a hermit?” said Annie. “什么是隐士？”安妮说。

“Hermits live far away from other people,” said the blind man. “We like to be alone to think. I live in the forest so I can learn from nature.” “隐士住得离其他人很远，”盲人说。 “我们喜欢独自待着思考。 我住在森林里，这样就能向大自然学习。”

“How do you learn?” asked Jack. “你怎么学习呢？”杰克问。

“I listen,” said the blind man. “我听，”盲人说。

“Listen to what?” asked Jack. “听什么？”杰克问。

“To the chatter of the monkeys, the rumble of the elephant, the roar of the tiger,” said the man. “I have listened for so long, they have all begun to sound like one voice—the one great voice of the forest.” “听猴子的叽喳，大象的隆隆声，老虎的吼叫，”那人说。 “我听了这么久，它们都开始听起来像一个声音——森林那一个伟大的声音。”

“Did the voice tell you that a tiger got caught in a trap last night?” asked Annie. “那声音告诉你昨晚有只老虎掉进陷阱了吗？”安妮问。

“Yes,” the hermit said. “是的，”隐士说。

“And did it tell you that after we saved him, he tried to attack us?” said Jack. “它也告诉你，我们救了他之后，他想攻击我们吗？”杰克说。

The blind man smiled. 盲人微笑了。

“Please bring me one of the white flowers floating on the stream,” he said. “请给我采一朵漂在溪上的白花，”他说。

Jack wondered why the hermit was changing the subject. 杰克纳闷隐士为什么要换话题。

But Annie jumped up and hurried to the stream. She pulled at one of the large flowers. It came up, muddy root and all. She took it to the blind man. 可安妮跳起来，赶紧跑到溪边。 她轻轻拉其中一朵大花。 它被拔了出来，连带着泥泞的根什么的。 她把它拿给盲人。

“Thank you,” he said. “谢谢您。”他说。

The man touched the flower’s large white petals and its dirty root. 那人摸着花大大的白花瓣和脏脏的根。

“This perfect lotus blossom grows from dark, thick mud,” he said. “Its beauty cannot live without its ugliness. Do you understand?” “这朵完美的莲花是从又黑又厚的淤泥里长出来的。”他说。 “它的美离不开它的丑。 你们明白吗？”

“Yes,” said Jack and Annie. “明白，”杰克和安妮说。

“When you saved the tiger, you saved all of him,” said the blind man. “You saved his graceful beauty— and his fierce, savage nature. You cannot have one without the other.” “你们救老虎的时候，救的是他的全部，”盲人说。 “你们救下的是它优雅的美——也是它凶猛、野性的天性。 你不能只要其中一个，不要另一个。”

“Oh … right,” said Jack. “哦……对，”杰克说。

“Take this lotus as a thank-you gift from all the forest for saving our fierce friend,” said the blind man. “Our world would not be complete without him.” “把这朵莲花当作整座森林的感谢礼物，谢谢你们救了我们凶猛的朋友。”盲人说。 “没有它，我们的世界就不会完整。”

Annie took the gift from the hermit. 安妮从隐士手里接过礼物。

“A gift from a forest far away,” she said. “来自遥远森林的礼物，”她说。

Arf! Arf! Teddy wagged his tail. 汪！汪！ 泰迪摇着尾巴。

The langurs clapped. 叶猴拍起手来。

“We can go home now,” said Jack, “if we can just find the way.” “我们现在可以回家了，”杰克说，“只要能找到路。”

“Do not worry,” said the blind man. “Your house in the trees is close by. The elephants walked in a large circle. So you are back at the place where you started.” “不必担心，”盲人说。 “你们树上的房子就在附近。 大象走了一个大圈。 所以你们又回到了出发的地方。”

“Really?” said Jack. “真的？”杰克说。

The blind man pointed to the sky. 盲人指向天空。

There was the magic tree house, high in a nearby tree. 魔法树屋就在附近一棵高高的树上。

“Oh, great,” breathed Jack. “太好了，”杰克轻声说。

“I told you not to worry,” said Annie. She and Jack pulled on their socks and shoes and stood up. “我说过不用担心，”安妮说。 她和杰克穿上袜子和鞋子，站了起来。

Before they left, Annie touched the hand of the blind man. 离开前，安妮碰了碰盲人的手。

“Thanks for everything,” she said. “谢谢你的一切，”她说。

The man held her hand for a moment. Then he took Jack’s hand. Jack felt a wave of calm wash over him. 那人握住她的手片刻。 然后他握住杰克的手。 杰克感到一阵平静涌上心头。

“Thank you,” he said to the blind man. “谢谢您，”他对盲人说。

Kah and Ko chattered and held out their long arms. Jack and Annie hugged the two langurs. 卡和科叫着，伸出长长的胳膊。 杰克和安妮拥抱了两只叶猴。

“We’ll miss you,” said Annie. “我们会想你们的，”安妮说。

“You were great tour guides,” said Jack. “Good-bye.” “你们是很棒的导游。”杰克说。 “再见。”

Then he and Annie took off for the magic tree house with Teddy scampering after them. 然后他和安妮朝魔法树屋跑去，泰迪一蹦一跳跟在后面。

At the rope ladder, Jack put Teddy into his pack and climbed up. 到了绳梯旁，杰克把泰迪放进背包，往上爬。

Annie carried the lotus blossom as she followed them. 安妮捧着莲花跟着他们。

Inside the tree house, Jack picked up the Pennsylvania book. But before he made a wish, he looked out the window with Annie. 在树屋里，杰克拿起宾夕法尼亚的书。 但在许愿之前，他和安妮一起望向窗外。

In the distance, they saw Saba and the other elephants bathing in the stream. 远处，他们看见萨芭和其他大象正在溪里洗澡。

They saw Kah and Ko swinging on vines. 他们看见卡和科在藤蔓上荡来荡去。

They saw the tiger sunbathing in the grass, licking his sore leg. 他们看见老虎在草地上晒太阳，舔着酸痛的腿。

They saw tiny deer grazing. 他们看见小小的鹿在吃草。

They saw bright birds in the trees. 他们看见树上鲜艳的鸟。

They saw the blind man sitting in front of his cave. He was smiling. 他们看见盲人坐在洞前。他在微笑。

Jack opened the book. He pointed to a picture of the Frog Creek woods. 杰克打开书。 他指着一幅蛙溪树林的图画。

“I wish we could go home,” he said. “我希望我们能回家，”他说。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

The wind started to blow. 风开始刮起来。

It blew harder and harder. 风越刮越大。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 绝对静止。
