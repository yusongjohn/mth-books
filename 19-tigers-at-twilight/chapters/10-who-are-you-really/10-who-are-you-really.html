10. Who Are You, Really? 10. 你到底是谁？

Jack opened his eyes. 杰克睁开眼睛。

Late afternoon sunlight shined into the tree house. 傍晚的阳光照进树屋。

“Our third gift,” said Annie. “我们的第三样礼物，”安妮说。

She put the lotus blossom beside the pocket watch from the Titanic and the eagle’s feather from the Lakota Indians. 她把莲花放在泰坦尼克号的怀表和拉科塔印第安人的鹰羽旁边。

“One more gift,” she said to Teddy, “and you’ll be free from your spell.” “再有一件礼物，”她对泰迪说，“你就能摆脱咒语了。”

The little dog licked her hand. 小狗舔了舔她的手。

“Hey, tell me this,” said Jack. “How did you know Teddy wanted us to hide behind that rock?” “嘿，告诉我，”杰克说。 “你怎么知道泰迪想让我们躲到那块岩石后面？”

Annie shrugged. 安妮耸了耸肩。

“I just knew,” she said. “I think I saw it in his eyes.” “我就是知道，”她说。 “我觉得我从他眼里看出来了。”

“You did?” Jack looked into Teddy’s eyes. “真的？”杰克看着泰迪的眼睛。

The little dog tilted his head and stared back at Jack. 小狗歪着头，盯着杰克看。

Teddy’s eyes twinkled, as if they held many secrets. 泰迪的眼睛闪着光，好像藏着许多秘密。

“Who are you, really?” whispered Jack. “你到底是谁？”杰克低声说。

Teddy just smiled a doggy smile and wagged his tail. 泰迪只是露出一个狗狗式的笑容，摇了摇尾巴。

“Come for us again soon,” Annie said. “Okay?” “很快再来找我们，”安妮说。 “好吗？”

Teddy sneezed, as if to say, Of course! 泰迪打了个喷嚏，好像在说：当然啦！

Jack grabbed his pack. Then he and Annie climbed down the rope ladder. 杰克抓起背包。 然后他和安妮顺着绳梯爬下去。

When they stood on the ground, they looked up. A little black nose was poking out the tree house window. 他们站在地上时，抬头望去。 一个小黑鼻子正从树屋窗口探出来。

“Bye!” they called. “再见！”他们喊道。

Arf! Arf!

Jack and Annie took off between the trees. 杰克和安妮钻进树丛跑了。

Birds sang in the twilight. Squirrels scampered playfully through the leaves. 鸟儿在暮色中歌唱。 松鼠顽皮地在叶子间蹦来蹦去。

The Frog Creek woods were very tame after the forest in India. 比起印度的森林，蛙溪镇的树林显得非常温顺。

Soon they came to their street. As they walked to their house, the last bit of daylight was slipping away. 很快他们到了自己的街道。 他们往家走时，最后一点日光正悄悄溜走。

Before they went inside, Jack and Annie sat on their steps. 进屋前，杰克和安妮坐在台阶上。

“I have two questions,” said Jack. “If the hermit couldn’t see, how did he know about the tree house? And how did he know that we had traveled all night with the elephants?” “我有两个问题，”杰克说。 “如果隐士看不见，他怎么知道树屋的事？ 他又怎么知道我们整晚都和大象一起走？”

“Easy,” said Annie. “The one great voice of the forest told him.” “简单，”安妮说。 “森林那一个伟大的声音告诉他的。”

“Hmm,” said Jack. “嗯，”杰克说。

He closed his eyes for a moment and listened. 他闭了一会儿眼睛，听着。

He heard a car going down the street. 他听见一辆车驶过街道。

He heard a woodpecker pecking. 他听见一只啄木鸟在啄木头。

He heard crickets chirping. 他听见蟋蟀在叫。

He heard a screen door opening. 他听见纱门打开了。

He heard a mom saying, “Time for dinner, kids.” 他听见一位妈妈说：“孩子们，该吃晚饭了。”

All the sounds were like one great voice—the one great voice of home. 所有这些声音合在一起，像一个伟大的声音——家的那一个伟大的声音。
