4. Scary Things 4. 可怕的事

Jack pulled out his book and found a picture of the strong men. He read aloud: 杰克抽出书，找到那些壮汉的图画。他大声读道：

Gladiators were slaves or criminals who fought in the amphitheater (AM-fuh- thee-uh-ter). They were forced to fight each other or wild animals like lions or bears. The people of Pompeii thought a gladiator fight was great fun. 角斗士是奴隶或罪犯，他们在圆形剧场里战斗。他们被迫互相战斗，或者和狮子、熊这样的野兽战斗。庞贝的人认为角斗士比赛非常有趣。

“That’s not fun!” said Annie. “No wonder I think it’s weird here.” “那一点都不好玩！”安妮说。 “怪不得我觉得这儿不对劲。”

“No kidding,” said Jack. “That’s not like our time at all.” “说真的。”杰克说。 “跟我们的时代完全不一样。”

Jack and Annie watched as the guards led the gladiators away. They went toward a building that looked like an outdoor stadium. 杰克和安妮看着卫兵把角斗士带走。 他们朝一座像露天体育场的建筑走去。

“That must be the amphitheater,” said Jack. “Let’s check it out.” “那一定就是竞技场。”杰克说。 “我们去看看吧。”

“Oh, all right, but it’s not a library,” said Annie. “哦，好吧，可那不是图书馆。”安妮说。

He and Annie started up the street toward the amphitheater. A large crowd had gathered near the entrance. Men and women cheered as the gladiators marched inside. 他和安妮沿街朝圆形剧场走去。 入口附近聚集了一大群人。 角斗士列队走进去时，男女都在欢呼。

Jack and Annie started to follow them, but a guard held up his spear. 杰克和安妮刚要跟上，一个卫兵举起长矛拦住他们。

“No children allowed,” he said stiffly. “Run along now.” “小孩不许进。”他生硬地说。 “快走开。”

“Yes, run along! Run for your lives!” came a raspy, hissing voice. “对，快走开！ 快逃命吧！”一个沙哑、嘶嘶作响的声音传来。

Jack and Annie whirled around. It was the soothsayer. She was waving her bony finger at them. 杰克和安妮猛地转过身。 是那个占卜者。 她正朝他们挥着瘦骨嶙峋的手指。

“Oh, no! Her again!” said Jack. “Let’s get away from here.” He started to take off. “哦，不！ 又是她！”杰克说。 “我们离开这儿。”他拔腿就跑。

“Wait!” said Annie. “I want to talk to her!” “等等！”安妮说。 “我想跟她谈谈！”

“Are you crazy?” said Jack. “你疯了吗？”杰克说。

But before he could stop her, Annie ran up to the soothsayer. 可他还没来得及拦住她，安妮就朝占卜者跑了过去。

Jack watched from a distance as the woman talked to Annie. 杰克站在远处看着那个女人跟安妮说话。

“Jack, come here! Quick!” called Annie. “杰克，过来！ 快！”安妮喊道。

“Oh, brother,” said Jack. He sighed and went over to Annie and the soothsayer. “哎呀，真是的。”杰克说。 他叹了口气，朝安妮和占卜者走过去。

“Tell him,” said Annie. “告诉他。”安妮说。

The woman fixed her gaze on Jack. 那女人把目光盯在杰克身上。

“All the streams of Pompeii have dried up,” she said. “庞贝所有的溪流都干了。”她说。

“Remember the stream near the olive grove?” said Annie. “还记得橄榄林附近那条小溪吗？”安妮说。

“So what?” said Jack. “Maybe they just need rain.” “那又怎样？”杰克说。 “也许它们只是需要雨。”

“No,” said Annie. “There’re more scary things. Tell him.” “不。”安妮说。 “还有更吓人的事。告诉他。”

“All the birds have flown away,” the soothsayer said. “所有的鸟都飞走了。”占卜者说。

Jack just stared at her. 杰克只是盯着她。

“She said that all the rats left, too,” said Annie. “And the cows are making strange noises!” “她说老鼠也都走了。”安妮说。 “还有，那些牛在发出奇怪的声音！”

“But why?” said Jack. “可为什么？”杰克说。

“The sea is boiling hot,” the old woman said. “And the ground shakes and speaks.” “海水烫得像开了锅。”老妇人说。 “而且地面会震动，还会说话。”

“See, I told you!” Annie said to Jack. “看，我告诉过你！”安妮对杰克说。

“But why are these things happening?” Jack asked the soothsayer. “可这些事为什么会发生？”杰克问占卜者。

“Because the end is near,” she whispered hoarsely. “因为末日将近。”她沙哑地低声说。

“We have to leave now!” said Annie. “我们现在就得走！”安妮说。

“But what about the library?” said Jack. “可图书馆怎么办？”杰克说。

“What library?” the soothsayer asked. “什么图书馆？”占卜者问。

“Show her the story title, Jack,” said Annie. “把故事标题给她看，杰克。”安妮说。

Jack took the piece of paper from his bag. He showed the Latin writing to the soothsayer. “A book with this title is in a library in this town,” he said. 杰克从袋子里取出那张纸。 他把那段拉丁文给占卜者看。 “这城里有一座图书馆，里面有一本这个书名的书。”他说。

“So?” the old woman said. “那又怎样？”老妇人说。

“So we have to save it!” said Annie. She pulled out her secret library card. “所以我们必须把它救出来！”安妮说。 她抽出自己的秘密图书卡。

The soothsayer stared for a moment at the card and the shimmering letters on it. Then she smiled warmly at Jack and Annie. 占卜者盯着那张卡片和上面闪闪发光的字母看了一会儿。 接着她热情地对杰克和安妮笑了笑。

“Yes, I understand now,” she said. “The only library I know is in the house of Brutus.” She pointed to a large villa at the end of the street. “Look there. Quickly.” “是的，我现在明白了。”她说。 “我知道的唯一一座图书馆在布鲁图斯家里。”她指向街尽头一座大别墅。 “去那儿看看。快。”

“Will Brutus mind?” said Jack. “布鲁图斯会介意吗？”杰克说。

“Brutus and his household are all in Rome,” the soothsayer said. “That is merely their vacation villa.” “布鲁图斯和他一家人全在罗马。”占卜者说。 “那不过是他们度假的别墅。”

“But we can’t just go in and take something of theirs,” said Jack. “可我们不能就这么进去拿走他们的东西。”杰克说。

The old woman shook her head sadly. “After today, there will be nothing left in Pompeii,” she said. “Nothing at all.” 老妇人悲伤地摇了摇头。 “过了今天，庞贝什么都不会剩下了。”她说。 “什么都不会留下。”

Jack felt a chill go down his spine. 杰克觉得脊背发凉。

“Go and get what you came here for,” said the soothsayer. “Then leave at once.” “去拿你们来这儿要找的东西。”占卜者说。 “然后马上离开。”

“Thanks!” said Jack. He grabbed Annie’s hand. “Come on!” “谢谢！”杰克说。 他抓住安妮的手。 “快点！”

“Thanks!” Annie called to the old woman. “You should leave, too!” she added. “谢谢！”安妮对老妇人喊道。 “你也应该走！”她又加了一句。

Then Annie and Jack started running to the vacation villa. They ran as fast as they could. 然后安妮和杰克朝那座度假别墅跑去。 他们拼命地跑。
