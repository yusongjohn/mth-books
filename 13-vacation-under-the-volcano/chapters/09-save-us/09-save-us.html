9. Save Us! 9. 救救我们！

“The bridge must have caved in!” cried Annie. “桥一定塌了！”安妮喊道。

They stared at the dried-up stream. Pumice had piled up in huge drifts, like snow. 他们盯着那条干涸的小溪。 浮石堆积成巨大的堆，像雪一样。

“We’ll have to get through that stuff to get across!” said Jack. “我们得从那些东西里穿过去，才能到对岸！”杰克说。

He and Annie slid down the bank onto the piles of pumice. As they started to move across it, more and more fell. 他和安妮顺着岸滑下去，踩到一堆堆浮石上。 他们刚开始往前走，浮石就越落越多。

Jack tried to move through the millions of warm, grayish white pebbles. But he was trapped. 杰克想从那数百万颗温热的浅灰白色石子中走过去。 可他被困住了。

“I’m stuck!” cried Annie. “我卡住了！”安妮喊道。

“Me too!” said Jack. “我也是！”杰克说。

“Remember what Morgan said!” said Annie. “记住摩根说的话！”安妮说。

At the moment, Jack couldn’t remember anything. He was too tired and dazed. 这一刻，杰克什么都想不起来了。 他太累了，也太晕了。

“ ‘In your darkest hour, only the ancient story can save you!’ ” cried Annie. “Where’s your bag?” “‘在你最黑暗的时刻，只有古代故事能救你！’”安妮喊道。 “你的包呢？”

Jack lifted his bag into the air, above the sea of pumice. Annie grabbed it and pulled out the ancient scroll. She held it up to the dark sky. 杰克把包举到空中，举过那片浮石的海洋。 安妮抓起包，抽出那卷古代卷轴。 她把它举向黑暗的天空。

“Save us, story!” she shouted. “救救我们，故事！”她大喊。

Jack felt himself sink deeper and deeper into the pumice. Suddenly he heard a deep voice say, “Rise, son!” 杰克觉得自己在浮石里沉得越来越深。 突然，他听见一个低沉的声音说：“起来，孩子！”

Then someone lifted Jack up into the air. 接着有人把杰克举到了空中。

A great flash of fire lit the dusty darkness. In the red light, Jack saw the biggest, strongest man he’d ever seen in his life. The man looked like a gladiator—but even bigger than the ones they had seen earlier. 一道巨大的火光点亮了满是灰尘的黑暗。 在红光里，杰克看见了他这辈子见过的最高大、最强壮的人。 那人看起来像个角斗士——但比他们先前看见的还要高大。

He held Jack with one hand and Annie with the other. He placed them both on the other bank of the stream. 他一只手拎着杰克，另一只手拎着安妮。 他把他们两个都放到了小溪对岸。

“Run!” the giant gladiator boomed. “Before it’s too late!” “跑！”那个巨大的角斗士低沉地吼道。 “趁还来得及！”

Jack and Annie didn’t stop to ask any questions. Together, they charged through the olive grove. 杰克和安妮顾不上问任何问题。 他们一起冲过橄榄树林。

They jumped over fallen branches. They leaped over great cracks in the earth. Finally they came to the tree with the magic tree house. 他们跳过倒下的树枝。 他们跃过地上巨大的裂缝。 终于，他们来到了有魔法树屋的那棵树下。

They grabbed the rope ladder and scrambled up to the tree house. 他们抓住绳梯，爬上了树屋。

“Where’s the Pennsylvania book?” Jack shouted. He was too blinded by ash and dust to find the book that always took them home. “宾夕法尼亚的书呢？”杰克喊道。 他被火山灰和尘土迷得太厉害，找不到那本总能带他们回家的书。

“I’ve got it!” cried Annie. “I wish we could go there!” “我拿到了！”安妮喊道。 “我希望我们能去那儿！”

Jack felt the tree house start to spin. 杰克感觉到树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster and faster. 它转得越来越快，越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后，一切都静止了。

Absolutely, wonderfully, peacefully still. 绝对地、奇妙地、平静地静止了。
