3. Gladiators! 3. 角斗士！

Jack and Annie hid behind a fruit stall. They waited for a moment, then peered around the corner. 杰克和安妮躲在水果摊后面。 他们等了一会儿，然后偷偷绕过角落。

“I don’t see her,” said Jack. “我没看见她，”杰克说。

“Who was she?” asked Annie. “她是谁？”安妮问。

“I don’t know. But she looked nuts to me,” said Jack. “我不知道。 但她看起来有点疯疯癫癫的，”杰克说。

“What’s the book say about her?” said Annie. “书里关于她写了什么？”安妮说。

“She won’t be in there,” said Jack. “她肯定不在那儿，”杰克说。

“Just look,” said Annie. “快看，”安妮说。

Jack sighed and pulled out the book again. To his surprise he found a picture of the old woman. He read aloud: 杰克叹了口气，又拿出那本书。 让他吃惊的是，他发现了一张老妇人的照片。他大声读道：

In Roman times, there were people who could see into the future and warn others about what they saw. These people were called soothsayers. 在罗马时代，有一些人可以看到未来，并警告别人他们看到的事情。这些人被称为占卜师。

“See? She wasn’t nuts,” said Annie. “She was giving us a warning. Just like my nightmare.” “看到了吗？ “她没疯，”安妮说。 “她是在给我们一个警告。 就像我的噩梦一样。”

“Don’t pay attention to that stuff,” said Jack. “Soothsayers are from olden times. People from our time don’t believe in them.” “别理会那些东西，”杰克说。 “预言家来自很久很久以前。” 我们这个时代的人不相信它们。”

“Well, I do,” said Annie. “I’m sure something bad is about to happen.” “嗯，我是的，”安妮说。 “我肯定有什么坏事要发生了。”

Jack sighed. “Come on, we have to keep going. We have to find the lost library. Then we’ll leave at once.” 杰克叹了口气。 “快点，我们必须继续前进。 我们必须找到那座失落的图书馆。 然后我们马上离开。”

“We’d better,” said Annie. “我们最好快点,”安妮说。

They left the fruit stand and kept walking through the forum. Soon they came to a large building. Crowds were streaming in and out of it. 他们离开了水果摊，继续穿过广场走着。 不久他们来到一座大建筑物前。 人群正不断地进出那里。

“Is that a library?” said Annie. “那是图书馆吗？”安妮说。

“Let’s look,” said Jack. He looked in the Roman book. He found a picture of the building and read aloud: “我们去看看吧，”杰克说。 他看了那本罗马书。 他找到了那座建筑物的图片，并大声读出来：

Most people in Pompeii did not have a bathtub at home, so they went daily to the Public Baths. Not only did people wash at the baths, but they also swam, played sports, and visited with their friends. 庞贝的大多数人家里没有浴缸，所以他们每天都去公共浴场。人们不仅在浴场洗澡，还游泳、运动，和朋友一起聊天。

“That sounds like going to the pool,” said Annie. “But it’s not a library. Let’s keep walking.” “听起来像是去游泳池，”安妮说。 “但这不是图书馆。 我们继续走吧。”

They kept walking, until soon they came to a fancy building with large columns. 他们继续走，直到不久后来到一座有大柱子的豪华建筑前。

“Is that a library?” said Annie. Jack found a picture of the building and read aloud: “那是图书馆吗？”安妮说。 杰克找到了这座建筑的图片，并大声读出来：

The people of Pompeii believed that many gods and goddesses ruled the world. This is the Temple of Jupiter, their chief god. In this temple, they prayed to Jupiter and offered him gifts. Today, we call the stories about Jupiter and other gods and goddesses “myths.” 庞贝的人们相信，许多神和女神统治着世界。这是朱庇特神庙，他们是主神。在这个神庙里，他们向朱庇特祈祷，并送给他礼物。今天，我们把关于朱庇特和其他神和女神的故事叫做“神话”。

“My teacher read myths to us,” said Jack. “I remember stories about Hercules and Apollo.” “我的老师给我们读过神话故事，”杰克说。 “我记得关于赫拉克勒斯和阿波罗的故事。”

“Yeah, my teacher read them, too,” said Annie. “I like stories about Venus and Medusa.” “是的，我的老师也读过它们，”安妮说。 “我喜欢关于维纳斯和美杜莎的故事。”

“Hey, maybe the story we’re looking for is a myth,” said Jack. “嘿，也许我们要找的故事是一个神话，”杰克说。

“Right,” said Annie. “Come on, hurry. Let’s keep looking.” “对，”安妮说。 “快点，快点。 我们继续找。”

They left the forum and turned onto a wide street. Jack gasped. In front of them was an incredible sight. 他们离开了论坛，转上了一条宽阔的街道。杰克惊呼起来。 在他们面前是一个令人难以置信的景象。

Tall warriors with huge muscles were walking in a line. They wore fancy helmets and carried heavy shields. 高高的战士们肌肉发达，排着队走着。 他们戴着华丽的头盔，拿着沉重的盾牌。

Soldiers, thought Jack. 士兵们，杰克想。

Then he saw that the warriors’ feet were chained together, and guards walked with them. 然后他看到战士们的脚被锁链拴在一起，还有卫兵和他们一起走。

“Gladiators!” he whispered. “角斗士！”，他小声说。
