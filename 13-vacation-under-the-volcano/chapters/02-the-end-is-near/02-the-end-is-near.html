2. The End Is Near 2. 末日将近

Jack opened his eyes. He pushed his glasses into place. 杰克睁开眼睛。 他把眼镜推正。

Morgan was gone. And so were Jack’s jeans, T-shirt, sneakers, and backpack. 摩根已经不在了。 杰克的牛仔裤、T恤、运动鞋和背包也不见了。

Instead, he had on a white tunic with a belt, sandals that laced up, and a leather bag. 取而代之的是，他穿着一件带腰带的白色短袍，脚上是系带凉鞋，还挎着一个皮袋子。

He looked at Annie. She was dressed the same way. Morgan had made them look just like kids in an ancient Roman town. 他看着安妮。 她也穿着同样的衣服。 摩根让他们看起来就像古罗马小镇里的孩子。

“I guess this is what Morgan meant,” said Jack, “when she said she’d help us blend in.” “我想这就是摩根说的意思，”杰克说，“她说会帮我们融入当地。”

“I feel like Cinderella,” said Annie. “I like these clothes.” “我觉得自己像灰姑娘。”安妮说。 “我喜欢这些衣服。”

“Yeah,” said Jack, although he felt a little as if he was wearing a dress. “嗯。”杰克说，虽然他觉得自己有点像穿着裙子。

Annie looked out the window. 安妮向窗外望去。

“It’s pretty here,” she said. “这儿真漂亮。”她说。

Jack looked with her. They had landed in a grove of trees. On one side of the grove rose a gentle-looking mountain. On the other, a town sparkled in the sunlight. 杰克也跟着看。 他们降落在一片小树林里。 树丛的一边耸立着一座看起来很平缓的山。 树丛的一边耸立着一座看起来很平缓的山。另一边，一座城镇在阳光下闪闪发亮。

“I wonder where we are,” said Jack. He opened the book about Roman times. He read aloud: “不知道我们在哪儿。”杰克说。 他打开那本关于罗马时代的书，大声读道：

Almost 2,000 years ago, on August 24, A.D. 79, the seaside town of Pompeii (pom-PAY) was a typical Roman town. Many Romans went there on their vacation. They built large houses called villas and planted groves of olive trees along the slopes of a mountain called Mount Vesuvius (vuh-SOO-vee-us). 大约2000年前，公元79年8月24日，海边的庞贝镇（Pompeii）是一个典型的罗马城镇。很多罗马人去那里度假。他们在那里建造了叫做别墅的大房子，并在一座叫做维苏威火山（Mount Vesuvius）的山的山坡上种了很多橄榄树。

Annie kept looking out the window as Jack pulled out his notebook and pencil. He wrote: 安妮继续看着窗外，杰克拿出笔记本和铅笔。他写道：

图中文字：Vacation in Pompeii

August 24, A.D. 79

houses called villas 图中文字：庞贝的假期 公元79年8月24日 叫做别墅的房子

Jack looked out the window again. 杰克又向窗外望去。

“It does look like a nice place to go on vacation,” he said. “这儿看起来确实是个度假的好地方。”他说。

“We must have landed in one of the olive groves,” said Annie. “我们一定是落在其中一片橄榄树林里了。”安妮说。

“Yeah, and that town must be Pompeii,” said Jack. “对，那座城一定就是庞贝。”杰克说。

He looked in the opposite direction. “And that mountain must be Mount Vesuvius.” 他朝相反的方向望去。 “那座山一定就是维苏威火山。”

Annie shivered. “That name sounds scary,” she said. 安妮打了个哆嗦。 “那名字听起来好吓人。”她说。

“Really?” said Jack. “Not to me.” He looked down at his notes. “真的吗？”杰克说。 “我倒不觉得。”他低头看着自己的笔记。

“Hey! Did you feel that?” said Annie. “嘿！ 你感觉到了吗？”安妮说。

“What?” Jack looked up. “什么？”杰克抬起头。

“The ground shook. I heard it rumble, too,” said Annie. “地面在晃。 我还听见它轰隆隆响。”安妮说。

Jack frowned. “I think you were just dreaming again,” he said. 杰克皱起眉头。 “我觉得你又在做梦了。”他说。

“No, I wasn’t,” said Annie. “Something feels wrong about this place. I really think we should go home now.” “不，我没有。”安妮说。 “这地方感觉不对劲。 我真觉得我们现在就该回家。”

“Are you nuts?” said Jack. “We have to find that lost story for Morgan. Besides, I’ve always wanted to see a Roman town.” “你疯了吗？”杰克说。 “我们得为摩根找到那个失落的故事。 而且，我一直想看看罗马城镇。”

He threw his notebook and the Roman book into his leather bag. Then he went down the rope ladder. 他把笔记本和罗马书扔进皮袋。 然后顺着绳梯爬下去。

“Come on!” he called when he stepped onto the ground. “快点！”他踩到地面时喊道。

Annie just stared down at him. 安妮只是低头盯着他。

“Don’t be chicken,” he said. He pushed his glasses into place. “Come on. It’ll be fun.” “别当胆小鬼。”他说。 他把眼镜推正。 “快点。会很好玩的。”

Annie still didn’t move. 安妮还是不动。

What’s wrong with her? Jack wondered. Usually I’m the worried one. 她怎么了？杰克想。 平时担心的都是我。

“Come on,” he pleaded. “We can’t let Morgan down.” “拜托了。”他恳求道。 “我们不能让摩根失望。”

Annie let out a big sigh. “Okay, but we’d better find that story fast,” she said. Finally she climbed down the rope ladder. 安妮长长地叹了口气。 “好吧，可我们最好快点找到那个故事。”她说。 她终于顺着绳梯爬了下来。

The sun was bright and hot as they took off between the olive trees. 阳光又亮又热，他们钻进橄榄树丛往前走。

With Mount Vesuvius at their backs, they headed for the town of Pompeii. 维苏威火山在他们身后，他们朝庞贝城走去。

“That’s weird,” said Annie. “I don’t hear any birds.” “真奇怪。”安妮说。 “我听不见鸟叫。”

She was right. The grove was strangely silent. 她说得对。 那片树丛出奇地安静。

“Don’t worry about it,” said Jack. “Maybe they’re all at the beach. Come on—let’s go over that bridge.” “别客气。”杰克说。 “也许它们都去海边了。 来吧——我们过那座桥。”

He led the way to a small wooden bridge that crossed a narrow stream. But when they got to the stream, they found it had dried up. 他带头走向一座跨过窄溪的小木桥。 可等他们走到溪边，却发现溪水已经干了。

“That’s really weird,” said Annie. “这真奇怪。”安妮说。

“Don’t worry,” said Jack. “It just means there hasn’t been much rain.” “别担心。”杰克说。 “只是说明这儿好久没下雨了。”

They crossed the bridge and stepped onto a busy street. It was paved with stone. 他们过了桥，走上一条热闹的街道。 路面是用石头铺成的。

People were buying things in the open shops that lined the street. Some looked rushed and busy. Others moved slowly. Kids walked with their parents. Groups of teenagers talked and laughed. 人们在街两边敞开的店铺里买东西。 有些人看上去匆匆忙忙。 另一些人则走得很慢。 孩子们跟着父母走。 一群群青少年有说有笑。

They don’t seem very different from people shopping in Frog Creek, Jack thought. Except for the clothes, of course. 他们看起来和蛙溪镇购物的人没什么两样，杰克想。 当然，除了衣服以外。

“How will we ever find the ancient lost library?” said Annie, glancing around. “我们到底怎么才能找到那座古代失落的图书馆？”安妮四下张望着说。

“I don’t know,” said Jack. “Just be on the lookout.” “我不知道。”杰克说。 “只要留意着就行。”

They walked past shops that had huge jars in them. When Jack looked closely, he saw that they were filled with grain, dried fruit, and olives. Meat hung from the ceilings of some of the stores. 他们走过里面摆着大罐子的店铺。 杰克仔细一看，发现里面装满了谷物、干果和橄榄。 有些店里的天花板上挂着肉。

They passed a noisy tavern where people were eating and drinking. A young man played a stringed instrument. 他们经过一家热闹的小酒馆，里面的人正在吃喝。 一个年轻人在弹一种弦乐器。

“See, there’s nothing to worry about,” said Jack. “This place isn’t all that different from our time.” “看，没什么好担心的。”杰克说。 “这地方跟我们的时代没那么不一样。”

“It’s not that,” said Annie with a worried look. “不是那个意思。”安妮忧心忡忡地说。

“See, there’s a barbershop and a shoe shop,” said Jack, pointing. “看，还有理发店和鞋店。”杰克指着说。

A boy was getting his hair cut by the barber. A girl was trying on a new pair of sandals with her mother. 一个男孩正在让理发师剪头发。 一个女孩正和妈妈一起试穿一双新凉鞋。

“It is kind of like home,” Annie agreed. “还真有点像家里。”安妮同意道。

They kept walking, until they passed a bakery filled with freshly baked flat breads. 他们继续走，直到经过一家摆满刚出炉扁面包的面包店。

“That bread is like pizza,” said Annie, smiling. “那面包像比萨饼。”安妮笑着说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. “嗯。”杰克说。

The good smells from the bakery made him feel even more at home. Jack looked at Annie. She was still smiling as they walked on. 面包店里的香味让他觉得更自在了。 杰克看着安妮。 他们继续往前走时，她还在笑。

Soon they came to a large square filled with people, carts and horses, and more shopping stalls. 很快他们来到一个大广场，里面挤满了人、马车和更多货摊。

“Honey cakes! Stuffed dates! Peacock eggs!” sellers called. “蜂蜜蛋糕！夹心枣！ 孔雀蛋！”小贩们喊道。

Farmers were selling grapes, garlic, and onions. Fishermen were selling all kinds of fish. A few people stood on boxes, giving speeches to small crowds. 农民在卖葡萄、大蒜和洋葱。 渔夫在卖各种各样的鱼。 有几个人站在箱子上，对着一小群人演讲。

“Hey, I bet this is the forum!” said Jack. He reached in his bag for his book. He flipped through it until he found a picture of the square. He read: “嘿，我敢打赌这就是广场！”杰克说。 他伸手到袋子里拿书。 他一页页翻着，直到找到广场的图画。他读道：

The center of a Roman town was called a forum. The forum was the main place where people met to sell goods and discuss politics. 罗马城镇的中心叫做论坛。论坛是人们买卖商品和讨论政治的主要地方。

“I was right!” said Jack. He pulled out his notebook. He wrote: “我说对了！”杰克说。 他抽出笔记本，写道：

图中文字：forum: center of town 图中文字：论坛：市中心

“Jack,” whispered Annie. “Jack!” She tugged on his tunic. “Look.” “杰克，”安妮小声说。 “杰克！”她扯了扯他的短袍。 “看。”

Jack glanced up. Annie nodded toward an old woman who was staring straight at them. 杰克抬起头。 安妮朝一位正盯着他们的老妇人点了点头。

She wore a black cloak. Her gray hair was tangled and wild. She seemed to be missing her teeth. 她穿着一件黑色斗篷。 她的灰头发乱糟糟的，十分蓬乱。 她好像缺了牙。

The woman pointed a bony finger at Jack and Annie. 那妇人用瘦骨嶙峋的手指指着杰克和安妮。

“The end is near!” she said in a raspy voice. “Go home, strangers!” “末日将近！”她用沙哑的声音说。 “回家去，陌生人！”

“Yikes,” said Annie. “呀。”安妮说。

“We’d better get away from her,” said Jack, “before people wonder who we are.” “我们最好离她远点，”杰克说，“免得人家怀疑我们是谁。”

He put away his notebook. Then he and Annie hurried away. The old woman cackled behind them. 他把笔记本收了起来。 然后他和安妮匆匆走开了。 那个老妇人在他们身后咯咯地笑。
