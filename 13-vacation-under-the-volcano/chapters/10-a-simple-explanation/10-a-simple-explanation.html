10. A Simple Explanation 10. 一个简单的解释

Jack didn’t move. He had never been so tired in all his life. 杰克一动不动。 他这辈子从来没有这么累过。

“Breathe,” said Annie. “呼吸。”安妮说。

Jack gulped in cool, clean air. He opened his eyes. He couldn’t see a thing. 杰克大口吸进凉爽干净的空气。 他睁开眼睛。 他什么也看不见。

“Take off your glasses,” said Annie. “They’re filthy.” “把眼镜摘下来。”安妮说。 “眼镜脏死了。”

Jack took off his glasses. The first thing he saw was his backpack. The white tunics and lace-up sandals were gone. So were their pillow hats and the leather bag. 杰克摘下眼镜。 他看见的第一样东西是自己的背包。 白色短袍和系带凉鞋都不见了。 枕头帽和皮袋也不见了。

Jack let out a long, deep breath. As he cleaned his glasses on his shirt, a voice came from behind him. “I’m very glad to see you safe and sound.” 杰克长长地松了一口气。 他用衬衫擦眼镜时，身后传来一个声音。 “看见你们安然无恙，我真高兴。”

Morgan le Fay stood in the corner of the tree house. She looked as lovely and mysterious as ever. 摩根·勒菲站在树屋的角落里。 她看上去还是像往常一样美丽而神秘。

“Happy to be home?” she asked. “高兴回家了吗？”她问。

Jack nodded. The sound of the erupting volcano still echoed in his ears. 杰克点了点头。 火山喷发的声音还在他耳边回响。

“It—it was pretty scary,” he said in a hoarse voice. “那——那真的好吓人。”他声音沙哑地说。

“I know. But you were truly brave,” said Morgan. “You witnessed a famous event in history. Nowadays, scientists study the remains of Pompeii to find out more about Roman times.”

“I feel bad for all those people,” said Annie. “我为那些人感到难过。”安妮说。

“Yes,” said Morgan. “But most of the people of Pompeii did escape. The city wasn’t completely buried by ash until the next day.” “是的。”摩根说。 “不过庞贝的大多数人确实逃出来了。 这座城直到第二天才被火山灰完全埋住。”

“We were almost trapped,” said Annie. “But we asked the ancient story to save us. Then a huge gladiator helped us.” “我们差点被困住。”安妮说。 “可我们请那个古代故事救了我们。 后来一个巨大的角斗士帮了我们。”

Jack reached into his pack. He breathed a sigh of relief. The scroll was still there! He took it out. It was covered with dust and ashes. 杰克把手伸进背包。 他如释重负地松了一口气。 卷轴还在！他把它拿了出来。 它上面满是灰尘和灰烬。

“Here’s the story,” said Jack. “故事在这儿。”杰克说。

He handed it to Morgan. 他把它递给摩根。

“I am deeply grateful,” she said softly. “You risked everything to bring this to me. I can never thank you enough.” “我深深感激。”她轻声说。 “你们冒着一切危险把它带给了我。 我对你们的感谢说不尽。”

“Don’t worry about it,” said Jack. He didn’t want Morgan to know how scared he had been. “别客气。”杰克说。 他不想让摩根知道自己有多害怕。

“Yeah, no problem,” said Annie. “嗯，没什么。”安妮说。

Morgan smiled. “You are amazing Master Librarians,” she said. “Do you think you’re up to rescuing another ancient story?” 摩根笑了。 “你们真是了不起的图书大师。”她说。 “你们觉得自己还能再去救另一个古代故事吗？”

“Yes!” said Annie. “好啊！”安妮说。

“Now?” said Jack. Actually he was a little tired now. “现在？”杰克说。 其实他现在有点累了。

Morgan laughed. “No. Take a nice vacation. Come back two weeks from today. Then you will take a trip to ancient China,” she said. 摩根笑了。 “不。 好好度个假吧。 两周后再回来。 那时你们将去古代中国旅行。”她说。

“Ancient China? Wow,” said Annie. “古代中国？哇。”安妮说。

“Oh, man,” said Jack. “天哪。”杰克说。

“Go home now and rest,” said Morgan. She handed Jack his pack. “现在回家休息吧。”摩根说。 她把背包递给杰克。

“Thanks,” he said. “Bye.” “谢谢。”他说。 “再见。”

“Bye,” said Annie. “再见。”安妮说。

Morgan gave them a little wave. Then Annie and Jack left the tree house and headed down the rope ladder. When they reached the ground, Jack looked up. 摩根向他们轻轻挥了挥手。 接着安妮和杰克离开树屋，沿绳梯下去。 他们落到地面后，杰克抬起头。

“Morgan,” he called. “What is that story about—the one we just rescued?” “摩根，”他喊道。 “那个故事讲的是什么——我们刚救回来的那个？”

“It’s called The Strongest Man in the World, ” said Morgan. “It’s a lost tale about Hercules.” “它叫《世界上最强壮的人》。”摩根说。 “那是一个关于赫拉克勒斯的失落故事。”

“Hercules?” said Jack. “赫拉克勒斯？”杰克说。

“Yes, he was one of the heroes of the Greeks and the Romans,” said Morgan. “He was a son of Jupiter.” “是的，他是希腊人和罗马人的英雄之一。”摩根说。 “他是朱庇特的儿子。”

“Oh, wow. Now I get it,” said Annie. “哦，哇。 现在我懂了。”安妮说。

“I’m taking it back now to Camelot’s library,” said Morgan. “Everyone will be so excited to read it, thanks to you.” She waved to them. “Good-bye for now!” “我现在就把它带回卡米洛特的图书馆。”摩根说。 “大家都会很兴奋去读它，多亏了你们。”她向他们挥手。 “暂时再见！”

As she waved, the wind began to whirl. The tree house began to spin. In a blur of shadow and light, Morgan and the magic tree house were gone. 她一挥手，风就开始打转。 树屋开始旋转。 在一片光影的模糊中，摩根和魔法树屋消失了。

Jack and Annie started walking through the woods. 杰克和安妮开始穿过树林往前走。

“Get it? Get it?” said Annie. “明白了吗？ 明白了吗？”安妮说。

“Get what?” said Jack. “明白什么？”杰克说。

“We were saved by Hercules!” said Annie. “We asked the story to save us—and Hercules appeared!” “救我们的是赫拉克勒斯！”安妮说。 “我们请故事救我们——赫拉克勒斯就出现了！”

“That’s not possible,” said Jack. “It was just some gladiator guy. The story of Hercules is a myth. That means he never really lived.” “那不可能。”杰克说。 “那只是某个角斗士家伙。 赫拉克勒斯的故事是个神话。 意思是他从来没有真正活过。”

They left the woods and started up their street. 他们离开树林，沿着自家的街道往上走。

“I know it’s a myth,” said Annie. “But I have a simple explanation.” “我知道那是神话。”安妮说。 “可我有一个简单的解释。”

“What?” said Jack. “什么？”杰克说。

“Hercules is a myth to people in this time,” said Annie. “But in Roman times, lots of people believed he was real. So, since we were in Roman times, he was real to us.” “对这个时代的人来说，赫拉克勒斯是神话。”安妮说。 “可在罗马时代，很多人相信他是真的。 所以，既然我们当时在罗马时代，他对我们来说就是真的。”

“I don’t know … ” said Jack. “我不知道……”杰克说。

“Did you ever hear the saying?” said Annie. “When in Rome, do as the Romans do.” “你听过那句谚语吗？”安妮说。 “入乡随俗。”

Jack laughed. “Yeah.” He looked up at the sky. “Thanks, Hercules,” he said softly, “whatever you are.” 杰克笑了。 “对。”他抬头望着天空。 “谢谢你，赫拉克勒斯。”他轻声说，“不管你是什么。”

“Jack! Annie!” their dad called from their front porch. “Time to go!” “杰克！ 安妮！”爸爸从前廊喊道。 “该走了！”

“Oh, brother, I forgot,” said Jack. “哎呀，我都忘了。”杰克说。

“Yeah, I hope nothing exciting happens on our vacation,” said Annie. “是啊，我希望我们这次度假别再发生什么刺激的事。”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. “I hope it’s really, really boring.” “嗯。”杰克说。 “我希望这次真的、真的很无聊。”

“Hurry!” their dad said. “快点！”爸爸说。

“Coming!” they called. “来了！”他们喊道。

Then they took off running for home—and a restful vacation. 然后他们拔腿朝家跑去——去过一个安安静静的假期。
