11. Swing that Music 11. 摆动那音乐

As the streetcar rumbled down Canal Street, Jack looked over his shoulder. Dipper was right. African Americans were sitting in the back, while only white people were in the front. Jack hadn’t even noticed it on their first streetcar ride. 有轨电车沿着运河街缓缓驶过时，杰克回头看了看。迪普说的没错。 非洲裔美国人坐在后面，而只有白人坐在前面。 杰克第一次坐有轨电车的时候，甚至都没注意到这一点。

Jack’s heart felt heavy. Why would anyone not want to sit next to someone just because they are a different color? he wondered. How could anyone ever be mean to Dipper? Dipper, who is gentle and friendly and kind? Dipper, who hears music everywhere? 杰克的心感觉很沉重。 为什么有人会因为别人肤色不同，就不想坐在一起呢？他想。 怎么有人会对迪普这么不友好呢？ 迪普，那个温柔、友善、善良的迪普？ 迪普，那个到处都能听到音乐的迪普？

Jack listened to the click- clack of the streetcar rolling down the tracks. The sound had a good beat. Jack tapped his hand against his knee, until the streetcar came to a stop. 杰克听着有轨电车在轨道上咔哒咔哒地滚动声。 声音节奏感很强。 杰克一边拍膝盖，一边等电车停下来。

“Let’s go,” said Annie. “我们走吧，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie stood up. Canal Street was bright with lights. People were still selling things on the sidewalk. 杰克和安妮站了起来。 运河街灯火通明。 人们还在人行道上卖东西。

“What time is it, please?” Jack asked the conductor. “请问现在几点了？”杰克问司机。

“Five minutes after nine, boy,” said the conductor. “Y’all better head for home.” “九点零五分，小子，”司机说。 “你们最好赶紧回家。”

“We are!” said Annie. “我们会的！”安妮说。

Jack and Annie hopped off the streetcar and started to run. They ran to the bottom of Canal Street and crossed to the palm trees in the grove near the Mississippi River. In the dark, they found the rope ladder and scrambled up it into the tree house. 杰克和安妮跳下有轨电车，开始跑起来。 他们跑到运河街的最下面，穿过马路，跑到密西西比河附近的棕榈树林里。 在黑暗中，他们找到了绳梯，手脚并用地爬上了树屋。

Jack grabbed the Pennsylvania book that would take them home. 杰克抓起了那本会带他们回家的宾夕法尼亚书。

“Wait!” said Annie, looking out the window. “Look!” “等一下！”安妮看着窗外说。 “看！”

A brightly lit Mississippi showboat was rolling up the river. A large paddle wheel behind the boat was churning the water. Music was coming from on board. 一艘明亮的密西西比河游轮正沿着河面缓缓驶来。 船后方有一个大大的螺旋桨，正在搅动河水。 船上传来了音乐声。

Annie grabbed Jack’s arm. “Listen! The ‘Heebie-Jeebies’!” she said. 安妮抓住了杰克的手臂。 “听！ “‘Heebie-Jeebies’！”她说道。

Jack listened. He could hear the trio singing: Little Mack, Happy, and Big Nose Sidney. But the best sound of all was the bright sound of a horn sailing through the New Orleans night. 杰克听着。 他能听到三个人在唱歌：小马克、快乐和大鼻子西德尼。 但最好的声音是小号在新奥尔良的夜晚中悠扬响起的声音。

“It’s Dipper!” said Annie. “It’s got to be!” “是迪普！”安妮说。 “肯定是他！”

“He caught the boat!” said Jack. “Just in time!” “他抓住了船！”杰克说。 “刚好及时！”

Jack and Annie listened to the joyful swinging sound of Dipper’s music until the riverboat rolled out of sight. 杰克和安妮听着迪普音乐欢快的摇晃声，直到河船滚出视线。

Jack heaved a happy sigh. Then he pointed at their Pennsylvania book. “I wish we could go home,” he said. 杰克开心地叹了口气。 然后他指着他们的宾夕法尼亚州的书。 “我希望我们能回家，”他说。

The wind began to blow. 风开始吹起来。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。

Jack and Annie were wearing their own clothes again. Rain tapped gently against the roof of the tree house. A cool breeze blew through the window. 杰克和安妮又穿上了自己的衣服。 雨点轻轻敲打着树屋的屋顶。 一阵凉风吹进了窗户。

“We have to get home before Mom and Dad wake up,” said Annie. “我们必须在爸爸妈妈醒来之前回家，”安妮说。

“Right,” said Jack. He pulled A History of New Orleans Music out of his backpack and left it on the floor. “对，”杰克说。 他从背包里拿出一本《新奥尔良音乐史》，然后把它放在了地上。

Annie put the trumpet beside it. Then they headed down the rope ladder. They pulled up their rain hoods. As they tramped in their boots through the wet autumn woods, they were both quiet. 安妮把小号放在它旁边。 然后他们顺着绳梯往下爬。 他们拉起了雨帽。 当他们穿着靴子踩着湿漉漉的秋天树林走的时候，两个人都沉默不语。

Finally Annie said, “I feel good.” 最后，安妮说：“我感觉很好。”

“I feel good, too,” said Jack. “We accomplished our mission.” “我也感觉很好，”杰克说。 “我们完成了任务。”

“But I feel other things, too,” said Annie. “I feel mad that Dipper couldn’t sit on the streetcar with us.” “但我也感觉到了其他事情，”安妮说。 “我感到很生气，迪珀不能和我们一起坐电车。”

“Me too,” said Jack. “And it wasn’t just in New Orleans. A long time ago, they had laws like that in lots of places.” “我也是，”杰克说。 “而且这不仅仅发生在新奥尔良。” 很久以前，在很多地方都有这样的法律。

“Unbelievable,” said Annie. “难以置信，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. “是的，”杰克说。

“And I feel scared when I think of those spiders in their webs,” said Annie. “而且当我想到那些蜘蛛在它们的网上时，我感到害怕，”安妮说。

“Oh, they didn’t bother anybody,” said Jack. “But I still feel scared when I think of ghosts.” “哦，它们没有打扰任何人，”杰克说。 “但是当我想到鬼的时候，我仍然感到害怕。”

“Actually, they didn’t bother anybody, either,” said Annie. “其实，他们也没打扰任何人，”安妮说。

“True,” said Jack. “And neither did that bat.” “对，”杰克说。 “那只蝙蝠也没有。”

Annie laughed. “That’s true, too,” she said. “I feel sad when I think we’ll never see Dipper again.” 安妮笑了。 “这也是真的，”她说。 “当我想到我们再也看不到迪普了，我感到很难过。”

“We can listen to his music,” said Jack. “我们可以听他的音乐，”杰克说。

“Yeah… and I guess the music all around us will always make us think of him,” said Annie. “Like right now Dipper would hear the music of the rain.” “是啊……我想我们周围的声音总会让我们想起他，”安妮说。 “就像现在，迪普会听到雨的声音。”

“Tappity-tap-tap,” said Jack. “叮叮叮——”，杰克说。

“And the music of our feet scrunching over the dead leaves—” said Annie. “还有我们踩在枯叶上的脚步声——”，安妮说。

“Scrunchity-scrunch-scrunch,” said Jack. “嘎吱嘎吱嘎吱——”，杰克说。

“Patti-pat-pat!” sang Annie. “啪嗒啪嗒啪嗒！”，安妮唱道。

“Skid-dat-de-dat!” sang Jack. “哧哒哧哒哧哒！”，杰克唱道。

“Skid-dat-de-doh!” sang Annie. “哧哒哧哒哆——”，安妮唱道。

“Go, Mama! Go!” shouted Jack. “快点，妈妈！ “快点！”杰克喊道。

And the two of them ran out of the Frog Creek woods and up their street, heading for home. 然后他们两个人从蛙溪镇的树林里跑出来，沿着他们的街道跑回家。
