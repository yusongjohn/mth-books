9. Working Man Blues 9. 工人蓝调

Dipper stopped playing. The three boys stopped singing. Jack and Annie stopped drumming. 迪普停止了演奏。 三个男孩停止了唱歌。 杰克和安妮停止了敲鼓。

There was silence. They all crept to the open doorway and stepped outside. The rain had stopped, and the wind had died down. The air felt clean and cool. Stars shone overhead. The pirate ghosts were gone. 周围一片寂静。 他们都悄悄地走到敞开的门口，走了出去。 雨停了，风也停了。 空气感觉干净又凉爽。 头顶的天空中闪烁着星星。 海盗幽灵不见了。

“Whoa, that was something!” said Little Mack. “What just happened?” “哇，那真是太神奇了！”，小马克说道。 “刚才发生了什么？”

“Was it a dream?” asked Big Nose Sidney. “Were those ghosts real?” “这是梦吗？”，大鼻子西德尼问道。 “那些鬼是真的吗？”

“I don’t know,” said Dipper. “But I’ll tell you this: you’ll never, ever get me back in that shop again.” “我不知道，”迪普说。 “但我要告诉你：你永远、永远别想再让我进那家店了。”

Everyone laughed. Even Happy looked happy. “Hey, Dipper, how’d you get so good on that horn?” he asked, grinning. 大家都笑了。 就连快乐也看起来很开心。 “嘿，迪普，你怎么吹号这么厉害？”他微笑着问。

“I practiced for two years at the Waif’s Home,” said Dipper. “That’s how.” “我在弃儿之家练习了两年，”迪普说。 “就是这样。”

“You got to blow that horn while we sing!” said Big Nose Sidney. “Come with us now! We really do have an important gig tonight on a riverboat!” “你们得在我们唱歌的时候吹那个号！”大鼻子西德尼说。 “现在就跟我们走吧！” 今晚我们在河船上真的有一个很重要的演出！

“A riverboat?” Jack and Annie said together. They looked at each other. Their research book said that Louis Armstrong developed his musical talents performing on riverboats! “河船？”杰克和安妮异口同声地说。 他们互相看了看。 他们的研究书上说，路易斯·阿姆斯特朗是在河船上表演时发展了他的音乐才华！

“That’s right,” said Little Mack. “没错，”小麦克说。

“Oh, wow, Dipper, you have to go with them!” said Annie. “哦，哇，迪普，你得跟他们一起去！”安妮说。

“Yeah, man!” said Jack. “对啊，伙计！”杰克说。

But Dipper just shook his head. “Sorry, folks, but I can’t play tonight. I have to get up early in the morning to haul coal.” 但迪普只是摇了摇头。 “对不起，各位，但我今晚不能玩了。 我明天早上要早起去运煤。”

“Aww, Dipper,” said Little Mack. “啊，迪普，”小马克说。

“Aww, Dipper,” said Jack. “啊，迪普，”杰克说。

“Don’t y’all worry about me,” said Dipper. “Have a good time on the boat, fellas. Hang on to that smile, Happy.” “你们别担心我，”迪普说。 “祝你们在船上玩得开心，伙计们。 紧紧抓住那张笑脸，哈皮。”

“I’ll try,” said Happy. “我会试试的，”哈皮说。

“See you later,” said Big Nose Sidney. “再见了，”大鼻子西德尼说。

“So long to y’all, too,” Little Mack said to Jack and Annie. “你们也再见了，”小马克对杰克和安妮说。

“Bye,” said Jack. “再见，”杰克说。

“Good luck on your gig,” said Annie. The three boys waved and took off. “祝你演出顺利，”安妮说。 三个男孩挥挥手，然后飞快地跑开了。

Dipper looked after them for a long moment. Then he turned to Jack and Annie. 迪普盯着他们看了好一会儿。 然后他转身面对杰克和安妮。

“Here’s your horn back,” he said. “Thanks for letting me play it.” He handed the trumpet to Annie. “这是你的号，还给你，”他说。 “谢谢你让我玩它。”他把小号递给了安妮。

“Do you want to keep it?” she said. “你还想留着它吗？”她说。

“No, thanks. I have my own horn back at my house, a cornet they gave me at the Waif’s Home,” said Dipper. “Someday when I’m grown, maybe I’ll bring it out again.” “不用了，谢谢。 我家里有一个自己的号，是我在孤儿院时他们给我的短号，”迪普说。 “等我长大了，也许还会再拿出来吹。”

“I think you should have gone with the fellas, Dipper,” said Jack, “to share your musical gifts—” “我觉得你本应该和那几个男孩一起去，迪普，”杰克说，“去分享你的音乐天赋——”

“I know, I know,” said Dipper, “to share my musical gifts with the world.” He shook his head as if he were shaking off the thought. Then he beamed a big smile at Jack and Annie. “Hey! Weren’t we talking about dessert a little while back? That’s a gig I can get behind and still get some sleep. Come on!” “我知道，我知道，”迪普说，“我要把我的音乐才华分享给全世界。”他摇了摇头，好像要把这个想法甩掉。 然后他对着杰克和安妮露出了灿烂的微笑。 “嘿！ 我们刚才不是还谈到甜点吗？ 那是一个我可以支持的演出，还能睡个好觉。来吧！”，

As Dipper led Jack and Annie down the rain-slicked street, the wet sidewalks glistened like silver. Life had returned to the French Quarter. Horses and mules splashed through puddles. Streetlamps burned brightly outside dance halls and restaurants. Waiters carried tables and chairs back outside. 当迪普带着杰克和安妮沿着被雨水打湿的街道走下时，湿漉漉的人行道闪闪发亮，就像银子一样。 生活又回到了法国区。 马和骡子在水坑里踩出水花。 街灯在舞厅和餐馆外明亮地燃烧着。 服务员们把桌椅搬回外面。

When Dipper, Jack, and Annie came to Jackson Square, they found kids playing music again. A band was playing a song Jack knew: “When the Saints Go Marching In.” A few people in costumes were wandering about. 当迪普、杰克和安妮来到杰克逊广场时，他们又看到孩子们在演奏音乐。 一个乐队正在演奏一首杰克熟悉的歌：‘当圣徒们行进时。’一些穿着服装的人在四处走动。

Dipper led Jack and Annie to the back door of the River Café. “The cook here is a friend of mine. So don’t worry about the waiters,” he said with a wink. “Be back in a minute.” He slipped into the café kitchen. 迪普带杰克和安妮走到河畔咖啡馆的后门。 ‘这里的厨师是我朋友。' ‘所以不用担心服务员，’他眨了眨眼说道。 ‘马上回来。’他溜进了咖啡馆的厨房。

As Jack and Annie waited for Dipper, they could hear the jazzy music coming from the square: 当杰克和安妮等着迪普时，他们能听到从广场传来的爵士乐：

Oh, when the saints go marching in,

Oh, when the saints go marching in. 哦，当圣徒们开始行进时，哦，当圣徒们开始行进时。

“Teddy was right. I love New Orleans,” said Annie. ‘泰迪说得对。' ‘我爱新奥尔良，’安妮说。

“Me too,” said Jack. “But how are we ever going to accomplish our mission for Merlin? Dipper seems like he’s never going to change his mind about making music.” ‘我也是，’杰克说。 “但是，我们怎么才能完成梅林交给我们的任务呢？ 迪普看起来好像永远都不会改变他要制作音乐的想法。”

“I know,” said Annie. “In fact, I was just thinking that we might have to do something really drastic.” “我知道，”安妮说。 “事实上，我刚刚在想，我们可能不得不做一件非常极端的事情。”

“What’s that?” said Jack. “那是什么？”杰克说。

“Show him our research book,” said Annie. “给他看看我们的研究书，”安妮说。

“Whoa!” said Jack. “Do you really think—” “哇！”，杰克说。 “你真的觉得——”

Before Jack could finish, Dipper came back outside. “Mmm-mmm! Talk about something good!” he said. He clutched a greasy napkin filled with freshly made doughnuts. “Follow me.” 杰克还没说完，迪珀就从外面回来了。 “谈谈一些好事！”，他说。 他紧紧抓着一张沾满油渍的餐巾，上面放着刚做好的甜甜圈。 “跟我来。”

As Jack and Annie followed Dipper to the river, Jack’s mind was racing. Should they show Dipper the research book? It would prove they’d come from the future. What would Dipper say? What would he think? It’s too weird, Jack thought. 当杰克和安妮跟着迪普走向河边时，杰克的脑海里飞速思考着。 他们应该把研究书给迪普看吗？ 这能证明他们来自未来。 迪普会说什么呢？ 他会怎么想呢？ 这太奇怪了，杰克心想。

“Let’s just try talking to him first,” Jack whispered to Annie. She nodded. “我们先试着和他谈谈吧，”杰克低声对安妮说。她点了点头。

Dipper led them to a bench near the water. Jack and Annie sat on the wet wood on either side of him. Dipper unwrapped the doughnuts and handed one to Jack and one to Annie and kept one for himself. 迪普带他们来到水边的一张长椅旁。 杰克和安妮坐在他两边的湿木头上。 迪普解开甜甜圈，给了杰克一个，给了安妮一个，自己留了一个。

“Careful,” Dipper said. “The sugar will get all over y’all.” “小心，”迪普说。 “糖会弄得到你们身上。”

Jack lifted the warm, sticky doughnut to his mouth and took a big bite. Powdered sugar, flour, butter, vanilla—all melted in his mouth. It was mighty good. 杰克把又热又粘的甜甜圈举到嘴边，咬了一大口。 糖粉、面粉、黄油、香草——全都融化在他的嘴里。 味道好极了。

Nobody talked while they ate their doughnuts. When they were finished, they all wiped their sticky fingers on their shirts and pants. By now, Jack’s clothes were soaked with rainwater, coal dust, kitchen grease, sweat, and grime. A little powdered sugar and butter won’t make a bit of difference, he thought. 他们吃甜甜圈的时候，谁都没说话。 吃完后，他们都用衬衫和裤子擦了擦粘着糖的手指。 到这时候，杰克的衣服已经被雨水、煤灰、厨房油渍、汗水和污垢浸透了。 一点糖粉和黄油不会有什么影响，他心想。

“So, Dipper,” said Annie. “You know you’re a really great musician, don’t you?” “所以，迪普，”安妮说。 “你知道你是个很棒的音乐家，对吧？”

Dipper smiled at her. 迪普对她笑了笑。

“Actually, you’re a creative genius,” added Jack. “其实，你是个有创意的天才，”杰克补充道。

Dipper laughed out loud. “And y’all know who you are, don’t you?” 迪普大声笑了起来。 “你们知道你们是谁，对吧？”

“Yup, the biggest potato heads you ever met,” finished Jack. “没错，你们见过最大的土豆脑袋，”杰克说完。

“But this time we’re right,” said Annie. “但这次我们是对的，”安妮说。

“Nope, sorry,” said Dipper. “I’m definitely no genius. The truth is I never got past elementary school. I don’t even know how to read music.” “不，对不起，”迪普说。 “我肯定不是天才。” 说实话，我连小学都没毕业过。 我甚至不知道怎么读乐谱。”

“But don’t you love to play music?” said Annie. “但是你不爱弹音乐吗？”安妮说。

“Yeah, sure, I do. Somehow I’ve got music in my bones,” said Dipper. “Sad to say, all I really ever want to do is blow my horn.” “当然爱，我确实喜欢。” 不知怎么的，我骨子里就喜欢音乐，”迪普说。 “说实话，我真正想做的，就是吹我的号。”

“Then why don’t you?” said Jack. He felt desperate. Even without their mission for Merlin, it seemed incredibly sad that Dipper had turned his back on his music. “那你怎么不吹呢？”杰克说。 他感到很绝望。 即使没有他们为梅林执行的任务，迪普背弃音乐这件事看起来也实在太让人伤心了。

“Yeah, why don’t you go play on the riverboat with the fellas?” said Annie. “It wouldn’t hurt you to miss a few hours’ sleep.” “是啊，你去和那些男孩子一起在河轮上玩吧。”安妮说。 “你多睡几个小时也不会对你有什么坏处。”

Dipper took a deep breath and let it out slowly. “When I was twelve years old, I got too rowdy one time,” he said. “It was New Year’s Eve. I was singing with the fellas, and I got carried away and fired off a gun… just into the air. I wasn’t trying to hurt anybody. But I got caught and I got sentenced to two years in the Waif’s Home. I just got out a little while ago. I feel really bad for letting my family down like that. So right now, all I want to do is help them by keeping a steady job.” 迪普深吸了一口气，然后慢慢地吐了出来。 “我十二岁的时候，有一次太调皮了，”他说。 “那是新年的前一晚。 我和那些男孩子一起唱歌，结果我太兴奋了，朝天上开了一枪…… 我并不是想伤害任何人。 但我还是被抓到了，被判了两年去少年之家。 我刚刚才出来不久。 我因为让家人失望而感到非常难过。 所以现在，我唯一想做的就是通过一份稳定的工作来帮助他们。”

“But what about a job playing music?” said Jack. “Great musicians can make a lot of money and help their families.” “那有没有一份演奏音乐的工作呢？”杰克说。 “伟大的音乐家可以赚很多钱，还能帮助他们的家人。”

“Not playing the music I want to play” said Dipper. “At least I haven’t met any. Have you?” “我没有演奏我想演奏的音乐。”迪普说。 “至少我没遇到过。你遇到过吗？”

“Yes. Actually we have,” said Annie. She turned and looked at Jack. “We have to do it.” “是的。” “其实我们有，”安妮说。 她转过身看着杰克。 “我们必须这么做。”

Jack sighed, then nodded slowly. Annie was right. He reached into his bag and pulled out their research book. 杰克叹了口气，然后慢慢地点了点头。安妮是对的。 他从包里拿出他们的研究书。
