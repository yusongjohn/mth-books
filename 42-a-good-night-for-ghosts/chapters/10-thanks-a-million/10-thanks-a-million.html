10. Thanks a Million 10. 谢谢你一万次

Jack pushed his glasses into place and opened A History of New Orleans Music. 杰克把眼镜推到正确的位置，打开了一本《新奥尔良音乐史》。

“What’s that, man?” Dipper asked. “那是什么，伙计？”迪普问。

“It’s a history book,” said Jack. “这是一本历史书，”杰克说。

“Teddy and Kathleen gave it to us,” said Annie. “泰迪和凯瑟琳送给我们的，”安妮说。

“Oh, yeah, my best friends,” said Dipper. “哦，对，我最好的朋友，”迪普说。

Jack looked in the index of the book. He found the right page and turned to it. 杰克在书的目录里查找。 他找到了正确的那页，翻到了那页。

“I’m going to read something to you, man,” said Jack. “Just listen.” And Jack read: “我要给你读点东西，伙计，”杰克说。 “听着。”杰克开始读：

As a young teenager, Louis Armstrongoften performed with “kid bands” on the streets of the city. Eventually he played with older musicians in dance halls, and he developed his musical talents performing on Mississippi river-boats. When he was twenty-one, he moved to Chicago, where he played in the well-known band of his old friend Joe Oliver. 年轻的时候，路易斯·阿姆斯特朗经常和一些“少年乐队”在城市街头表演。后来，他和年长的音乐家们在舞厅里一起演奏，并在密西西比河的轮船上发展了他的音乐才华。他21岁的时候搬到了芝加哥，在那里和他老朋友乔·奥利弗的著名乐队一起演奏。

“Joe Oliver?” said Dipper. “Joe’s in that book? I’ m in that book?” “乔·奥利弗？”迪普问。 “乔在那本书里？ 我在那本书里？

“Yep. Hold on, there’s more,” said Jack. He read: “是的。 等一下，还有更多，”杰克说。他继续读：

Over time, Louis Armstrong became world-famous, but he always called New Orleans home. The city honored him by naming a large park the Louis Armstrong Park. It also named its airport the Louis Armstrong International Airport. 随着时间的推移，路易斯·阿姆斯特朗变得世界闻名，但他一直把新奥尔良当作自己的家。这座城市为了纪念他，把一个大公园命名为路易斯·阿姆斯特朗公园，还把机场命名为路易斯·阿姆斯特朗国际机场。

“What’s that? International airport?” asked Dipper. “那是什么？ 国际机场？”迪普问。

“That’s where planes fly in and out from all over the world,” said Annie. “那是飞机从世界各地飞进飞出的地方，”安妮说。

Dipper started laughing. “Y’all are pulling my leg.” 迪普开始笑起来。 “你们在骗我。”

“No,” said Jack. “We’re not. Look at this, Dipper.” He held up the book and showed Dipper the picture of Louis Armstrong, the King of Jazz, playing the trumpet in front of a huge crowd. “That’s you, Dipper.” “没有，”杰克说。 “我们没骗你。 你看这个，迪普尔。”他举起书，给迪普尔看路易斯·阿姆斯特朗——爵士乐之王——在一大群人面前吹小号的图片。 “那就是你，迪普尔。”

Jack waited for Dipper to laugh and say the picture wasn’t him. But Dipper stopped smiling and nodded. “Yeah. Yeah, I’ve seen that picture before,” he said softly. 杰克等着迪普笑一笑，说照片里的人不是他。 但迪普停下了微笑，点了点头。 “是的。 是的，我以前见过这张照片，”他轻声说道。

“You have?” said Jack, stunned. “你见过？”杰克惊讶地问。

“You’ve seen this picture before?” said Annie. “Where?” “你以前见过这张照片吗？”安妮说。 “在哪里？”

“Here.” Dipper touched his chest. “Here, in my heart. It’s the picture I’ve had in my heart for a long time, like a dream. Hey, y’all, is this a dream?” “在这里。”迪普摸了摸自己的胸口。 “在这里，在我的心里。 这是我一直藏在心里的图片，就像一个梦一样。 嘿，你们，这是梦吗？

Jack and Annie laughed. Annie smiled. “Yes, you could say that,” she said. 杰克和安妮笑了。安妮微笑着。 “可以说是的，”她说。

“But it’s a true dream,” said Jack. “但这是一个真实的梦，”杰克说。

“Keep that picture in your heart, Dipper,” said Annie. “Hold it close to you, and one day you’ll be living it. We promise.” “迪普，把这张图片一直留在你心里，”安妮说。 “把它紧紧握在手里，总有一天你会真正拥有它。我们向你保证。”

For a long time the three of them just stared at the picture. When Jack looked at Dipper again, he saw tears glisten in Dipper’s eyes. 很长一段时间，他们三个人只是盯着那张照片。 当杰克再次看向迪普时，他看见迪普的眼里闪着泪光。

“Okay, I’ll do that,” said Dipper. He wiped his eyes with the palms of his hands. “I think I can keep my jobs and start making a little room for my music, too.” “好吧，我会做到的，”迪普说。 他用手掌擦了擦眼睛。 “我想我可以继续工作，同时也能为我的音乐腾出一点空间。”

“Yes!” said Annie. “太好了！”安妮说。

“Maybe you should go play with the fellas on the riverboat tonight,” said Jack. “也许你今晚应该去和河轮上的那些小伙子们一起玩玩，”杰克说。

“Yeah, maybe I’ll try and do that,” said Dipper. “嗯，也许我会试试看，”迪普说。

“Great!” said Annie. “太棒了！”安妮说。

Jack heaved a sigh and put away their book. 杰克叹了口气，把他们的书收了起来。

Dipper stood up. “But now y’all have to get going. On the eve of All Saints’ Day, there’s a curfew for kids under thirteen. They have to be off the streets by nine, or the paddy wagon will pick ’em up.” 迪普站起来。 “但是现在你们得赶紧走了。 万圣节前夕，十三岁以下的孩子有宵禁。 他们必须在九点前离开街道，否则警车会把他们带走。”

“So… you’re definitely on the path to sharing your musical gift with the world. Right, man?” asked Jack. “所以……你肯定已经走上分享你音乐天赋的道路了。 对吧？”杰克问。

Dipper laughed. “I reckon I am,” he said. “Thanks to a couple of potato heads.” 迪普笑了。 “我想是吧，”他说。 “多亏了那两个土豆脑袋。”

“Okay, good,” said Jack. He and Annie stood up. “To get home, we need to walk to Bourbon Street and catch a streetcar to Canal Street.” “好，太好了，”杰克说。 他和安妮站了起来。 “要回家，我们需要走到波本街，然后坐有轨电车到运河街。”

“Let’s go,” said Dipper. “我们走吧，”迪普说。

The three of them left the waterfront. They walked past Jackson Square and headed past the cathedral on St. Peter Street. 他们三个人离开了 waterfront（ waterfront 通常翻译为“ waterfront”或“水边”） 他们走过杰克逊广场，朝圣彼得街上的大教堂走去。

“I’ll walk y’all back to Bourbon Street,” said Dipper. “我送你们大家回波本街，”迪普说。

“Then do you have time to ride back with us to Canal Street?” said Annie. “So we can hang out together a little longer on the train?” “那你能和我们一起去运河街吗？”安妮说。 “这样我们就能在火车上多待一会儿？”

“You know I can’t do that,” said Dipper. “They won’t let me sit with y’all on the streetcar.” “你知道我不能这么做，”迪普说。 “他们不让我和你们一起坐在有轨电车上。”

“Why not?” asked Jack. “为什么呢？”杰克问。

“I’d have to sit in the back while y’all sit up front,” said Dipper. “我得坐在后面，而你们坐在前面，”迪普说。

“What are you talking about?” said Annie. “你在说什么？”安妮说。

“Y’all are white. I’m black,” said Dipper. “你们是白人。” “我是黑人，”迪普说。

“So?” said Annie. “那又怎样？”安妮说。

“Black folks aren’t allowed to sit with white folks. That’s the way it is,” said Dipper. “黑人不能和白人坐在一起。 事情就是这么规定的，”迪普说。

“Are you kidding? That’s crazy!” said Annie. “你在开玩笑吗？” “这太疯狂了！”安妮说。

“No, I’m not kidding,” said Dipper. He stopped walking and looked closely at Jack and Annie. “Where are y’all from? How’d you get that crazy history book with my picture in it? And why did y’all come here to find me?” “不，我没开玩笑，”迪普说。 他停下脚步，仔细看了看杰克和安妮。 “你们是从哪儿来的？ 你们是怎么得到那本有我照片的奇怪历史书的？ 为什么你们要来这里找我？”

“It’s really, really hard to explain, Dipper,” said Jack. “But just know this: one day things are going to change. Everybody will sit together on trains and buses and planes.” “真的很难解释，迪普，”杰克说。 “但你要知道：总有一天事情会发生改变。 大家都会一起坐在火车、公共汽车和飞机上。”

“And one day an African American man will run for president of the United States,” said Annie. “And millions of people—of all colors—will vote for him.” “总有一天，一位非裔美国人会竞选美国总统，”安妮说。 “而且有成千上万不同肤色的人都会投票给他。”

“And he’ll win!” said Jack. “他肯定会赢！”杰克说。

Dipper laughed and shook his head. “Okay, now I know I’m dreaming,” he said, “but I really like this dream.” 迪普笑了，摇了摇头。 “好吧，现在我知道我在做梦，”他说，“但我真的很喜欢这个梦。”

“It’s the truth,” said Annie. “We promise.” “这是真的，”安妮说。 “我们承诺。”

By now they had come to the corner of St. Peter and Bourbon Street. “This is where I leave you,” said Dipper. 这时他们已经来到了圣街的拐角处。 皮特和波本街。 “我在这里送你们，”迪普说。

Annie threw her arms around Dipper and gave him a big hug. “Good-bye, Dipper!” 安妮张开双臂抱住迪普，给了他一个大大的拥抱。 “再见，迪珀！”

Jack did the same. “Bye, man,” he said. “Thanks a million.” 杰克也做了同样的事。 “再见，伙计，”他说。 “谢谢你的一百万次。”

“Same to you,” said Dipper. “Hey, there’s your streetcar now.” “也祝你一切顺利，”迪珀说。 “嘿，你的有轨电车来了。”

The streetcar glided to a stop at the corner. Jack and Annie hopped aboard and sat in front. As the streetcar went up Bourbon Street, they stuck their heads out the window and waved to Dipper. He waved back. Jack and Annie waved and waved, until they couldn’t see Dipper anymore. 有轨电车滑行着停在了拐角处。 杰克和安妮跳上电车，坐在前面。 当有轨电车沿着波本街行驶时，他们把头伸出窗外，向迪珀挥手。他回了挥手。 杰克和安妮一直挥手，直到再也看不到迪珀了。
