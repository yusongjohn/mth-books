7. Skid-Dat-De-Dat! 7. 滑哒哒！

Dipper closed the door. 迪普关上了门。

It was pitch-black inside the shop. As the wind howled outside, the broken shutters banged against the brick walls. A gust of damp air blew through the room. 铁匠铺里黑得伸手不见五指。 外面的风呼啸着，破旧的百叶窗在砖墙上砰砰作响。 一股潮湿的风吹进了房间。

“It’s dark in here,” said Annie. “And cold.” “这里好黑，”安妮说。 “而且好冷。”

“It’s creepy,” said Jack. “这里好吓人，”杰克说。

“Yup,” said Dipper. “Let’s leave. I’ve changed my mind about staying here. We can find some other place to get out of the storm.” “是啊，”迪普说。 “我们走吧。” 我改变了留在这里的想法。 “我们可以找另一个地方躲过这场暴风雨。”

“Good idea,” said Annie. “好主意，”安妮说。

“Great idea!” said Jack. “太好了！”杰克说。

Jack heard Dipper rattle the door handle. “Uh-oh,” said Dipper. 杰克听到迪珀摇晃门把手。 “糟了，”迪珀说。

“Uh-oh what?” said Jack. “糟了什么？”杰克说。

“Won’t open,” said Dipper. “It’s stuck.” “打不开，”迪珀说。 “它卡住了。”

Jack felt the hair rise on the back of his neck. The shutters banged in the wind. 杰克感觉后颈的头发都竖起来了。 窗户在风中砰砰作响。

“Hold on a second, I’ve got some matches in my pocket,” said Dipper. “I just hope they didn’t get wet in the rain.” “等一下，我口袋里有火柴，”迪普说。 “我只是希望火柴没有被雨淋湿。”

Jack heard Dipper trying to strike a match. He tried one, two, three times—then he held up a small flame. 杰克听到迪普试着擦火柴。 他试了一次，两次，三次——然后举起了一个小火苗。

“Yay” breathed Annie. “太好了，”安妮轻声说。

Jack looked around the room. In the dancing light, he could make out wooden buckets on the floor, a couple of broken chairs, a doorway leading to a back room, and— 杰克环顾了一下房间。 在摇曳的灯光下，他看清了地板上的木桶、几把破椅子、一扇通往后屋的门，还有——

Squeak! A bat flapped above their heads. 吱呀！ 一只蝙蝠在他们头顶上扑棱着翅膀。

“Ahhh!” Jack, Annie, and Dipper ducked. “啊啊啊！”杰克、安妮和迪普扑闪着躲开了。

The match went out. 火柴熄灭了。

Dipper quickly lit another match. He held it up high, trying to light the room. Jack didn’t see the bat, but he saw a bricked-over fireplace, some rusty lanterns, and lots of spiderwebs. 迪普赶紧又划亮了一根火柴。 他高高举起来，试图照亮整个房间。 杰克没看到蝙蝠，但他看到了一个被砖块封住的壁炉、一些生锈的提灯，还有许多蜘蛛网。

“Yikes!” said Annie. “天哪！”安妮说道。

The match went out. 火柴又熄灭了。

“Help,” said Annie in a small voice. “More light, please.” “帮帮忙，”安妮用很小的声音说。 “请再亮一点。”

“Nobody panic,” said Dipper. “I got two matches left.” “别慌张，”迪普说。 “我只剩下两根火柴了。”

“Just two?” said Annie. “才两个？”安妮说。

“Hey, I thought I saw some lanterns,” said Jack. “Near the fireplace. Maybe we could light them.” “嘿，我以为我看到一些灯笼了，”杰克说。 “在壁炉旁边。 也许我们可以点燃它们。

“Good idea,” said Dipper. “Maybe there’s oil still in ’em. I just hope I can light the wicks. Or we’ll be left in the dark with the bats.” “好主意，”迪普说。 “也许里面还有油。 我希望我能点燃蜡芯。 否则我们就会和蝙蝠一起陷入黑暗。

“And spiders,” said Annie. “还有蜘蛛，”安妮说。

And ghosts, Jack thought. 还有鬼魂，杰克想。

Dipper struck his next-to-last match. He held it up to find the lanterns. 迪普点燃了他倒数第二根火柴。 他举起来找灯笼。

“Over here,” said Jack, pointing. “在这儿，”杰克指着说。

“I see,” said Dipper. But as he knelt on the floor, the match went out. “I got just one more,” he whispered. “So we better be real careful.” “我看见了，”迪普说。 但当他跪在地上时，火柴熄灭了。 “我只剩最后一根了，”他小声说。 “所以我们得格外小心。”

Dipper lit his last match. Jack slowly lifted the glass on both the lanterns. Very carefully, Dipper touched the match flame to the wick of the first lantern. The wick sputtered and flickered to life. A yellow glow filled the room. 迪普点燃了他最后一根火柴。 杰克慢慢地把两个灯笼上的玻璃盖都拿了起来。 迪珀非常小心地把火柴的火焰靠近第一个灯笼的灯芯。 灯芯猛地一跳，然后亮了起来。 房间里充满了黄色的光芒。

“Ahh!” said Dipper. He lit the second lantern. “Beautiful. You can each carry one.” “啊！”迪珀说道。 他点着了第二盏灯笼。 “真漂亮。 你们每人可以拿一盏。

Jack picked up one of the lanterns. Annie put down her trumpet and picked up the other one. The firelight cast eerie shadows on the walls. 杰克捡起了一盏灯笼。 安妮放下她的喇叭，捡起了另一盏。 火光在墙上投下诡异的影子。

Screeeee! 嘶——！

A sound from the back room startled them. It sounded like the creak of a door opening. Jack’s heart began to pound. 从后面的房间传来的声音吓了他们一跳。 听起来像是门吱呀一声打开了。 杰克的心开始怦怦跳。

WHAM! The sound of a door slamming shut! 砰！ 门砰地一声关上了！

Jack’s heart beat faster and faster. 杰克的心跳得越来越快。

“Hey!” called Dipper. “Who’s back there?” “嘿！”迪普喊道。 “后面是谁？”

No one answered. 没人回答。

Clump. Clump. Clump. The sound of footsteps on stairs! 啪嗒。啪嗒。 楼梯上有脚步声！

Jack held his breath. 杰克屏住呼吸。

“Who’s there?” yelled Dipper. “谁在那里？”迪普大喊。

“WOOOOOOO!”

“Show yourself!” yelled Dipper. “出来吧！”迪普大喊。

The shutters outside banged harder against the walls. 外面的百叶窗猛烈地撞着墙壁。

“WOOOOOOO!”

“Mercy,” whispered Dipper. “求饶，”迪普低声说。

Jack’s hand trembled, shaking the lantern and making the shadows in the room dance even more wildly. 杰克的手颤抖着，晃动了提灯，让房间里的影子跳得更加疯狂。

The moaning came again: “WOOOOOOO!” 又传来一声呜咽：“WOOOOOOO!”

“Annie!” said Jack. “The trumpet! The time is right!” “安妮！”杰克说。 “是喇叭！ 时机到了！

“I know it! I feel it!” said Annie. She put down her lantern and grabbed the trumpet. “Sing, Jack!” “我知道！ 我感觉到了！“安妮说。 她放下她的提灯，抓起了号角。 “杰克，唱吧！“

Annie lifted the magic trumpet to her lips and blew. A pure, smooth sound flowed from the trumpet and filled the room. As Annie played, Jack started singing: 安妮把魔法号角举到嘴边，吹了起来。 一个纯净、流畅的声音从号角中流出，充满了整个房间。 安妮在玩耍时，杰克开始唱歌：

Ghost, ghost,

Leave us alone!

Stop, stop!

Stop your moan… ing! 鬼啊，鬼啊，别打扰我们！停一停！停住你的呜咽……！

What a stupid song, Jack thought, but they were the only words that came to his mind. Then he remembered Dipper’s advice: When you can’t think of words, just sing sounds. Make ’em up. Put your heart in it. 杰克心想，这是一首很傻的歌，但这是他脑子里唯一能想到的词。 然后他想起了迪珀的建议：当你想不出词的时候，就唱一些声音。编一些出来。 把你的感情放进歌声里。

So Jack started singing nonsense sounds. He sang with all his heart, pouring all his feelings into the sounds, telling the ghost to go: 于是杰克开始唱一些毫无意义的声音。 他全心全意地唱着，把所有的感情都倾注在这些声音里，告诉鬼魂快走：

Skid-dat-de-dat!

Skid-dat-de-dow!

Skiddle-skiddle

Daddle-daddle

Outta here NOW! 哧哒哧哒！哧哒哧哒！哧iddle哧iddle 哒iddle哒iddle 快走开，现在！

Thumping noises came from the other room, as if heavy things were falling. 从另一个房间传来咚咚的响声，好像有什么重的东西在掉下来。

“Who’s there?” shouted Dipper. “谁在那里？”迪珀喊道。

Annie stopped playing. 安妮停止了玩耍。

Jack stepped back in fear, waiting for something awful to happen. Then he heard laughing and whispering. 杰克吓得后退了一步，等着可怕的事情发生。 然后他听到了笑声和窃窃私语。

“Hey!” shouted Dipper. He picked up Annie’s lantern and headed into the other room. Jack and Annie followed. “嘿！”迪普喊道。 他捡起安妮的提灯，走进了另一个房间。 杰克和安妮跟了上去。

Little Mack, Happy, and Big Nose Sidney were crawling toward the back door. 小麦克、哈皮和大鼻子西德尼正爬向后门。

“Stop!” yelled Dipper. “What are y’all doing here?” “停！”迪普大喊道。 “你们在这里干什么？”

The three boys all talked at once: “We fell out of the attic!” “Felt like something was pushing us out!” “Yeah! Then down the stairs and toward the door!” 三个男孩同时说话：“我们从阁楼掉下来了！”“感觉有什么东西在推我们出去！”“对！” 然后下楼，朝门口走去！”

“Jack’s song pushed you downstairs!” said Annie, laughing. “He ordered the ghost to leave us alone! His singing was magic.” “杰克的歌把你推到楼下啦！”安妮笑着说。 “他命令鬼魂离开我们！” 他的歌声有魔力。”

“Your playing was magic, too!” Dipper said to Annie. “You two really put your hearts in it.” “你弹得也很有魔力！”迪普对安妮说。 “你们两个真的全身心投入了。”

“Thanks,” said Jack. “谢谢，”杰克说。

Dipper looked down at Little Mack. “Now tell us what you three fools were up to!” he said. 迪普看着小马克。 “现在告诉我们，你们三个傻瓜刚才在干什么！”他说。

“We decided to play a joke on y’all,” said Little Mack. “So we snuck through the back door and up to the attic.” “我们决定要捉弄你们一下，”小马克说。 “所以我们从后门溜进去，然后爬到了阁楼。”

“We thought you left because you were scared of ghosts!” said Annie. “我们认为你们是因为害怕鬼才离开的！”安妮说。

“Heck no, man,” said Little Mack. “绝对不行，伙计，”小马克说。

“We’re not afraid of ghosts,” said Happy. “我们不怕鬼，”哈皮说。

“Not even a little bit,” said Big Nose Sidney. “一点都不怕，”大鼻子西德尼说。

Suddenly a cold wind blasted through the room. The lanterns flickered out. An eerie green light lit the blacksmith shop. 突然，一阵冷风席卷了整个房间。 灯笼熄灭了。 一种诡异的绿光照亮了铁匠铺。

“WHAT?” a voice roared. “ NOT AFRAID OF GHOSTS?” “什么？”一个声音怒吼道。 “不怕鬼？”

The voice seemed to come from everywhere—and nowhere. 这个声音似乎来自四面八方，又似乎无处不在。

“AHHHHHHH!” the kids all screamed together. “啊啊啊啊啊！”孩子们一起尖叫起来。

“HAH-HAH-HAH!” Mean-sounding laughter echoed through the shop. It grew louder and louder. “HAH-HAH-HAH!” “哈哈哈！”一个听起来很坏笑声响彻了整个店铺。 声音越来越大。 “哈哈哈！”，

“AHHHHHHH!” the kids all screamed again. “啊——！”孩子们又尖叫起来。

Stomping noises thundered in the attic overhead. 阁楼上传来踩踏的声音，像雷一样轰隆隆的。

Everyone shrieked and froze with terror. 大家都尖叫着，吓得一动不动。

Down from the attic came a pirate. His face was hidden by the brim of a black hat. He wore a gray jacket with a double row of buttons, a red sash, and dark pants tucked inside black boots. 一个海盗从阁楼下来了。 他的脸被一顶黑帽子的帽檐遮住了。 他穿着一件灰色的夹克，上面有两排纽扣，一条红色的腰带，还有深色的裤子，塞进黑色的靴子里。

The pirate looked like a real person, except you could see right through him. 这个海盗看起来像一个真正的人，不过你一眼就能看穿他是假的。
