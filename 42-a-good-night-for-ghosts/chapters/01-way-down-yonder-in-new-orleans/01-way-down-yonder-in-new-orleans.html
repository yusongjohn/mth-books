1. Way Down Yonder in New Orleans 1. 在新奥尔良的遥远地方

Jack was asleep. He was dreaming that he was sleeping on a boat. It was rocking back and forth, back and forth… 杰克睡着了。 他梦见自己正在一艘船上睡觉。 船来回摇晃，来回摇晃……

“Jack.” “杰克。”

Jack opened his eyes. It was just getting light outside. Rain was tapping against the window-pane. Tappity-tap- tap. Jack closed his eyes again. 杰克睁开了眼睛。 外面刚刚开始亮起来。 雨点正敲打着窗户。滴答滴答滴。 杰克又闭上了眼睛。

“Jack, get up.” “杰克，快起来。”

Jack opened one eye and looked up. Annie stood next to his bed. She was already dressed. She was even wearing her raincoat. “They’re here,” she whispered. 杰克睁开一只眼睛，朝上看。 安妮站在他的床边。 她已经穿好了衣服。 她甚至已经穿上了雨衣。 “他们来了，”她小声说。

“No, they’re not,” Jack said. He closed his eye. “不，他们还没来，”杰克说。 他闭上了眼睛。

“Yes, they are,” said Annie. “They’re waiting for us.” “是的，它们在那儿，”安妮说。 “它们在等我们。”

“How do you know?” Jack asked. “你怎么知道的？”杰克问。

“I dreamed it,” said Annie. “我梦见了，”安妮说。

“Oh, you dreamed it.” Jack turned over and pulled the covers over his head. “Go back to bed. It’s really early, and it’s raining.” “哦，你梦见了。”杰克翻了个身，把被子拉到头上。 “回床上去吧。 现在真的很早，而且还在下雨。”

“Come on, Jack,” said Annie. “I saw them. They were wearing their cloaks and looking out the tree house window.” “杰克，快起来，”安妮说。 “我看见它们了。 它们穿着斗篷，正从树屋的窗户往外看。”

“Great,” said Jack. “I just dreamed I was sleeping on a boat.” “太好了，”杰克说。 “我刚才梦见自己在船上睡觉。”

“But my dream was real, Jack,” said Annie. “但我的梦是真的，杰克，”安妮说。

Jack pretended to snore. 杰克假装打起了呼噜。

“Okay,” said Annie. “I guess you want me to go there all by myself. You want me to have a great adventure while you just lie here, dreaming about sleeping. If that’s really what you want, I’ll leave you alone.” “好吧，”安妮说。 “我想你是想让我一个人去那儿。 你想让我去经历一场精彩的冒险，而你只是躺在这儿， dreaming about sleeping。 如果这真的是你想要的，我会让你一个人待着。”

“Good,” said Jack. “Have fun.” “很好，”杰克说。 “玩得开心。”

“Don’t worry, I will,” said Annie, and she left Jack’s room. “别担心，我会的，”安妮说，然后离开了杰克的房间。

Jack lay still for a moment, listening to the rain fall outside. Darn, he thought, what if she’s right? 杰克在房间里躺了一会儿，听着外面的雨声。 哎呀，他心想，万一她说得对呢？

Jack heaved a sigh. Then he climbed out of bed. He pulled on his clothes and grabbed his backpack. He slipped down the stairs, put on his rain boots and raincoat, then headed out the front door. 杰克叹了口气。 然后他爬下床。 他穿上衣服，拿起背包。 他悄悄地走下楼梯，穿上雨靴和雨衣，然后走出了前门。

Annie was standing on the porch, waiting for him. “Ready?” she said. 安妮正站在门廊上，等着他。 “准备好了吗？”，她说。

Jack just grunted. But as he and Annie took off into the cool, rainy dawn, he woke up completely. As they charged up the sidewalk, Jack’s heart pounded with excitement. By the time they headed into the Frog Creek woods, Jack felt like he’d dreamed Annie’s dream, too. 杰克只是咕哝了一声。 但是当杰克和安妮冲进凉爽、下雨的黎明时，他完全醒了过来。 当他们冲上人行道时，杰克的心因为兴奋而砰砰跳。 等他们走进蛙溪镇的树林时，杰克感觉他也梦到了安妮的梦。

Raindrops tapped on tree branches. Jack and Annie scrunched over fallen red and gold leaves until they came to the tallest oak. Jack looked up. 雨滴敲打着树枝。 杰克和安妮踩着掉落在地上的红色和金色的叶子，直到他们来到最高的橡树前。杰克抬头看去。

“Ta-da!” said Annie. “太棒了！”安妮说。

The tree house was back. And Teddy and Kathleen were dressed in their dark cloaks, looking out the window. 树屋回来了。 泰迪和凯瑟琳穿着他们的黑色斗篷，正从窗户往外看。

A Good Night for Ghosts

“Good morning!” called Kathleen. “早上好！”凯瑟琳喊道。

“We dreamed about you!” said Jack. “At least Annie did.” “我们梦到你了！”杰克说。 “至少安妮做到了。”

Teddy and Kathleen smiled, as if this news didn’t surprise them at all. 泰迪和凯瑟琳微笑着，好像这个消息一点也不让他们吃惊。

Annie and Jack started up the rope ladder. When they climbed inside the tree house, they hugged the two young enchanters. “Welcome,” said Kathleen. Her beautiful sea-blue eyes sparkled. 安妮和杰克开始爬绳梯。 当他们爬进树屋时，他们拥抱了那两个年轻的魔法师。 “欢迎，”凯瑟琳说道。 她美丽的大海蓝色眼睛闪闪发亮。

“Do you have a new mission for us?” said Jack. “你们有新的任务要我们做吗？”杰克问道。

“Indeed,” said Teddy, smiling. “Just like last time, Merlin wants you to help a creative person bring his gifts to the world.” “确实有，”泰迪微笑着说。 “就像上次一样，梅林希望你们帮助一位有创造力的人把他的礼物带到世界上去。”

“And this will help you,” said Kathleen. She pulled a book from her cloak. “这会帮助你们，”凯瑟琳说道。 她从斗篷里拿出一本书。

“Great!” said Jack. He took the book from Kathleen. The cover showed a street parade with musicians playing trumpets and trombones. The title was A History of New Orleans Music. “太棒了！”杰克说。 他从凯瑟琳手里接过书。 封面显示了一条街道上的游行，音乐家们正在演奏小号和长号。 书名是《新奥尔良音乐的历史》。

“New Orleans?” said Annie. “新奥尔良？”安妮说。

“Yes, New Orleans, Louisiana,” said Kathleen. “是的，新奥尔良，路易斯安那州，”凯瑟琳说。

“You will love this city,” said Teddy. “你会喜欢这个城市的，”泰迪说。

“Cool,” said Annie. “真酷，”安妮说。

“And here is your magic flute.” Kathleen picked up a gleaming silver flute from the corner of the tree house. It was the magic flute Jack had played on their adventure in Vienna, Austria. “Only this time…” Kathleen tossed the flute into the air. It hovered for a moment, then began to twirl around and around. There was a flash of blue light—and the flute was gone! Floating in its place was a shining brass instrument. “还有，这是你的魔法笛子。”凯瑟琳从树屋角落拿起一支闪闪发光的银色笛子。 这是杰克在他们去奥地利维也纳冒险时用过的魔法笛子。 “这次不一样了……”凯瑟琳把笛子抛向空中。 笛子在空中停了一下，然后开始不停地旋转。 突然闪过一道蓝光——笛子不见了！ 浮在原地的是一件闪亮的黄铜乐器。

Kathleen plucked the instrument from the air. “This time you will play a magic trumpet,” she said. 凯瑟琳从空中摘下了这个乐器。 “这次你要演奏一支魔法号角，”她说。

“Oh, man,” breathed Jack. “That’s incredible.” “哇哦，”杰克低声说。 “太不可思议了。”

“Yeah,” said Annie. “I’ve always wanted to play the trumpet.” “是啊，”安妮说。 “我一直都想吹小号。”

Kathleen laughed. “Well, this is your chance,” she said. “The trumpet’s magic will make you a brilliant performer.” 凯瑟琳笑了。 “好吧，这是你的好机会，”她说。 “小号的魔法会让你成为一个出色的表演者。”

“But the magic can only happen once,” Teddy reminded Jack and Annie, “just as on your last journey with the magic flute. Play the trumpet only when you face your greatest danger.” “但魔法只能发生一次，”泰迪提醒杰克和安妮，“就像你们上次用魔法笛子旅行时一样。 “只在面对最大危险时，才能吹奏小号。”

“And while one of us plays, the other has to make up a song, right?” said Annie. “And whatever we sing will come true.” “当一个人在吹奏时，另一个人得编一首歌，对吧？”安妮说。 “无论我们唱什么，都会成真。”

“Precisely,” said Teddy. “没错，”泰迪说。

“Um… what danger will we face in New Orleans?” asked Jack. “呃……我们在新奥尔良会遇到什么危险呢？”杰克问。

“Perhaps none,” said Teddy. “But keep the magic trumpet with you just in case. And remember, after you have played it, the magic will be gone and it will become an ordinary trumpet.” “也许没有，”泰迪说。 “但把它随身带着，以防万一。 而且记住，当你吹完它之后，魔法就会消失，它就会变成一把普通的号。”

“Got it,” said Jack. He took a deep breath. “明白了，”杰克说。 他深吸了一口气。

“Okay,” said Annie. “Ready?” “好吧，”安妮说。 “准备好了吗？”

“Wait,” said Jack. “Can you tell us what kind of creative genius we’re looking for?” “等等，”杰克说。 “你能告诉我们我们要找的是哪种有创意的天才吗？”

“We can do more than that,” said Kathleen with a smile. “We can tell you his name. It is Louis Armstrong.” “我们能做得更多，”凯瑟琳微笑着说。 “我们可以告诉你他的名字。” 它是路易斯·阿姆斯特朗。”

“Louis Armstrong,” repeated Jack. He knew that name. “路易斯·阿姆斯特朗，”杰克重复道。 他知道这个名字。

“He is the King of Jazz,” said Teddy. “他是爵士乐之王，”泰迪说道。

“The King of Jazz?” said Annie. “Cool!” “爵士之王？” said 安妮。 “酷！”

“Yes,” said Kathleen. “But Louis Armstrong won’t know that when you meet him. It is your job to put him on the right path.” “是的，”凯瑟琳说。 “但当你见到路易斯·阿姆斯特朗时，他还不知道这些事。 你的任务就是把他引上正确的道路。”

“To give his gifts to the world,” said Annie. “Got it.” “To give his gifts to the world,” said 安妮。 “Got it.”

“Good,” said Teddy. “And now you should go.” “Good,” said 泰迪。 “And now you should go.”

“Right,” said Jack. He pointed at the cover of the book. “I wish we could go there,” he said. “To New Orleans!” “Right,” said 杰克。 他指着书的封面。 “希望我们能去那里，”他说。 “去新奥尔良！”

“To meet the King of Jazz!” said Annie. “去见爵士乐之王！”安妮说。

“Good luck!” said Teddy as he and Kathleen waved good-bye. “祝你们好运！”泰迪一边和凯瑟琳挥手告别一边说。

The wind started to blow. 风开始吹起来了。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转起来。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。
