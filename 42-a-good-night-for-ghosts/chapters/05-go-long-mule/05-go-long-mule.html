5. Go ’Long, Mule 5. 哥们儿，走吧，骡子

The sky had grown cloudy, and a warm breeze was blowing. 天空变得阴云密布，暖暖的风儿吹拂着。

Dipper stopped the cart and listened. “Hear that?” he said. “Parade’s coming this way.” 迪普停下马车，仔细听了起来。 “你听到了吗？”他说。 “游行队伍要过来了。”

Jack heard band music in the distance. 杰克听到了远处传来的乐队音乐。

“Is that parade for All Saints’ Day?” asked Annie. “那是万圣节的游行吗？”安妮问。

“Maybe. Or maybe for a million other things,” said Dipper. “Folks in this city will find any excuse for a parade.” “也许吧。” “或者是为了其他一百万件事情，”迪普说。 “这个城市的人会找任何借口来举办游行。”

Soon the parade came into view. 很快，游行队伍就出现在视野中。

Horseback riders wore plumed hats and black masks. Following them were people dressed up as clowns, kings, queens, fairies with fluttering wings, ghosts, and skeletons. 骑马的人戴着羽毛帽子，戴着黑色面具。 他们后面跟着一群打扮成小丑、国王、王后、有飘动翅膀的仙女、鬼魂和骷髅的人。

“We read that New Orleans might be the most haunted city in the country,” Annie said to Dipper, “especially on the eve of All Saints’ Day. We heard there’re real ghosts in a cathedral, a hotel, and a blacksmith shop.” “我们读到新奥尔良可能是全国最闹鬼的城市，”安妮对迪普说，“尤其是在万圣节前夕。 我们听说在一座大教堂、一家旅馆和一家铁匠铺里真的有鬼魂。”

“Yeah, and plenty more places, too,” said Dipper. “But I’m not afraid of ghosts. I’m not afraid of anything.” “是啊，还有许多其他地方，”迪普说。 “但我一点都不怕鬼。” “我什么都不怕。”

“Me neither!” said Annie. “我也不怕！”安妮说。

“Um, me neither,” said Jack. “呃，我也不怕，”杰克说。

A band followed the people in costumes. The musicians were playing trumpets, tubas, trombones, and drums—lots of drums. The joyous music filled the New Orleans air. Jack and Annie couldn’t help nodding their heads in time to the beat. Jack noticed that Dipper was nodding his head, too. 一群乐手跟着穿戏服的人走。 音乐家们正在演奏小号、长号、萨克斯和鼓——很多很多鼓。 欢快的音乐充满了新奥尔良的空气。 杰克和安妮忍不住随着节奏点头。 杰克注意到迪珀也在点头。

“Hey, there’re the fellas!” said Annie. “嘿，是那些家伙！”安妮说。

Happy, Little Mack, and Big Nose Sidney were walking alongside the band, singing their hearts out. 快乐、小马克和大鼻子西德尼正和乐队一起走，尽情地唱歌。

“Looks like they’re having fun!” said Annie. She nudged Jack. “Doesn’t it?” “看起来他们玩得很开心！”安妮说。她推了推杰克。 “不是吗？”

“Yeah!” said Jack. “A lot of fun! Music is so much fun! I wish I had musical talent! You’re so lucky, Dipper!” “是啊！”杰克说。 “玩得可真开心！ 音乐真的很有趣！ 我希望我也有音乐天赋！ 你真是太幸运了，迪普！”，

“You really are, Dipper!” said Annie. “Musical talent is really a great gift to share with the world!” “你确实很幸运，迪普！”，安妮说。 “音乐天赋真的是一件很棒的礼物，可以和全世界分享！”，

Dipper just shook his head, as if he thought they were crazy. “Biggest potato heads I’ve ever met,” he said. 迪普只是摇了摇头，好像觉得他们疯了一样。 “我见过最大的土豆脑袋，”他说。

Jack and Annie laughed. 杰克和安妮笑了。

Dipper gave the reins a shake. “Go ’long, Lady. Keep me on my path.” 迪珀摇晃了一下缰绳。 “走吧，莉迪。 让我沿着我的路走。”

How weird that Dipper said that, Jack thought. Their mission was to keep him on the right path, too—the path to becoming the King of Jazz. 杰克心想，迪珀这么说真奇怪。 他们的任务也是要让他走上正确的道路——成为爵士乐之王的道路。

As Lady plodded along the bank of the Mississippi, Dipper started singing nonsense words: 当莉迪沿着密西西比河岸慢慢走时，迪珀开始唱起一些毫无意义的词：

Skid-dat-de-dat

Skid-dat-de-doo! Skid-dat-de-dat Skid-dat-de-doo！

“That sounds so cool,” said Jack. “What’s that song?” “听起来真酷，”杰克说。 “这是什么歌？”

“Not a song. I’m just scat-singing, man,” said Dipper. “不是一首歌。” 我只不过是随便哼哼而已，老兄，”迪普说。

“Scat-singing?” said Annie. “What’s that?” “乱唱？”安妮说。 “那是什么？”

“When you can’t think of words, just sing sounds,” said Dipper. “Make ’em up. If you put your heart in it, folks will understand you.” “当你想不出词的时候，就唱声音，”迪普说。 “编出来就行。” 如果你用心去做，大家就会明白你。”

“I didn’t know a person could make music like that,” said Jack. “我真不知道一个人能弹出这么美妙的音乐，”杰克说。

“Heck, yeah, you can make music any way you want,” said Dipper. “Just listen to the world: There’s church bells, the washerwoman singing about her wash, the ragman blasting his tin horn for folks to bring out their rags. Folks selling things, like that pie man. Listen to him.” “嘿，当然可以，你想怎么弹就怎么弹，”迪普说。 “只要听听这个世界：有教堂的钟声，洗衣妇人唱着她洗的衣物，还有拾荒人用铁皮喇叭大声叫卖让大家拿出破烂。” 卖东西的人，比如那个卖派的先生。听他喊。”

Dipper pointed to a man sitting in a red wagon, calling out in a strong, rich voice, “Sweet potato! Sweet potato pie! Lemon pie! Apple pie! Any pie you like!” 迪普指着一个坐在红色手推车里的人，他用响亮而富有磁性的声音喊道：“甜薯！甜薯派！ 柠檬派！苹果派！ 你喜欢什么派就吃什么派！

“Listen to that voice,” said Dipper. “That’s music. And listen to those sounds—” Dipper pointed to some women walking beside the road. “听那声音，”迪普说。 “那真是音乐。” 听这些声音——”迪普指着路边走着的一些女人。

The women carried baskets on their heads and called out in singsong voices, “Blue-berries!” “Rasp-berries!” “Black-berrieeeeeeeeeeeeeees!” 这些女人头上扛着篮子，用唱歌般的语气喊着：“蓝莓！”“树莓！”“黑莓eeeeeeeeeeee！”

“I see what you mean,” said Annie. “Music is everywhere.” “我明白你的意思了，”安妮说。 “音乐无处不在。”

“You got it, girl,” said Dipper. “You can even hear it in Lady’s hoofbeats. Listen.” “你明白了吧，姑娘，”迪普说。 “你甚至能从女士的蹄声中听出来。听。”

Jack listened to the rhythmic clippity-clop, clippity- clop of the mule. 杰克听了骡子有节奏的踢踏声，踢踏踢踏。

“See? There you go!” said Dipper. “That’s a song—Go ’long, mule, go ’long, mule.” “看到了吗？ 就是这样！”迪普说。 “那是一首歌——走开吧，骡子，走开吧，骡子。”

Jack and Annie listened to the steady music of Lady’s hoofbeats, until finally the mule came to a stop. 杰克和安妮听着女士的马蹄声发出的稳定节奏，直到最后骡子停了下来。

“Well, here we are at the coal yard,” said Dipper. “I’ll leave Lady here till tomorrow.” “好吧，我们到了煤场，”迪普说。 “我会把女士留在这儿到明天。”

They all jumped to the ground. 他们都跳下了地面。

“Thanks for your music, Lady.” Dipper patted the mule on her nose. Then he turned to Jack and Annie. “Afraid I have to leave y’all now,” Dipper said. “But it’s been great.” “谢谢你的音乐，女士。”迪普拍了拍骡子的鼻子。 然后他转向杰克和安妮。 “恐怕我现在得离开你们了，”迪普说。 “但和你们在一起真的很开心。”

“Yeah, um…,” Jack began, trying to think of a good reason to stay with Dipper. “嗯……,”杰克开始说，努力想出一个好理由让自己留下来。

“I’d like to hear you blow that horn when the time’s right,” Dipper said to Annie. “And don’t forget to say hi to Teddy and Kathleen for me.” He winked, then waved and started walking away. “我希望你能在合适的时候吹响那个号角，”迪普对安妮说。 “别忘了替我向泰迪和凯瑟琳问好。”他眨了眨眼，然后挥手，开始走开。

“But—but, Dipper!” called Jack. “但是——但是，迪普！”杰克喊道。

“Sorry, man! I’m late!” Dipper shouted over his shoulder. “Thanks a million!” He waved again and kept going. “对不起，伙计！ 我迟到了！”迪普回头喊道。 “谢谢你啦！”他再次挥手，然后继续往前走。

Jack and Annie looked at each other in a panic. “We have to stay with him!” said Annie. “Wait, wait, Dipper!” She and Jack ran after him. 杰克和安妮惊慌地看着对方。 “我们必须和他在一起！”安妮说。 “等等，等等，迪普！”她和杰克追了上去。

“Where are you going now?” Jack asked. “你现在要去哪儿？”杰克问。

“My next job,” said Dipper. “I have to haul bananas till dark.” “我的下一份工作，”迪普说。 “我得一直运香蕉到天黑。”

“Really? Another job?” said Jack. “真的？ 还有另一份工作？”杰克说。

“Hey guess what?” shouted Annie. “We love to haul bananas!” “嘿，你猜怎么着？”安妮喊道。 “我们最喜欢搬运香蕉了！”，

“Yeah. Yeah, we do,” said Jack. “是啊。 “没错，我们确实喜欢，”杰克说。

Dipper stopped and stared at them. “What is wrong with y’all?” he said. “Don’t you know how to have fun?” 迪普停下了，盯着他们看。 “你们怎么了？”他问。 “你们不知道怎么玩得开心吗？”，

Jack didn’t know what to say. 杰克不知道该说什么。

“Seriously,” said Annie. “We do love to haul bananas. And you make everything more fun with your singing.” “真的，”安妮说。 “我们确实喜欢搬运香蕉。 而且你唱歌的时候，让一切变得更有趣。”

“Yeah, you’re a good singer!” piped up Jack. “That’s a gift!” “是啊，你唱得真好！”杰克插话道。 “这是个天赋！”

Dipper just shook his head. “Remember when I said you didn’t have any more brains than a pair of potatoes? Well, I take it back. I don’t think you have one potato’s brain between you.” 迪普只是摇了摇头。 “还记得我之前说过你脑袋里装的脑子还不如一对土豆吗？ 好吧，我收回那句话。 我觉得你们两个加起来的脑子还不如一个土豆。”

Jack and Annie laughed. 杰克和安妮笑了。

“Well, come on, then,” said Dipper. “那还等什么，快走吧，”迪普说道。

Jack and Annie hurried with Dipper down to the loading dock on the riverfront. At least fifty workers were hauling huge loads of bananas out of the cargo hold of a ship. 杰克和安妮赶紧跟着迪普走到河岸的码头。 至少有五十个工人正从一艘船的货舱里搬运着成堆的香蕉。

“Wait here,” Dipper told them. “待在这里，”迪普对他们说。

Dipper walked over to a man checking people in to work. He pointed to Jack and Annie. The man shrugged, then nodded. Dipper waved for them to come join him. 迪普走到一个正在检查人们上班的人身边。 他指着杰克和安妮。 那个人耸了耸肩，然后点了点头。 迪普挥手让他们过来和他一起。

Jack and Annie ran down to join Dipper and the other workers in the cargo hold. Dipper picked up a giant bunch of bananas. The bunch was almost as big as Annie! He hauled it onto his shoulder and then picked up another one. 杰克和安妮跑下来，加入迪普和其他工人到了货舱里。 迪普捡起一大串香蕉。 这串香蕉几乎和安妮一样大！ 他把它扛到肩上，然后又捡起另一串。

“Grab a bunch of bananas and follow me!” Dipper said. He headed to the counter, where men in white suits were inspecting the banana bunches. “抓一串香蕉，跟着我走！“迪普说。 他走到柜台前，那里有穿白西装的人正在检查一串串香蕉。

“No way either of us can pick up one of those bunches,” Jack said to Annie. “我们俩谁也抬不起那么重的一串香蕉，”杰克对安妮说。

“Let’s try it together,” said Annie. “我们一起试试看，”安妮说。

Annie hid their trumpet behind a large wooden box. Jack put his bag there, too. Jack and Annie loaded a bunch of bananas into their arms. They walked closely together, taking short, clumsy steps, following Dipper. They delivered the bunch to the inspectors and then hurried back to get another. 安妮把他们的喇叭藏在了一个大木箱后面。 杰克也把他的包放在那里。 杰克和安妮把一串香蕉装进了他们的手臂里。 他们紧紧挨着走，迈出短而笨拙的步子，跟着迪普走。 他们把一串香蕉交给检查员，然后赶紧回去拿另一串。

As the sun sank toward the river, Jack, Annie, and Dipper hauled bananas back and forth between the cargo hold and the inspectors. They hauled bananas until it was almost dark. Jack was so tired he could hardly see straight. He was afraid Dipper would never call it quits. 当太阳快要落进河里时，杰克、安妮和迪普在货舱和检查员之间来回搬运香蕉。 他们一直搬运香蕉，直到天快要黑了。 杰克累得都快睁不开眼了。 他担心迪珀永远不会放弃。

“Last one,” Dipper said finally. “最后一个，”迪珀终于说道。

Yes! thought Jack. They grabbed their last loads. 太好了！杰克心想。 他们抓起了最后的货物。

Suddenly a large rat jumped out of the cargo hold! Dipper let out a scream. He dropped his bananas and took off running. Annie and Jack dropped their bananas, too. Annie grabbed her trumpet. Jack grabbed his bag, and they ran after Dipper. 突然，一只大老鼠从货舱里跳了出来！ 迪珀尖叫了起来。 他扔掉了香蕉，撒腿就跑。 安妮和杰克也扔掉了他们的香蕉。 安妮抓起了她的喇叭。 杰克抓起他的包，他们追着迪普跑了起来。

Running like crazy, Dipper led Jack and Annie far from the loading area—and the rat. When he finally stopped, Jack bumped into Dipper, and Annie bumped into Jack. They all started laughing. Dipper laughed the hardest. He collapsed on the curb of Decatur Street and laughed so hard his whole body shook. Jack and Annie sat down next to him, laughing until they cried. Finally they all managed to calm down. 迪普拼命地跑，带着杰克和安妮远离了卸货区——还有那只老鼠。 当他终于停下来时，杰克撞到了迪普，安妮撞到了杰克。 他们全都开始笑起来。 迪普笑得最厉害。 他倒在德卡特街的路边，笑得浑身发抖。 杰克和安妮坐在他旁边，笑得眼泪都流出来了。 最后，他们终于都平静了下来。

“I know… I know I said I wasn’t afraid of anything,” Dipper said breathlessly. “But I lied. I’m afraid of one thing: rats. Rats give me the heebie-jeebies.” “我知道……我知道我说过我什么都不怕，”迪普气喘吁吁地说。 “但我撒谎了。” 我最怕的就是老鼠。 老鼠让我起鸡皮疙瘩。

“Yeah, yeah,” Annie said, catching her breath. “I know how you feel. Spiders give me the heebie-jeebies.” “是啊，是啊，”安妮说着，喘了口气。 “我知道你的感觉。 蜘蛛也让我起鸡皮疙瘩。

“Yeah,” Jack said, panting. “Yeah, actually, ghosts give ’em to me.” “是啊，”杰克喘着气说。 “其实，鬼才让我最害怕。”

“That’s cool, that’s cool, man,” said Dipper. “Between the three of us, we got all the scaredy stuff covered.” “这很酷，这很酷，伙计，”迪普说。 “我们三个加起来，害怕的东西全包了。”

That made them all start laughing again. As the twilight deepened, they sat on the curb, catching their breath. They laughed now and then, out of relief and friendship. 这句话让他们又笑起来。 天色渐暗，他们坐在路边，喘着气休息。 他们时不时地笑一笑，因为感到轻松，也因为有朋友。

Then Dipper stood up. “Before we part ways, I better get our pay from the boss. Hold on.” 然后迪珀站了起来。 “在我们分开之前，我得先去跟老板要工资。等一下。”

Jack and Annie kept sitting on the curb as Dipper ran back down to the dock. “ Part ways? We can’t part ways yet,” said Jack. 杰克和安妮仍然坐在路边，迪珀则跑回码头去要工资。 “分开走？ “我们还不能分开，”杰克说。

“I know!” said Annie. “We haven’t even begun to accomplish our mission.” “我知道了！”安妮说。 “我们还没开始完成我们的任务呢。”

“Umbrella! Buy an umbrella!” a man shouted as he walked by. He carried a load of umbrellas on his back. “Storm a-comin’! Big storm comin’ for the eve of All Saints’!” “雨伞！ “买把伞！”一个男人边走边喊。 他背上扛着一大堆伞。 “暴风雨要来了！ 万圣节前夜要来大暴风雨了！”

“Oh, no, now a storm’s coming,” said Jack. He was confused about what they should do next. “哦，天哪，现在要来暴风雨了，”杰克说。 他不知道接下来他们应该怎么做。

A moment later, Dipper returned. “Thirty cents!” he said. “We each get ten.” 过了一会儿，迪珀回来了。 “三十美分！”他说。 “我们每人分十美分。”

“No, no, Dipper,” said Annie. “Please use it to take care of your family.” “不，不，迪珀，”安妮说。 “请用它来照顾你的家人。”

“Yeah, do that,” said Jack. “对，就这么做，”杰克说。

“We insist,” said Annie. “我们坚持，”安妮说。

Dipper smiled. “Oh, you do, do you? Why? What’s this game y’all are playing?” 迪普笑了。 “哦，是吗？为什么？ 你们在玩什么游戏呀？

“It’s no game,” said Jack. “这不是游戏，”杰克说。

“We’re just a couple of potato heads,” said Annie. “Get used to it.” “我们只是两个土豆头，”安妮说。 “习惯一下吧。”

“Well, then, you two potato heads, let me give you something else,” said Dipper. “Come along with me.” “那好吧，你们这两个土豆头，让我再给你们点别的东西，”迪普说。 “跟我来。”

“Great!” said Jack. They weren’t “parting ways” yet! He and Annie jumped up from the curb and bounded off with Dipper. “太好了！”杰克说。 他们还没“分道扬镳”呢！ 他和安妮从路边跳起来，和迪普一起跑开了。
