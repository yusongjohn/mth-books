3. Coal Cart Blues 3. 煤车蓝调

The streetcar turned onto a crowded, busy street. Lively music blared from restaurants and dance halls. 有轨电车驶入了一条拥挤而繁忙的街道。 热闹的音乐从餐馆和舞厅里传来。

“We’re in the French Quarter now, and you’re the next stop,” the conductor said to Jack and Annie. “Head down St. Peter Street toward the Mississippi River. You’ll run right into Jackson Square.” “我们现在到了法国区，你就是下一站，”司机对杰克和安妮说。 “往圣路易斯街走。” 皮特街，朝密西西比河方向走。 你会直接走到杰克逊广场。”

“Thanks!” Jack put the research book back in his bag. Annie tucked the trumpet under her arm. When the streetcar came to a stop, they hopped off. “谢谢！”杰克把研究书放回他的包里。 安妮把小号夹在她的胳膊下。 当有轨电车停下来时，他们跳了下来。

“Good luck playing that horn, missy!” said the conductor. “祝你吹那个号角好运，小姐！”司机说。

“Thanks, I’ll need it!” said Annie. “谢谢，我需要呢！”安妮说。

As the streetcar pulled away, Jack and Annie looked around. “Hey, this is Bourbon Street,” said Annie, pointing to a street sign. “Isn’t that the street with the haunted blacksmith shop?” 有轨电车开走后，杰克和安妮四处张望。 “嘿，这是波本街，”安妮指着路牌说。 “那不是有鬼的铁匠铺所在的街道吗？”

“Don’t think about that,” said Jack. “Let’s head down St. Peter.” “别想那件事，”杰克说。 “我们去圣彼得街吧。”

Jack and Annie left Bourbon Street and started down St. Peter Street. They passed tall, narrow houses painted pale green, yellow, and pink. Vines grew up walls and twined around iron balconies. Alleys led to courtyards with trickling fountains. 杰克和安妮离开了波本街，开始沿着圣彼得街走下去。 他们经过一些又高又窄的房子，房子被涂成了浅绿色、黄色和粉色。 藤蔓沿着墙壁生长，并缠绕在铁制的阳台上。 小巷通向有流水喷泉的庭院。

“I like the buildings in New Orleans,” said Annie. “我喜欢新奥尔良的建筑物，”安妮说。

“Yeah, and it smells good, too,” said Jack. “是啊，而且味道也好，”杰克说。

Delicious food smells filled the air. From a mule-drawn cart, an old woman cried out, “Waffles! Get your waffles here! Yes sir, fresh, hot waffles!” 空气中飘着美味食物的香味。 一辆骡子拉的推车上，一位老太太喊道：“松饼！” 来拿你的华夫饼啦！ 是的先生，新鲜热乎的华夫饼！

Jack was getting hungry. Outside a restaurant was a sign that read: 杰克开始饿了。 餐厅外面挂着一块牌子，上面写着：

Special! Dinner 10 cents. 特惠！晚餐只要10美分。

“Gosh, things are cheap in 1915,” said Jack. “Too bad we don’t even have a dime.” “天啊，1915年的东西真便宜，”杰克说。 “太糟糕了，我们连一毛钱都没有。”

“Yeah,” said Annie. “Oh, look!” She pointed to a grand cathedral with soaring spires. “A cathedral! That must be the place with the ghost of the Spanish priest.” “是啊，”安妮说。 “哦，快看！”她指着一座有着高耸尖顶的宏伟大教堂。 “一座大教堂！ 那一定就是有西班牙神父鬼魂的地方。”

“Why do you like ghosts so much?” said Jack. “你为什么这么喜欢鬼魂？”杰克说。

“I don’t like ghosts so much,” said Annie. “我其实不太喜欢鬼魂，”安妮说。

“Well, you keep talking about them,” said Jack. “好吧，你一直在谈论它们，”杰克说。

“I’m just pointing things out,” said Annie. “You’re the one who brought up ghosts in the first place.” “我只是在指出一些事情，”安妮说。 “是你先提起鬼魂的。”

“Well, let’s forget them,” said Jack. He didn’t like ghosts. He didn’t even like thinking about them. “那我们忘掉它们吧，”杰克说。 他不喜欢鬼魂。 他甚至不喜欢去想它们。

Soon Jack and Annie came to a huge green park with an iron fence around it. A sign at the entrance read: JACKSON SQUARE. 不久，杰克和安妮来到一个巨大的绿色公园，周围围着铁栅栏。 入口处的牌子上写着：杰克逊广场。

“We found it!” said Jack. “我们找到了！”杰克说。

Outside the iron fence, in the hot afternoon sunlight, small bands of barefoot kids were playing music. Some strummed banjos. Others played harmonicas or long tin horns. Three boys sang in harmony. A couple of the smallest kids passed around hats, collecting money for the different bands. 铁围栏外面，在炎热的下午阳光下，一群光着脚的小孩正在演奏音乐。 一些人弹着班卓琴。 其他人吹着口琴或长长的铁皮号角。 三个男孩一起和谐地唱着歌。 有几个最小的孩子拿着帽子，为不同的乐队筹钱。

“Where’s Louis Armstrong?” Annie asked Jack. “No one here looks like the picture in our book.” “路易斯·阿姆斯特朗在哪里？”安妮问杰克。 “这里没有人看起来像我们书里的照片。”

“Of course not,” said Jack. “The picture shows him as a grown-up. He wouldn’t look the same when he’s fourteen.” “当然不是，”杰克说。 “照片里显示的是他长大后的样子。” 他十四岁的时候就不会看起来一样了。”

“I’ll ask,” said Annie. She went up to a small girl passing a hat. “Excuse me, is there a musician here named Louis Armstrong?” “我来问一下，”安妮说。 她走到一个正在传递帽子的小女孩面前。 “打扰一下，这里有叫路易斯·阿姆斯特朗的音乐家吗？”

“Louis Armstrong? You mean Dipper?” asked the girl. “路易斯·阿姆斯特朗？ 你是说迪珀吗？”小女孩问。

“I guess…,” said Annie. “我想是的……，”安妮说。

“Hey, Little Mack!” the girl shouted to the biggest kid in the singing trio. “Where’s Dipper Armstrong?” “嘿，小马克！”小女孩朝唱歌三人组里最大的那个孩子喊道。 “迪珀·阿姆斯特朗在哪里？”

“Just saw him—he’s at the River Café!” shouted Little Mack. “我刚看到他——他在河畔咖啡馆！”小马克喊道。

“Where’s that?” Annie asked. “那是在哪儿？”安妮问。

“Down by the river,” said the small girl. She pointed beyond the square. “Walk down Decatur Street and you’ll see it.” The girl held up the hat, as if asking for a donation. “在河边，”小女孩说。 她指着广场那边。 “沿着德卡特街走，你就会看到它。”女孩举起帽子，好像在请求捐款。

“Sorry, no money,” said Jack. “对不起，没有钱，”杰克说。

“But thanks for your help,” said Annie. She and Jack left the square. They hurried along Decatur Street. “但谢谢你帮忙，”安妮说。 她和杰克离开了广场。 他们快步沿着德卡特街走。

“So Dipper must be a nickname for Louis Armstrong,” said Jack. “所以迪珀一定是路易斯·阿姆斯特朗的昵称，”杰克说。

“Yeah,” said Annie. “And Dipper must be playing music at the café.” “是的，”安妮说。 “而且迪普应该在咖啡馆里弹音乐。”

“There it is,” said Jack. He pointed to a red sign on a slanted rooftop. “找到了，”杰克说。 他指着倾斜屋顶上的一块红色招牌。

图中文字：RIVER CAFE

COFFEE AND DOUGHNUTS 图中文字：河岸咖啡店（RIVER CAFE）咖啡和甜甜圈

“Doughnuts, yum,” said Annie. “甜甜圈，真好吃，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie walked under a striped red awning. Waiters in white jackets were running around with trays of coffee and delicious-smelling doughnuts. Jack’s mouth watered. 杰克和安妮走到了一条红色条纹的雨棚下。 穿着白色外套的服务员们拿着装满咖啡和香味诱人的甜甜圈的托盘四处奔跑。 杰克的嘴馋得流口水了。

“That’s funny. There’s no music here,” said Annie. “Excuse us,” she called to one of the waiters. “Have you seen Louis Armstrong? Or Dip—” “那很奇怪。 这里没有音乐，”安妮说。 “请问一下，”她对其中一位服务员喊道。 “你见过路易斯·阿姆斯特朗吗？还是迪普—”

“Did you come here to buy something, kids?” the waiter interrupted rudely. “孩子们，你们是来买东西的吗？”服务员粗鲁地打断道。

“No, we don’t have any money—” started Jack. “不，我们没有钱—”杰克开口说道。

“Then out, boy!” the waiter shouted. “No begging in here!” “滚出去，小子！”服务员喊道。 “这里不许乞讨！”，

“He’s not begging!” said Annie. “We’re looking for—” “他不是在乞讨！”安妮说道。 “我们在找—”

“I know beggars when I see them! Out!” said the waiter. “我一看就知道是乞丐！”， “出去！”服务员说道。

“Let’s go. I don’t think Dipper’s in here, anyway,” said Jack. “我们走吧。” 我觉得迪珀不在这里，不管怎么说，”杰克说。

“Wait a minute, I have to tell them that we’re not beggars!” Annie said. “等一下，我得告诉他们我们不是乞丐！”安妮说。

“It’s not worth it, come on,” said Jack. “不值得，快走吧，”杰克说。

Jack was mad, too, but he pulled Annie out from under the awning. “I think it’s the way we look,” he said. “Our clothes make us look poor. And we don’t have shoes on.” 杰克也很生气，但他把安妮从雨棚下拉了出来。 “我觉得是我们看起来的样子，”他说。 “我们的衣服让我们看起来很穷。 而且我们还没穿鞋。”

“It’s not fair,” said Annie. “这不公平，”安妮说。

“Forget it. We’ll ask someone else where to find Dipper,” said Jack. “算了。 我们会去问别人怎么找到迪珀，”杰克说。

On the street by the café was a mule cart filled with coal. A young teenager was putting a bucket and shovel into the back of the cart. He was barefoot and wore clothes like Jack and Annie’s. 在咖啡馆旁边的街上，有一辆骡子拉的车，车里装满了煤。 一个年轻的青少年正把桶和铲子放进车的后面。 他光着脚，穿着和杰克、安妮一样的衣服。

“Excuse me!” Jack called. “Do you know Louis Armstrong? Or Dipper?” “请问一下！”杰克喊道。 “你知道路易斯·阿姆斯特朗，或者迪珀吗？”

The boy turned. When he saw Jack and Annie, he grinned. He had the friendliest smile Jack had ever seen. “Louis Armstrong?” he said. “That’s me. How can I help you, man?” 男孩转过身来。 当他看到杰克和安妮时，他露出了笑容。 他笑得是杰克见过的最友好的笑容。 “路易斯·阿姆斯特朗？”他说道。 “那就是我。 我能帮你什么吗，先生？“

Jack was at a loss for words. He hadn’t thought about what to say to Louis Armstrong when they found him. 杰克一时语塞。 他没想到见到路易斯·阿姆斯特朗时该说什么。

“Hi, Dipper!” said Annie, walking up to the boy. “We’re Jack and Annie from Frog Creek, Pennsylvania. Friends of ours told us to find you when we came to New Orleans.” “你好，迪珀！”安妮走到男孩面前说道。 “我们是来自宾夕法尼亚州蛙溪镇的杰克和安妮。 我们的朋友告诉我们，当我们来到新奥尔良时，要找你。

“What friends?” asked Dipper. “什么朋友？”迪普问。

“Teddy and Kathleen,” said Annie. “泰迪和凯瑟琳，”安妮说。

The boy looked puzzled. But then his gaze fell on Annie’s trumpet. “Hey, nice horn. Can you play that thing?” 男孩看起来很困惑。 但随后他的目光落在了安妮的喇叭上。 “嘿，漂亮的号角。” 你能玩那个东西吗？“

“Only when the time is right,” said Annie. “只有在时机成熟的时候，”安妮说。

“And when’s that?” asked Dipper. “那是什么时候呢？”迪普问。

“I won’t know till I feel it,” said Annie. “我要等到感觉到了才行，”安妮说。

Dipper smiled his radiant smile again. “ Ha! I know just what you mean!” he said. He wiped his hand on his pants and held it out to shake. “Any friends of Teddy and Kathleen’s are friends of mine.” 迪普又露出了他那灿烂的笑容。 “我完全明白你的意思！”他说。 他用手擦了擦裤子，然后伸出手来握手。 “泰迪和凯瑟琳的朋友，都是我的朋友。”

As Jack shook Dipper’s hand, he sputtered, “You—you know Teddy and Kathleen?” 当杰克和迪普握手时，他结结巴巴地说：“你——你认识泰迪和凯瑟琳吗？”

“No, man, never heard of them,” said Dipper. “But I consider everybody my friend.” “不，先生，我从来没听说过他们，”迪普说。 “但我认为每个人都是我的朋友。”

“Oh. Oh!” said Annie. She and Jack laughed. “哦。哦！”安妮说。 她和杰克笑了起来。

“Only problem is I can’t hang out with y’all right now,” said Dipper. He climbed onto the mule cart. “唯一的问题是我现在不能和你们一起玩，”迪普说。 他爬上了骡子拉的车。

“Where—where you going, man?” asked Jack. “你——你要去哪儿，先生？”杰克问。

“I’m making my rounds with this coal cart,” said Dipper. “And I have lots more work to do today. But be sure to look me up the next time you come to town. And say hi to my pals, Teddy and Kathleen.” Dipper waved at them and then shook the reins. “Go ’long, mule,” he said. “我在用这辆运煤车巡游，”迪普说。 “而且我今天还有好多活要干。 但下次你们再来镇上时，一定要来找我。” 跟我的朋友泰迪和凯瑟琳打个招呼。”迪普朝他们挥了挥手，然后拉了拉缰绳。 “走开吧，骡子，”他说。

The mule clopped over the brick street, pulling the coal cart away from the River Café. 骡子踏着砖铺的街道走过来，拉着煤车离开了河畔咖啡馆。

Louis Armstrong was gone. 路易斯·阿姆斯特朗不见了。
