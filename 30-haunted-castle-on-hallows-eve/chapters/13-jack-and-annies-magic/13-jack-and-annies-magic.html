13. Jack and Annie’s Magic 13. 杰克和安妮的魔法

Jack, Annie, and Teddy crunched through fallen leaves until they came to Merlin’s oak. They found the hidden door near the rope ladder. Teddy pushed on the bark. 杰克、安妮和泰迪踩着落叶走过去，直到来到梅林的橡树前。 他们在绳梯旁边找到了那扇隐藏的门。 泰迪推了推树皮。

The door opened. One by one, they slipped into the candlelit hollow of the tree trunk. Merlin was sitting in his tall wooden chair. 门打开了。 一个接一个，他们溜进了树干里烛光闪烁的树洞里。 梅林正坐在他那把高高的木椅上。

“So you restored order to the castle?” he said calmly. “所以你们把城堡恢复秩序了？”他平静地说道。

“Yes, sir,” said Teddy. “Had to use a bit of magic, but now all is well.” “是的，先生，”泰迪说。 “不得不使用了一点魔法，但现在一切都好了。”

“Your rhyming must have improved,” Merlin said to Teddy. “你的押韵肯定进步了，”梅林对泰迪说。

Teddy grinned sheepishly. “Well, to be truthful, the real magic was not in my rhymes. ’Twas the magic of Jack and Annie’s courage and kindness that saved the day—and saved me, too.” 泰迪不好意思地笑了笑。 “说实话，真正的魔法并不在我押的韵里。 是杰克和安妮的勇敢和善良拯救了这一天——也拯救了我。”

“Indeed?” said Merlin. “真的吗？”梅林说。

“Aye,” said Teddy. “They have a magic as powerful as any sorcerer’s rhymes or enchanted hazel twig.” “是的，”泰迪说。 “他们有一种魔法，和任何巫师的押韵或被施了魔法的榛树枝一样强大。”

Merlin raised a bushy eyebrow. “Enchanted hazel twig?” he said. 梅林挑起了他浓密的眉毛。 “神奇的榛树枝？”他说。

“’Tis only a figure of speech,” Teddy said quickly. “这只是个比喻，”泰迪赶紧说。

Merlin turned to Jack and Annie. “I thank you for your help,” he said. “All the realm of Camelot thanks you.” 梅林转向杰克和安妮。 “谢谢你们的帮助，”他说。 “卡梅洛特的全体子民都感谢你们。”

“You’re welcome,” they said. “谢谢你们，”他们说。

Merlin stood up. “Come along, my boy,” he said to Teddy. “I will help speed you back to Morgan now. My research is done. We must return these rare books to her library.” 梅林站了起来。 “跟我来，我的男孩，”他对泰迪说。 “我会帮助你尽快回到摩根身边。 我的研究已经完成了。 我们必须把这些珍贵的书还回她的图书馆。

He reached down and picked up a stack of ancient-looking books from the floor. He piled them into Teddy’s arms. 他弯下腰，从地上捡起一堆看起来古老的书。 他把书堆到泰迪的臂弯里。

Teddy turned awkwardly with his books. Then he and Jack and Annie followed Merlin out of the heart of the oak. 泰迪笨拙地抱着书转了个身。 然后他和杰克、安妮跟着梅林走出了橡树的中心。

The sun had risen higher in the sky. The woods were still. 太阳已经升得更高了。 树林里一片寂静。

Teddy peered over the tops of the books. “I suppose we must say good-bye now,” he said to Jack and Annie. 泰迪从书的顶部探出头来。 “我想我们现在得说再见了，”他对杰克和安妮说道。

“When will we see you again?” asked Annie. “我们什么时候才能再见到你们呢？”安妮问道。

“When duty calls, I suppose,” said Teddy. He looked at Merlin. “当职责召唤时，我想是这样，”泰迪说。 他看着梅林。

The magician smiled. 这位魔术师笑了。

“Will you be able to find your way home all right?” Teddy asked them. “你们能顺利回家吗？”泰迪问他们。

“Oh, sure,” said Jack. “The tree house will take us back.” “哦，当然可以，”杰克说。 “树屋会带我们回去的。”

He and Annie looked up at the magic tree house at the top of the oak. A sudden gust of wind rustled the leaves. 他和安妮抬头看向那棵橡树顶端的魔法树屋。 一阵突如其来的风吹动了树叶。

Jack and Annie turned back to Merlin and Teddy. But they were gone. Bright yellow leaves swirled and danced in the spot where they’d been standing. 杰克和安妮转身看向梅林和泰迪。 但他们已经不见了。 金黄色的叶子在他们刚才站着的地方旋转着跳舞。

“Wow …,” said Annie. “哇……”，安妮说。

“Yeah …,” said Jack. “是啊……”，杰克说。

“Well,” said Annie, sighing. “Onward?” “好吧，”安妮叹了口气说。 “向前走吗？”，

“Home ward!” said Jack. “回家！”，杰克说。

Annie started up the rope ladder. Jack followed. When they climbed inside the tree house, Merlin’s leaf invitation was fluttering off the floor. Before it could blow out the window, Annie grabbed it. She pointed to the words Frog Creek. 安妮开始爬绳梯。杰克跟在后面。 当他们爬进树屋时，梅林的树叶邀请函正从地板上飘起来。 在它被吹出窗户之前，安妮抓住了它。 她指着上面写着“蛙溪镇”的字。

“I wish we could go there!” she said. “我希望我们能去那里！”她说。

The wind started to blow. 风开始吹起来了。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转起来。

It spun faster and faster! 它转得越来越快！

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。

* * *

Jack opened his eyes. He and Annie sat quietly on the floor of the tree house for a moment. Jack looked out the window. High overhead, a bird was soaring through the dusky sky. 杰克睁开了眼睛。 他和安妮在树屋的地板上安静地坐了一会儿。 杰克看向窗外。 高高的天空中，一只鸟正在暮色的天空中翱翔。

Jack could hardly believe that just a little while ago, he had been a bird himself. 杰克简直不敢相信，就在刚才，他还是一只鸟呢。

“Ready to go home?” said Annie. “准备回家了吗？”安妮说。

Jack nodded. There was no way to explain what had just happened to them, he thought. There was no way to even talk about it. 杰克点了点头。 他觉得，根本没有办法解释刚刚发生的一切。 甚至连谈论这件事都很难。

Annie carefully placed Merlin’s autumn leaf in the corner of the tree house, next to their Royal Christmas Invitation. Then she and Jack climbed down the ladder and started through the woods. 安妮小心地把梅林的秋叶放在树屋角落，放在他们的皇家圣诞邀请函旁边。 然后她和杰克顺着梯子爬下来，开始穿过树林。

In the gathering dark of Halloween night, nothing seemed very spooky. Jack knew all the trees. He knew the familiar path out to their street. 在万圣节夜晚渐渐变暗的天色里，似乎没什么特别吓人的。 杰克认识所有的树。 他知道通往他们街道那条熟悉的路。

As he and Annie headed toward home, three creatures stepped onto the sidewalk in front of them—a hideous witch, a grinning skeleton, and a huge, hairy eyeball. 当杰克和安妮往家走的时候，有三个怪物突然出现在他们面前——一个可怕的女巫、一个咧嘴笑的骷髅，还有一个又大又毛茸茸的独眼怪物。

The creatures cackled and rattled and hissed. 那些怪物咯咯地笑，发出咔咔的声音，还嘶嘶地叫。

Jack and Annie laughed. 杰克和安妮笑了起来。

“Oh, brother,” said Jack. “哦，天哪，”杰克说。

“Good costumes,” said Annie. “你们的服装真棒，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie crossed their yard and climbed their front steps. “Are you ready for trick-or-treating?” said Annie. 杰克和安妮穿过他们的院子，爬上了前台阶。 “你准备好去要糖果了吗？”安妮问。

Jack pushed his glasses into place. “You know, I think maybe I’ll stay home this year,” he said, “and help Mom and Dad give out the treats.” 杰克把眼镜推到正确的位置。 “你知道吗，我觉得我今年可能待在家里，”他说，“帮妈妈和爸爸分发糖果。”

“Yeah, maybe I will, too,” said Annie. “But I think I’ll wear my vampire-princess costume anyway.” “是啊，我可能也会这么做，”安妮说。 “不过我想我还是穿我的吸血鬼公主服装吧。”

Jack smiled. “Cool,” he said. 杰克笑了。 “太酷了，”他说。

Then he and Annie slipped inside their warm, cozy house—and closed the door against the dark of All Hallows Eve. 然后他和安妮溜进了他们温暖舒适的家里——并关上了门，挡住了万圣节前夕的黑暗。
