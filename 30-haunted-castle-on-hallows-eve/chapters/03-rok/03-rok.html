3. Rok 3. 罗克

It was cooler outside now. Daylight was fading quickly. The wind had picked up. 现在外面凉快多了。 天光正在迅速变暗。 风也变大了。

“A grand adventure for us, eh?” said Teddy. “我们该有一次伟大的冒险了吧？”泰迪说。

“Yes!” said Annie. “是的！”安妮说。

Jack was excited, too, but he had lots of questions. As Teddy started off through the oak forest, Jack hurried to keep up. 杰克也很兴奋，但他有很多问题。 泰迪开始穿过橡树林时，杰克赶紧跟上。

“What do you think our mission is exactly?” he asked. “你认为我们的任务到底是什么？”他问。

“Merlin said we should bring order to the castle,” said Annie. “梅林说我们应该让城堡恢复秩序，”安妮说。

“Perhaps he wants us to mop the floors and wash the dishes,” Teddy joked. “也许他希望我们打扫地板、洗碗呢，”泰迪开玩笑说。

“And make the beds!” said Annie. She and Teddy laughed. “然后把床铺好！”安妮说道。 她和泰迪笑了起来。

“Our mission has to be harder than just doing chores,” said Jack. “What about the tunnel of fear?” “我们的任务一定不只是做家务这么简单，”杰克说道。 “那怕不怕恐惧隧道？”

“Oh, you need not be afraid of fear,” said Teddy. “I know magic, remember?” “哦，你不需要害怕恐惧，”泰迪说道。 “我知道魔法，还记得吗？”

“Teddy, did you know any magic before you met Morgan and Merlin?” asked Annie. “泰迪，你遇见摩根和梅林之前，知道任何魔法吗？”安妮问。

“Ah, indeed. My father was a sorcerer,” Teddy said. “And my mother was a wood sprite from the Otherworld.” “啊，确实如此。 我爸爸是个巫师，”泰迪说。 “我妈妈是来自异世界的精灵。”

“That is so cool,” said Annie. “那太酷了，”安妮说。

They crunched through piles of dead leaves. A gust of wind shook the tree branches. Golden oak leaves spun to the ground. 他们踩着一堆堆枯叶，发出咔嚓咔嚓的声音。 一阵风吹动了树的枝条。 金黄的橡树叶旋转着落到了地上。

Jack’s thoughts were spinning, too. Merlin in the heart of the oak, sorcerers, wood sprites— none of these things would ever make sense back in Frog Creek. 杰克的思绪也像树叶一样在旋转。 梅林、橡树中心、巫师、木精灵——这些事情在蛙溪镇永远都不会有道理。

At last Teddy led them out of the forest and into a clearing. “Halt!” he said. 最后，泰迪带领他们走出了森林，来到了一片空地。 “停下！”，他说。

They all stopped walking. Beyond the clearing was a small village of thatched-roof cottages. The cottage windows twinkled with candlelight. Chimney smoke rose into the dusky sky. 他们都停下了脚步。 空地的另一边是一个有茅草屋顶的小村庄。 小屋的窗户闪着烛光。 烟囱冒出的烟飘向暮色中的天空。

Teddy held up his lantern. “Onward!” he said. 泰迪举起了他的提灯。 “前进！”他说。

They went down a dirt path that passed through the village. Several children in ragged clothes peeked out their front doors. 他们沿着一条穿过村庄的土路走下去。 几个穿着破旧衣服的孩子从门前探出头来。

“Greetings!” called Teddy. “Can you tell us how to get to the castle of the duke?” “你好！”泰迪喊道。 “能告诉我们怎么去公爵的城堡吗？”，

“The castle?” a boy said in a frightened voice. “’Tis just beyond the wood!” He pointed at a forest on the other side of the village. “Follow the path and you’ll come to it!” “城堡？”一个男孩用害怕的声音说道。 “城堡就在树林那边！”他指着村子另一边的森林。 “沿着小路走，你就会找到它！”

“Oh, but you mustn’t go there!” a girl cried. “哦，但是你不能去那里！”一个女孩喊道。

“Why not?” asked Annie. “为什么不能去？”安妮问。

“Something’s been very wrong at the castle,” said the girl. “Ever since the ravens came!” “自从乌鸦来了以后，城堡里一直出了很多问题，”女孩说。 “自从乌鸦来了以后！”

“Has anyone been there to see what’s going on?” asked Jack. “有人去城堡看过发生了什么吗？”杰克问。

“Only old Maggie, who used to work there,” said the girl. “Two weeks ago, she went to the castle as usual. But she came running back, scared out of her wits.” “只有老玛吉去过，她以前在那里工作，”女孩说。 “两周前，她像往常一样去了城堡。 但她跑回来，吓得魂飞魄散。”

“Maggie says the castle is all haunted with ghosts,” said a boy. “She keeps repeating the same rhyme over and over.” “玛吉说城堡里到处都是鬼魂，”一个男孩说。 “她一直在重复同样的童谣。”

“Ghosts?” said Jack. His mouth felt dry. “幽灵？”杰克说。 他的嘴巴感到干干的。

But Teddy just laughed. “Ghosts don’t scare me!” he said. 但泰迪只是笑了笑。 “幽灵吓不倒我！”他说。

“Have you ever seen one, Teddy?” asked Annie. “泰迪，你见过幽灵吗？”安妮问。

“No! But I should like to!” Teddy said with a grin. “没有！ 但我很想见一个！”泰迪带着微笑说。

“Look!” One of the girls pointed at the sky. “The ravens are back!” “看！”其中一个女孩指着天空说。 “乌鸦回来了！”

A flock of large black birds was flying low in the dark gray sky. The village kids screamed. Several grown-ups rushed out of their cottages. 一群又大又黑的鸟在灰暗的天空中低飞。 村里的孩子们尖叫起来。 好几个大人从他们的小屋里跑了出来。

“Go away!” a woman yelled at the ravens. She picked up a handful of stones and began throwing them at the birds. “Leave us alone!” “快走开！”一个女人对着乌鸦大喊。 她捡起一把石头，开始朝鸟儿扔去。 “别打扰我们！”

“Stop! Stop!” cried Annie. “You’ll hurt them!” “住手！ “停下！”安妮喊道。 “你会伤害它们的！”

A stone struck one of the ravens. It fell to the ground. 一块石头击中了一只渡鸦。 它掉到了地上。

“Oh, no!” cried Annie. “哦，天哪！”安妮喊道。

The grown-ups pulled their children inside. Doors slammed and shutters closed. 大人把孩子们拉进了屋里。 门砰地关上了，窗户也关紧了。

Annie dashed to the fallen bird and knelt beside it. 安妮冲到掉下来的鸟旁边，跪在它身边。

Jack and Teddy hurried over to Annie and the fallen bird. The bird was crouching, slightly spreading its wings. Its head was bowed as it made low, squeaking sounds. One of its tail feathers was bent. 杰克和泰迪赶紧跑到安妮和那只掉下来的鸟那里。 那只鸟蹲着，稍微张开翅膀。 它的头低着，发出低低的吱吱声。 它的一根尾羽弯了。

“COO-COO!” Teddy said loudly. He looked at Jack. “I once journeyed to the Isle of Birds to study their language,” he said. “I learned a bit of Dove, but no Raven.” “咕咕！”泰迪大声说道。 他看着杰克。 “我曾经去过鸟之岛，研究他们的语言，”他说。 “我学会了点鸽子语，但不会乌鸦语。”

“Don’t worry,” said Jack. “Annie talks to birds and animals in her own language.” “别担心，”杰克说。 “安妮用她自己的语言和鸟儿、动物说话。”

“I’m sorry for what they did to you,” Annie said softly to the raven. She stroked its silky black head. “What’s your name?” “对不起他们对你做的事，”安妮轻声对乌鸦说。 她抚摸着乌鸦丝滑的黑色头颅。 “你叫什么名字？”

“ROK,” the raven croaked. “洛克，”乌鸦嘶哑地说。

“Rok? Your name is Rok?” said Annie. “洛克？” “你的名字是洛克吗？”安妮说。

“ROK! ROK!” croaked the raven. “洛克！ “洛克！”渡鸦沙哑地说。

“See, I told you,” Jack said to Teddy. “你看，我早就告诉过你，”杰克对泰迪说。

“Rok, they were afraid of you for some reason,” Annie said. “洛克，他们之前是出于某种原因害怕你，”安妮说。

Rok made soft, bell-like sounds: “CRONG? CRONG?” 洛克发出柔和的、像铃铛一样的声音：“聪格？聪格？”

“Yes, that’s why they knocked you out of the sky,” said Annie. “One of your tail feathers is bent. But your wings don’t seem hurt.” “是的，这就是他们把你从天上打下来的原因，”安妮说。 “你的一根尾羽有点弯曲了。 但你的翅膀看起来好像没受伤。”

Rok fluttered his long black wings. He took a few feeble steps. 洛克扇动着他长长的黑色翅膀。 他迈出几步，脚步很虚弱。

“Go on, Rok,” Annie coaxed. “You can do it.” “加油，罗克，”安妮温柔地劝道。 “你能做到的。”

The raven flapped his wings again. “QUORK!” he croaked. 渡鸦又拍了拍翅膀。 “QUORK！”他咕哝道。

He lifted off the ground. 他离开了地面。

“Great!” said Annie, clapping. “太棒了！”安妮拍手说道。

Rok flapped his wings. He glided up into the twilight. He swooped with ease back down to Annie. “ CAW! CAW!” he called, as if thanking her. 罗克拍了拍翅膀。 他滑翔着飞向暮色中。 他轻松地俯冲下来，回到安妮身边。 “嘎嘎！”他喊道，好像在感谢她。

“Be careful, Rok!” Annie shouted. “小心点，罗克！”安妮喊道。

They all waved as the raven sailed off into the sky. 乌鸦飞向天空时，他们都挥手告别。

Annie smiled at Jack and Teddy. “He was really nice,” she said. 安妮对杰克和泰迪笑了笑。 “他真的很友善，”她说。

“Indeed he was,” said Teddy. “I think your gentle words were healing to him.” “确实如此，”泰迪说。 “我想你温柔的话语对他来说是一种治愈。”

“I wonder why the people here are so afraid of ravens,” said Annie. “我想知道为什么这里的人这么害怕乌鸦，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. “And what was that stuff about ghosts?” “是啊，”杰克说。 “那关于鬼魂的事情又是什么意思呢？”

“Ghosts?” said Teddy. He smiled. “You needn’t fear ghosts if you’re with me.” “幽灵？”泰迪说。他笑了。 “如果你和我在一起，就不需要害怕幽灵。”

Jack shrugged. “I’m not really afraid,” he said. 杰克耸了耸肩。 “我其实一点都不害怕，”他说。

“Not afraid?” said a feeble voice. “不害怕？”一个虚弱的声音说。

Jack, Annie, and Teddy whirled around. 杰克、安妮和泰迪猛地转过身。

An old woman stood in the dark doorway of a cottage. She leaned forward. In a cracked voice, she said: 一位老妇人站在小屋黑暗的门口。 她向前倾了倾身子。 她用沙哑的声音说：

Where is the girl

who spins wool into thread?

Where are the boys

who play chess before bed?

Where is the hound

who waits to be fed? “纺线变成线的女孩在哪里？睡前下棋的男孩在哪里？等着吃饭的猎犬在哪里？”

The old woman stared at them with a fearful look in her eyes. Then she stepped back into her cottage and closed her door. 那个老妇人用害怕的眼神盯着他们。 然后她退回小屋，关上了门。

A shiver went up Jack’s spine. “That was strange,” he said. 杰克的脊背一阵发凉。 “那太奇怪了，”他说。

“She must have been old Maggie, who worked at the castle,” said Annie. “I wonder what she was talking about.” “她一定是老玛吉，曾在城堡里工作过，”安妮说。 “真想知道她刚才在说什么。”

“I don’t know,” said Teddy. Then he grinned. “But she was good at rhyming, eh?” “我不知道，”泰迪说。然后他咧嘴笑了。 “但她很擅长押韵，对吧？”

Jack nodded. “Indeed she was,” he said softly. 杰克点了点头。 “确实如此，”他轻声说。

“Let’s hurry along!” said Teddy. “Night comes fast upon us!” “我们快点走吧！”泰迪说。 “夜晚来得很快！”

Leaving the cottages behind, the three hurried on in the gathering dark. They left the village and followed the path through the woods. 他们把小屋留在身后，三个人在渐渐暗下来的天色中加快了脚步。 他们离开了村庄，沿着小路走进了树林。

Teddy held up his lantern to light their way. The wind blew the branches of the trees, making them whisper in the chilly autumn night. 泰迪举着他的手电筒，照亮他们的路。 风把树的枝条吹动，让它们在寒冷的秋夜中沙沙作响。

When they finally came out of the woods, they all gasped in wonder. 当他们终于走出树林时，他们都惊讶地发出了惊叹声。

“Oh, man,” said Jack. “哦，天哪，”杰克说。

Towering before them in the moonlight were the walls of a huge stone castle. 在月光下，一座巨大的石头城堡的墙壁高高耸立在他们面前。
