9. Tiptoe 9. 踮着脚尖

Jack and Annie crouched in the tall grass. 杰克和安妮蹲在高高的草丛里。

There was a big lion, three lionesses, and a bunch of cubs. 那儿有一头大雄狮、三头母狮，还有一群幼崽。

“I think they’re sleeping,” whispered Annie. “我觉得它们在睡觉，”安妮小声说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. “是啊，”杰克说。

“But for how long?” “可能睡多久？”

He pulled the Africa book from his pack and opened it. 他从背包里拿出非洲的书打开。

He found a picture of lions sleeping under a tree. 他找到一幅狮子在树下睡觉的图画。

He read in a whispery voice: 他用窃窃私语的声音读了起来：

After a pride of lions has eaten, they rest for a few hours. The other— 一群狮子吃完后，会休息几个小时。另一只——

“What did they have for lunch?” Annie broke in. “它们午饭吃了什么？”安妮插嘴道。

“Don’t ask,” said Jack. He kept reading: “别问了，”杰克说。他继续读：

Sensing that the lions are not hunting at the moment, the other animals graze nearby. 感觉到狮子们现在没有在狩猎，其他动物就在附近吃草。

“If they can graze, then we’re safe,” said Annie. “如果它们能吃草，那我们就安全了，”安妮说。

She started to stand. 她开始站起来。

“Wait!” Jack pulled her down. “等等！”杰克把她拉下去。

“Not so fast.” “别那么急。”

He peered around. 他向四周窥视。

The words in the book seemed true: the zebras and giraffes didn’t seem to be bothered by the lions at all. 书上的话好像是真的：斑马和长颈鹿似乎完全不把狮子当回事。

“They might be safe. “它们也许安全。

But I’m not sure about us,” said Jack. 可我们就不一定了，”杰克说。

“We need a plan.” “我们需要一个计划。”

“What if we wait till they leave?” said Annie. “要是我们等它们离开呢？”安妮说。

“That could take hours,” said Jack. “那可能要花好几个小时，”杰克说。

“Plus they might be hungry again by then.” “而且到那时候它们可能又饿了。”

“Oh, right,” said Annie. “哦，对，”安妮说。

“So here’s the plan—we tiptoe,” said Jack. “所以计划是这样——我们踮着脚尖走，”杰克说。

“Tiptoe?” “踮着脚尖？”

“Yeah?” “嗯？”

“That’s your whole plan?” said Annie. “这就是你的全部计划？”安妮说。

“Yeah, tiptoe to the rope ladder,” said Jack. “对，踮着脚尖走到绳梯那儿，”杰克说。

“Very quietly.” “非常轻。”

“Good plan,” Annie teased. “好计划。”安妮取笑道。

“Just do it,” said Jack. “就这么办，”杰克说。

He stood up slowly. 他慢慢站起来。

Annie stood with him. 安妮和他一起站起来。

They began tiptoeing through the grass very slowly. 他们开始非常缓慢地踮着脚尖穿过草地。

The lion flicked his tail. 狮子轻轻甩了甩尾巴。

Jack and Annie froze. 杰克和安妮愣住了。

When his tail was still again, they moved again. 等它的尾巴又不动了，他们才又往前走。

Suddenly, high-pitched laughter split the air. 突然，尖厉的笑声划破空气。

Jack and Annie stopped. 杰克和安妮停住了。

The hyenas were back! 鬣狗又回来了！

They were standing off to the side, watching Jack and Annie. 它们站在旁边，看着杰克和安妮。

Jack and Annie made silent monster faces and shook their fists. 杰克和安妮做出无声的怪物脸，挥着拳头。

But the hyenas only laughed some more. 可鬣狗只是又笑了起来。

The big lion stirred lazily. 那头大雄狮懒洋洋地动了动。

He opened his golden eyes. 他睁开金色的眼睛。

Jack felt the hair rise on the back of his neck. 杰克觉得后颈的汗毛都竖起来了。

But he didn’t move an inch. 可他一寸也没动。

The lion lifted his head and yawned. 狮子抬起头，打了个哈欠。

His giant teeth gleamed in the sunlight. 它巨大的牙齿在阳光下闪闪发亮。

The lion turned his head as he looked around sleepily. 狮子转过头，睡眼惺忪地四处看。

Jack held his breath as the lion’s gaze rested on him. 杰克屏住呼吸，狮子的目光落在他身上。

The lion sat straight up. 狮子笔直地坐了起来。

His piercing yellow eyes met Jack’s. 它锐利的黄眼睛对上了杰克的目光。

Jack’s heart raced. His mind raced. 杰克的心跳得飞快。脑子也转得飞快。

He remembered something he’d read—lions avoid giraffes. 他想起读过的东西——狮子会避开长颈鹿。

Jack looked around. 杰克四处张望。

There was a giraffe walking toward the tree that the magic tree house was in. 有一头长颈鹿正朝魔法树屋所在的那棵树走去。

Suddenly, he had a new plan. 突然，他有了新计划。

“Get under that giraffe,” he whispered. “到那头长颈鹿下面去，”他小声说。

“Now you’re the one who’s nuts,” Annie whispered back. “现在疯的人是你，”安妮小声回道。

But Jack grabbed her hand. 可杰克抓住了她的手。

He pulled her over to the giraffe and underneath it. 他把她拉到长颈鹿那儿，钻到它身子下面。

The giraffe’s legs were so long, Jack and Annie could stand up under it. 长颈鹿的腿很长，杰克和安妮能站在它下面。

Jack’s head barely brushed the giraffe’s golden belly. 杰克的头几乎碰到了长颈鹿金色的肚子。

The tall creature froze for a few seconds. 那只高大的动物愣了几秒。

Then she moved slowly toward the tree. 然后她慢慢朝树走去。

Jack and Annie walked in the same rhythm as the giraffe. 杰克和安妮跟着长颈鹿的节奏一起走。

They got closer and closer to the tree house—and closer and closer to the pride of lions. 他们离树屋越来越近——也离那群狮子越来越近。

The big lion had stood up. 那头大狮子已经站了起来。

He watched them moving under the giraffe. 它看着他们在长颈鹿下面移动。

When the rope ladder was just a few feet away, Jack and Annie dashed out from under the giraffe to the rope ladder. 等到绳梯就在几英尺外时，杰克和安妮从长颈鹿身下冲向绳梯。

Annie scrambled up first. 安妮先赶紧往上爬。

Jack followed right behind her. 杰克紧跟在她后面。

As they climbed, the lion growled and leaped at the ladder. 他们往上爬时，狮子咆哮着朝梯子扑过来。

The hyenas laughed. 鬣狗笑着。

Jack climbed faster than he’d ever climbed. 杰克爬得比以往任何时候都快。

He leaped after Annie into the tree house. 他跟着安妮跳进树屋。

Annie had already unrolled the scroll. 安妮已经展开了卷轴。

The riddle was gone. 谜语不见了。

In its place was one shimmering word: 原来的位置上闪着一个字：

HONEY 蜂蜜

Jack grabbed the Pennsylvania book. 杰克抓起宾夕法尼亚的书。

He opened it and found the picture of the Frog Creek woods. 他打开书，找到蛙溪树林的图画。

“Iwishwecouldgothere!” he said. “我希望我们能去那儿！”他说。

Just then, the giraffe stuck her head through the window. 就在这时，长颈鹿把头伸进了窗口。

“Bye, honey!” said Annie, and she kissed the giraffe on the nose. “再见，亲爱的！”安妮说着，亲了亲长颈鹿的鼻子。

The wind started to blow. 风开始刮起来。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 绝对静止。
