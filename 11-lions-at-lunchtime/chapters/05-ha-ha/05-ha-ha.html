5. Ha-Ha 5. 哈——哈

“Oh, no! No! No!” shouted Annie. “哦，不！不！ 不！”安妮喊道。

She raced down the bank, until she reached a sandy, clear spot. 她沿着河岸往下跑，一直跑到一块沙质的空地。

“Here! Here!” she called. “这儿！ 这儿！”她喊道。

The wildebeests followed her with their wild eyes. 角马们用狂野的眼睛跟着她。

Jack watched in disbelief as the swimmers changed their course. Slowly, all the wildebeests swam to where Annie stood. She waved them in like a traffic policeman. 杰克难以置信地看着那些游泳的角马改变了路线。 慢慢地，所有角马都游到了安妮站的地方。 她像交通警察一样挥手指挥它们过来。

Jack grabbed his backpack. 杰克抓起背包。

“Annie,” he cried, “let’s go before we get trampled!” “安妮，”他喊道，“咱们快走，别被踩到！”

“Keep it up,” she shouted to the wildebeests as she took off after Jack. “继续保持！”她一边朝角马喊，一边跟着杰克跑开。

They ran farther up the river, away from the incoming wildebeests. Finally, they stopped to catch their breath. They looked back. 他们沿着河边往上跑，躲开正在过来的角马。 最后，他们停下来喘了口气。他们回头看了看。

Everything seemed fine. The wildebeests were scrambling safely over the riverbank. Soon they would graze on the grass prepared by the zebras. 一切似乎都很好。 角马正安全地爬上河岸。 很快它们就会去吃斑马准备好的草了。

“Good work,” Jack said to Annie. “干得好，”杰克对安妮说。

“Thanks,” she said. “Okay, now for our riddle … ” “谢谢，”她说。 “好了，现在该看我们的谜语了……”

“No, first we’ve got to get clean,” said Jack. “You look like you’re in a mud suit.” “不，我们得先弄干净，”杰克说。 “你看起来像穿着一套泥衣服。”

High-pitched laughter rang through the air. It sounded mocking and mean. 尖厉的笑声响彻空中。 那声音听起来又嘲弄又刻薄。

Jack and Annie turned around. They saw two spotted brown animals standing in the tall grass. 杰克和安妮转过身。 他们看见两只带斑点的棕色动物站在高草里。

The creatures had bodies like dogs, but with sloping backs. They laughed again. 那些动物的身体像狗，可背是向下斜的。 它们又笑了。

“Ha-ha,” said Annie. “You don’t look so great yourself.” “哈哈，”安妮说。 “你自己也好不到哪儿去。”

“What are they?” said Jack. He took out the book. He tried not to get mud on it as he looked for a picture. When he found it, he read aloud: “它们是什么？”杰克说。 他拿出书。 他尽量不把泥弄到书上，一边找图画。 找到后，他大声念道：

On the African plains, the hyena (hi-EE-nuh) is the ruling predator after the lion. It makes a sound similar to a high-pitched human laugh. 在非洲大草原上，鬣狗（hi-EE-nuh）是仅次于狮子的统治者。它发出的声音类似于高音调的人类笑声。

“What’s ‘predator’ mean?” said Annie. “‘捕食者’是什么意思？”安妮说。

“It means it catches things and eats them,” said Jack. “意思是它会抓东西吃，”杰克说。

“Oh,” said Annie. “Yuck.” “哦，”安妮说。 “恶心。”

The two hyenas laughed again. And they moved closer to Jack and Annie. 那两只鬣狗又笑了起来。 它们朝杰克和安妮靠得更近了。

Quietly, Jack read more: 杰克静静地继续读：

The hyena has a reputation for being a thief and a coward. 鬣狗以偷东西和胆小而闻名。

“Let’s see if they’re cowards,” whispered Annie. “Let’s try to scare them.” “看看它们是不是胆小鬼。”安妮低声说。 “我们试着吓吓它们。”

The hyenas laughed and moved a little closer. 鬣狗笑着，又靠近了一点。

“How?” Jack asked. “怎么吓？”杰克问。

“Act like a monster!” said Annie. “Now!” “装成怪物！”安妮说。 “现在！”

Jack and Annie made terrible monster faces. They put out their hands and rushed at the hyenas. 杰克和安妮做出可怕的怪物表情。 他们伸出双手，朝鬣狗冲过去。

“ARGGGGHH!” they shouted. “啊啊啊啊！”他们大喊。

The hyenas yelped and scurried off. 鬣狗尖叫着慌忙跑开了。

“Scaredy-cats!” Annie shouted after them. “胆小鬼！”安妮在他们身后喊。

“Come on,” said Jack. “快点，”杰克说。

Annie and Jack took off in the other direction. They ran around a bend in the river. 安妮和杰克朝另一个方向跑开了。 他们绕过河流的一个弯道。

Jack heard the hyena laughter again. It sounded far away. 杰克又听见鬣狗的笑声。 听起来很远了。

“Good,” he said, “they’re gone.” “好，”他说，“它们走了。”

“Hey, maybe we can wash over there,” said Annie. “嘿，也许我们可以去那边洗洗，”安妮说。

She pointed to the edge of the forest. There was a small pond surrounded by tall grass. Zebras were drinking the water. 她指着森林的边缘。 那儿有一个被高草环绕的小池塘。 斑马正在喝水。

“Yeah,” said Jack. “If it’s safe enough for them to drink … ” “是啊，”杰克说。 “如果它们喝着都安全……”

The zebras ignored them as they walked toward the pond. 他们走向池塘时，斑马并不理会他们。

When they reached the edge of the water, Jack set his heavy pack down in the dry grass. He glanced around. No lions were in sight. But then he heard something. 走到水边时，杰克把沉重的背包放在干草上。 他四处瞥了一眼。 看不见狮子。 可接着他听见了什么声音。

On the far side of the pond, something very big was coming out of the trees. 在池塘远处那一边，有个很大的东西正从树里走出来。
