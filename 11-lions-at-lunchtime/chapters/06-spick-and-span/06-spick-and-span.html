6. Spick-and-Span 6. 干干净净

“Be still,” said Jack. “别动。”杰克说。

Jack and Annie stood frozen as an elephant stepped out of the shadows. It waded into the pond and dipped its trunk into the water. 杰克和安妮一动不动地站着，一头大象从阴影里走了出来。 它蹚进池塘，把鼻子浸进水里。

“Oh, wow,” said Annie. “哦，哇，”安妮说。

Jack breathed a sigh of relief. An elephant wasn’t going to chase them and eat them. Still, the elephant was huge. 杰克松了一口气。 大象不会追他们、也不会吃他们。 尽管如此，这头大象还是很大。

“Let’s sneak away,” said Jack. “咱们偷偷溜走吧。”杰克说。

“But I want to watch,” said Annie. “可我想看，”安妮说。

“Fine,” said Jack. He was tired of Annie getting sidetracked. “I’m going to solve the riddle by myself. I’ll meet you back at the tree house.” “好吧，”杰克说。 他厌烦安妮老是跑题。 “我自己去解谜语。 我们树屋里见。”

He turned to go. A spray of water rained down on him. It came from behind. He shouted with surprise and looked back. 他转身要走。 一阵水花洒在他身上。 是从后面来的。 他吃惊地喊了一声，回头看。

The elephant’s trunk was pointed straight at Annie. 大象的鼻子正对着安妮。

“Cool!” she cried. “He’s giving me a shower!” “酷！”她叫道。 “它在给我洗澡！”

The elephant sprayed her again … then again. The mud slowly rolled off her face, her braids, her T-shirt, her shorts, her legs, and her sneakers 大象又朝她喷水……然后又喷。 泥巴慢慢从她的脸、辫子、T恤、短裤、腿和运动鞋上滑落下来。

“I guess the elephant doesn’t like dirty kids!” said Annie, laughing. Her eyes were squeezed shut. “我猜大象不喜欢脏孩子！”安妮笑着说。 她的眼睛紧紧闭着。

Finally, she was clean and soaking wet. 最后，她干净了，也浑身湿透了。

“Now it’s your turn,” she said to Jack. “现在轮到你了。”她对杰克说。

Jack stepped forward and shut his eyes tight. A blast of water hit him. It did feel like a shower—a strong shower. 杰克走上前，也紧紧闭上眼睛。 一股水流打在他身上。 感觉真像在洗澡——冲得很猛的洗澡。

When Jack was clean, the elephant let out a grunt. Then he started to splash himself with water. 杰克洗干净后，大象发出一声哼叫。 然后他开始用水往自己身上泼。

“Thanks!” said Annie. “谢谢！”安妮说。

“Yeah, thanks!” said Jack. “是啊，谢谢！”杰克说。

“I’m spick-and-span now,” said Annie. “When the sun dries me off, I’ll be like new.” “我现在干干净净的了。”安妮说。 “等太阳把我晒干，我就跟新的一样了。”

“Good,” said Jack. “Now we can get serious.” “好，”杰克说。 “现在我们可以认真起来了。”

He picked up his heavy pack. “We have to figure out the answer to the riddle. So we can leave this place … before we run into real trouble.” 他提起沉重的背包。 “我们得把谜底弄清楚。 这样我们就能离开这儿……免得碰上真正的麻烦。”

He looked around nervously. Where are the lions? he wondered. 他紧张地四处看。 狮子在哪儿？他想。

A small bird flitted near his head. 一只小鸟在他头边轻快地飞来飞去。

“Hi,” Annie said to the bird. “你好，”安妮对那只鸟说。

Jack turned back to her. “According to the riddle, we’re looking for something gold and sweet.” 杰克又转向她。 “根据谜语，我们要找的东西是金色的，还很甜。”

“What do you want?” Annie asked the bird. “你想要什么？”安妮问那只鸟。

The bird twittered and flew around Jack and Annie. Its feathers were dull gray. But it had a bright, happy manner. 那只鸟叽叽喳喳地叫着，绕着杰克和安妮飞。 它的羽毛是暗淡的灰色。 可它的样子却明亮又快活。

“Annie, listen to me, not the bird,” said Jack. “安妮，听我说，别听鸟的，”杰克说。

The bird kept fluttering around them. 那只鸟不停地在他们周围拍着翅膀飞。

“She’s trying to tell us something,” said Annie. “她想告诉我们什么，”安妮说。

Jack let out a long sigh. “You are driving me crazy today,” he said. 杰克长长地叹了口气。 “你今天要把我逼疯了。”他说。

“But I feel like she needs our help,” said Annie. “Maybe her babies fell out of the nest.” “可我觉得她需要我们帮忙，”安妮说。 “也许她的宝宝从窝里掉出来了。”

“Annie, you can’t save every animal in Africa,” said Jack. “安妮，你不能拯救非洲的每一种动物，”杰克说。

“This bird is important,” said Annie. “Trust me.” “这只鸟很重要，”安妮说。 “相信我。”

The bird darted toward the trees. It landed on a branch and cocked its head at them. 那只鸟朝树林猛冲过去。 它落在一根树枝上，歪着头看着他们。

“She says follow, ” said Annie. “她说跟我来，”安妮说。

The bird headed into the forest. Annie started after it. 那只鸟朝森林飞去。 安妮跟了上去。

“Don’ t go in there!” said Jack. “You might run into—” “别进去！”杰克说。 “你可能会碰上——”

There was no need to finish. The bird and Annie had disappeared into the trees. 已经没必要把话说完了。 鸟和安妮已经消失在树林里。

“—a snake or a lion,” Jack said to himself. “——蛇或者狮子，”杰克自言自语。

“Come on!” Annie called. “快点！”安妮喊道。

Jack moaned. He pulled on his pack and ran. The peanut butter jar thumped against his back. 杰克哼了一声。 他背上背包就跑。 花生酱罐子一下下撞着他的背。
