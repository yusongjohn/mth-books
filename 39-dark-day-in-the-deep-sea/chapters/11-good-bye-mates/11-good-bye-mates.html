11. Good-bye, Mates 11. 再见了，伙伴们

Jack looked up. The captain, the professor, and the crew members were all gazing silently down at the water. 杰克抬头看了看。 船长、教授和船员们都默默地望着水面。

Henry gave the captain a salute. The captain saluted back. Then everyone on board clapped and shouted. 亨利向船长敬了个礼。 船长也回敬了一个礼。 然后船上所有人都鼓起掌来，大声欢呼。

Overhead the clouds had disappeared, as if a curtain had been pulled away from the sky. Pink and red swirls of light streaked the blue. 头顶上的云朵消失了，就像有人把天空的窗帘拉开了一样。 粉红色和红色的光束在蓝色的天空中划过。

The cheering died down. “Let us disturb these waters no more!” commanded the captain. “Unfurl the sails! Get the steam up in the boilers!” 欢呼声渐渐平息下来。 “我们不要再打扰这些水域了！”船长命令道。 “把帆张开！ 锅炉里加足蒸汽！”

Henry turned to Jack and Annie. “While they prepare the ship, shall we return you to shore?” he asked. 亨利转向杰克和安妮。 “在他们准备船只的时候，我们送你们回岸边好吗？”他问。

“Yes, please,” said Annie. “好啊，请吧，”安妮说。

Jack just nodded. He was totally exhausted. 杰克只是点了点头。 他完全累垮了。

As Joe and Tommy started rowing back toward land, the wind shifted and died down to a breeze. 当乔和汤米开始划船返回陆地时，风向变了，风也渐渐变成了微风。

“I predict a fine, clear night tonight,” said Tommy. “我预测今晚会是个晴朗美好的夜晚，”汤米说。

“And tomorrow, too,” said Joe. “The wind should be brisk.” “明天也是，”乔说。 “风应该很有力。”

“A good time to sail,” said Tommy. “现在是划船的好时机，”汤米说。

When they drew close to shore, Tommy and Joe both jumped out of the boat. Splashing through the shallows, they pulled the boat up onto the beach. Jack and Annie hopped out. Henry climbed out after them. 当他们靠近岸边时，汤米和乔都跳出了小船。 他们踩着浅滩，把船拉上了沙滩。 杰克和安妮跳了下去。 亨利跟着他们爬了出来。

“Thanks!” Jack said to the sailors. “谢谢！”杰克对水手们说。

Henry walked with Annie and Jack up to the dunes. “Are you sure you’ll be all right?” he asked. “What will you do when night comes on?” 亨利陪着安妮和杰克走到沙丘边。 “你们确定自己没问题吗？”他问。 “夜幕降临时你们打算怎么办？”

“Don’t worry,” said Jack. “We’ll get back to our parents before dark.” “别担心，”杰克说。 “天黑之前我们就能回到父母身边。”

“Lucky you,” Henry said wistfully. “I wish I could be back to my own family before dark.” “你们真幸运，”亨利羡慕地说。 “我希望在天黑之前能回到我自己的家人身边。”

“I hope you keep having a good voyage,” said Annie. “希望你一路顺风，”安妮说。

“And I hope you learn a lot,” said Jack. “Bye.” “也希望你学到很多东西，”杰克说。 “再见。”

“Wait, before you go—” said Henry. “I want to give you something.” Henry reached into the pocket of his vest and took out the beautiful nautilus shell. “The real reason I ran into my lab when the storm came was to get this,” he said. “I didn’t want to risk losing it. But now I would like to give it to you.” “等等，你走之前——”亨利说。 “我想送你一件礼物。”亨利伸手到他背心口袋里，拿出了一只漂亮的鹦鹉螺壳。 “我遇到那场风暴时冲进实验室的真正原因，就是想拿到这个，”他说。 “我不想冒险把它弄丢。 但现在我想把它送给你。”

“Oh, no, Henry. You love it. You keep it,” said Annie. “哦，不，亨利。你很喜欢它。” “你留着吧，”安妮说。

“I do love it,” said Henry. “But I want you two to have it. Today you taught me—no, you taught all of us—an important lesson. It is a dark day in the deep sea when we cause innocent creatures to suffer. The professor said we can conquer our fears through knowledge. But you taught us that our fears can best be conquered through compassion. Even we scientists must never forget to have compassion for all living creatures. My compassion for the little creature that once lived in this shell made me very happy.” “我真的很喜欢，”亨利说。 “但我希望你们两个拥有它。 今天你们教会了我——不，你们教会了我们所有人——一个重要的教训。 当我们在深海中让无辜的生物受苦时，那是深海中一个黑暗的日子。 教授说，我们可以通过知识战胜恐惧。 但你教我们，通过同情心，我们才能最好地战胜恐惧。 即使我们这些科学家，也永远不能忘记对所有生物怀有同情心。 我对曾经住在这个贝壳里的小生物的同情，让我非常开心。

Oh, man! thought Jack. That’s the secret! 哦，天哪！杰克心想。 这是秘密！

Henry held out the gleaming nautilus shell, and Annie took it from him. “Thanks, Henry,” said Annie. “We promise to take very good care of it.” 亨利拿出闪闪发光的鹦鹉螺壳，安妮从他手里接了过去。 “谢谢，亨利，”安妮说。 “我们保证会好好照顾它的。”

“Thank you, ” said Henry. He brushed his hand across his eyes. “Well, I must say good-bye now. Enjoy the rest of your vacation.” “谢谢，”亨利说。 他用手擦了擦眼睛。 “好吧，我现在得说再见了。 祝你们剩下的假期玩得开心。”

Henry saluted them. Before Jack and Annie could say anything, the scientist turned and walked briskly back to the rowboat. 亨利向他们敬礼。 杰克和安妮还没来得及说话，科学家就转身快步走回了划艇。

“Let’s go,” Annie said to Jack. “我们走吧，”安妮对杰克说。

Jack and Annie walked around the dunes and headed through the sea grass to the rope ladder. Then they climbed back into the tree house and looked out the window. The rowboat was gliding over the shimmering water of the sunlit sea, heading back to the HMS Challenger. 杰克和安妮绕过沙丘，穿过海草，走向绳梯。 然后他们爬回树屋，从窗户往外看。 小船正在阳光照耀的海面上滑行，朝着HMS挑战者号返回。

“Good-bye, mates,” said Jack. “再见了，伙伴们，”杰克说。

“Our mission!” said Annie, as if she’d just remembered. She looked at Jack. “We didn’t discover a secret of happiness!” “我们的任务！”，安妮好像刚刚想起来似的说。 她看着杰克。 “我们没有发现幸福的秘密！”，

“Yes, we did,” said Jack with a smile. “Henry said it, actually. A secret of happiness is having compassion for all living creatures.” “是的，我们发现了，”杰克微笑着说。 “其实是亨利说的。” “幸福的秘密是善待所有有生命的生物。”

“Compassion?” said Annie. “善待？”安妮说。

“Yeah, that means feeling sympathy and love for them,” said Jack. “对，就是对它们有同情心和爱心，”杰克说。

“Oh, sure,” Annie said matter-of-factly. “Loving other creatures makes me really happy. I wonder why some people don’t get that.” “哦，当然，”安妮若无其事地说。 “爱其他生物让我特别开心。 真奇怪，为什么有些人不明白这个道理。”

“I don’t know,” said Jack. “我不知道，”杰克说。

“Too bad they have to see a miracle first, like an octopus talking,” said Annie. “真希望他们能亲眼看到奇迹，比如章鱼说话，”安妮说。

“I know what you mean,” said Jack. “The octopus just being an octopus is the real miracle.” “我知道你是什么意思，”杰克说。 “章鱼只是做它自己，这才是真正的奇迹。”

Annie smiled. “And Henry’s seashell—the little creature inside—him too,” she said. “He was a miracle, too.” She held out the shining shell. 安妮笑了。 “还有亨利的贝壳——里面的小生物——他也是，”她说。 “他也是一个奇迹。”她拿出那颗闪闪发光的贝壳。

Jack took the shell from her and carefully put it in his backpack. “The shell will help us remember the secret,” he said. 杰克从她手里接过贝壳，小心地放进他的背包里。 “这颗贝壳会帮助我们记住这个秘密，”他说。

“I think we always knew it,” said Annie. “Henry just put it into words for us. Come on, let’s go.” “我想我们一直都知道,”安妮说。 “亨利只是帮我们说出来了。” 快点，我们走吧。”

Annie picked up the Pennsylvania book and turned to the picture of the Frog Creek woods. “Lehhh meee gooo hooome,” she said, repeating the strange sounds from the octopus. 安妮拿起宾夕法尼亚的书，翻到蛙溪镇森林的那幅图。 “莱hh我ee回hoo家，”她说，重复着章鱼发出的奇怪声音。

Jack smiled. He started to tell her to say it right. But before he could, the tree house started to spin. 杰克笑了。 他开始告诉她要正确地说出来。 但就在他要说话之前，树屋开始旋转起来。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。

图中文字：光光光 图中文字：光光光

Soft raindrops pattered against the roof of the tree house. 柔软的雨滴轻轻打在树屋的屋顶上。

“I love being home,” said Annie. “我爱回家的感觉，”安妮说。

“Me too,” said Jack with a sigh. “我也是，”杰克叹了口气说。

Jack and Annie strapped on their bike helmets and pulled on their sneakers. Then Jack took their research book out of his pack and put in their library books. 杰克和安妮系上了他们的自行车头盔，穿上了运动鞋。 然后杰克从背包里拿出他们的研究书，放进图书馆的书里。

“All I want to do now is go into our warm, dry house and see Mom and Dad,” said Annie. “我现在只想回到我们温暖干燥的家里，看看妈妈和爸爸，”安妮说。

“Me too,” said Jack. “And read one of my new library books.” “我也是，”杰克说。 “然后读我新借的图书馆的书。”

“And eat dinner,” said Annie. “然后吃晚饭，”安妮说。

“A good dinner,” said Jack. “No stale biscuits or lime juice.” He slipped on his backpack. “And no pea soup.” “一顿好吃的晚饭，”杰克说。 “不要吃发硬的饼干或酸橙汁。”他背上背包。 “也不要喝豌豆汤。”

“Pea soup?” asked Annie. “豌豆汤？”安妮问。

But Jack had already started down the ladder. “And then read a little more and climb into bed,” he continued. 但杰克已经开始往下爬梯子了。 “然后多读一会儿，再爬上床，”他继续说。

“A warm, dry bed in a warm, dry house,” said Annie, following him. “一个温暖、干燥的床，躺在一个温暖、干燥的房子里，”安妮跟着他说。

“Yep,” said Jack, stepping onto the wet ground. “I guess the octopus just wanted to go home, too. Hey, I wonder if he has a name.” “是啊，”杰克踩着湿漉漉的地面说。 “我想这只章鱼也是想回家了， 嘿，我想知道他有没有名字。”

“Hmm …,” said Annie. She and Jack climbed on their bikes. “I think his name might be … Charles!” “嗯……，”安妮说。 她和杰克爬上他们的自行车。 “我觉得他的名字可能是……查尔斯！”，

“That sounds right,” said Jack, laughing. “I wonder if Charles has an octopus wife—and octopus kids.” “听起来对，”杰克笑着说。 “我想查尔斯是不是还有一只章鱼妻子，还有章鱼孩子。”

“I bet he does,” said Annie. “And I bet he couldn’t wait to get home and hold them in all eight of his arms.” “我打赌他确实会,” 安妮说。 “我打赌他迫不及待想回家，用他全部八条手臂抱着它们。”

Jack laughed again. “Yeah, I bet you’re right,” he said. Then he and Annie took off riding through the Frog Creek woods. 杰克又笑了。 “是啊，我打赌你没错，”他说。 然后他和安妮骑着马，穿过蛙溪镇的森林。

The rain fell harder. But it didn’t matter. They were heading home. 雨下得更大了。 但那没关系。 他们正回家呢。
