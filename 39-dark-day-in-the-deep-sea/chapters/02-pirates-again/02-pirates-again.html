2. Pirates Again? 2. 再次遇到海盗？

J ack opened his eyes. Teddy and Kathleen were gone. The warm air was filled with mist. 杰克睁开了眼睛。 泰迪和凯瑟琳不见了。 温暖的空气中充满了雾气。

Jack and Annie peered out the window together. The tree house had landed in a tall tree with spreading branches. The mist was so thick they couldn’t see anything around them. But Jack heard the caws of gulls and the swooshing of rolling waves. He smelled salt water and seaweed. 杰克和安妮一起探出窗户望去。 树屋降落在一棵枝叶茂盛的高大树木上。 雾气太浓了，他们什么都看不见。 但是杰克听到了海鸥的叫声，还有海浪滚滚的声音。 他闻到了海水和海藻的味道。

“The ocean’s out there. Feel it?” said Annie. “大海就在外面。 你能感觉到吗？”安妮说。

“I hear it and smell it,” said Jack. “我听到了，也闻到了，”杰克说。

“Then let’s go play in it!” said Annie. She pulled off her sneakers and socks. “那我们去玩吧！”安妮说。 她脱下了运动鞋和袜子。

“We can’t just play,” Jack said. “We have to look for a secret of happiness.” “我们不能只为了玩，”杰克说。 “我们得去找幸福的秘密。”

“Well, I’m happiest when I’m playing in the ocean!” Annie started down the rope ladder. “那我最快乐的时候，就是玩海水的时候！”安妮开始顺着绳梯往下爬。

I’m sure our mission’s harder than that, Jack thought. He took their library books out of his pack and replaced them with the deep-sea book. 杰克心想，我确定我们的任务比这难多了。 他从背包里拿出他们的图书馆书籍，换成了那本深海书籍。

“Hurry!” said Annie. “快点！”安妮说。

Jack put on his pack and started down after her. He stepped off the ladder onto the misty ground. 杰克背上背包，开始跟在她后面往下走。 他从梯子上跳下来，踩在雾蒙蒙的地面上。

“Come on!” said Annie. “快点！”安妮说。

Jack followed Annie toward the sounds of seabirds and waves. They walked through feathery ferns and climbed a sloping sand dune. When they rounded the top, Jack saw waves rolling onto a wide, sandy beach. But the ocean itself was still shrouded in a gray haze. 杰克跟着安妮朝海鸟和海浪的声音走去。 他们穿过长满羽毛的蕨类植物，爬上了一座倾斜的沙丘。 当他们绕到沙丘顶部时，杰克看见海浪涌向一片宽阔的沙滩。 但大海本身仍然笼罩在一层灰蒙蒙的雾气中。

“Wow,” said Annie. “哇，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. “是啊，”杰克说。

“Come on, let’s go in,” Annie said. “快点，我们进去吧，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie hurried down the dune and ran toward the ocean. While Annie waded into the water, Jack stood at the edge and pulled out their research book. “Listen to this, Annie,” he called. 杰克和安妮赶紧跑下沙丘，朝大海跑去。 当安妮走进水里时，杰克站在岸边，拿出他们的研究书。 “安妮，你听这个，”他喊道。

Jack read loudly: 杰克大声读道：

Water covers three-quarters of our earth. Most of the ocean is an enormous plain a little more than two miles deep. But some ocean trenches are more than six miles deep. 水覆盖了地球的四分之三。大部分海洋是一个深度略超过两英里的巨大平原。但有些海沟的深度超过六英里。

“More than six miles?” Annie asked, splashing the water with her hands. “It’s six miles from our house to Aunt Libby’s.” “超过六英里？”安妮问，用手拍打着水。 “从我们家到莉比阿姨家有六英里。”

“I know,” said Jack. He read more: “我知道，”杰克说。他继续读道：

The ocean is home to thousands and thousands of sea creatures. Mountains and volcanoes are also hidden deep beneath the surface of the sea. 大海是成千上万海洋生物的家。海底还隐藏着山脉和火山。

“Mountains and volcanoes?” asked Annie. “Under the water?” “山和火山？”安妮问。 “在水下？”

“That’s what the book says,” said Jack. “The ocean’s a whole world we don’t know anything about.” “书上就是这样写的，”杰克说。 “海洋是一个我们一无所知的整个世界。”

“Well, some people know about it,” said Annie. “Or that book couldn’t have been written.” “好吧，有些人是知道的，”安妮说。 “否则那本书也不可能被写出来。”

Good point, thought Jack. 杰克心想，这是个好观点。

“Put the book away and come in, Jack,” said Annie. “The sun’s coming out!” “把书收起来，进来吧，杰克，”安妮说。 “太阳出来了！”

Jack looked up from his book. The sun was burning away the mist, making the day hotter. 杰克从书上抬起头。 太阳正在驱散雾气，让天气变得越来越热。

“Let’s go swimming!” said Annie. “我们去游泳吧！”安妮说。

As Annie dove into a wave, Jack put his book back into his backpack. He left his pack on the beach, then he waded into the water. 当安妮跳进海浪里时，杰克把书放回了他的背包里。 他把背包留在了海滩上，然后走进了水里。

“Great, huh?” called Annie. “太棒了，对吧？”安妮喊道。

“Yeah,” Jack said as he dug his toes into the soft, gooey sand. Cool seawater lapped around his calves. He felt the warm sunshine on his face. “是啊，”杰克一边把脚趾埋进柔软黏糊的沙子里，一边说。 清凉的海水漫到了他的小腿上。 他感觉到温暖的阳光照在脸上。

“Let’s swim farther out,” said Annie. “Maybe the secret of happiness is in the deep sea.” “我们游得更远一点吧，”安妮说。 “也许幸福的秘密在深海里。”

“How do we go down there without a submarine?” asked Jack. “我们怎么下去那儿，没有潜水艇吗？”杰克问。

“The wand,” said Annie. “Maybe it will turn us into fish or something.” “魔杖，”安妮说。 “也许它会把我们变成鱼或者别的什么。”

Jack closed his eyes and pictured the darkness of the deep filled with thousands of weird creatures. “But the wand can be used only after we’ve tried our hardest. I don’t think we’ve done that yet,” he said. 杰克闭上眼睛，想象着深海的黑暗中充满了成千上万奇怪的生物。 “但是只有在我们尽了最大努力之后，魔杖才能使用。” “我想我们还没做过那件事，”他说。

“Oh, right,” said Annie. “Plus, it has to be for the good of others.” “哦，对，”安妮说。 “而且，这必须是为了别人好。”

“So first we have to find some others,” Jack said, his eyes still closed. “所以，我们得先找到一些其他人，”杰克说，眼睛还闭着。

“Jack, you won’t believe it,” said Annie. “杰克，你不会相信的，”安妮说。

“What?” Jack asked dreamily. “什么？”杰克迷迷糊糊地问。

“Take a look!” said Annie. “快看！”安妮说。

Jack sighed and opened his eyes. The mist had cleared a bit, and the day was becoming bright and hot. 杰克叹了口气，睁开了眼睛。 雾气散了一些，天变得又亮又热。

“I think we just found the others!” said Annie. She pointed out to sea. “我想我们刚刚找到其他人了！”安妮说。 她指着海的方向。

Jack shaded his eyes and squinted. Through the wavy sunlight, he saw a large wooden ship with three tall masts. “Whoa,” he breathed. “That’s a ship from a long time ago.” 杰克用手遮住眼睛，眯起眼看着。 在波光粼粼的阳光下，他看见了一艘大型的木船，有三根高高的桅杆。 “哇，”他轻声说。 “这是一艘很久以前的船。”

“Yeah. Remember when we ran into the pirate ship?” said Annie. “This ship looks the same, doesn’t it?” “是的。” “还记得我们上次遇到海盗船的时候吗？”安妮说。 “这艘船看起来好像一样，对不对？”

“Oh, no,” said Jack. “Pirates again?” “哦，不，”杰克说。 “又是海盗？”

“Look! A rowboat’s leaving the ship,” said Annie. “看！ 一艘划艇正离开船，“安妮说。”

“Oh, man …,” said Jack. “哦，天哪……，”杰克说。

“It’s heading toward us,” said Annie. “Just like that other time, remember? The pirates came ashore and chased us. Remember Pinky, Stinky, and Captain Bones?” “它正朝我们这边来，”安妮说。 “就像上次那样，还记得吗？ 海盗们上岸了，他们追着我们。 还记得皮妮、斯廷基和骨头船长吗？

“Don’t panic,” said Jack, panicking. He splashed out of the water and ran up on the beach. “别慌张，”杰克说，但他自己却慌了神。 他从水里扑腾着跳出来，跑到沙滩上。

“Where should we go?” asked Annie, hurrying after him. “我们该去哪儿？”安妮一边问，一边赶紧跟了上去。

“To the tree house!” said Jack. He grabbed his backpack. “去树屋！”杰克说。 他拿起了他的背包。

“But the pirates climbed up to the tree house,” said Annie. “Pinky and Stinky found—” “但是海盗们爬到了树屋，”安妮说。 “粉红和臭臭发现了—”

“Forget Pinky and Stinky!” said Jack. “Let’s just get out of here!” “别提粉红和臭臭了！”杰克说。 “我们赶紧离开这里吧！”

Jack and Annie charged toward the sand dune. They raced up over the top and ran through the tall ferns and grass until they came to the rope ladder. 杰克和安妮冲向沙丘。 他们跑上沙丘顶部，穿过高高的蕨类植物和草地，直到来到绳梯前。

“Up, up!” cried Jack. “往上爬，往上爬！”杰克喊道。

Jack and Annie climbed into the tree house. “Pull up the ladder!” said Jack. Together they hauled the rope ladder after them. 杰克和安妮爬进了树屋。 “把梯子拉上来！”杰克说。 他们一起把绳梯拉了上来。

“Where’s the Pennsylvania book?” said Jack. He looked around wildly for the book that always took them home. He grabbed it and found a picture of Frog Creek. “宾夕法尼亚的书在哪里？”杰克说。 他疯狂地四处寻找那本总能带他们回家的书。 他拿到了书，发现上面有一幅蛙溪镇的图画。

“Wait, wait! Don’t make a wish yet!” said Annie. She was looking out the window. “I’m not so sure these guys are pirates.” “等等，等等！ “别急着许愿！”安妮说。 她正望着窗外。 “我不确定这些人是不是海盗。”

Clutching the Pennsylvania book to his chest, Jack looked out the window with Annie. There were three men in the rowboat. The boat rode the top of a wave and came close to the beach. 杰克紧紧抱着那本宾夕法尼亚的书，和安妮一起望向窗外。 船上有三个男人。 船随着海浪的顶部，靠近了海滩。

Two of the men scrambled out and dragged the boat from the shallow water onto the sand. They both wore huge, bulky vests over white puffy-sleeved shirts. They wore round white hats and white pants rolled up to their knees. 其中两个人爬了出去，把船从浅水里拖到了沙滩上。 他们两个人都穿着白色蓬松袖子衬衫上面的大而笨重的背心。 他们戴着圆圆的白帽子，穿着白色裤子，裤腿卷到了膝盖那里。

“Those two don’t look at all like Pinky and Stinky,” said Annie. “那两个人看起来一点都不像粉红和臭臭，”安妮说。

“You’re right,” said Jack. “Pirates never wear such clean-looking clothes.” “你说得对，”杰克说。 “海盗从来不会穿这么干净的衣服。”

“And look at that third guy,” said Annie. “看看第三个男人，”安妮说。

The third man stepped out of the boat, carrying a butterfly net. He pulled off his bulky vest, revealing an old-fashioned suit and a bow tie. 第三个男人从船上走出来，拿着一个捕蝶网。 他脱下厚重的背心，露出一套老式的西装和一条领结。

“He definitely doesn’t look like a pirate,” said Annie. “他肯定不是海盗，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. “He looks like he’s never been on a boat before in his life.” “是啊，”杰克说。 “他看起来这辈子都没上过船。”

As the two sailors pulled the rowboat farther onto the beach, the man in the bow tie picked up a stick. He started poking at clumps of seaweed. 当那两个水手把划艇划得更靠近海滩时，那个戴领结的男人捡起了一根棍子。 他开始戳那些海藻团。

“What’s he doing?” asked Jack. “他在干什么？”杰克问。

The man dropped his stick and picked up something small from the sand. He studied it for a moment. Then he knelt down, pulled a small book out of his pocket, and started to write. 那人扔下他的棍子，从沙子里捡起一样小东西。 他盯着看了片刻。 然后他蹲下来，从口袋里掏出一本小书，开始写起来。

“Who is he?” said Jack. “他是谁？”杰克说。

“I don’t know,” said Annie. “But one thing’s for sure—pirates don’t carry butterfly nets or write in notebooks.” “我不知道，”安妮说。 “但有一件事可以肯定——海盗不会带着捕蝶网，也不会在笔记本上写字。”

“You’re right,” said Jack. He put down the Pennsylvania book. “So what’s going on?” “你说得对，”杰克说。 他放下了宾夕法尼亚的书。 “那到底发生了什么？”

“Let’s go find out,” said Annie. She dropped the rope ladder back to the ground and started down. “我们去看看吧，”安妮说。 她把绳梯放回地面，开始往下爬。

Jack grabbed his pack and hurried after her. Together they ran barefoot over the hot sand and through the feathery ferns. They climbed to the top of a sand dune and looked down. The three men were still at the edge of the water while the big ship drifted offshore. 杰克抓起他的背包，赶紧跟了上去。 他们光着脚跑过滚烫的沙子，穿过长满羽毛的蕨类植物。 他们爬到沙丘的顶部，往下看。 那三个男人仍然在水边，而那艘大船正漂向海中。

“Hey, look. You can see the name of the ship,” said Annie. “嘿，快看。 你能看到船的名字，”安妮说。

Jack peered through the haze and read on the side of the ship, HMS Challenger. “I’ll look it up,” he whispered. He pulled out their research book and searched the index. “It’s here!” he whispered. He found the right page and read: 杰克透过雾气望去，在船的侧面看到了HMS Challenger。 “我查一下，”他小声说。 他拿出他们的研究书，翻到索引部分。 “找到了！”他小声说。 他找到了正确的那一页，开始读起来：

The HMSChallenger(HMS stands for Her Majesty’s Ship) was a British navy vessel that served as the first dedicated scientific exploration ship in the history of the world. HMS Challenger（HMS代表Her Majesty’s Ship）是一艘英国海军舰艇，是世界上第一艘专门用于科学考察的船只。

“Oh, man,” said Jack, looking up. “That is so cool.” “哦，天哪，”杰克抬头说。 “太酷了。”

“Yeah, read more,” whispered Annie. Jack read on: “嗯，再读一点，”安妮小声说。杰克继续读下去：

From 1872 to 1876, the HMSChallengercircled the globe, exploring the dark depths of the ocean. There were over 200 seamen and six scientists on board. 从1872年到1876年，HMS Challenger号环游了世界，探索了海洋的黑暗深处。船上共有200多名水手和六位科学家。

“So we landed in the 1870s,” said Jack, looking up again. “所以我们降落在1870年代，”杰克抬头说。

“And that guy with the butterfly net must be one of the scientists,” said Annie. “Come on, let’s go meet him!” “而拿着蝴蝶网的那个人一定是科学家之一，”安妮说。 “来吧，我们去见他！”

Before Jack could tell her to wait, Annie darted down the sand dune. “Hey, guys!” she called. She waved her arms. “Hi!” 杰克还没来得及让她等一下，安妮就冲下了沙丘。 “嘿，大家！”她喊道。 她挥舞着双臂。 “你好！”

The three men whirled around. Their eyes grew wide and their mouths dropped open. They looked at Annie as if they were staring at a ghost. 那三个男人猛地转过身来。 他们的眼睛睁得大大的，嘴巴张得老大。 他们看着安妮，好像在看一个鬼魂。
