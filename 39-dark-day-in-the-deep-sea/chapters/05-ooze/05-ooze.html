5. Ooze 5. 泥浆

The captain turned and strode away.

“Sorry, friends,” Henry said to Jack and Annie. “But the captain’s in charge.” He looked around. “I see Joe and Tommy are helping the men bring up the catch now. As soon as they are free, I will ask them to take you back to shore. I am very sorry.” “对不起，朋友们，”亨利对杰克和安妮说道。 “但船长是负责人。”他环顾四周。 “我看到乔和汤米正在帮那些人把捕到的鱼拉上来。” “等他们一有空，我就让他们送你们回岸上去。非常抱歉。”

“That’s okay,” said Annie. “You did what you could.” “没关系，”安妮说。 “你已经尽力了。”

“Before you young people leave, perhaps you would like to see today’s specimens?” asked the professor. “在你们年轻人离开之前，也许你们想看看今天的标本吗？”教授问。

“Sure!” said Jack and Annie together. “当然！”杰克和安妮异口同声地说。

“Then come along!” said the professor. “Every single day we make new discoveries!” “那走吧！”教授说。 “我们每天都会有新的发现！”

Hurrying with Annie across the deck, Jack couldn’t wait to see what had just been brought up from the deep. They followed Henry and the professor to a team of sailors pulling up large nets. The nets were shaped like giant bags with mops attached to the bottom. 和安妮一起快步走过甲板时，杰克迫不及待地想看看刚从深海里打捞上来的到底是什么。 他们跟着亨利和教授，来到一群水手面前，水手们正在拉起巨大的渔网。 这些网的形状像巨大的袋子，底部还连着扫帚。

“What are those mops for?” asked Annie. “这些扫帚是干什么的？”安妮问。

“They sweep up sea animals from the floor of the ocean,” explained Henry. “它们用来把海底的动物扫起来，”亨利解释道。

“We pull them from pitch-darkness into the light of day,” said the professor. “我们把它们从漆黑的海底拉到阳光下，”教授说。

“That must scare them!” said Annie. “那一定把它们吓坏了！”安妮说。

But the professor didn’t seem to hear her. “Over the years, we’ve hauled up tens of thousands of specimens!” he said. 但教授好像没听见她的话。 “多年来，我们已经打捞上来了成千上万的标本！”他说。

As the sailors dumped the bags onto the deck, Jack saw mostly mud. But lying in the mud were tiny, squirmy pink and yellow fish and fiery orange sea stars. 当水手们把袋子倒到甲板上时，杰克看到的大多是泥巴。 但泥巴里躺着一些小小的、扭来扭去的粉红色和黄色的小鱼，还有火红色的海星。

“No monsters there, huh?” asked Annie. “那里没有怪物，对吧？”安妮问。

The professor looked at her. “Not today, my dear,” he said. 教授看着她。 “今天不行，亲爱的，”他说。

“I was just kidding,” said Annie. “Do you believe in monsters?” “我只是在开玩笑，”安妮说。 “你们相信怪物吗？

“Oh. Well …” The professor looked serious. “The sea is very deep, my dear. It covers nearly three-quarters of the world. So I say to myself, ‘Might it not indeed hold many mysterious creatures, including monsters?’” 嗯……”教授看起来很严肃。 “大海很深，亲爱的。 它覆盖了世界上几乎四分之三的地方。 所以我对自己说，‘它里面会不会真的有很多神秘的生物，包括怪物呢？’”

Good point, thought Jack. 杰克心想，这是个好观点。

“But don’t be afraid, children!” said the professor. “Someday we’ll catch all the monsters and study them! We will conquer our fears through knowledge! Won’t we, Henry?” “但孩子们，不要害怕！”教授说。 “总有一天我们会抓住所有怪物，研究它们！ 我们将通过知识战胜恐惧！不是吗，亨利？

“Yes, sir,” said Henry. “是的，先生，”亨利说道。

“Conquer our fears through knowledge!” The professor repeated his thought with enthusiasm. “I shall add that to my lectures.” “用知识战胜我们的恐惧！”教授充满热情地重复了他的想法。 “我会把这一点加到我的讲座中。”

Low thunder rolled in the sky. 低沉的雷声在天空中滚动。

Jack looked up. Black clouds hovered overhead. A strong gust of wind swept over the deck. 杰克抬头看了看。 乌云在头顶上盘旋。 一阵强风掠过甲板。

“Attention!” the captain shouted, heading toward them. “A squall is headed our way! For now, take the children below to the main deck.” “注意！”船长喊道，朝他们走来。 “暴风雨要来了！” 现在，把下面的孩子带到主甲板上。

“Yes, sir!” said Henry. He smiled at Jack and Annie. “Well, friends, I suppose you get to stay with us a while longer, after all.” “是的，先生！”亨利说道。 他微笑着看向杰克和安妮。 “好吧，朋友们，我想你们还得和我们待一会儿。”

“Yay!” Annie said softly. “太好了！”安妮轻声说。

“Only those on watch stay on deck!” the captain shouted to his crew. “Everyone else get below.” “只有守夜的人才能留在甲板上！”船长对他的船员大喊。 “其他人下去。”

Henry led Jack and Annie away just as the rain began to pour down. Water dripped from Jack’s hair and backpack and life vest as he and Annie followed Henry down a steep flight of stairs to a dimly lit hallway. 亨利带着杰克和安妮离开，就在这时雨开始倾盆而下。 杰克和安妮跟着亨利沿着陡峭的楼梯走下去，进入一条昏暗的走廊，水从杰克的头发、背包和救生衣上滴落下来。

“Our ship was converted from a war vessel to a ship of sea laboratories,” Henry said. “The navy removed sixteen of the eighteen guns on board to make room for them. Would you like to see mine?” “我们的船是从一艘战舰改造成的海洋实验室船，”亨利说。 “海军把船上的十八门大炮中十六门拆了下来，腾出地方给实验室。 你想看看我的吗？“

“Oh, yes!” said Jack. He couldn’t wait to see an actual sea laboratory. “哦，想！”杰克说。 他迫不及待想看看真正的海洋实验室。

“Follow me.” Henry unlocked a door and showed Jack and Annie into a large, dim room. There was a small skylight overhead. Rain pounded against the glass. “跟我来。”亨利打开一扇门，带杰克和安妮走进一个又大又昏暗的房间。 天花板上有一个小天窗。 雨点猛烈地打在玻璃上。

Henry struck a match and lit a couple of oil lamps. Shadows danced around the room. 亨利擦亮一根火柴，点燃了几盏油灯。 房间里到处都是跳舞的影子。

Jack smiled and let out a deep sigh. He loved the sea lab. Shelves were lined with hundreds of different-size bottles. The bottles were filled with floating blobs. In the middle of the room was a wooden table. It held maps, rulers, thermometers, bowls with gooey-looking stuff, and a big microscope. 杰克笑了笑，深深地叹了口气。 他喜欢海洋实验室。 架子上摆满了各种大小的瓶子。 瓶子里装着漂浮的黏糊糊的东西。 房间中间放着一张木桌子。 桌子上放着地图、尺子、温度计、装着看起来黏糊糊的东西的碗，还有一个大显微镜。

Henry pointed at the microscope. “Would you like to see something remarkable?” he asked. 亨利指着显微镜。 “你想看看什么特别的东西吗？”他问。

“Oh, yes!” said Annie. She peered through the eyepiece. “Whoa—that’s amazing,” she breathed. “哦，想！”安妮说。 她透过目镜看了起来。 “哇——太神奇了，”她惊叹道。

“Let me see,” said Jack. He put his backpack down on the table and looked in the microscope. He saw the tiny skeleton of a sea horse. “Cool …,” he said. “让我看看，”杰克说。 他把背包放在桌子上，然后用显微镜观察。 他看到了一匹海马小小的骨架。 “真酷……，”他说。

“That sea horse is no bigger than a grain of sugar,” said Henry. “But of course I’m fascinated by larger creatures as well. Why, just yesterday I spent several hours studying the ear bone of a dolphin and the tooth of a shark.” “那匹海马没有一粒糖那么大，”亨利说。 “不过当然，我也对更大的动物很感兴趣。 昨天，我花好几个小时研究了海豚的耳骨和鲨鱼的牙齿。”

“And what’s in all those bottles?” asked Annie. She pointed at the rows and rows of bottles on Henry’s shelves. “那些瓶子里装的是什么？”安妮问。 她指着亨利书架上一排排的瓶子。

“Many curiosities,” said the scientist. “That large one, for instance, holds a creature that looks like a giant sock. Some call it a blubber fish. But it’s not a fish at all! It is made up of millions of tiny sea creatures.” “很多奇怪的东西，”科学家说。 “比如那个大的，里面住着一种看起来像大袜子的生物。 有些人叫它脂肪鱼。 但它根本不是鱼！ 它是由数以百万计的小海洋生物组成的。”

“Eww,” said Annie. “哇，”安妮说。

“And there’s a rare sea slug,” said Henry. He pointed to a bright yellow blob floating in a clear liquid. “还有一个稀有的海蛞蝓，”亨利说。 他指着一个漂浮在透明液体中的亮黄色团块。

“Nice color,” said Jack. “颜色真好看，”杰克说。

“We study whatever we bring up from the deep,” said Henry. “We measure the specimens and identify them. Then we preserve them in bottles of alcohol and label the bottles.” “我们研究从深海里打捞上来的任何东西，”亨利说。 “我们测量这些标本，并给它们命名。” 然后我们将它们保存在酒精瓶中并给瓶子贴上标签。

“So all those bottles are full of dead sea creatures?” said Annie. “所以所有这些瓶子都装满了死掉的海洋生物吗？”安妮说。

“Oh, no. Many bottles are simply filled with ooze from the bottom of the sea,” said Henry. “哦，不， 很多瓶子只是装满了海底的淤泥，”亨利说。

“Ooze?” said Jack. “泥浆？”杰克说。

“The official name for mud,” said Henry, smiling. “Here, feel it.” Henry picked up one of the plates on the table and held it out to Jack and Annie. They rubbed the sticky, wet mud between their fingers. “泥的正式名字，”亨利微笑着说。 “来，摸摸看。”亨利拿起桌上的一个盘子，递给了杰克和安妮。 他们用手指在黏糊糊的泥浆间来回搓了搓。

“Ooze is a good name for it,” said Jack. “泥浆这个名字真合适，”杰克说。

Henry gave them a cloth to wipe their hands. Then he picked up a large book from the table. “And here is the notebook where I transfer my drawings of natural history specimens,” he said. He opened the book. Inside were beautiful watercolors of shells, plants, and butterflies. 亨利给了他们一块布，用来擦手。 然后他从桌子上拿起了一本大书。 “这就是我用来记录自然历史标本画的笔记本，”他说。 他打开了这本书。 里面是贝壳、植物和蝴蝶的美丽水彩画。

“These are great,” said Jack. Henry’s notebook reminded him of the notebooks he’d seen in Leonardo da Vinci’s studio. “这些太棒了，”杰克说。 亨利的笔记本让他想起了他在达·芬奇工作室里见过的笔记本。

“This is beautiful,” said Annie. She reached across Henry’s table and picked up a gleaming, round seashell. The shell was white with curved reddish brown lines. “这太漂亮了，”安妮说。 她伸出手，从亨利的桌子上拿起了一枚闪亮的、圆形的贝壳。 这个贝壳是白色的，上面有弯曲的红棕色线条。

“Yes,” Henry said softly. “My nautilus shell.” “是的，”亨利轻声说。 “我的鹦鹉螺壳。”

“Is this one of your specimens?” asked Annie. “这是你的标本之一吗？”安妮问。

“No,” said Henry. “I don’t consider that a specimen. It’s more like a treasure. I’m afraid I grew quite fond of the little creature who once lived inside that shell.” “不，”亨利说。 “我不认为那是一个标本。 它更像是一个珍宝。 我担心我非常喜爱曾经住在那个壳里的小生物。”

“What did he do?” asked Annie. “他做了什么？”安妮问。

“Oh, he just swam around a small tub I had for him,” said Henry. “But he moved backwards. Rather funny. He filled himself with water and then squirted it out—all over me!” Henry smiled. “I was quite sad when he died. I wished I had returned him to the sea.” Henry put the shell down and let out a quiet sigh. “Silly to think that way, I know.” “哦，他只是在我为他准备的小盆里游来游去，”亨利说。 “但他往后游。还挺有趣的。” 他把自己灌满水，然后喷出来——全喷在我身上了！”亨利笑了。 “他去世的时候，我很难过。 我希望我当时能把他送回大海。”亨利放下贝壳，轻轻叹了口气。 “认为这样想很傻，我知道。”

“Not silly at all,” said Annie. “一点都不傻，”安妮说。

The ship’s bell rang. 船上的钟声响了。

“Ah, time to go,” said Henry. “The captain runs a tight ship. It’s against the rules to be late. So let us be off to the wardroom.” “啊，该走了，”亨利说。 “船长管理得很严格。 迟到是违反规矩的。 所以我们该去军官室了。”

“What’s the wardroom?” Jack asked. “什么是军官餐厅？”杰克问。

“That’s where the scientists and naval officers eat,” said Henry. “那是科学家和海军军官吃饭的地方，”亨利说。

“Eat?” Jack said weakly. The thought of food made him feel queasy again. “吃饭？”杰克虚弱地说。 一想到食物，他又觉得恶心了。

“Yes,” said Henry. He blew out the two oil lamps. “Come along! You must join us! It’s lunchtime!” “是的，”亨利说。 他吹灭了两盏油灯。 “快点！你必须和我们一起去！ 是午餐时间！”
