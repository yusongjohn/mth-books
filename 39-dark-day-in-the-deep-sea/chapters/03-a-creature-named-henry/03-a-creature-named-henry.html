3. A Creature Named Henry 3. 一个叫亨利的生物

J ack threw their book into his pack and quickly ran to join Annie. “Hi,” he said to the three men. 杰克把他们的书塞进背包，赶紧跑过去加入安妮。 “你好，”他对那三个男人说道。

“Don’t be afraid,” said Annie. “We come in peace.” “别害怕，”安妮说。 “我们是来和平的。”

The three men kept gawking. “Who—who are you?” one of the sailors sputtered. 那三个男人一直盯着看。 “谁……你们是谁？”一个水手结结巴巴地说。

Jack and Annie walked closer. “I’m Jack, and this is Annie,” said Jack. 杰克和安妮走近了一些。 “我是杰克，这是安妮，”杰克说。

The man in the bow tie stepped forward. He had a long mustache and a big, friendly smile. “My name is Henry,” he said. “I came ashore to look for rare butterflies, plants, and shells. But it seems I have found a rare creature called a Jack-and-Annie instead.” 系领结的男人走上前。 他有一根长长的胡子和一个又大又友好的微笑。 “我叫亨利，”他说。 “我上岸是为了寻找稀有的蝴蝶、植物和贝壳。 但看起来我找到了一种稀有的生物，叫做杰克-and-安妮。”

“And we’ve found a creature called a Henry,” said Annie, giggling. “That’s what we named a pteranodon when we went to the time of the dinosaurs.” “我们找到了一种叫做亨利的生物，”安妮笑着说。 “那是我们去恐龙时代时给翼龙起的名字。”

“I beg your pardon?” said Henry. “请问你说什么？”亨利说。

“Uh … she’s teasing,” said Jack. “呃……她在开玩笑，”杰克说。

“What an unusual wit,” said Henry, his eyes sparkling. “Well, you have not only found a Henry. You’ve also found a Joe and a Tommy, the two able seamen who rowed me here.” “真是不同寻常的幽默感，”亨利说，眼睛闪闪发亮。 “好吧，你不仅找到了一个亨利。 你还找到了乔和汤米，这两个水手划着船把我带到这里来的。”

“Hi, Joe and Tommy,” said Annie. She gave the two sailors a big smile. “你好，乔和汤米，”安妮说。 她对着这两个水手露出了灿烂的微笑。

But Joe and Tommy didn’t smile back. They were both scowling. “Where are you from?” Joe asked suspiciously. 但是乔和汤米没有回笑。 他们两个都皱着眉头。 “你们是从哪里来的？”乔怀疑地问。

“Frog Creek, Pennsylvania,” said Annie. “宾夕法尼亚州的蛙溪镇。”安妮说。

“Where is that?” asked Tommy. “那是在哪里？”汤米问。

“America,” said Jack. “在美国。”杰克说。

“How nice!” said Henry. “Why are you here?” “真不错！”亨利说。 “你们为什么来这里？”

“We, uh … we’re on vacation,” said Annie. “We’re camped with our family back there somewhere….” She waved vaguely. “呃……我们在度假。”安妮说。 “我们和家人在那边的某个地方露营……”她模模糊糊地挥了挥手。

“Vacation?” said Henry. “假期？”亨利说道。

“Our parents like to vacation in really out-of-the-way places,” said Annie. “我们的父母喜欢去一些非常偏僻的地方度假，”安妮说道。

Henry chuckled. “Americans!” 亨利笑了笑。 “美国人！”

“Are you a scientist from the HMS Challenger?” Jack said. He was eager to change the subject. “你是来自‘挑战者号’的科学家吗？”杰克说道。 他急于换一个话题。

“Why, yes,” said Henry. “I am one of the team trying to solve the riddles of the deep.” “为什么，是的，”亨利说道。 “我是试图解开深海谜题的团队的一员。”

Jack loved the sound of that: riddles of the deep. “What kind of riddles have you solved so far?” he asked. 杰克喜欢这个说法：深海的谜题。 “到目前为止，你们解决了哪些谜题？”他问道。

“Well, for one thing, we’ve learned that the ocean floor is absolutely teeming with life,” said Henry. “嗯，首先，我们了解到海底到处都是生命，”亨利说。

“You didn’t already know that?” asked Annie. “你之前不知道吗？”安妮问。

“We thought it was probably the case,” said Henry. “But mind you, many people cannot imagine that life can exist in the icy darkness of the deep. In fact, some still believe there is no floor to the ocean at all! They think the sea goes down forever.” “我们认为这可能是真的，”亨利说。 “不过，很多人无法想象在深海冰冷的黑暗中竟然有生命存在。 事实上，还有一些人甚至认为海底根本没有底！ 他们觉得大海会一直往下延伸到永远。”

“Are you serious?” said Annie. “Ha. I guess you’ll be letting them know that most of the ocean is not more than two miles deep. Of course, some ocean trenches are deeper than six miles. But—” “你是认真的吗？”安妮说。 我想你该告诉他们，大部分的海洋深度不超过两英里。 当然，有些海沟的深度超过了六英里。但——”

“Annie …,” said Jack. “安妮……，”杰克说。

“My, you know a lot about the sea,” said Henry, looking at Annie with curiosity. “哇，你对大海知道好多啊，”亨利好奇地看着安妮说。

“Well, I know that mountain ranges and volcanoes are down there, too,” said Annie. “嗯……我知道那里还有山脉和火山呢，”安妮说。

Jack leaned in close to Annie. “Stop showing off,” he said under his breath. 杰克靠近安妮。 “别炫耀了，”他小声说。

“Um … I mean maybe, just maybe,” Annie said to Henry. “Of course, I’m only guessing.” “呃……我是说，也许，只是也许，”安妮对亨利说。 “当然，我只是在猜。”

“You are a very good guesser,” said Henry. “On our voyage, we’ve collected many volcanic rocks from the deep.” “你猜得可真准，”亨利说。 “在我们的航行中，我们从海底收集了很多火山岩石。”

“Cool,” said Annie. “How’d you get them? Mini-subs?” “太酷了，”安妮说。 “你们是怎么得到它们的？用迷你潜水艇吗？”

“Mini-subs?” asked Henry. “微型潜水艇？”亨利问。

“You know, they carry you down into the deep sea,” said Annie. “你知道，它们能带你潜入深海，”安妮说。

“Annie!” said Jack between his teeth. He gave her a look that said Just stop! Jack was pretty sure mini-subs hadn’t been invented by the early 1870s. He quickly changed the subject again. “And you study butterflies, too?” he said, pointing to Henry’s net. “And seashells?” “安妮！”杰克咬着牙说。 他给了她一个眼神，意思是赶紧住嘴！ 杰克很确定，在19世纪70年代初还没有发明微型潜水艇。 他赶紧又换了个话题。 “你也会研究蝴蝶吗？”他指着亨利的网说。 “还有贝壳？”

“Indeed I do,” said Henry. “In fact, I came ashore to search for a rare shell just now. And I think I may have found its cousin.” He opened his book to a page that showed a pencil drawing of the shell. “确实如此，”亨利说。 “事实上，我刚刚上岸就是为了寻找一种稀有的贝壳。” “我觉得我可能找到了它的表兄弟。”他翻开书，指着一页上用铅笔画的贝壳图案。

“Would you like us to help you look for more?” asked Annie. “你们想帮我们找更多吗？”安妮问。

“Why, thank you,” said Henry. “But I have quite successfully found what I came for. We’ll be going back to the ship now.” “哦，谢谢你，”亨利说。 “不过我已经成功找到了我想要的东西。 我们现在要回船上了。”

“Oh, please, can we visit the HMS Challenger?” Annie blurted out. “哦，请让我们参观HMS挑战者号吧！”安妮突然喊道。

“Out of the question,” said the sailor named Joe. “这绝对不行，”名叫乔的水手说。

“Well, I’m—” said Henry. “好吧，我——”亨利说。

“Sir, the captain would never approve of bringing children on board,” Tommy broke in. “It’s absolutely against the rules.” “先生，船长绝不会同意带孩子上船，”汤米插话道。 “这完全违反规定。”

Henry looked at Jack and Annie. Jack didn’t want to break any rules, but at the same time, he really wanted to visit the ship. “We’d really like to learn more about sea exploration,” he said. He gave the scientist a hopeful smile.

“We promise not to get in anyone’s way,” said Annie. “And our parents like us to learn new things.” “我们保证不会打扰任何人，”安妮说。 “而且我们的父母希望我们学习新东西。”

Henry turned back to the two seamen. “Surely such bright and curious young people should be allowed to visit our ship,” he said. “Since we’ll be drifting and dredging in this area all day, we can return them to shore before nightfall.” 亨利转身面对那两个水手。 “像你们这样聪明又好奇的年轻人，应该被允许参观我们的船，”他说。 “既然我们今天整天都会在这个区域漂流和挖泥，那在天黑之前就能把你们送回岸上。”

Joe and Tommy frowned at each other. But Joe finally gave a quick nod. 乔和汤米互相皱了皱眉头。 但乔最后还是快速地点了点头。

“Yay!” Annie said. “太棒了！”安妮说。

“Thanks!” said Jack. “We promise we won’t cause any trouble.” “谢谢！”杰克说。 “我们保证不会惹麻烦。”

“See that you don’t,” said Henry with a smile. “Joe and Tommy, would you mind lending our two visitors your life preservers?” “希望你们能做到。”亨利微笑着说。 “乔和汤米，你们能借给我们两位客人你们的救生圈吗？”

“Never fit ’em, sir,” said Joe. “从来没用过，先生。”乔说。

“Well, we must do what we can to keep them safe,” said Henry. “好吧，我们必须尽我们所能保护他们。”亨利说。

The two seamen grudgingly handed Jack and Annie their bulky vests. 两名水手不情愿地把厚重的救生衣交给了杰克和安妮。

So these are old-fashioned life preservers! thought Jack. The vests were made of blocks of cork tied together. Even though Jack buckled his tightly, it hung low on his body, almost slipping off. 这些是老式的救生圈！杰克心想。 救生衣是用绑在一起的软木块做的。 即使杰克把带子系得很紧，它还是低低地挂在身上，几乎要掉下来。

“I’m afraid they are rather big,” said Henry, “but still they might save you should we capsize.” “我担心它们有点大，”亨利说，“不过如果船翻了，它们也许能救你们。”

“Don’t worry,” said Annie. “We’re really good swimmers.” “别担心，”安妮说。 “我们游泳很棒的。”

“We should go now, sir,” Joe said impatiently. “我们该走了，先生，”乔不耐烦地说。

“Yes, yes,” Henry said. “Let us be off!” “是的，是的，”亨利说。 “我们快走吧！”

Joe and Tommy pushed the rowboat into the shallow water. While Jack and Annie had been talking with Henry, the water had turned choppy. Dark gray clouds had rolled in, hiding the sun. 乔和汤米把划艇推进浅水里。 当杰克和安妮和亨利说话时，水变得波浪起伏了。 暗灰色的云朵涌了过来，遮住了太阳。

“Climb in after me!” Henry told Jack and Annie, and they followed him into the rowboat. “快爬进来！”亨利对杰克和安妮说，他们跟着他爬进了划艇。

Jack’s backpack wouldn’t fit over his life jacket. So he clutched the pack to his chest as Joe and Tommy pushed the boat farther into the ocean. The sailors jumped in, picked up their oars, and began rowing back to the ship. 杰克的背包太大了，套不上救生衣。 所以他紧紧抱着背包，乔和汤米把船往海里划得更远了。 水手们跳进船里，拿起船桨，开始划船回船上。
