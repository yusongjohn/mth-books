CHAPTER ONE

The Fourth Thing

Jack heard knocking on his door. Teddy and Kathleen were knocking! They were calling to him! He had to help them save Penny! Jack tried to cross his room … but his legs wouldn’t move. He couldn’t get to the door! 杰克听到有人敲门。 泰迪和凯瑟琳在敲门！ 他们在喊他！ 他必须帮助他们救出佩妮！ 杰克试着穿过房间……但他的腿动不了。 他到不了门边！

The knocking grew louder. “Jack?” his dad called. 敲门声越来越响了。 “杰克？”他爸爸喊道。

Jack opened his eyes. Where was he? 杰克睁开了眼睛。他在哪里？

“Jack, get up! School!” came his dad’s voice. “杰克，快起来！ 上学啦！”他爸爸的声音传来。

What a dream, thought Jack. He quickly sat up in bed. He’d overslept! 真是一场梦，杰克心想。 他迅速从床上坐了起来。他起晚了！

“Jack? Are you awake?” said his dad, peeking into his room. “杰克？ 你醒了吗？”他爸爸探头看了看他的房间。

“Yep, thanks, Dad!” said Jack. “嗯，谢谢爸爸！”杰克说。

“Better hurry,” said Jack’s dad. He closed the door. “最好快点，”杰克的爸爸说。 他关上了门。

Jack hopped out of bed. Where’s Annie? he wondered. They had planned to go to the tree house before school! Today was the day to find the fourth thing to save Penny. 杰克从床上跳了下来。安妮在哪里？ 他心想。 他们计划在上学前去树屋！ 今天就是找到第四样东西来拯救彭妮的日子。

Jack pulled on his jeans and a T-shirt and rushed out of his room. Annie charged out of her room at the same moment. “We overslept!” she said. 杰克穿上牛仔裤和T恤，然后冲出了房间。 安妮在同一时刻冲出了她的房间。 “我们睡过头了！”，她说。

“No kidding,” said Jack. “Hurry! We have to get to the tree house before school starts.” “可不是嘛，”，杰克说。 “快点！ 我们必须在上学前赶到树屋。”

“I know!” said Annie. “我知道了！”安妮说。

Annie and Jack charged down the steps. They rushed into the kitchen. 安妮和杰克冲下楼梯。 他们冲进厨房。

“Good morning, sleepyheads,” their mom said. “You have just enough time for breakfast. I made you egg sandwiches.” “早上好，睡懒觉的。”他们的妈妈说。 “你们还有足够的时间吃早餐。 我给你们做了三明治。”

“Thanks, Mom,” said Jack. “But would you mind if we take them with us? We have to head to school early. We have a special project.” “谢谢，妈妈，”杰克说。 “不过，你介意我们带着它们走吗？ 我们必须早点去上学。” 我们有一个特别的项目。

“You guys really seem to love school,” their dad said. “你们俩真的好像很喜欢上学，”他们的爸爸说。

“Yep, school’s great,” said Jack. “对啊，上学很棒，”杰克说。

Their mom wrapped up their sandwiches and put them in a paper bag. She handed the bag to Annie. “You two have a good day,” she said. 他们的妈妈包好了三明治，放进了一个纸袋里。 她把袋子递给了安妮。 “你们俩今天过得愉快，”她说。

“Thanks, Mom!” said Annie. “谢谢，妈妈！”，安妮说。

Jack and Annie hurried to the front door and stepped outside into the moist spring air. 杰克和安妮赶紧跑到前门，走出去，迎面是湿润的春天空气。

“We have exactly twenty-five minutes before we have to head to school,” Jack said. “我们还有正好二十五分钟就要去上学了，”杰克说。

“No problem,” said Annie. “Hold on.” She put their sandwiches in Jack’s backpack. “Okay. Let’s go!” They raced across their yard and charged up the sidewalk. They ran across the street and headed into the Frog Creek woods. They ran between trees full of new green leaves until they came to the tallest oak. “没问题，”安妮说。 “抓紧了。”她把他们的三明治放进杰克的背包里。 “好的。” “走吧！”他们冲过院子，跑上人行道。 他们穿过街道，跑进了蛙溪镇的树林里。 他们在长满新绿叶子的树之间奔跑，直到来到最高的橡树前。

The magic tree house was high up in the branches. Annie grabbed the rope ladder and started up. Jack followed. Inside the tree house they both sat on the floor, catching their breath. 神奇的树屋在树枝的高处。 安妮抓住绳梯开始往上爬。杰克跟在后面。 他们俩走进树屋，坐在地板上，喘着气休息。

“Whew … it’s all still here,” gasped Annie. “哇……这里的一切都还在，”安妮喘着气说。

Dusty rays of morning light shone through the tree house window onto the three things they’d already found to help Teddy and Kathleen: a rose carved out of an emerald from India, a dried white and yellow flower from the Swiss Alps, and a goose-feather quill pen from President Abraham Lincoln. 清晨的阳光透过树屋的窗户照进来，照在他们已经找到的三样东西上，这些能帮助泰迪和凯瑟琳：一朵用印度祖母绿雕刻成的玫瑰，一朵来自瑞士阿尔卑斯山的干白花和黄花，还有一支来自亚伯拉罕·林肯总统的鹅毛羽毛笔。

“I hope Teddy and Kathleen were able to translate the last part of the rhyme,” said Jack. “Do we have a new note?” “我希望泰迪和凯瑟琳能翻译出韵文的最后一部分，”杰克说。 “我们有新留言吗？”

They both looked around the tree house. “Aha!” said Annie. She pointed to a book and a small scroll lying in the corner. 他们俩环顾了一下树屋。 “啊哈！”安妮说。 她指着角落里的一本书和一卷小卷轴。

Jack picked up the book, and Annie grabbed the scroll. “Interesting,” said Jack. He showed the cover of the book to Annie. 杰克拿起书，安妮抓起了卷轴。 “有趣，”杰克说。 他把书的封面展示给安妮看。

“Cool, China!” said Annie. “太酷了，中国！”，安妮说。

图中文字：TRAVEL GUIDE 图中文字：旅行指南

“We’ve been there before,” said Jack. “Remember the emperor who burned the books and tried to have us killed?” “我们以前去过那里，”杰克说。 “还记得那个烧书还想杀我们的皇帝吗？”

“That was more than two thousand years ago,” said Annie. “Maybe we’re going to a different time now.” “那是两千多年前的事了，”安妮说。 “也许我们现在要去另一个时代了。”

Annie unrolled their scroll and read aloud: 安妮展开他们的卷轴，大声读道：

Dear Jack and Annie, 亲爱的杰克和安妮：

We haven’t yet finished translating the last lines of the secret rhyme to reverse the statue spell Teddy cast on Penny. But we do know the fourth object we need to break the spell. And we know that it can be found in Southwest China. It is: 我们还没有完成翻译秘密咒语的最后一部分，用来解除泰迪对彭妮施放的雕像咒语。 但我们已经知道打破咒语所需的第四个物品。 我们知道它可以在中国西南部找到。它就是：

A healthy food, grainy and good, baked with love, tough as wood, round in shape, the color of sand, given to those who have lost their land. 一种健康的食品，颗粒饱满味道好，用爱烘焙，坚硬如木，形状圆圆，颜色像沙，送给失去土地的人。

Once you have found the last object, please hurry to Camelot. Morgan and Merlin will return by break of day tomorrow! 一旦你们找到最后一个物品，请赶快去卡梅洛特。 摩根和梅林明天天一亮就会回来！

—Teddy and Kathleen —泰迪和凯瑟琳

“Healthy food in China?” said Annie. “That shouldn’t be hard to find.” “中国有健康的食物吗？”安妮说。 “那应该不难找到。”

“But don’t you think it sounds kind of weird?” said Jack. “How can anyone eat something that’s tough as wood?” “但是你不觉得这听起来有点奇怪吗？”杰克说。 “怎么有人能吃像木头一样硬的东西呢？”

“Good question,” said Annie. “But we’ll have fun looking for it. I love the food we get with Mom and Dad at Chinatown Palace, don’t you?” “好问题，”安妮说。 “但我们会玩得很开心，去找它。 我喜欢和爸爸妈妈在唐人街宫殿餐厅吃到的美食，你不也喜欢吗？”

“Yeah … but …,” said Jack. “是啊……但是……，”杰克说。

“Okay!” said Annie. “Now, did Teddy and Kathleen send a potion or anything else magical to help us?” “好！”安妮说。 “现在，泰迪和凯瑟琳是不是给我们寄了魔药或者其他什么东西来帮忙？”

Jack looked around the tree house again. 杰克再次环顾了树屋。

“There!” he said. A small glass bottle glinted in a shadowy corner. Jack picked it up, and he and Annie stepped to the window and read the writing on the bottle’s label. “在那里！”他说。 一个小玻璃瓶在角落的阴影中闪闪发光。 杰克捡起它，他和安妮走到窗边，读了瓶子标签上的文字。

Use only once. Take one sip and grow to five times your size. The magic lasts for one hour. 只能使用一次。 喝一口，就能长到你原来五倍那么大。 魔法持续一小时。

“Five times our size?” said Jack. “That would make us about as tall as a house.” “长到我们五倍那么大？”杰克说。 “那我们会和房子一样高。”

“Talk about weird,” said Annie. “真奇怪，”安妮说。

“Yeah, but it actually sounds like fun,” said Jack. “是啊，但听起来其实挺好玩的，”杰克说。

“More fun than when we became teeny-tiny in India,” asked Annie, “after we saw the cobras?” “比我们在印度变得超级小之后看到眼镜蛇时好玩多了吗？”安妮问，“那时候我们不是差点被眼镜蛇吓到吗？”

“Lots more,” said Jack, “especially if we have to get away from cobras again.” He put the rhyme and the tiny bottle into his backpack, then took a deep breath. “Okay! All set?” “好玩多了，”杰克说，“特别是如果我们还得再躲开眼镜蛇的话。”他把那首童谣和小瓶子放进背包里，然后深吸了一口气。 “好了！准备好了吗？”

“Ready,” said Annie. “准备好了，”安妮说。

Jack pointed to the cover of the guide book. “I wish we could go there!” he said. 杰克指着指南书的封面。 “真希望我们能去那里！”他说。

The wind started to blow. 风开始吹起来了。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转起来。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后，一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。
