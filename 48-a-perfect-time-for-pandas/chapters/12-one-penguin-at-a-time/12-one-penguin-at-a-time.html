CHAPTER TWELVE

One Penguin at a Time

12. One Penguin at a Time 12. 一次一只企鹅

“Penny! You came back! You are alive!” whispered Teddy. “佩妮！你回来了！ 你活下来了！”，泰迪轻声说。

“Hurray!” Annie shouted. “太棒了！”，安妮喊道。

Teddy started crying again, but this time, he was laughing, too. Kathleen and Jack and Annie laughed with him, and the only tears now were tears of joy and relief. 泰迪又开始哭，但这次，他也在笑。 凯瑟琳、杰克和安妮和他一起笑，现在唯一的眼泪是喜悦和安心的眼泪。

The little penguin blinked and looked at each of them, one at a time. Peep! Peep! Peep! Peep! she greeted them, waddling up and down the length of the table. 小企鹅眨了眨眼睛，依次看着他们每一个人。咕噜！ 她向他们打招呼，摇摇晃晃地沿着桌子走了一遍。

Teddy picked up Penny. He rubbed his cheek against her fuzzy head. “It’s a miracle,” he said softly. 泰迪捡起了佩妮。 他把脸蹭了蹭她毛茸茸的头。 “这是一个奇迹，”他轻声说。

Trumpet blasts came from the distance. Startled, they all looked toward the window. Dawn light was creeping into Merlin’s cottage. 远处传来了号角声。 他们都被吓了一跳，全都朝窗户望去。 黎明的光线正悄悄地进入梅林的小屋。

“I’m afraid you must go now,” Kathleen said to Jack and Annie. “The sound of the trumpeters means that Merlin and Morgan have returned from Avalon. We could never explain all of this to them!” Kathleen laughed as she put the emerald, the flower, the feather, and the panda bread into a deep pocket of her cloak. “恐怕你们现在必须离开了，”凯瑟琳对杰克和安妮说。 “号角声意味着梅林和摩根已经从阿瓦隆回来了。 我们永远无法向他们解释这一切！”凯瑟琳一边说，一边把翡翠、花朵、羽毛和熊猫面包放进斗篷的深口袋里。

Then she looked at Teddy. He was hugging Penny and grinning as if in a daze. “Let us bring Penny with us, Teddy,” she said. “We will lead Jack and Annie back to the tree house!” 然后她看了看泰迪。 他正抱着彭妮，笑得好像着了迷一样。 “我们带彭妮一起去吧，泰迪，”她说。 “我们要带杰克和安妮回树屋！”，

The sun was rising when they left the cottage. Kathleen’s cloak billowed behind her as she led them all through the orchard back to the tree house. 他们离开小屋时，太阳正在升起。 凯瑟琳的斗篷在她身后飘扬，她带领他们穿过果园，回到树屋。

“Good-bye,” said Teddy. “I—I thank you a million times. All of Camelot thanks you—or would thank you if they knew how you had helped me.” “再见，”泰迪说。 “我……我要感谢你一百万次。 整个卡梅洛特都感谢你——或者会感谢你，如果他们知道你帮我做了什么的话。

“Yes, yes, they would!” said Kathleen. She hugged Jack and Annie. “是的，是的，他们会的！”凯瑟琳说。 她拥抱了杰克和安妮。

“Wait,” said Jack, “what was the last part of the rhyme again, the part you just translated? Do you remember?” “等一下，”杰克说，“你刚才翻译的那句咒语，最后的部分是什么来着？你还记得吗？”

“Yes, I do,” said Teddy. Then he recited the words from memory: “记得，”泰迪说。 然后他凭记忆背诵起来：

When these four things come together with the one who cast the spell, the spell will then be broken and all again be well. 当这四样东西与施咒之人齐聚时，咒语就会被打破，一切都会恢复正常。

“Oh, now I get it,” said Annie. “You broke the spell, Teddy. You broke it all by yourself. By feeling all the feelings you felt for Penny, you broke the spell.” “哦，我明白了，”安妮说。 “你打破了咒语，泰迪。 你完全是靠自己打破的。 通过感受到你对彭妮的所有感情，你打破了这个咒语。

“Yes,” said Kathleen. “I quite think that’s what happened. The four things that Jack and Annie brought back did not break the spell. They only served to awaken feelings deep within you, Teddy. And that is what really broke the spell.” “是的，”凯瑟琳说。 “我想事情就是这样发生的。” 杰克和安妮带回的四样东西并没有打破魔法。 它们只是唤醒了你内心深处的情感，泰迪。 “而且这就是真正打破咒语的事情。”

“Yes. You must be right,” said Teddy. “I’m sorry you two had to go to such trouble to help me, and that Kathleen had to worry so. Not only Penny, but all of you suffered on my behalf.” “是的。” “你一定是对的，”泰迪说。 “对不起，你们俩为了帮我费了这么大的劲，也让凯瑟琳这么担心。” “不仅 penny，还有你们所有人，都因为我而受苦了。”

“That’s okay. We loved all our missions,” said Annie. “We helped an elephant find her baby. We helped a funny dog find out that he was a great dog, and a president find hope, and some pandas find their way home.” “没关系。” 安妮说：“我们喜欢所有任务。” “我们帮一头大象找到了她的宝宝。” 我们帮一只搞笑的狗发现他其实是个很棒的狗，还帮一位总统找到希望，也帮一些熊猫找到回家的路。

“All because you were trying to help Penny,” said Kathleen. “全是因为你在帮助宾妮，”凯瑟琳说。

“True,” said Jack, stroking the little penguin. “It’s weird how that works sometimes.” “没错，”杰克一边抚摸着小企鹅，一边说。 “有时候事情就是这样奇怪地发生。”

The trumpets sounded again. 号角声再次响起。

“Hurry,” said Kathleen. “They’re getting closer.” Annie brushed her hand down Penny’s back. “快点，”凯瑟琳说。 “他们越来越近了。”安妮用手轻轻顺着宾妮的背。

“Bye, Penny,” she said. “再见，宾妮，”她说。

Peep.

“And peep to you,” said Jack. He kissed the top of the penguin’s fuzzy head. “也希望你一切安好，”杰克说。 他亲了亲企鹅毛茸茸的头顶。

Jack and Annie then scrambled up the rope ladder into the tree house. Annie grabbed the Pennsylvania book, while Jack hurried to the window. 杰克和安妮赶紧爬着绳梯爬进树屋。 安妮抓起了宾夕法尼亚的书，而杰克则急忙跑到窗户前。

Sunlight poured into the tree house, making it blindingly bright. Trumpet sounds filled the fresh morning air. 阳光照进树屋，亮得睁不开眼。 号角声充满了清新的早晨空气。

“I wish we could go home!” Annie said, pointing to a picture of the Frog Creek woods. “我希望我们能回家！”安妮指着一张蛙溪镇森林的图片说。

“Farewell!” Teddy shouted from the dazzling light. “Thank you!” “再见！”泰迪从耀眼的光中喊道。 “谢谢！”

The wind began to blow. 风开始吹起来了。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转起来。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后，一切都安静下来了。

Absolutely still. 非常非常安静。

* * *

“We’re back,” said Jack. He and Annie looked out the tree house window. Dappled light danced in the woods. As always, no time at all had passed in Frog Creek while they were gone. “我们回来了，”杰克说。 他和安妮从树屋的窗户往外看。 斑驳的光线在树林里跳动。 就像往常一样，他们在蛙溪镇离开的时间里，一点时间都没有过去。

“Time for school,” said Annie. “该上学了，”安妮说。

Jack groaned. He was exhausted. Every muscle in his body ached. He opened his backpack and took out their guide book to Southwest China. “Hey, we forgot. Our breakfast sandwiches are in here,” he said. 杰克呻吟了一声。他累坏了。 他全身的肌肉都疼。 他打开背包，拿出他们的中国西南部指南书。 “嘿，我们忘了。” 我们的早餐三明治在这里，”他说。

“They must be cold by now,” said Annie. “他们现在一定很冷，”安妮说。

“Who cares?” said Jack. “I’m starving.” “谁在乎呢？”杰克说。 “我饿死了。”

“Me too,” said Annie. “我也是，”安妮说。

Jack gave Annie a cold egg sandwich. Then he unwrapped his own and took a big bite. “Yum!” he said. “It really tastes great.” 杰克给安妮一个冷鸡蛋三明治。 然后他拆开自己的，咬了一大口。 “真好吃！”，他说。 “味道真的很棒。”

“This is our panda bread,” said Annie, “made with love by Mom, one of our keepers.” “这是我们的熊猫面包，”安妮说，“是妈妈做的，她是我们的饲养员之一，而且充满了爱。”

Jack laughed. “Right.” 杰克笑了。 “对。”

“So the panda bread stands for protecting the pandas,” Annie said between bites. “I think it stands for protecting birds, too, like Penny.” “所以熊猫面包代表保护熊猫，”安妮边吃边说。 “我觉得它也代表保护鸟，比如彭妮。”

“Of course,” said Jack, his mouth full. “当然，”杰克说，嘴里还嚼着东西。

“And trees and other plants,” said Annie. “还有树木和其他植物，”安妮说。

Jack nodded as he chewed. 杰克一边嚼一边点头。

“And dolphins and whales and fish,” said Annie. “All the stuff in the ocean …” “还有海豚、鲸鱼和鱼，”安妮说。 “海洋里所有的东西……”

Jack nodded again. 杰克又点了点头。

Annie finished her sandwich and wiped her mouth. “In fact, there are millions of things to protect,” she said. 安妮吃完了三明治，擦了擦嘴巴。 “事实上，有很多很多东西需要保护，”她说。

“Whoa, slow down,” said Jack. He pulled on his backpack. “One panda at a time.” “哇，慢点说，”杰克说。 他背上背包。 “一次只救一只熊猫。”

“Right,” said Annie. “One penguin at a time.” “对，”安妮说。 “一次只救一只企鹅。”

“Right,” said Jack. “对，”杰克说。

The two of them climbed down the rope ladder. Then they hurried through the Frog Creek woods, finally on their way to school.





 他们两个人顺着绳梯爬了下来。 然后他们赶紧穿过蛙溪镇的树林，终于要出发去学校了。
