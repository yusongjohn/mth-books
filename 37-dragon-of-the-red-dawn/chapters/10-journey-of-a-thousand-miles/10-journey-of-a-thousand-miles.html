10. Journey of a Thousand Miles 10. 千里之行

Jack couldn’t speak. 杰克说不出话来。

“Our passports?” said Annie. “They—um— they got destroyed in a fire—on the other side of the Great Bridge.” “我们的护照？”安妮说。 “他们——呃——在大桥另一边的一场大火中被毁了。”

The samurai narrowed his eyes. “Your passports burned?.” he said. “Why were you on the other side of the Great Bridge?” 武士眯起了眼睛。 “你们的护照烧毁了？”，他说。 “你为什么在大桥的另一边？”

“We were with Master Basho,” said Jack. “我们和巴沙大师在一起，”杰克说。

“Master Basho?” said the samurai. “巴沙大师？”武士说。

“Yes,” said Annie. “We are students of his.” “是的，”安妮说。 “我们是他的学生。”

“Ah!” The samurai’s expression brightened. “So you study poetry with Master Basho?” “啊！”武士的表情亮了起来。 “所以你和芭蕉大师一起学习诗歌吗？”

“Yes,” said Annie. “Would you like to hear some poems?” “是的，”安妮说。 “你想听一些诗吗？”

Oh, no! thought Jack. Not again! 哦，不！杰克心想。 又来了！

“Yes, please,” said the samurai. “是的，请吧，”武士说。

“No problem.” Annie thought for a moment and then said: “Here’s a simple little poem.” She recited: “没问题。”安妮想了一会儿，然后说：“这里有一首简单的诗。”她背诵道：

Rain falls outside,

But the tiny cricket on the hearth

Is dry tonight. 外面下着雨，但壁炉上的小蟋蟀今晚是干的。

The samurai nodded. “Hmm,” he said. “Yes. Very simple, but very lovely.” 武士点了点头。 “嗯，”他说。 “是的。” “非常简单，但非常可爱。”

“Thank you,” said Annie. “谢谢你，”安妮说。

The man turned to Jack. Jack could hardly breathe. His mind was blank. He looked to Annie for help. But Annie just smiled, waiting to hear his poem. 那个男人转向杰克。 杰克几乎喘不过气来。 他的脑海一片空白。 他看向安妮寻求帮助。 但安妮只是微笑着，等着听他的诗。

Jack cleared his throat. He tried to stay calm. He closed his eyes and let his mind roam over their visit to Japan. He opened his eyes. He looked up at the cloudless sky and said: 杰克清了清嗓子。 他努力让自己保持冷静。 他闭上眼睛，让脑海回想起他们去日本的旅行。 他睁开眼睛。 他抬头看着没有云的天空，说：

The sun is shining,

The day is hot.

But moonlight

And cool breezes

Still fill my heart,

Left over from last night. 太阳照耀，天气很热。但月光和凉爽的微风，依然让我心里感到舒服，那是昨晚留下的感觉。

“Whoa!” whispered Annie. “Good!” “哇！”安妮小声说。 “好！

“Yes, very good!” said the samurai. He looked at the sky. “The moonlight, the cool breeze,” he mused, “left over from the night. Very good. Master Basho has taught you well!” “是的，非常好！”武士说。 他看着天空。 “月光，凉爽的微风，”他沉思着，“是昨晚留下的。非常好。 巴夏大师教得很好！

The samurai strolled away, shaking his head and murmuring to himself. He let out a happy laugh, as if delighted with the day. 武士悠闲地走开了，一边摇头，一边自言自语。 他开心地笑了，好像特别喜欢这一天。

Jack couldn’t believe it. They were free! “Hurry, let’s go,” he said. “Before someone else sees us!” 杰克简直不敢相信。他们自由了！ “快点，我们走吧，”他说。 “不然别人会看见我们！”，

Jack and Annie ran to the rope ladder and started up. As soon as they climbed inside the tree house, Jack grabbed the Pennsylvania book. He found a picture of the Frog Creek woods. “As the famous samurai once said, ‘A journey of a thousand miles begins with one step,’” said Jack. 杰克和安妮跑向绳梯，开始往上爬。 他们一爬进树屋，杰克就拿起了宾夕法尼亚的书。 他找到了一张蛙溪镇森林的图片。 “就像那位著名的武士曾经说过的，‘千里之行，始于足下，’”杰克说。

“Or one sentence,” said Annie. “或者说，始于一句话，”安妮说。

“Right,” said Jack. He pointed at the picture. “I wish we could go home.” “对，”杰克说。 他指着那幅画。 “真希望我们能回家。”

Annie gasped. “Wait a minute!” she said. “We forgot our mission!” 安妮惊叫起来。 “等一下！”她说。 “我们忘了我们的任务！”

“What?” said Jack. “什么？”杰克说。

But the wind started to blow. 但风开始吹起来了。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止不动。

The early-morning air was cold. 清晨的空气很冷。

Jack and Annie were back in Frog Creek, wearing their own clothes. Jack’s burlap bag was a backpack again. He looked inside to make sure the Wand of Dianthus was there. It was. And Basho’s frog poem was there, too. 杰克和安妮已经回到了蛙溪镇，穿着自己的衣服。 杰克的麻布袋又变成了背包。 他打开看了看，确认迪安图斯的魔杖还在里面。它在。 巴肖的青蛙诗也在里面。

“I can’t believe it!” said Annie. “We didn’t look for a secret of happiness for Merlin. How could we forget that?” “我简直不敢相信！”安妮说。 “我们本来是为梅林寻找幸福的秘密的。 我们怎么能忘记呢？”

“Oh, man,” said Jack. “We were so busy worrying about the samurai and putting out the fire that we forgot our mission.” “哦，天哪，”杰克说。 “我们太忙于担心武士和灭火，把任务给忘了。”

“What will Morgan say?” said Annie. “We’ve never forgotten a mission before! And this one was the most important we’ve ever had! To help save Merlin!” Annie was near tears. “摩根会说什么呢？”安妮说。 “我们以前从没忘记过任务！ 这次的任务是我们经历过最重要的！ 要帮助梅林！”安妮差点哭了出来。

“Wait, wait. Calm down,” said Jack. “Let’s think a minute. Maybe we actually found a secret of happiness. Maybe we just don’t know we found it.” “等等，等等。 冷静点，”杰克说。 “让我们想一分钟。 也许我们真的发现了一个幸福的秘密。 也许我们只是不知道自己已经发现了。”

“You mean, like—were we extra happy at any time?” asked Annie. “你是说，比如——我们什么时候特别开心过？”安妮问。

“Yeah, sort of,” said Jack. “Were we?” “是的，有点像那样，”杰克说。 “我们有吗？”

“I don’t know, were you?” said Annie. “我不知道，你呢？”安妮说。

“I think a few times …,” said Jack. “我想有几次……”杰克说。

“Like when?” said Annie. “比如什么时候？”安妮问。

“Like when we were crossing the Great Bridge with Basho, I felt sort of happy then,” said Jack. “比如当我们和巴肖一起走过大桥的时候，我当时感觉有点开心，”杰克说。

“Me too,” said Annie. “And I was pretty happy eating sushi.” “我也是，”安妮说。 “还有吃寿司的时候我也很开心。”

“Yeah, but I got scared when I saw the samurai watching me,” said Jack. “是的，但是当我看到武士在看着我的时候，我有点害怕，”杰克说。

“What about the sumo-wrestling match?” asked Annie. “那场相扑比赛呢？”安妮问。

“That was fun,” said Jack. “But I don’t know if it really made me happy.” “那很有趣，”杰克说。 “但我不知道它真的让我开心了没有。”

“Riding the dragon and putting out the fire?” said Annie. “骑龙灭火吗？”安妮问。

“That was great,” said Jack. “But I was too worried about saving the city to be happy.” “那很棒，”杰克说。 “但我太担心拯救城市了，没时间感到开心。”

“Making up poems for the samurai?” said Annie. “为武士写诗吗？”安妮问。

“Too nervous,” said Jack. “太紧张了，”杰克说。

“Well, when were you purely happy?” said Annie. “那么，你什么时候是纯粹开心的呢？”安妮问。

“I think it was when …” Jack stopped. He felt silly saying what he was about to say. “我想是在……”杰克停了下来。 他说出接下来要说的话时，觉得自己有点傻傻的。

“Go ahead,” said Annie. “说吧，”安妮说。

“I think it was when I was lying on the mat in Basho’s house,” said Jack, “and I touched that little patch of moonlight on the floor and I listened to the banana leaves in the wind.” “我想是在我躺在巴肖家的垫子上时，”杰克说，“那时我碰到了地板上的一小片月光，还听了风吹过香蕉叶的声音。”

“Oh, yes!” said Annie. “Before we fell asleep, and I heard the cricket and I felt like I was a cricket myself going to sleep in a cozy place.” “哦，是的！”安妮说。 “在我们睡着之前，我听见了蟋蟀的声音，我感觉自己就像那只蟋蟀一样，正躺在一个温暖的地方睡觉。”

“Yeah, like that,” said Jack. “对，就是那样，”杰克说。

“It’s like what Basho said about finding beauty in the small things of nature,” said Annie, “like his poem about that frog splashing in the water.” “这就像巴肖说的，要从大自然的小事中发现美，”安妮说，“比如他写的那首关于青蛙跳进水里的诗。”

“I think that’s it!” said Jack. “A secret of happiness is paying really close attention to the small things in nature.” “我想就是这个！”杰克说。 “幸福的秘密就是非常仔细地注意大自然中的小事。”

“Wow,” said Annie. “I think that’s a great secret to share with Merlin.” “哇，”安妮说。 “我觉得这是个可以和梅林分享的好秘密。”

“It is,” said Jack, “and Basho’s poem will help Merlin understand.” “是的，”杰克说，“巴肖的诗会帮助梅林理解。”

“Right,” said Annie. “对，”安妮说。

“Let’s go,” said Jack. “我们走吧，”杰克说。

Annie climbed down the rope ladder. Jack put his pack on his back and followed her. 安妮爬下绳梯。 杰克把背包背在背上，跟着她。

As Jack and Annie started through the chilly woods together, Jack noticed things he hadn’t seen before. He saw tiny blue wild-flowers sprouting up from the winter-weary ground. 当杰克和安妮一起穿过寒冷的树林时，杰克注意到了一些以前没看到的东西。 他看到 tiny 蓝色的野花从冬天疲惫的土地上冒出来。

He saw fresh anthills in the dirt. 他看到泥土里有新鲜的蚂蚁山。

He saw leaf buds on twigs and green moss on a rock, bright in the March sunlight. 他看到枝条上有嫩叶芽，岩石上有绿色的苔藓，在三月的阳光下闪闪发亮。

“I feel like I’m seeing spring for the first time,” said Jack. “我觉得我好像第一次看到春天，”杰克说。

“Me too,” said Annie. “我也是，”安妮说。

“Not just for the first time this year,” said Jack. “But for the first time in my whole life.” “不只是今年第一次，”杰克说。 “而是我这辈子第一次。”

“Me too,” said Annie. “我也是，”安妮说。

Jack felt happy, really happy, as he and Annie headed for home in the sparkling morning light. 杰克感到非常开心，非常非常开心，他和安妮在清晨闪亮的阳光下朝家走去。
