9. Flowers of Edo 9. 江户的花朵

“We did it!” said Annie. “We made our own magic!” “我们成功了！”安妮说道。 “我们创造了属于自己的魔法！”，

“Yeah, we got the Cloud Dragon to put out the fire,” breathed Jack. He felt dazed. “Do you think we’re near Basho’s house?” “是啊，我们让云龙灭火了，”杰克喘着气说。他感觉晕晕的。 “你觉得我们离芭蕉的家近吗？

“I think so,” said Annie. “We asked the Cloud Dragon to take us there, so I bet she did. Come on, let’s find Basho.” “我想是的，”安妮说。 “我们让云龙带我们去那里，所以我猜她做到了。 来吧，我们去找芭蕉。

Jack and Annie stood up and started walking along the edge of the river. They walked barefoot through the mud, under dripping trees. 杰克和安妮站起来，沿着河边开始走。 他们光着脚踩着泥巴，走在滴着水的树下。

“Hey, there’s the clearing,” said Annie. “嘿，那是空地，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie headed toward the clearing beyond the trees and tall grass. 杰克和安妮朝树后和高草丛那边的空地走去。

“Oh, no!” cried Annie. “ Look!” “哦，不！”安妮喊道。

In the clearing, rain was falling on the charred ruins of Basho’s house. The shingled roof and the bamboo walls of the little hut had burned and collapsed. 在空地上，雨落在巴肖房子的焦黑废墟上。 小屋的瓦片屋顶和竹子墙壁已经烧毁并倒塌了。

“Where’s Basho?” said Jack, scared. “巴肖在哪里？”杰克害怕地问。

“There!” said Annie. “在那里！”安妮说。

The famous poet sat on a log next to his banana tree in the gray rain. His clothes were blackened. His face was covered with soot. He clutched his writing box. 这位著名的诗人坐在他香蕉树旁边的木头上，灰蒙蒙的雨下着。 他的衣服被烧黑了。 他的脸上满是煤灰。 他紧紧抱着他的写字盒。

“Basho!” yelled Annie. “巴肖！”安妮喊道。

Basho looked up. A smile crossed his dirty, weathered face. “I looked for you by the river, but didn’t see you!” he said. “I am glad you are safe.” 巴肖抬起头。 一个微笑浮现在他脏脏的、布满皱纹的脸上。 “我沿着河找了你，但没看到你！”他说。 “我很高兴你平安无事。”

“We’re glad you’re safe, too,” said Jack. “我们也很高兴你平安无事，”杰克说。

“But your castle! Your castle burned down,” said Annie. “但是你的城堡！ 你的城堡烧毁了，”安妮说。

“Yes. It burned before the miracle of the rain,” said Basho, sighing. “是的。” “在奇迹般的雨降临之前，这里曾经被烧毁过，”巴肖叹了口气说。

Jack and Annie sat on the log next to him. Through the smoky drizzle, they all stared at the rubble. Trees and plants dripped with rain. A pigeon cooed. 杰克和安妮坐在他旁边的木头上。 在烟雾弥漫的细雨中，他们全都盯着废墟看。 树木和植物上滴着雨水。一只鸽子咕咕叫着。

For a long moment, no one spoke. Then Annie broke the silence. “I’m glad you still have your banana tree,” she said. “I like the sound of the rain falling on the leaves.” 很长一段时间，没有人说话。 然后，安妮打破了沉默。 “我很高兴你还有你的香蕉树，”她说。 “我喜欢雨水落在树叶上的声音。”

Basho looked up, but didn’t say anything. 巴肖抬头看了看，但什么也没说。

“Yeah, and I like the sound of the river,” said Jack. “It’s louder now since the rain came.” “是的，我喜欢河水的声音，”杰克说。 “自从下雨以来，声音变得更大了。”

Basho tilted his head as if listening to the rain on the leaves of the banana plant and to the steady rushing sounds of the river. His face softened. “Yes, I like those things, too,” he said. He held up his wooden box. “And I still have my poems.” 巴肖歪着头，好像在听着雨滴落在香蕉叶上，还有河水持续流淌的声音。 他的表情变得柔和了。 “是的，我也喜欢这些事情，”他说。 他举起他的木盒子。 “我仍然保留着我的诗。”

“Don’t worry, Basho,” said Annie. “Everything can be built again.” “别担心，巴肖，”安妮说。 “一切都可以重新建造。”

“And your castle will be even more beautiful than before,” said Jack. “而且你的城堡会比以前更加美丽，”杰克说。

Basho smiled. “I suppose that is why the ancients called our fires ‘the flowers of Edo,’” he said. 巴肖笑了。 “我想这就是为什么古人称我们的火为‘江户之花’，”他说。

“What do you mean?” asked Jack. “你是说什么意思？”杰克问。

“After something is destroyed by fire, a good new thing often takes its place,” said Basho. “Just as after the bleakest winter, beautiful flowers return with the spring.” “当某样东西被火烧毁后，常常会有美好的新事物取而代之，”巴肖说。 “就像最寒冷的冬天过后，春天会带来美丽的花朵一样。”

“I’m sure you’ll make many beautiful flowers,” said Annie. “我确信你会创造出许多美丽的花朵，”安妮说。

“Thank you,” said Basho. “I am sorry, though, that you and Jack will not have a place to stay now.” “谢谢你，”巴肖说。 “不过，我很抱歉你和杰克现在没有地方可以住。”

“Don’t worry,” said Annie. “We have to travel back to our own house.” “别担心，”安妮说。 “我们必须回到我们自己的家。”

“How far away is that?” asked Basho. “那有多远？”巴肖问。

“Very far,” said Annie. She and Jack stood up. “But we just need to get back to the Imperial Garden. We’ll know the way from there.” “很远，”安妮说。 她和杰克站了起来。 “但我们只需要回到御花园。 从那里我们就能知道路了。”

“Good,” said Basho. He stood up. “Come, I will accompany you back to the garden.” “好，”巴肖说。他站了起来。 “来吧，我陪你回花园。”

“Thanks, that would be great!” said Jack. “谢谢，那太好了！”杰克说。

Basho picked up his walking stick. Then he led Jack and Annie along the bank of the river. Through the light drizzle, they saw a ferryboat moving upstream. Basho waved, and the pilot steered the boat to shore. 巴肖捡起了他的拐杖。 然后他带领杰克和安妮沿着河岸走。 透过细雨，他们看到一艘渡船正在向上游行驶。 巴肖挥手，船夫把船划到了岸边。

Jack and Annie followed Basho onto the ferry. The three of them sat together on a wooden bench. The other passengers stared at them. Many had ashes on their clothes and soot on their faces. Jack was relieved to see that no samurai were on board. 杰克和安妮跟着巴肖上了渡船。 他们三个人一起坐在一条木椅上。 其他乘客盯着他们看。 很多人衣服上沾着灰烬，脸上满是煤灰。 杰克松了口气，发现船上没有武士。

“Greetings, Master Basho,” the pilot said. The other passengers nodded respectfully to Basho. They smiled as if the presence of the great poet gave them hope. “您好，巴肖大师，”船夫说道。 其他乘客向巴肖恭敬地点头。 他们好像因为这位大诗人的存在而感到有了希望，脸上露出了微笑。

“The rains were a miracle, were they not, Master Basho?” an old woman said. “这场雨真是个奇迹，不是吗，巴沙大师？”一位老妇人说道。

“Indeed they were,” said Basho. “确实是这样，”巴沙说道。

“I guess the Cloud Dragon showed up just in time,” said Annie. “我想云龙刚好及时出现了，”安妮说道。

“Annie,” whispered Jack. “安妮，”杰克小声说。

Basho smiled at her. “I am afraid no one believes in the Cloud Dragon anymore, Annie,” he said. “But it is lovely to pretend, is it not?” 巴沙对她笑了笑。 “恐怕现在没人再相信云龙了，安妮，”他说。 “但假装相信不是很美好吗？”

“Yes, it is lovely,” said Annie. “是的，假装是很美好的，”安妮说道。

The rain stopped as the ferryboat traveled on up the river. Mist rose from the water, and birds began to sing. 渡船继续沿着河向上行驶时，雨停了。 水面上升起薄雾，鸟儿开始唱歌。

When they passed the teahouses, Jack and Annie saw firefighters cleaning up after the fire, sweeping up broken tiles and scrubbing the walkways. Waiters were bringing them tea. 当他们经过茶馆时，杰克和安妮看到消防员们正在清理火灾后的现场，扫着碎瓦片，还擦洗着小路。 服务员们给他们端来茶。

The sun was shining by the time they passed the puppet stage and the charred lumberyard. Wisps of smoke still rose from the black piles of burned logs. 等他们经过木偶戏台和烧焦的木材厂时，太阳已经出来了。 烧焦的木头堆上还有缕缕烟雾升起。

The ferryboat floated on through the bright morning. It glided under the Great Bridge and past the crowded fish market. Fishermen were hauling in their catch from the night before. 渡船在明亮的早晨继续向前漂浮。 它滑过了大桥，经过了拥挤的鱼市。 渔民们正把昨晚捕到的鱼拉上来。

By the time they moved past the samurai castles and came near the moat of the Imperial Garden, the sun had completely dried out Jack’s and Annie’s kimonos. 等他们经过武士城堡，靠近皇宫花园的护城河时，太阳已经把杰克和安妮的和服完全晒干了。

The boat pulled up to the landing. Basho helped Jack and Annie onto the dock. He waved good-bye to the boat passengers. 船靠岸了。 巴肖帮杰克和安妮上了码头。 他向船上的乘客挥手告别。

Jack, Annie, and Basho walked over the stone bridge that crossed the moat. They walked through the massive gate in the high wall. Then they followed the paths of the Imperial Garden, around the big rocks and the pond with the swans. 杰克、安妮和巴肖走过横跨护城河的石桥。 他们穿过高墙上的巨大铁门。 然后他们沿着御花园的小路走，绕过大石头和有天鹅的池塘。

Jack kept an eye out for horses and samurai warriors. But the garden was as peaceful as when they had first landed. It was filled with birdsong. The willows swayed. Water from the waterfall tumbled into the green pool. Jack caught sight of the sun shining on the tree house high in the cherry tree. 杰克一直在留意马和武士。 但花园和他们刚到达时一样平静。 到处都是鸟叫声。 柳树在轻轻摇摆。 瀑布的水从高处落入绿色的池塘里。 杰克看见阳光照在樱桃树上高高的树屋里。

Jack stopped walking. “We know our way home from here,” he said to Basho. 杰克停下了脚步。 “从这里我们知道回家的路，”他对巴肖说。

“Are you certain?” asked Basho. He did not seem to notice the tree house in the cherry tree. “你确定吗？”巴肖问。 他似乎没有注意到樱桃树里的树屋。

“We’re sure,” said Annie. “Once we start on our way, the trip will be easy.” “我们很确定，”安妮说。 “一旦我们开始出发，这段旅程就会变得很容易。”

Basho nodded. “You remind me of the famous saying of the samurai Musashi,” he said. “‘ A journey of a thousand miles begins with one step.’” 巴肖点了点头。 “你让我想起了武士宫本武藏的一句著名的话，”他说。 “‘千里之行，始于足下。’”

“I’ve heard that saying before,” said Jack. “我以前听过这句话，”杰克说。

“Words can outlive their creators,” said Basho. “Though I will never be so lucky as to have mine outlive me.” “词语可以比创造它们的人活得更久，”巴修说。 “不过我这辈子恐怕没那么幸运了。”

“Don’t be too sure of that,” said Annie. “别太确定，”安妮说。

Basho gave them a little smile. “I hope you both will return to Edo someday,” he said. “Look for me when you come back. I should have a lovely new castle on the river by then.” 巴修给他们笑了笑。 “希望你们俩有一天能回到江户，”他说。 “等你们回来时，来找我吧。 到那时，我应该已经在河边建好一座漂亮的新城堡了。”

“Thanks,” said Jack. “谢谢，”杰克说。

“Good-bye,” said Annie. “再见，”安妮说。

They both bowed to Basho. 他们俩向巴修鞠了一躬。

Basho bowed to them. Then the great poet turned and left them. Falling cherry blossoms floated on the wind as he walked away. 巴修向他们鞠了一躬。 然后这位伟大的诗人转身离开了他们。 他走远的时候，飘落的樱花在风中飞舞。

Jack and Annie watched Basho until they could no longer see him. Then they turned to go. Just as they started walking, a man stepped out from the shadows of the garden. The man wore a blue coat and two swords hung from his belt. 杰克和安妮一直看着巴修，直到再也看不见他。 然后他们转身准备离开。 就在他们开始走路时，一个男人从花园的阴影中走了出来。 这个男人穿着一件蓝色的外套，腰间挂着两把剑。

“Excuse me,” said the samurai. “May I see your passports, please?” “对不起，”武士说道。 “请出示你们的护照好吗？”
