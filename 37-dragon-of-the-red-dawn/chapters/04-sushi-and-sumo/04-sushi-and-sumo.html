4. Sushi and Sumo 4. 寿司和相扑

B asho led Jack and Annie off the Great Bridge and down a crowded road. They passed huge stacks of lumber. Then they came to a row of stages built along the riverbank. On one stage women were dancing. Their faces were painted white. They wore shimmering kimonos and waved fans. 巴什奥带着杰克和安妮离开了大桥，沿着一条热闹的路走下去。 他们经过了一堆堆巨大的木材。 接着，他们来到河岸边一排沿着河边搭建的舞台。 在一个舞台上，一些女人正在跳舞。 他们的脸被涂成了白色。 他们穿着闪闪发光的和服，挥舞着扇子。

Musicians played on a second stage. They plucked three-stringed instruments and blew on bamboo flutes. Their music was high-pitched and strange, but Jack liked it. 音乐家们在第二个舞台上表演。 他们弹奏着三弦乐器，吹着竹笛。 他们的音乐听起来又尖又奇怪，但杰克喜欢。

On another stage was a puppet show. Puppeteers wearing black clothes moved a giant dragon puppet around the stage. A man stood to the side and told a story to the audience. From the back of the crowd, it was hard to hear him. 另一个舞台上正在演木偶戏。 穿着黑衣服的木偶师在舞台上操控着一只巨大的龙木偶。 一个男人站在一旁，给观众讲故事。 从人群后面，很难听清他说的话。

“What’s he saying?” said Annie. “他在说什么？”安妮说。

“He is telling the legend of the Cloud Dragon,” said Basho. “The Cloud Dragon is one of the guardian animals of the four directions. She has the power of flight and commands the rain clouds.” “他在讲述云龙的传说，”巴肖说。 “云龙是四方守护动物之一。 她拥有飞行的力量，能指挥雨云。”

“Cool,” said Annie. “真酷，”安妮说。

Basho led them on past rows of stalls where craftspeople sold beads, cloth, kites, and paper lanterns. Some boys were holding up yo-yos for sale. Jack was surprised to see yo-yos in old Japan. 巴肖带他们穿过一排摊位，手工艺人正在售卖珠子、布料、风筝和纸灯笼。 一些男孩正在兜售溜溜球。 杰克惊讶地看到古代日本也有溜溜球。

Beyond the craft stalls was a row of inns and cafés. The smell of spices and grilled fish filled the air. 在手工艺摊位的另一边，是一排旅馆和咖啡馆。 空气中弥漫着香料和烤鱼的香味。

“Yum,” said Annie. “真香，”安妮说。

Jack was hungry, too. 杰克也饿了。

“Would you like to stop at a teahouse?” Basho asked them. “你们想停在一家茶馆吗？”巴肖问他们。

“Yes!” Jack and Annie said together. “好啊！”杰克和安妮异口同声地说。

Basho led them toward a small building with an open front. At the entrance, Basho slipped off his sandals. Jack and Annie did the same. They placed their sandals in a row of shoes that other people had left by the door. 巴肖带他们朝一座前面敞开的小房子走去。 到了入口，巴肖脱下了他的拖鞋。 杰克和安妮也照做了。 他们把拖鞋放在门口一排其他人留下的鞋子旁边。

Inside the teahouse, cooks stirred steaming pots over a wood-burning stove. People sat at long low tables, eating with chopsticks and drinking from small cups. Several customers smiled shyly and bowed before Basho. 在茶馆里，厨师们在木柴炉上搅拌着冒着热气的锅。 人们坐在长长的矮桌旁，用筷子吃饭，从小杯子里喝水。 几位顾客害羞地朝巴肖微笑并鞠躬。

Basho must be a really famous teacher of the samurai, thought Jack. It made Jack feel important to be with him. 杰克心想，巴修一定是个非常著名的武士老师。 和他在一起让杰克感觉很重要。

Basho led them to a table and sat cross-legged on a straw mat. Jack and Annie did the same. A waiter with a kerchief around his head hurried to the table. “Welcome to our humble teahouse, Master Basho!” he said. 巴修带他们来到一张桌子前，然后盘腿坐在草席上。 杰克和安妮也做了同样的动作。 一个头缠头巾的服务员急忙走到桌边。 “欢迎光临我们简陋的茶馆，巴修大师！”他说。

“Thank you,” said Basho. “谢谢，”巴修说。

Everyone is so polite in Japan! Jack thought. 日本的人真是太有礼貌了！杰克想。

The waiter handed Jack, Annie, and Basho warm wet towels. “Thank you,” said Jack and Annie. 服务员给杰克、安妮和巴修递上了温热的湿毛巾。 “谢谢，”杰克和安妮说。

Jack watched Basho wipe his hands on the towel. He and Annie did the same. Then they all gave their towels back to the waiter. 杰克看着巴肖用毛巾擦手。 他和安妮也做了同样的事。 然后他们把毛巾还给了服务员。

“I would like plates of sushi for me and my two students, please,” said Basho. “请给我和我的两个学生来一份寿司，谢谢，”巴肖说。

“Thank you,” said the waiter, bowing. “谢谢，”服务员鞠了一躬说。

While they waited for their food, Jack studied the people in the room. Jack noticed that even little kids were eating with chopsticks. He and Annie had never had much luck using chopsticks in Asian restaurants back home. 他们在等食物的时候，杰克观察了房间里的人。 杰克注意到，就连小孩子们也用筷子吃饭。 他和安妮以前在家里的亚洲餐厅里用筷子时，运气都不太好。

Soon the waiter brought over three plates of small cakes of sticky rice wrapped in dark green, paper-like strips. He also brought napkins and three pairs of chopsticks. 不久，服务员端来了三盘用深绿色纸一样的条状物包裹的小米糕。 他还带来了纸巾和三双筷子。

When the waiter had gone, Basho spoke to Jack and Annie in a soft voice so no one else could hear. “We call this sushi,” he said. “It is rice with pieces of raw fish in the middle.” 等服务员走后，巴肖用轻柔的声音对杰克和安妮说话，这样其他人就听不到了。 “我们叫这个寿司，”他说。 “这是米饭，中间夹着生鱼片。”

“Raw fish?” said Jack. He gulped. “生鱼片？”杰克说。他咽了口口水。

“And what’s this part?” Annie pointed at the papery wrapping. “那这个部分呢？”安妮指着那层纸一样的包装。

“Seaweed,” said Basho. “海藻，”巴肖说。

“Seaweed?” said Jack. “海藻？”杰克说。

“It is very good,” said Basho. “这非常美味，”巴肖说。

Jack was so hungry he was willing to try anything, even raw fish and seaweed. His only problem was the chopsticks. 杰克饿得什么都愿意尝试，甚至生鱼和海藻。 他唯一的问题是筷子。

“Try it this way, Jack,” said Annie. She carefully picked up a piece of sushi between the wooden ends of her chopsticks. Jack copied her. But as they both tried to bring their food to their mouths, their pieces of sushi fell onto the table. “试试这个方法，杰克，”安妮说。 她小心地用筷子夹起一块寿司。杰克模仿她。 但当他们试图把食物送到嘴边时，寿司却掉在了桌子上。

Jack and Annie laughed and tried a second time. Annie was successful. But Jack dropped his sushi again. Without thinking, he grabbed it with his fingers and popped it into his mouth. 杰克和安妮笑了，又试了一次。 安妮成功了。 但杰克又把寿司掉了。 他没多想，直接用手抓起寿司塞进嘴里。

“Mmm!” said Jack. The vinegar-tasting rice, the raw fish, and the green, salty seaweed were delicious! “嗯！”杰克说。 醋味的米饭、生鱼和又绿又咸的海藻真好吃！

But Jack stopped in mid-chew. Two samurai at another table were glaring at him. One of the men had a big scar on his face. The other had fierce dark eyes. 但杰克在咀嚼到一半时停了下来。 另一张桌子旁的两名武士正瞪着他。 其中一个人脸上有一道大伤疤。 另一个人有一双凶狠的黑眼睛。

Jack’s throat was dry as he swallowed. They saw me mess up with my chopsticks! he thought. They can tell I’m not from Japan! He picked up another piece of sushi with his chopsticks. He glanced again at the samurai. They were watching him like hawks. 杰克咽下口水时，感觉喉咙干得像沙漠。 他们看到我用筷子弄翻了饭！他心想。 他们能看出来我不是日本人！ 他用筷子又夹起了一块寿司。 他又看了一眼武士。 他们像老鹰一样盯着他。

Jack’s hand felt shaky. He tried to stay calm. He remembered a passage from their research book: Samurai did not show their feelings. They had great powers of concentration. 杰克的手感觉有点发抖。 他努力让自己保持冷静。 他想起了他们研究书里的一段话：武士不会表露自己的情绪。 他们有很强的专注力。

Jack tried very hard not to show his fear. He concentrated on holding the sushi with his chopsticks. He raised the sushi to his mouth and ate it calmly. He lowered his chopsticks and picked up another piece of sushi. He ate it calmly. 杰克非常努力地不表现出自己的害怕。 他专注地用筷子夹起寿司。 他把寿司举到嘴边，平静地吃掉了。 他放下筷子，拿起另一块寿司，平静地吃掉了。

When Jack looked back at the samurai, they were no longer watching him. Jack let out his breath. He picked up his last piece of sushi with his chopsticks and ate it calmly. 当杰克再次看向武士时，他们不再看他了。 杰克长长地吐出一口气。 他用筷子夹起最后一块寿司，平静地吃掉了。

“Very good,” said Basho, smiling at him. “很好，”巴肖微笑着对他说。

“Thanks,” said Jack. “谢谢，”杰克说。

“Let us go now,” said Basho. “我们现在出发吧，”巴肖说。

Basho folded his napkin neatly and left it beside his plate. Jack and Annie did the same. Basho paid for their meals, and then they stopped at the entranceway to put on their shoes. As they stepped outside, a drum began beating loudly. A huge crowd had gathered on the grassy riverbank. 巴肖把餐巾叠得整整齐齐，放在盘子旁边。 杰克和安妮也这样做。 巴肖付了他们的餐费，然后他们在入口处停下，穿上鞋子。 当他们走出去时，一阵鼓声大声地响起来。 一大群人已经聚集在河岸的草地上。

“What’s happening?” Annie asked. “发生什么事了？”安妮问。

“Come, I will show you,” said Basho. “来吧，我给你看，”巴肖说。

People moved aside so Basho could lead Jack and Annie to the front of the crowd. 人们让开路，好让巴肖带杰克和安妮走到人群前面。

A large ring had been marked off on the ground with straw. In the middle of the circle, two enormously fat men squatted at opposite sides of the ring. Each man looked as if he weighed more than four hundred pounds. They clapped their hands. Then each began stomping his feet. 地上用稻草标出一个大圆圈。 圆圈中间，有两个非常胖的男子坐在圆圈两边。 每个人看起来都重达四百磅以上。 他们鼓起掌来。 然后每个人都开始跺脚。

“Who are they?” said Annie, her eyes wide. “他们是谁？”安妮问道，眼睛睁得大大的。

“Sumo wrestlers,” said Basho. “Sumo wrestling has been our most popular sport for over a thousand years.” “相扑选手，”巴肖说。 “相扑是我们最受欢迎的运动，已经有超过一千年了。”

The two wrestlers were now perfectly still. Crouching down with clenched fists, they were glaring at one another. The whole crowd seemed to hold its breath as the wrestlers stared into each other’s eyes. Suddenly one wrestler lunged forward and grabbed the other. Then the hugely fat men began pushing each other around. 两位相扑选手现在完全静止不动了。 他们蹲下身子，紧握着拳头，互相瞪着眼睛。 所有人似乎都屏住了呼吸，看着这两个摔跤手互相盯着对方的眼睛。 突然，一个摔跤手向前冲去，抓住了另一个人。 然后，这两个又高又胖的男子开始互相推搡。

“They are trying to force each other out of the ring,” said Basho. “他们正在试图把对方推出圈外，”巴肖说。

Grunting and groaning, the two men moved backward and forward as spectators cheered wildly. Then one wrestler made a quick move and shoved his rival out of the circle. The crowd roared. Jack found himself cheering, too. 两个男人一边发出咕哝声，一边前后移动，观众们大声欢呼。 接着，一个摔跤手迅速行动，把对手推出了圈外。人群大声欢呼起来。 杰克发现自己也忍不住在欢呼。

When the noise died down, Basho turned to Jack and Annie. “The first match is over,” he said. “Shall we go now?” 当嘈杂声渐渐平息后，巴肖转向杰克和安妮。 “第一场比赛结束了，”他说。 “我们现在出发吗？”

Before Jack and Annie could answer him, two samurai stepped in front of them. One had a big scar on his face. The other had fierce dark eyes. 杰克和安妮还没来得及回答，两名武士突然挡在他们面前。 其中一个人脸上有一道大伤疤。 另一个人有一双凶狠的黑眼睛。

“Excuse me,” the scar-faced man said. “May we see your passports, please?” “打扰一下，”那张脸有伤疤的男人说。 “请出示你们的护照好吗？”
