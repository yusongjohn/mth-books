2. The Imperial Garden 2. 帝国花园

Jack opened his eyes. Soft morning light shone across the floor of the tree house. Pink flowers bloomed on a branch outside the window. 杰克睁开了眼睛。 柔和的晨光洒在树屋的地板上。 窗外的树枝上开满了粉红色的花。

Jack and Annie were wearing brown baggy pants and brown silk robes with blue sashes. On their feet were stiff white socks and straw sandals. Jack’s backpack had turned into a burlap bag. 杰克和安妮穿着棕色的宽松裤子，还有棕色的丝绸长袍，腰间系着蓝色的腰带。 他们的脚上穿着硬硬的白色袜子和草编凉鞋。 杰克的背包变成了一个麻布袋。

“Are we wearing bathrobes?” asked Jack. “我们是不是穿着浴袍？”杰克问。

“I think they’re called kimonos,” said Annie. “我想这叫和服，”安妮说。

“Oh, right,” said Jack. “Where’d we land exactly?” Jack and Annie looked out the window. “哦，对，”杰克说。 “我们到底降落在哪里了？”杰克和安妮看向窗外。

Below the tree house was a beautiful garden filled with cherry trees and long-leafed willows. A waterfall tumbled into a sparkling green pool. 树屋下面是一个美丽的花园，种满了樱桃树和长叶子的柳树。 一道瀑布从山上倾泻而下，落入了闪闪发光的绿色水池里。

“Wow,” said Annie. “哇，”安妮说。

Jack opened the Japan book and found a painting that looked like the garden. He read aloud to Annie: 杰克打开日本的书，发现了一幅看起来就像那个花园的画。 他给安妮朗读：

In the 1600s, the Imperial Garden surrounded the Imperial Palace in the capital city of Japan. The city was called Edo (say EE-doh). In the mid-1800s, its name was changed to Tokyo (say TOH-kee-oh). 在1600年代，日本首都的御花园环绕着皇宫。这座城市叫江户（发音为EE-doh）。到了19世纪中期，它的名字改成了东京（发音为TOH-kee-oh）。

“Tokyo?” said Annie. “I’ve always wanted to go to Tokyo!” “东京？”安妮说。 “我一直想去东京！”，

“Me too,” said Jack. He read on: “我也是，”杰克说。他继续读下去：

The late 1600s in Japan were years of peace and prosperity. Art and culture thrived. But it was a time when the country was completely closed to the outside world. No one was allowed to come in. The citizens of Edo were frequently checked to make sure they had passports. 17世纪末的日本是和平繁荣的年代。艺术和文化蓬勃发展。但那也是日本完全封闭的年代，不允许任何人进入。江户的市民经常被检查，确保他们有护照。

“What’s a passport exactly?” said Annie. “护照到底是什么？”安妮问。

“It’s an official booklet that says who you are,” said Jack. “It also lists the different countries you traveled to.” He read more: “那是本官方的小册子，上面写着你是谁，”杰克说。 “它还会列出你去过哪些不同的国家。”他继续读：

Anyone who did not have a passport was considered a spy and punished severely. 没有护照的人会被视为间谍，并受到严厉的惩罚。

“Uh-oh,” said Annie. “We don’t have passports.” “糟了，”安妮说。 “我们没有护照。”

“Yeah, that’s a problem,” said Jack. “是啊，这确实是个问题，”杰克说。

“Hey! What if we use the Wand of Dianthus to make passports?” said Annie. “嘿！ “如果我们用迪安瑟斯的魔杖制作护照呢？”安妮说。

“Good idea!” said Jack. He peeked inside his bag. Good, the Wand of Dianthus was there. “好主意！”杰克说。 他往袋子里看了看。 很好，迪安瑟斯的魔杖在那儿。

“Wait, wait,” said Annie. “We can’t. Remember the rules. We can only use the magic wand for the good of others.” “等等，等等，”安妮说。 “我们不能。 记住规则。 “我们只能为了别人的好处使用魔杖。”

“Oh, right,” said Jack. “哦，对啊，”杰克说。

“And we have to try our hardest before we use the wand,” said Annie. “我们必须尽最大努力，才能使用魔杖，”安妮说。

“We haven’t tried anything yet,” said Jack. “我们还没尝试过任何事情呢，”杰克说。

“I guess we should just start looking for a secret of happiness and hope no one catches us,” said Annie. “我想我们应该开始寻找幸福的秘密，希望没人发现我们，”安妮说。

“Shh,” said Jack, “listen.” “嘘，”杰克说，“听。”

A bell was ringing in the distance. The ringing grew louder. Then came the sound of horses. Jack and Annie crouched down. They raised their heads just high enough to peek out the window. Through the flowery tree branches, they saw a small procession coming through the garden. 远处传来一阵钟声。 钟声越来越响了。 然后传来了马匹的声音。 杰克和安妮蹲了下来。 他们把头稍微抬高一点，从窗户往外看。 透过开满鲜花的树枝，他们看到一小队人正穿过花园走来。

The man leading the procession was ringing a bell. Two men walked behind him, holding up banners. Behind them, four men rode slowly on horseback. They all wore baggy trousers and puffy shirts. Their heads were shaved, except for knots of black hair. Each had two swords—a long one and a short one—hanging from his belt. 带领这支队伍的男人正在摇着铃铛。 他后面跟着两个人，举着横幅。 他们后面，四个人骑着马慢慢地走着。 他们都穿着宽松的裤子和蓬松的衬衫。 他们的头都剃光了，除了头上有一团黑头发。 每个人腰间都挂着两把剑——一把长的，一把短的。

At the very end of the procession rode a man in a billowing purple robe and a small purple hat. Red tassels hung from the bridle of his large black horse. 队伍的最后，骑着一匹大黑马的是一位身穿飘动的紫色长袍、戴着小紫色帽子的男人。 他那匹大黑马的缰绳上挂着红色的流苏。

Jack looked at their research book again. He found a picture that looked like the man on the black horse. He read the caption to himself: 杰克又看了看他们的研究书。 他找到了一张看起来像骑着黑马的男人的图片。 他小声地给自己读着字幕：

In the 1600s, the military ruler known as the shogun (say SHOW-gun) lived in the center of the Imperial Garden in a palace that had hundreds of rooms. 在1600年代，人们称军事统治者为将军（发音为SHOW-gun），他住在皇宫里，皇宫位于帝国花园的中心，有数百个房间。

“That last guy is a shogun,” Jack whispered to Annie. “He lives in a big palace in the garden.” He kept reading: “最后一个男人是个将军，”杰克对安妮小声说。 “他住在花园里的一座大宫殿里。”他继续读着：

Often the shogun’s warriors traveled with him. They were called samurai (say SAM-uh-rye). 幕府将军的武士们经常和他一起旅行。他们被称为武士（发音为SAM-uh-rye）。

“Oh, man,” whispered Jack. “Those other guys are samurai!” He and Annie had barely escaped an armored samurai on their earlier trip to Japan. “天啊，”杰克小声说。 “那些其他的人是武士！”他和安妮之前去日本的时候，差点被一个穿着盔甲的武士抓住。

Samurai were excellent horsemen well trained in the arts of fighting. The code of the samurai was strict. Samurai did not show their feelings. They had great powers of concentration. 武士是优秀的骑手，擅长战斗艺术。武士的规矩很严格。武士不会表露自己的感情，他们有很强的专注力。

“They’re gone,” said Annie. “他们走了，”安妮说。

Jack looked out the window. The shogun and his samurai warriors had disappeared down a tree-shaded dirt road. 杰克望向窗外。 将军和他的武士们已经沿着一条被树荫遮住的土路消失了。

“We should get out of the Imperial Garden fast,” said Jack. “If we stay here, we’re just asking to be caught.” “我们应该赶快离开皇室花园，”杰克说。 “如果我们待在这里，就等于主动被抓。”

“How do we get out?” asked Annie. “我们怎么出去？”安妮问。

Jack looked in the Japan book. He found a map of Edo. “Look,” he said, pointing at the map. “We have to get over this bridge that leads away from the Imperial Garden into the city. The bridge is on the east side of the garden.” 杰克看了看日本的书。 他找到了一张江户的地图。 “看，”他说，指着地图。 “我们必须穿过这座桥，从皇宫花园通往城市。 桥在花园的东边。”

“The morning sun is over there,” said Annie, squinting into the sunlight. “So that must be east. Let’s climb down and head that way.” “早晨的太阳在那边，”安妮眯着眼睛看着阳光说。 “所以那边就是东边。 我们下去吧，朝那个方向走。”

“Good plan. Then we’ll be walking in the opposite direction of those samurai,” said Jack. “好主意。 然后我们要朝那些武士相反的方向走，”杰克说。

“Right,” said Annie. She started down the rope ladder. “对，”安妮说。 她开始顺着绳梯往下爬。

“Be careful,” said Jack. “We don’t want anyone to see us sneaking around the Imperial Garden.” “小心点，”杰克说。 “我们可不想被人看到在皇宫花园里偷偷摸摸地溜来溜去。”

Jack put the Japan book into his burlap bag and slung the bag over his shoulder. As he stepped onto the ladder, he nearly tripped on his kimono. “Oh, brother,” he said. He held up the cloth and carefully climbed down. 杰克把那本关于日本的书放进他的麻布袋里，然后把袋子挎到肩膀上。 当他踏上梯子时，差点被他的和服绊倒。 “哦，天哪，”他说。 他举起那块布料，小心翼翼地爬了下来。

Jack joined Annie on a wide path. A gust of dry wind carried petals from cherry trees through the air. The long branches of the willows swayed over the grass. 杰克走到安妮身边，站在一条宽阔的小路上。 一阵干燥的风吹来了樱桃树的花瓣，飘在空中。 柳树长长的枝条在草地上摇晃。

Jack and Annie began heading east, keeping their eyes and ears open for more people. They walked past flower beds and big rocks. They walked around a pond with swans. They started down a narrow lane between blossoming cherry trees. 杰克和安妮开始向东走，睁大眼睛和竖起耳朵，留意有没有更多的人。 他们走过花坛，绕过大石头。 他们绕过一个有天鹅的池塘。 他们沿着两旁开满樱花树的窄小小路走下去。

Just as they came out from under the trees, Jack and Annie saw four men strolling toward them. One man was shorter and older than the others. He wore a straw hat and a tattered brown coat and used a walking stick. The other three had shaved heads with topknots, and two swords hung from each of their belts. 就在他们从树下走出来时，杰克和安妮看见四个男人朝他们走来。 其中一个人比其他人都矮，也更年长。 他戴着一顶草帽，穿着一件破旧的棕色外套，还拿着一根拐杖。 另外三个人都剃了光头，头上还留着发髻，每个人的腰带上都挂着两把剑。

“Samurai!” whispered Jack. “武士！”杰克小声说。

“Yikes,” said Annie. “天哪，”安妮说。

“Run!” said Jack. “跑！”杰克说。

Jack and Annie turned around and started running back down the narrow lane. 杰克和安妮转身，沿着狭窄的小路往回跑。

Jack heard the men running after them. “Halt!” cried a samurai. 杰克听到那些人正在后面追赶他们。 “站住！”一名武士喊道。

Jack grabbed Annie’s hand and they stumbled to a halt. Out of breath, they turned to face the three samurai who were rushing toward them. 杰克抓住安妮的手，他们一下子停住了。 气喘吁吁地，他们转身面对着朝他们冲来的三名武士。

“Who are you?” one of the samurai barked. He was holding up his sword. “Why do you run from us? Are you spies?” “你们是谁？”一名武士大声问。 他举起了自己的剑。 “你们为什么躲着我们？你们是间谍吗？”

Just as Jack was about to answer, he heard a voice shout, “Baku! Koto!” 就在杰克准备回答的时候，他听到一个声音喊道：“巴库！科托！”

The man with the walking stick and straw hat was hurrying toward them. “Baku, Koto, what are you doing here?” he called out to Jack and Annie. “Why did you not wait for me at the bridge?” 那个拿着拐杖、戴着草帽的男人正朝他们快步走来。 “巴库、科托，你们怎么会在这里？”他朝杰克和安妮喊道。 “为什么你们没有在桥上等我？”
