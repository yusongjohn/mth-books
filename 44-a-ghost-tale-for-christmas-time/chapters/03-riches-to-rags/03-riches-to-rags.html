3. Riches to Rags 3. 从富裕到贫穷

Jack looked at Annie. “She called that kid Charley Dickens,” he said. 杰克看着安妮。 “她叫那个小孩查理·狄更斯，”他说。

“I heard,” said Annie. “So Charles Dickens must be five or six years old.” “我听到了，”安妮说。 “所以查尔斯·狄更斯应该只有五、六岁。”

“Oh, man,” said Jack, groaning. “So Merlin wants us to help another little kid, like we helped Wolfie Mozart?” “哦，天哪，”杰克呻吟着说。 “所以梅林想让我们帮助另一个小孩，就像我们帮助沃尔夫·莫扎特那样？”

“Seems like it,” said Annie. “看起来是这样，”安妮说。

“Wait a minute. That doesn’t make sense. Let’s do the math,” said Jack. “Our book said Charles Dickens was born in 1812.” He pulled out their research book and looked at the first page. “And Queen Victoria became queen in 1837.” “等一下。 这不太对劲。 “我们来算算看。”杰克说。 “我们的书上说查尔斯·狄更斯出生于1812年。”他拿出他们的研究书，看了看第一页。 “而且维多利亚女王是在1837年成为女王的。”

“The driver said she’s been queen for six years …,” said Annie. “So now it’s 1843. Subtract 1812 from 1843.…” “司机说她当了六年女王……，”安妮说。 “那么现在是1843年。 用1843减去1812……

Jack squeezed his eyes shut. “And you get … thirty-one!” he said. 杰克闭紧了眼睛。 “你得到……三十一！”，他说。

“Good work!” said Annie. “干得不错！”安妮说。

“So Charles Dickens is thirty-one,” said Jack. “And Charley must be Charles Junior.” “所以查尔斯·狄更斯三十一岁了，”杰克说。 “而查利应该是查尔斯的小儿子。”

“Great,” said Annie. “Let’s go meet Charles Senior.” “太好了，”安妮说。 “我们去见见查尔斯的爸爸吧。”

Jack put away their book, and he and Annie walked to the front gate. Between the iron bars, they could see a beautiful three-story house with tall windows. 杰克把书收好，然后他和安妮走到前门。 透过铁栏杆，他们看到一座美丽的三层房子，窗户很高。

“Nice place,” said Annie. “It looks like Charles Dickens has already given his gifts to the world.” “真不错的地方，”安妮说。 “看起来查尔斯·狄更斯已经把他的礼物送给了世界。”

“Yeah, and gotten some back,” said Jack. “So I wonder what his problem is?” “是啊，还得到了一些回报，”杰克说。 “所以我想知道他的问题是什么？”

“We’ll have to figure that out when we meet him,” said Annie. She jangled a bell hanging from the gate. “我们得在见到他之后才能弄清楚，”安妮说。 她摇晃着挂在门上的铃铛。

A moment later, the front door opened. A stout woman in a white apron came out and walked to the gate. “Yes?” she said through the bars. 片刻之后，前门打开了。 一位穿着白色围裙的胖妇人走了出来，走到门前。 “怎么了？”她隔着栏杆说道。

“Is this the Dickens residence?” said Annie. “这是狄更斯家吗？”安妮问。

“Yes,” said the woman. “是的，”妇人说。

“Ah, excellent!” said Annie. “We’ve come to call on Charles Dickens.” “啊，太好了！”安妮说。 “我们是来拜访查尔斯·狄更斯的。”

“Indeed? Who are you?” asked the woman. “真的吗？ “你是谁？”女人问。

“We’re Jack and An—” started Annie. “我们是杰克和安—”安妮开始说。

“Andrew!” finished Jack. “安德鲁！”杰克接着说。

“Right,” said Annie, clearing her throat and deepening her voice. “We’re Jack and Andrew from Frog Creek. And who are you?” “对，”安妮说，清了清嗓子，把声音压低了。 “我们是来自蛙溪镇的杰克和安德鲁。你们是谁？”

“I am Mrs. Tibbs, the housekeeper,” said the woman. “And it is my sad job to tell you that Mr. Dickens can have no visitors today. He is working on his latest book and cannot be disturbed.” “我是凯瑟琳夫人。 “蒂布斯，管家，”那个女人说。 “很遗憾地告诉你们，今天 狄更斯先生不能接待访客。” 他正在写他的最新一本书，不能被打扰。”

“Mrs. Tibbs,” said Annie, “may we have just five short minutes of Mr. Dickens’s time, please?” “梅林夫人。 “蒂布斯，”安妮说，“我们能借用先生五分钟的时间吗，请？” “年轻人，恐怕现在先生

“Young man, I am afraid that right now Mr. Dickens needs every minute he can spare for his writing,” said Mrs. Tibbs. “Surely you know how important Mr. Dickens’s work is.” 正在忙，不能打扰。” 迪克恩需要每一分每一秒来写作，”蒂布斯太太说。 “你们肯定知道迪克恩的工作有多重要， ”

“Yes, ma’am, surely we do,” said Jack. “But—” “是的，夫人，我们当然知道，”杰克说。 “但是——”

“I am terribly sorry, young gentlemen,” said Mrs. Tibbs. “It grieves me to turn you away, but I must. I pray that you will bear no grudge against Mr. Dickens.” With that, Mrs. Tibbs turned and hurried back inside. “我非常抱歉，年轻的先生们，”蒂布斯太太说。 “我很遗憾必须把你们赶走，但这是必须的。” 我希望你不会对先生怀有怨恨 “狄更斯。”说完，Mrs. 蒂布斯转过身，赶紧跑回了里面。

“Well, that didn’t get us very far,” said Jack. “好吧，这并没有让我们走多远，”杰克说。

“Surely it did not,” said Annie. “确实没有，”安妮说。

“Pardon us, sirs,” someone said. “请原谅，先生们，”有人说道。

Jack and Annie turned around. 杰克和安妮转过身。

Two boys with dirty clothes and dirty faces were standing behind them. The taller boy wore an old top hat, and the smaller one wore a wool cap that was too big for him. He carried a large, round brush, a broom, and some rags. 两个穿着脏衣服、脸上脏兮兮的男孩正站在他们身后。 个子较高的男孩戴着一顶旧礼帽，个子较矮的男孩戴着一顶太大了的羊毛帽子。 他拿着一把大大的圆刷子、一把扫帚和一些破布。

“Oh … hi,” said Jack. “哦……你好，”杰克说。

“We was just needing to ring the bell, sir, if you don’t mind,” the bigger boy said. “先生，如果我们不打扰的话，我们正需要按响门铃，”那个较大的男孩说。

“Sure,” said Jack. “But the housekeeper’s not letting anyone in today. That’s why we’re leaving.” “当然可以，”杰克说。 “但是今天管家不让任何人进来。 这就是我们为什么要离开的原因。”

“She’ll let us in,” said the smaller boy. “We’ve come to sweep the chimneys.” “她会让我们进来的，”较小的男孩说。 “我们是来打扫烟囱的。”

“Really? She’ll let you into the house?” said Annie. “Hold on.” She turned to Jack. “I’ve got an idea!” “真的吗？ “她会让你进屋吗？”安妮说。 “等等。”她转向杰克。 “我有个主意！”，

“No you don’t,” said Jack. “See you, guys. Bye.” He tried to move Annie along, but she pulled away. “你没有，”杰克说。 “再见，大家。 “再见。”他试着拉着安妮往前走，但安妮挣脱了手。

“Wait, don’t ring the bell yet,” Annie said to the boys. “Would you be interested in trading places with us for a while?” “等一下，别按铃铛，”安妮对男孩们说。 “你愿意暂时和我们交换一下位置吗？”

“Annie—” said Jack. “安妮—”杰克说。

“Shh,” said Annie. “嘘，”安妮说。

The boys looked confused. “Trade places?” asked the bigger boy. “Why?” 男孩们看起来很困惑。 “交换位置？”年纪较大的男孩问。 “为什么？”

“Actually—” said Jack. “其实——”杰克说。

But Annie jumped in. “Here’s the deal,” she said. “We’ve come a long way. And we really, really want to talk to Mr. Dickens. So … maybe if we go in and clean the chimneys, we’ll have a chance.” 但安妮插话了。 “听好了，”她说。 “我们已经走了很远了。 我们真的很想和狄更斯先生谈谈。 所以……也许如果我们进去清理烟囱，我们就有机会了。”

“We can’t lose our wages,” said the bigger boy. “我们不能丢了工资，”那个较大的男孩说。

“How much do you get paid for this job?” asked Annie. “这份工作你挣多少钱？”安妮问。

“Twopence,” said the smaller boy. “两便士，”那个较小的男孩说。

Annie reached into her pocket and pulled out a handful of coins. She gave them to the boy. “Is that good?” 安妮把手伸进口袋，拿出一把硬币。 她把硬币给了那个男孩。 “这样够吗？”

Both boys looked at her with wide eyes and nodded eagerly. 两个男孩用惊讶的大眼睛看着她，兴奋地点头。

“And you’ll get our coats, too,” said Annie. “If you give us yours.” “你们还会拿到我们的外套，”安妮说。 “如果你们把你们的给我们。”

“Whoa, whoa. Excuse me,” Jack said. He pulled Annie aside. “This is not a good plan.” “等等，等等， 请原谅，”杰克说。 他把安妮拉到一边。 “这不是个好主意。”

“Yes it is,” Annie said. “Remember when we got kicked out of the Big House in Ireland? If we’d had a job, we could have hung around there longer.” “是的，是的，”安妮说。 “还记得我们以前在爱尔兰的大房子里被赶出去的时候吗？ 如果我们有一份工作，就能在那里待更久。

“But we don’t know how to sweep chimneys,” said Jack. “但我们不知道怎么打扫烟囱，”杰克说。

“How hard can it be?” said Annie. “It will get us inside the house. Then we can keep an eye out for Mr. Dickens. The next thing you know, we’ve figured out the problem and we’re playing our magic music and Mr. Dickens is giving more gifts to the world. Mission accomplished!” “这能有多难呢？”安妮说。 “这能让我们进到房子里。 然后我们就能留意一下狄更斯先生。 接下来你就会知道，我们已经想出了办法，正在演奏我们的魔法音乐，而先生 狄更斯正在给世界带来更多礼物。 任务完成！

Before Jack could protest, Annie turned back to the chimney sweeps. “So do you want to trade your coats for ours? Hats, too?” 杰克还没来得及抗议，安妮就转身回到了烟囱清洁工那里。 “你们想用你们的外套和我们的交换吗？还有帽子呢？”，

The boys stared at her in wonder. “Them is fine coats, Harry!” said the smaller boy. “And fine hats, too!” He threw down the brush, broom, and rags. He traded his coat for Annie’s velvet coat, and traded his dirty, ragged cap for her new, clean cap. 男孩们惊讶地看着她。 “These are fine coats, Harry!” 较小的男孩说道。 “还有很棒的帽子！”他扔下了刷子、扫帚和破布。 他用他的外套换上了安妮的天鹅绒外套，又用他那又脏又破的帽子换上了她那顶新的干净帽子。

“Hey, you’re a girl!” the boy said when he saw Annie’s braids. “嘿，你是个女孩！”男孩看到安妮的辫子时说道。

“So what if I am?” Annie asked, tucking her braids under the dirty cap. “那又怎样？”安妮一边把辫子藏在脏帽子下面，一边问道。

“Forget it, Colin. Rich folks are strange,” Harry said. He pulled off his coat and held it out to Jack. “Here,” he said with a big grin. “忘了它吧，科林。 “有钱人真奇怪，”哈利说。 他脱下外套，递给了杰克。 “给你，”他咧着嘴笑着说。

Jack sighed and traded his beautiful velvet coat and wool cap for Harry’s tattered coat and old top hat. 杰克叹了口气，把自己的漂亮天鹅绒外套和羊毛帽子，换成了哈利那件破旧的外套和旧礼帽。

“And what about your boots?” asked Harry. “那你的靴子呢？”哈利问。

Jack looked down at his shiny leather boots. He looked at Harry’s dirty, old shoes. 杰克低头看着他闪亮的皮靴。 他又看了看哈利那又脏又旧的鞋子。

“Come on, Jack,” said Annie. “These guys need them more than we do.” “快点，杰克，”安妮说。 “他们比我们更需要这些。”

Annie pulled off her boots. Colin took off his shoes and handed them to her. Jack sighed again and sat down to take off his boots. 安妮脱掉了她的靴子。 科林脱下鞋子，递给她。 杰克叹了口气，坐下来脱掉靴子。

Colin and Harry stood tall in their new boots and jackets and hats. 科林和哈利穿上新靴子、新夹克和新帽子，站得笔直。

“How ’bout it, Colin?” said Harry, shaking his head. “In two minutes, we went from rags to riches.” “怎么样，科林？”哈利一边摇头一边说。 “两分钟内，我们就从一无所有变成了富翁。”

Colin let out a wild whoop. Then the two boys linked arms and danced a jig, kicking up their boots. When they stopped dancing, Harry rang the gate bell wildly. 科林发出一阵狂野的欢呼。 然后两个男孩手拉手，跳起踢踏舞，把靴子踢得飞起。 他们停下跳舞时，哈利疯狂地按响了大门的门铃。

As the bell clanged, the front door of the house flew open. The housekeeper stuck her head outside. “Stop the noise, you idiots! I’m comin’!” she screeched. 随着门铃响彻，房子的前门猛地打开了。 女仆把头探了出来。 “别吵了，你们这些蠢货！ “我来了！”，她尖叫道。

“Let’s go, Colin, before they change their minds!” said Harry. “快走吧，科林，不然他们改变主意就不让我们走了！”，哈利说。

As the two boys ran off in their new velvet coats, Jack heard money jingling. “Oh, no!” he said. “We forgot to empty our pockets!” 当两个男孩穿着新买的天鹅绒外套跑开时，杰克听到钱串在一起发出叮当声。 “哦，天哪！”，他说。 “我们忘记把口袋里的钱掏空了！

“That’s okay,” said Annie. “I’m sure they need the money more than we do. They were so happy going from rags to riches.” “没关系，”，安妮说。 “我肯定他们比我们更需要这些钱。 他们从一无所有变成了富翁，高兴极了。

“Yeah, right,” said Jack, “and we just went from riches to rags.” “对，没错，”杰克说，“我们刚从富翁变成了穷光蛋。”

“Shh! Here comes Mrs. Tibbs!” said Annie. “We’d better smudge our faces with the rags so she won’t recognize us.” She rubbed her face with a dirty rag, then Jack’s. “There! You look like a real chimney sweep now.” “嘘！梅林太太来了。” “蒂布斯！”安妮说道。 “我们最好用破布把脸弄脏，这样她认不出我们。”她用一条脏破布擦了擦自己的脸，然后又擦了杰克的脸。 “好了！” 你现在看起来真像一个真正的烟囱清洁工。”

The housekeeper stomped to the gate and unlocked it. “Don’t break my eardrums next time, you knaves!” she cried. 管家踩着沉重的步伐走到大门前，把它打开了。 “下次再别把我的耳朵吵得受不了了，你们这些坏蛋！”她喊道。
