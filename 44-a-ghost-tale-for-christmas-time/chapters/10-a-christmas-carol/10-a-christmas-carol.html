10. A Christmas Carol 10. 《圣诞奇遇记》

The gas streetlamp flickered back on. Charles began pacing back and forth. 煤气路灯又亮起来了。 查尔斯开始来回踱步。

“Come on, Jack,” said Annie. “Let’s go talk to him! Quick!” “快点，杰克，”安妮说。 “我们去跟他谈谈吧！快点！”，

Jack packed up the violin and bow. Then he and Annie hurried down the hill to Charles. 杰克收拾好了小提琴和琴弓。 然后他和安妮赶紧下山去找查尔斯。

“Charles!” cried Annie. “Hello!” “查尔斯！”，安妮喊道。 “你好！”，

Charles whirled around. “Annie! Jack! You can’t imagine what just happened!” His voice was shaking. “I had the strangest visions! Three ghosts came to me! They showed me visions of myself in the past, the present, and the future!” 查尔斯猛地转过身来。 “安妮！杰克！ 你根本想不到刚刚发生了什么！”他的声音在发抖。 “我看到了一些最奇怪的幻象！ 三个鬼魂来找我了！ 他们向我展示了过去、现在和未来不同时间的我自己！

“Really?” said Annie. “That sounds like a scene in a book.” “真的吗？”安妮说。 “那听起来像是书里的一幕。”

“Yes, yes, it certainly does!” Charles said, laughing and wiping his eyes. “The ghosts made me want to keep writing. They taught me that I can truly help the world with my books! I—I must go home! I must find a cab and go home and get to work at once! I cannot waste another day! Another hour! Another minute! I must write! I love to write!” He laughed with joy. “是的，是的，确实如此！”查尔斯说，一边笑一边擦着眼睛。 “这些鬼魂让我想继续写下去。 他们教会了我，我可以用我的书真正帮助世界！我——我必须回家！ 我必须找个出租车，马上回家，然后立刻去上班！ 我不能浪费另一天！再浪费一小时！ 再浪费一分钟！我必须开始写作！ 我太喜欢写作了！”他高兴地笑了。

“Then I guess we’ll be going home, too,” said Jack, smiling. “Our job is done here.” “那么我想我们也该回家了，”杰克微笑着说道。 “我们的任务在这里完成了。”

“Shall I tell the cab to let you off somewhere?” asked Charles. “我让出租车司机在某个地方放下你，好吗？”查尔斯问道。

“We need to go to Hyde Park,” said Annie. “我们需要去海德公园，”安妮说。

“Wonderful! It’s on the way. Come along!” said Charles. He took off running up the hill, dashing ahead of Jack and Annie. When they caught up with him, they saw a horse and cab clattering over the cobblestones. “太棒了！就在路上。 快过来！”查尔斯说。 他飞快地跑上山，冲在杰克和安妮前面。 当他们追上他时，看见一匹马和一辆马车在鹅卵石路上飞驰而过。

“Stop, sir! Give us a ride, please!” Charles shouted to the driver. “停下，先生！ 请载我们一程！”查尔斯对司机喊道。

The driver brought his horse to a halt. Jack and Annie followed Charles to the cab. “Hyde Park! Then One Devonshire Terrace! Please hurry!” said Charles. 司机让他的马停下来。 杰克和安妮跟着查尔斯走到马车旁。 “海德公园！ 然后是德文郡街一号！ 请快一点！”查尔斯说道。

The driver looked delighted. “Yes, Mr. Dickens, sir!” he said. 司机看起来非常高兴。 “是的，先生。 “狄更斯，先生！”他说。

The three of them crowded into the cab, and the horse began to trot through the dark, foggy city, its hooves clopping on the cobblestones. 他们三个人挤进马车，马开始在黑暗、雾气弥漫的城市中慢跑，马蹄在鹅卵石上发出咔哒咔哒的声音。

“Ah!” said Charles, clapping his hands. “I know now what I shall write about! I will write a Christmas story about a man whose life is changed by three ghosts! The Ghost of Christmas Past! The Ghost of Christmas Present! And the Ghost of Christmas Future! What do you think of that?” “啊！”查尔斯拍着手说。 “我现在知道我要写什么了！ 我要写一个关于一个男人的圣诞故事，他的生活被三个幽灵改变了！ 过去的圣诞幽灵！ 现在的圣诞幽灵！ 还有未来的圣诞幽灵！ 你觉得怎么样？“

“It sounds brilliant,” said Jack. “听起来太棒了，”杰克说。

“I agree!” said Charles. “I’ll write about a greedy, selfish man who helps no one. Like—like Mr. Pinch! But I’ll call him—what shall I call him?” “我同意！”查尔斯说。 “我要写一个贪婪、自私、谁也不帮助的人。比如——比如先生。 皮奇！ 但我要叫他——我该叫他什么呢？

“How about Scrooge?” said Annie. “叫斯克鲁奇怎么样？”安妮说。

“Wonderful name!” Charles laughed. “Mr. Scrooge! I love it. The three ghosts will change Scrooge’s life! Do you like that idea?” “太妙的名字了！”查尔斯笑了。 “斯克鲁奇先生！ 我喜欢这个名字。 三个鬼魂会改变斯克鲁奇的一生！ 你喜欢这个主意吗？“

“Love it,” said Jack. “太棒了，”杰克说。

“Good!” said Charles. “I think I shall just be able to write the story in time for Christmas. Hah! Perhaps I’ll call it A Ghost Story of Christmas.” “很好！“查尔斯说。 “我想我刚好来得及在圣诞节前写完这个故事。哈哈！ 也许我会把它叫做《圣诞的鬼故事》。”

“Hmm. Or maybe you could just call it A Christmas Carol,” said Jack. “或者你可以直接叫它《圣诞颂歌》,” 杰克说。

“Oh! I love that!” said Charles. “Yes, perhaps I’ll call it A Christmas Carol. And then underneath those words, I’ll write: Being a Ghost Story of Christmas!” “我超爱这个名字!” 查尔斯说。 “是的，我可能会叫它《圣诞颂歌》。 然后在这些字下面，我会写上：《圣诞的幽灵故事》!”

“That sounds good,” said Annie. “听起来不错，”安妮说。

“That should make people want to read it,” said Charles. “Everyone loves a ghost story, don’t they?” “这样应该会让人想读这本书，”查尔斯说。 “每个人都喜欢鬼故事，不是吗？”，

“Well …,” started Jack. “嗯……”，杰克开始说。

“Of course they do!” said Charles. “It all makes sense to me now. I will keep using my books to fight greed and cruelty. My pen is my sword. Except my books will never celebrate wars and fighting. They’ll always show the joys and sorrows of real people. They’ll show how good always triumphs over evil.” “当然啦！”查尔斯说。 “现在一切都说得通了。” 我会一直用我的书来对抗贪婪和残忍。 我的笔就是我的剑。 不过我的书永远不会赞美战争和战斗。 它们会一直展示真实人们的快乐和悲伤。 它们会展示善良永远会战胜邪恶。

“Great,” said Jack. “太好了，”杰克说。

Charles sat back in his carriage seat and chuckled, his eyes shining in the lamplight. “I’m as light as a feather, as happy as an angel. Am I not the luckiest man on earth?” 查尔斯靠在马车座位上，发出笑声，煤油灯的光映亮了他的眼睛。 “我轻得像一根羽毛，快乐得像一个天使。 我是不是地球上最幸运的人？”

“I think you might be,” said Annie. “我想你可能是，”安妮说。

The horse came to a stop. “Hyde Park, Mr. Dickens, sir,” the driver called down. 马停了下来。 “海德公园，先生。 狄更斯先生，”车夫朝下喊道。

“Well, Jack and Annie, I hope you have enjoyed your time with me. I trust I have been of some help to you,” said Charles. He sounded like the old Charles, pleased and proud of himself. “好吧，杰克和安妮，我希望你们和我在一起的时光过得愉快。 我相信我帮了你们一些忙，”查尔斯说。 他听起来就像以前的查尔斯，既高兴又为自己感到骄傲。

Jack didn’t mind. A happy Charles was much better than a sad one. 杰克一点都不在意。 一个开心的查尔斯，比一个伤心的查尔斯好多了。

“Yes,” said Jack. “But we have to give this back to you.” He pulled out the leather wallet that Charles had given them. “Thanks, but we don’t need it.” “是的，”杰克说。 “但我们得把这个还给你。”他拿出查尔斯送给他们的皮质钱包。 “谢谢，但我们不需要它。”

“Oh, no, please keep it,” said Charles. “Buy food with the money. Buy boots. Buy books!” “哦，不，求你留着吧，”查尔斯说。 “用钱买食物。买靴子。 买书！”

“Actually, we have lots of books,” said Jack. “And we have food at home and shoes and parents. We have everything we need.” “其实，我们有很多书，”杰克说。 “而且我们在家有食物、鞋子和父母。” 我们什么都需要准备好了。”

“Charles, please share it with others who aren’t so lucky,” said Annie. “查尔斯， please 分享给那些没有这么幸运的人，” said 安妮。

“Why, I don’t know what to say,” said Charles. “You two are the most extraordinary children I’ve ever met. Clearly you have good, generous hearts. You already live the message I want to write in A Christmas Carol.” “为什么，我不知道该说什么，” said 查尔斯。 “你们两个是我见过最特别的孩子。 显然，你们有善良、慷慨的心。 你们已经活出了我想写在《圣诞奇缘》里的信息。

Annie smiled. “Yeah, well, it’s always good to be reminded,” she said. “I can’t wait to read your story.” 安妮笑了。 “是啊，总是很好被提醒，”她说。 “我迫不及待想读你的故事。”

“And where will you go now?” asked Charles. “Will you be safe?” “你现在要去哪儿呢？”查尔斯问。 “你会安全吗？”

“Yes, our parents take good care of us,” said Jack. “You don’t have to worry.” “是的，我们的父母会好好照顾我们，”杰克说。 “你不用担心。”

“I shall never forget you,” said Charles. “我永远不会忘记你，”查尔斯说。

“We won’t forget you, either,” said Annie. “Good-bye, Charles.” “我们也不会忘记你的，”安妮说。 “再见了，查尔斯。”

Jack and Annie hopped down from the cab and took off through the park. The fog was so thick it was impossible to see the tree house. Jack couldn’t even see Annie. He was relieved when she called out, “I found it!” 杰克和安妮从出租车上跳下来，穿过公园跑开了。 雾气太浓了，根本看不到树屋。 杰克甚至都看不到安妮。 他松了一口气，听到她喊道：“我找到了！”

Jack ran toward the sound of Annie’s voice. She was already halfway up the ladder. As he started up after her, church bells began to bong, striking the hour. 杰克朝安妮的声音跑去。 她已经爬到梯子的一半了。 当他开始往上爬的时候，教堂的钟声响起，敲响了整点。

Jack and Annie climbed inside the tree house and looked out the window. But there was nothing to see, nothing except the heavy fog. 杰克和安妮爬进树屋，从窗户往外看。 但什么也看不到，除了浓浓的雾气。

“Charles is going to be fine,” said Annie. “查尔斯会没事的，”安妮说。

“Yep,” said Jack with a smile. “He never even knew that it was us who helped him.” “是啊，”杰克微笑着说。 “他甚至都不知道是我们在帮助他。”

“That’s the best way to help someone, I think,” said Annie. “我想，这是帮助别人最好的方式，”安妮说。

“Why?” asked Jack. “为什么？”杰克问。

“Then you know you’re not helping them just to get a lot of credit,” said Annie. “You’re helping because it’s the right thing to do.” “那么你就知道，你不是为了得到很多表扬才帮助他们的，”安妮说。 “你是帮助他们，因为这是正确的事。”

Jack nodded. What Annie had just said felt true. “Ready to go home now?” he said. He picked up the Pennsylvania book. 杰克点了点头。 安妮刚刚说的话听起来是真的。 “现在准备回家了吗？”他说。 他捡起了宾夕法尼亚的书。

Annie nodded. 安妮点了点头。

Jack pointed to a picture of the Frog Creek woods. “I wish we could go home,” he said. 杰克指着一张蛙溪镇森林的图片。 “我希望我们现在能回家，”他说。

The wind began to blow. 风开始吹起来了。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转起来。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。
