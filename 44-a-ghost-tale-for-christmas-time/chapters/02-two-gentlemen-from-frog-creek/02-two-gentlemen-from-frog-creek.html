2. Two Gentlemen from Frog Creek 2. 来自蛙溪镇的两位绅士

Jack and Annie were both dressed in brown-and-green velvet jackets and dark pants. They wore loose wool caps and shiny new boots. Jack’s backpack had become a green velvet bag with a brass buckle. 杰克和安妮都穿着棕绿色的天鹅绒夹克和深色裤子。 他们戴着宽松的羊毛帽子，穿着崭新的靴子。 杰克的背包变成了一只绿色天鹅绒的袋子，上面有一个黄铜扣。

“Fancy clothes,” said Jack. “漂亮的衣服，”杰克说。

“Well, at least I’m not wearing a giant skirt, like I wore in Vienna,” said Annie. “好吧，至少我没有像在维也纳时那样穿着大裙子，”安妮说。

“Yeah, and these boots aren’t full of holes, like the boots we wore in Ireland,” said Jack. “是啊，而且这些靴子不像我们在爱尔兰时穿的那些靴子，到处都是破洞，”杰克说。

“So where did we land?” said Annie. She and Jack looked out the window. “那我们降落在哪里了？”安妮说。 她和杰克看向窗外。

The tree house had landed in a row of big trees. Beyond the trees was a grassy park with gardens and pathways. 树屋降落在一排大树之间。 树的后面是一片长满草地的公园，有花园和小路。

Bordering the park was a busy road filled with horse-drawn carriages. Autumn sunlight shone on rooftops, towers, and church steeples. Black smoke rose from hundreds of chimneys. 公园旁边是一条热闹的马路，路上有很多马车。 秋天的阳光照在屋顶、塔楼和教堂尖顶上。 黑色的烟从成百上千的烟囱里升起。

“London looks great,” said Annie. “Let’s start looking for Charles Dickens.” “伦敦看起来真棒，”安妮说。 “我们开始找查尔斯·狄更斯吧。”

“Wait, I’ll check the book first,” said Jack. “等等，我要先看看书，”杰克说。

Jack looked in the index of their book and found Charles Dickens. He turned to a page with a photograph of a handsome man with wavy brown hair and huge eyes. Jack read: 杰克在他们的书的索引里找到了查尔斯·狄更斯。 他翻到一页，上面有一张照片，照片里是一个英俊的男人，他有着波浪般的棕色头发和大大的眼睛。杰克读到：

Charles Dickens was born in England in 1812. He is one of the most famous writers of all time. 查尔斯·狄更斯于1812年出生在英格兰。 他是有史以来最著名的作家之一。

“Of all time?” said Annie. “Wow, he shouldn’t be hard to find.” “有史以来？”安妮说。 “哇，他应该不难找到。”

“Maybe he isn’t famous yet,” said Jack. “If he is, why would he need our help?” “也许他还没出名呢，”杰克说。 “如果他已经出名了，为什么还需要我们的帮助呢？”

“Good question,” said Annie. “Let’s start looking for the answer.” “好问题，”安妮说。 “我们开始找答案吧。”

“Okay, but first you’d better hide your pigtails,” said Jack. “好吧，不过你最好先把你的麻花辫藏起来，”杰克说。

“Oh, right. I’m supposed to be a boy,” said Annie. She tucked her pigtails under her cap. “In the past, girls didn’t have many choices, did they? How do I look?” “哦，对啊。” “我应该是个男孩。”安妮说道。 她把她的双马尾塞进了帽子下面。 “在过去，女孩们没有太多选择，对吧？我看起来怎么样？”

“Fine,” Jack said. “很好，”杰克说。

“Good, let’s go,” said Annie. “好，我们走吧，”安妮说。

As Annie climbed out of the tree house, Jack unbuckled the green velvet bag. He put their research book and magic violin and bow inside. Then he buckled the bag and followed Annie down the ladder. 当安妮从树屋爬出来时，杰克解开了绿色天鹅绒袋子的扣子。 他把他们的研究书、魔法小提琴和琴弓放了进去。 然后他扣上袋子，跟着安妮顺着梯子往下爬。

When Jack stepped off the ladder, he heard jingling in his coat pocket. He reached in and pulled out a handful of coins. “Hey, I have money!” he said. 当杰克从梯子上下来时，他听到外套口袋里发出叮叮当当的声音。 他伸手进去，抓出一把硬币。 “嘿，我有钱！”他说。

Annie dug into her pockets. “Me too!” she said. 安妮翻遍了她的口袋。 “我也有！”她说。

“Great,” said Jack. “I didn’t like being poor on our last two missions.” “太好了，”杰克说。 “上次的两次任务，我都不喜欢穷兮兮的感觉。”

Jack and Annie crunched through fallen leaves until they came to the open park area. A man stood on a platform giving a speech. Women pushed baby buggies. Children sailed toy boats on a pond. 杰克和安妮踩着落叶，走到了开阔的公园区域。 一个男人站在平台上发表演讲。 妇女们推着婴儿车。 孩子们在池塘上划着玩具船。

“We have to ask someone for help,” said Annie, looking around. “Excuse me!” she called to a woman in a flowery bonnet. “Do you know where Charles Dickens lives?” “我们必须找人帮忙，”安妮环顾四周说。 “请问一下！”她对一个戴着花边帽子的女士喊道。 “你知道查尔斯·狄更斯住在哪里吗？”，

“Why, yes, he and his family live at One Devonshire Terrace,” the woman said, “below Regent’s Park.” “哦，是的，他和家人住在德文郡街1号，”女士说，“在摄政公园下面。”

“Thanks,” said Annie. “谢谢，”安妮说。

“That was easy,” said Jack as the woman strolled on. “这太容易了，”杰克说，看着女士继续走远。

“I guess he’s already famous,” said Annie. “我想他已经很有名了，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack, pulling out their book again. “Let’s see if there’s a map.” He thumbed through the book until he found a map of London. “Here it is. Regent’s Park.” He looked up. “Where are we now?” “是啊，”杰克说，又拿出他们的书。 “我们看看有没有地图。”他翻着书，直到找到一张伦敦的地图。 “找到了。” “蛙溪镇。”他抬头看了看。 “我们现在在哪里？”

“That sign over there says Hyde Park,” said Annie. “那边的牌子写着海德公园，”安妮说。

“Wrong park,” said Jack. He looked at the map again. “Okay. Hyde Park is here … and Regent’s Park is here. They’re pretty far away from each other.” “不对，是另一个公园，”杰克说。 他又看了看地图。 “好的， 海德公园在这里……而蛙溪镇在这里。 它们离得有点远。”

“That’s okay,” said Annie. “We have money, and London has carriages. This will be fun!” “没关系，”安妮说。 “我们有钱，伦敦有马车。” “这会很有趣！”

“Right,” said Jack. “Let’s go catch a ride.” “对，”杰克说。 “我们去搭车吧。”

Jack and Annie hurried over the grass to the busy street bordering the park. Carriages of all shapes and sizes clattered over the cobblestones. Four high-stepping horses pulled an elegant black coach. Two donkeys pulled an old wooden wagon with benches. 杰克和安妮赶紧穿过草地，跑到公园边的热闹街道上。 各种形状和大小的马车在鹅卵石路上叮当作响。 四匹高大的马拉着一辆优雅的黑色马车。 两只驴子拉着一辆老旧的木制有座位的货车。

“Excuse me!” Jack called to the driver of a small red carriage waiting on the street. “Can you take us to One Devonshire Terrace, below Regent’s Park?” “请问一下！”杰克对街上等在那儿的一辆小红马车的司机喊道。 “能带我们去德文郡街1号，就在摄政公园下面吗？”

The driver smiled down from his seat. “Indeed, sir!” he said. “I would be proud to carry two fine young gentlemen across our fair city.” 司机从座位上朝下笑了笑。 “确实，先生！”他说。 “我很荣幸能带两位优秀的年轻绅士游览我们美丽的城市。”

Jack caught Annie’s eye and smiled. “Thank you,” he said. 杰克看了安妮一眼，微笑了。 “谢谢，”他说。

“Climb into my cab, if you please,” said the driver. “Where are you gentlemen from?” “请上我的马车吧，”司机说。 “你们先生是从哪里来的？”

“Frog Creek,” said Annie in a deep voice. “蛙溪镇，”安妮用低沉的声音说。

“Frog Creek!” said the driver. “Lovely place, I’m sure.” Then he flicked the reins, and his fat little horse started clopping up the cobblestone street. “蛙溪镇！”司机说。 “那一定是个漂亮的地方，”他说着，抽动了一下缰绳，他那胖乎乎的小马便开始沿着鹅卵石街道哒哒地走起来。

In the cool autumn air, the red cab clattered by toy shops, bootmakers, and hatmakers. The cab passed brick mansions with gardens and ornate buildings with turrets and towers. 在凉爽的秋风中，红色的马车叮叮当当地驶过玩具店、鞋匠铺和帽子店。 出租车经过了有花园的砖砌豪宅，还有带有尖顶和塔楼的华丽建筑。

“This is a beautiful city,” said Annie. “这是一个美丽的城市，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. He pulled out their book and turned to the beginning. One of the first pages showed a portrait of a plump-faced woman in a red robe. She wore a small crown on her head. “是啊，”杰克说。 他拿出他们的书，翻到最开始的那一页。 第一页上有一幅胖脸女人穿着红色长袍的画像。 她头上戴着一顶小皇冠。

The Victorian era in England began when Queen Victoria came to the throne in 1837. She ruled for the rest of the century and helped her country become a powerful world empire. 英国的维多利亚时代是从1837年维多利亚女王登基时开始的。 她统治了整个世纪，并帮助她的国家成为了一个强大的世界帝国。

“Wow,” said Annie. She stuck her head out the window of the cab and called up to the driver, “Excuse me, sir! Where’s the queen today?” “哇，”安妮说。 她把头伸出出租车的窗户，对司机喊道：“对不起，先生！ 今天女王在哪里？

“I believe she’s on holiday with Prince Albert,” said the driver. “我认为她和阿尔伯特王子一起度假去了，”司机说。

“How long has she been queen?” asked Annie. “她当女王有多久了？”安妮问。

“Let’s see, she was crowned and moved into Buckingham Palace six years ago,” the driver said. “让我想想，她六年前加冕并搬进了白金汉宫，”司机说。

“Thanks!” said Annie. She sat back in her seat. “I love this time in history, with a queen, a prince, and a palace.” “谢谢！”安妮说。 她坐回座位上。 “我最喜欢这个历史时期，有女王、王子和宫殿。”

“Yep,” said Jack. He read on: “是啊，”杰克说。他继续读下去：

During the reign of Queen Victoria, England went through a period known as the Industrial Revolution. People no longer worked mainly on farms. The invention of machines led to work in factories and mines. Many people became very wealthy. 维多利亚女王统治期间，英国经历了一个被称为工业革命的时期。 人们不再主要在农场工作。 机器的发明让人们开始在工厂和矿井里工作。 很多人变得非常富有。

Jack glanced out the window. The people on the sidewalks did look wealthy. Women and girls dressed in ruffles and ribbons strolled in and out of elegant shops. Boys wore frilly white shirts. Men wore top hats and suits. 杰克朝窗外看了一眼。 人行道上的人看起来确实很富有。 妇女和女孩穿着蕾丝和蝴蝶结，来来往往地走进走出高档的商店。 男孩们穿着有蕾丝的白衬衫。 男人们戴着高礼帽，穿着西装。

Jack read on: 杰克继续读着：

But while many people became rich in Victorian England, many more worked under terrible conditions. Even young children worked in factories and mines. Rich and poor lived side by side, but they lived in very different worlds. While the rich feasted in grand mansions, the poor often died young of hunger and disease. 但是虽然在维多利亚时代的英国，很多人变得富有，但更多的人却在极其恶劣的条件下工作。 就连年幼的孩子也必须在工厂和矿井里工作。 富人和穷人都住在一起，但他们的生活却完全不同。 富人住在豪华的大房子里大吃大喝，而穷人却常常因为饥饿和疾病早早死去。

“Whoa,” said Annie. “Maybe I don’t love this time as much as I thought.” “哇，”安妮说。 “也许我并没有像我想的那样喜欢这个年代。”

The horse and cab came to a halt. “Here we are!” the driver called. “One Devonshire Terrace.” 马车和马车夫停了下来。 “我们到了！”司机喊道。 “德文郡街一号。”

Jack closed their book and looked up. The cab had stopped in front of a high brick wall with an iron gate. Jack and Annie stepped down to the sidewalk. The driver climbed off of his perch. 杰克合上书，抬起头。 马车停在了一堵高高的砖墙前，墙边有一个铁门。 杰克和安妮走下马车，来到人行道上。 司机从他的座位上跳了下来。

“How much do we owe you?” asked Jack. “我们欠你多少钱？”杰克问道。

“One shilling, if you please, sir,” said the driver. “如果您愿意的话，一先令，先生，”司机说。

Jack reached into his pocket and took out a large coin. He had no idea how much it was worth, but he gave it to the driver. “I hope this is enough,” he said. 杰克把手伸进口袋，拿出了一枚大硬币。 他不知道这枚硬币值多少钱，但他把它给了司机。 “我希望这已经够了，”他说。

The man’s eyes grew wide. “Why, good heavens, I should say it is! Thank you, gentlemen! Thank you! Thank you! My children thank you! All the good, hardworking people of the world thank you! Shall I come to pick you up later?” 那人的双眼睁得大大的。 “哦，天哪，我觉得这已经够了！ 谢谢你们，先生们！谢谢你们！ 谢谢！ 我的孩子们谢谢你！ 全世界所有善良、勤劳的人都谢谢你！ 我待会儿再来接你们好吗？

“Sorry, but we don’t know how long we’ll be here,” said Jack. “对不起，但我们还不知道要在这里待多久，”杰克说。

“Well, perhaps I’ll pass by this way now and again,” the driver said. “If I see you, I shall certainly stop. I’m always pleased to carry such fine young gentlemen!” The man bowed. Then he climbed back up onto his bench. “Good day, good sirs!” he called, tipping his hat. “那我可能偶尔会从这儿经过，”司机说。 “如果我看到你们，我一定会停下来。 我很乐意载这样优秀的年轻绅士！“那人鞠了一躬。 然后他爬回了自己的座位上。 “祝你们一天愉快，先生们！“他一边喊，一边摘下帽子。

As the horse and cab clattered away, Jack looked at Annie. “He was nuts,” he said. 当马车渐渐远去时，杰克看着安妮。 “他疯了，”他说。

Annie laughed. “I think you gave him a lot more than the regular fare,” she said. 安妮笑了。 “我觉得你给他的东西比平常多得多，”她说。

The gate behind Jack and Annie swung open. Three small children bounded out to the sidewalk, two girls with curly hair and a round-faced boy. 杰克和安妮身后的门打开了。 三个小孩从门里蹦跳着跑到人行道上，两个卷发女孩和一个圆脸男孩。

A woman with a baby followed them out. “Mary! Kate! Charley! Wait for me!” 一个抱着婴儿的妇女跟着他们走了出来。 “玛丽！凯特！ 查理！等等我！”，

“Hurry, Mother!” yelled the little boy. “快点，妈妈！”小男孩喊道。

“You wait for me, Charley Dickens!” the young mother called. She caught up with the children, and they all vanished around the corner. “你等着我，查理·狄更斯！”年轻的母亲喊道。 她追上了孩子们，他们全都转过拐角消失了。

“Charley Dickens?” said Jack, stunned. “查理·狄更斯？”杰克惊讶地说。
