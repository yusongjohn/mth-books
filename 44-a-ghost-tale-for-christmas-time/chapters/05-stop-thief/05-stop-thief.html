5. Stop, Thief! 5. 停下，小偷！

“Oh, man,” said Jack, dazed. “She was nuts. Everyone here is nuts, including Mr. Dickens.” “天啊，”杰克呆呆地说。 “她疯了。 这里每个人都疯了，包括狄更斯先生。”

“It’s all my fault,” said Annie. “I shouldn’t have—” “都是我的错，”安妮说。 “我不应该—”

“No, no, don’t worry,” said Jack. “We just have to catch him and try to fix things.” “不，不，别担心，”杰克说。 “我们只要抓住他，然后试着把事情弄好。”

“He couldn’t have gone far,” said Annie. She and Jack glanced up and down the busy street. The afternoon sun had disappeared. Dark clouds filled the sky. “他不可能走远了，”安妮说。 她和杰克看了看繁忙的街道。 下午的太阳已经消失了。 天空中布满了乌云。

“Look, there he is!” said Annie, pointing. “看，他在那儿！”，安妮指着说。

Jack saw Mr. Dickens weaving through the horse-and-carriage traffic. He was signaling to a cabdriver with his walking stick. 杰克看到先生。 狄更斯在马车和马匹之间穿梭。 他正用拐杖向出租车司机示意。

“Mr. Dickens!” cried Annie. She started into the street, but Jack grabbed her just in time. Another horse and cab clattered by, barely missing them. “先生。 狄更斯！”安妮喊道。 她刚走进街上，但杰克及时抓住了她。 另一辆马车叮叮当当地驶过，差点就撞到他们。

“He’s getting away!” said Annie. “他要逃跑了！”安妮说。

“I know,” said Jack. “But we don’t want to get run over!” “我知道，”杰克说。 “但我们可不想被撞到！”

“Look!” said Annie. “There’s the driver who brought us here! I’ll bet he’s waiting for us!” “看！”安妮说。 “那就是带我们来这里的人！ 我敢打赌他正在等我们！”，

Across the street was the red cab pulled by the small, fat horse. 街对面停着一辆红色的马车，由一匹又小又胖的马拉着。

“Great!” said Jack. He dodged the carriage traffic and rushed toward the driver. “Hi! Hi there!” Jack yelled. “Thanks for coming back! We need a ride! We have to follow—” He started to climb into the cab. “太好了！”杰克说。 他躲开了马车，朝司机冲了过去。 “你好！ 嗨，那边的！”杰克喊道。 “谢谢你们回来！我们需要搭车！ 我们必须跟着——”他开始爬进马车。

“Sorry, no free rides today, boy!” the driver said. “I’ve got mouths to feed at home!” He jiggled his reins, and his horse took off. “对不起，今天不提供免费搭车，孩子！”司机说。 “我家里还有人要养呢！”他晃了晃缰绳，马儿立刻跑了起来。

Jack nearly fell backward onto the sidewalk. “Wait! Don’t you remember us?” he called. “We’re the young gentlemen from Frog Creek!” 杰克差点被甩到人行道上。 “等等！ 你不记得我们了吗？”他喊道。 “我们是来自蛙溪镇的年轻绅士！”

But the driver didn’t seem to hear him in all the traffic. 但司机似乎在这么多的车流中没听到他说话。

Annie touched Jack’s arm. “He didn’t recognize us. We don’t look like gentlemen anymore,” she said. “Our clothes are ragged, and we’re covered with soot.” 安妮碰了碰杰克的手臂。 “他没认出我们。 我们现在看起来不像绅士了，”她说。 “我们的衣服破破烂烂的，而且满身都是煤灰。”

“Oh, man,” said Jack. “Everyone was nice to us when we looked rich. Now it feels like the whole world’s against us.” “哦，天哪，”杰克说。 “当我们看起来有钱的时候，每个人都对我们很好。 现在感觉整个世界都在和我们作对。”

“I’m sorry, it’s all my fault,” said Annie. “对不起，都是我的错，”安妮说。

“Forget it,” said Jack. “We just have to find Mr. Dickens. Come on. Let’s try to find his carriage.” “忘了它，”杰克说。 “我们只需要找到狄更斯先生。 快点。 让我们试着找到他的马车。”

Clutching his green bag, Jack led the way down the street. He and Annie half ran, half walked past a long row of shops. As they looked for Mr. Dickens, they passed little girls sewing in the window of a dress shop. They saw boys sweeping trash and polishing boots, and girls selling matches and meat pies. Jack had never seen so many kids working at real jobs. 杰克抓着他的绿色袋子，带头沿着街道走下去。 他和安妮一边跑一边走，经过一排长长的商店。 当他们寻找先生时 狄更斯，他们看到小女孩们在服装店的窗户前缝衣服。 他们看到男孩们在扫垃圾、擦靴子，还有女孩在卖火柴和肉馅饼。 杰克从没见过这么多孩子在做真正的工怍。

“Hey, isn’t that him getting out of that carriage?” said Annie. She pointed to an intersection in the distance. “嘿，那不是他从马车里出来吗？”安妮说。 她指着远处的一个十字路口。

A small man wearing a top hat and carrying a walking stick was climbing down from a cab. 一个戴礼帽、拿着拐杖的小个子男人正从一辆马车里下来。

“Yeah, I think it is!” said Jack. “Hurry!” “是的，我想是的！”杰克说。 “快点！”

Jack and Annie ran up the sidewalk, dodging shoppers and merchants. By the time they got to the intersection, Mr. Dickens had disappeared again. 杰克和安妮沿着人行道跑起来，躲开了购物的人和商贩。 等他们跑到十字路口时，先生。 狄更斯又不见了。

“Darn,” said Annie. “真烦人，”安妮说。

“Let’s keep looking,” said Jack. “If we don’t find him soon, we’ll go back to his house and wait for him outside the gate.” “我们继续找吧，”杰克说。 “如果我们不能很快找到他，我们就得回到他家，在大门外等着他。”

A light rain began to fall as Jack and Annie started down a crowded, muddy road. They passed shabby shops and rows of small shacks. They passed vendors selling secondhand clothes and hats and shoes. They saw lots of ragged kids hanging around the street. 杰克和安妮开始沿着一条拥挤而泥泞的路走下去，天上下起了细雨。 他们经过了一些破旧的商店和一排排的小棚屋。 他们经过了一些卖二手衣服、帽子和鞋子的商贩。 他们看到街上有很多衣衫褴褛的孩子在四处闲逛。

Jack caught the eye of a big, tough-looking boy slouching against a lamppost with his hands in his pockets. As Jack passed him, the boy looked him over. Jack saw the boy say something to another kid. The two of them started walking after Jack and Annie. 杰克注意到一个看起来又高又壮的男孩，正懒洋洋地靠在路灯杆上，双手插在口袋里。 当杰克从他身边经过时，那个男孩打量了他一番。 杰克看到那个男孩对另一个孩子说了些什么。 那两个孩子开始跟着杰克和安妮走。

“I think we’re being followed,” Jack said. “我觉得我们被人跟踪了，”杰克说。

“Walk faster,” said Annie. “走快点，”安妮说。

As Jack and Annie hurried up the muddy street, black smoke from chimneys blended with the rain. The air felt grimy and dirty. 杰克和安妮匆忙沿着泥泞的街道跑上去，烟囱冒出的黑烟和雨水混在一起。 空气又脏又臭。

Jack glanced back. The two boys were getting closer to them. 杰克回头看了看。 那两个男孩正越来越靠近他们。

“Run!” said Jack. “跑！”，杰克喊道。

Jack and Annie ran past a butcher shop, a bakery, and a cigar store. Jack looked over his shoulder. The boys were running, too! 杰克和安妮跑过了肉铺、面包店和雪茄店。 杰克回头看了看。 那两个男孩也在跑！

Jack slipped in the mud and fell. Before he could get up, the tough-looking kid caught up with him. The boy grabbed Jack’s green velvet bag and took off. The magic violin, the bow, and their book were in the bag! 杰克在泥里滑了一跤，摔倒了。 杰克还没站起来，那个看起来很凶的男孩就追上他了。 那个男孩抓起杰克的绿色天鹅绒袋子就跑掉了。 魔法小提琴、琴弓和他们的书都在袋子里！

“Help! Stop him!” cried Jack. “He’s got my bag!” “救命！ 抓住他！”杰克喊道。 “他拿了我的包！”

Jack jumped up from the mud and charged after the kid. But the big boy tossed the bag to the other boy. 杰克从泥里跳起来，冲着那个男孩追了过去。 但那个大男孩把包扔给了另一个男孩。

“That one’s got it now!” Annie yelled, pointing. “现在那个男孩有包了！”安妮喊道，指着那个男孩。

Jack and Annie took off after the boy with the stolen bag. Fierce anger made Jack run as fast as he could. He caught up with the kid and wrestled the bag away from him. Then Jack turned around and started running back the way they’d come. Annie followed. 杰克和安妮赶紧追上了拿着偷来的包的男孩。 强烈的愤怒让杰克跑得飞快。 他追上那个男孩，把袋子从他手里抢了过来。 然后杰克转身，沿着他们来的路跑回去。安妮跟在后面。

“Stop, thief!” the boy yelled. “住手，小偷！”那个男孩喊道。

Jack and Annie kept running through the black rain, passing the same shops again. 杰克和安妮继续在黑雨中奔跑，又经过了那些同样的商店。

“Stop, thief!” Both boys were yelling now. “Stop, thief!” “住手，小偷！”现在两个男孩都在喊。 “住手，小偷！”，

Thief! Jack thought wildly. Why were they calling him thief? 小偷！ 杰克疯狂地想着。 他们为什么叫他小偷？

But others quickly joined the two boys, chasing after Jack and Annie: 但很快，其他一些人也加入了那两个男孩，追着杰克和安妮：

“Stop, thief!” the butcher yelled. “住手，小偷！”肉贩子喊道。

“Stop, thief!” the baker yelled. “住手，小偷！”面包师喊道。

“Stop, thief!” the cigar seller yelled. “住手，小偷！”香烟商喊道。

Even dogs and old ladies joined the chase through the muddy streets. 甚至狗和老奶奶也加入了这场在泥泞街道上的追逐。

Jack looked over his shoulder. The two boys and a bunch of grown-ups and dogs were running after him and Annie. Everyone was yelling, “Stop, thief!” 杰克回头看了看。 那两个男孩和一群大人、狗正追着他们俩。 每个人都喊着："住手，小偷！"

“What should we do?” cried Annie. “我们该怎么办？”安妮喊道。

“Keep running,” Jack answered. He clutched the green bag closer to his chest and spotted an alley up ahead. “Turn right!” he called. “继续跑，”杰克回答。 他紧紧抓住绿色的袋子，把它贴在胸前，然后看见前方有一条小巷子。 “向右转！”他喊道。

Jack and Annie ran into the narrow alleyway filled with junk—broken wagon wheels, cracked plates, old pots and pans. The crowd followed them down the alley. Everyone kept shrieking, “Stop, thief!” 杰克和安妮跑进了这条狭窄的小巷，里面堆满了杂物——破旧的车轮、碎裂的盘子、老旧的锅碗瓢盆。 人群跟着他们跑进了小巷。 大家不停地尖叫着：‘抓住小偷！’

As the black rain fell, Jack and Annie scrambled over the rubble, desperately looking for an escape. Soon they came to a dead end. There was nowhere else to run! 随着黑色的雨水落下，杰克和安妮在废墟上慌乱地爬行，拼命寻找逃生的路。 很快，他们来到了一条死胡同。 他们无处可逃了！

Jack and Annie turned around. The crowd was closing in on them. Jack clutched the green bag in his hand. “Get away from us!” he shouted. “This is my bag! It’s mine!” 杰克和安妮转过身来。 人群正一步步向他们逼近。 杰克紧紧抓着手里绿色的袋子。 “离我们远点！”他喊道。 “这是我的袋子！是我的！”

But the crowd kept moving toward him, led by the two boys who’d stolen the bag. They sneered at Jack. “We’ve got you now, thief,” said one. 但是人群继续朝他靠近，由那两个偷了袋子的男孩带头。 他们对杰克冷笑。 “现在我们抓住你了，小偷，”一个人说。

“Just hand the bag over to us,” said the other, “or we’ll have to take it.” “把袋子交给我们，”另一个人说，“否则我们就得自己拿走了。”

“Play the violin, Jack!” said Annie. “杰克，拉小提琴吧！”安妮说。

Of course! thought Jack. Only magic could save them now! Jack turned his back to the crowd. But before he could unbuckle the bag, someone grabbed him by his shirt collar. Jack looked up. 当然！杰克心想。 现在只有魔法才能救他们了！ 杰克背对着人群。 但就在他还没解开背包的时候，有人抓住了他的衣领。杰克抬头看了看。

A large man wearing a blue uniform loomed over him. “I’ll take that bag, boy,” the policeman said, holding out his hand. 一个穿着蓝色制服的大个子男人站在他面前。 “我把这个包拿走吧，孩子，”警察说，伸出手。
