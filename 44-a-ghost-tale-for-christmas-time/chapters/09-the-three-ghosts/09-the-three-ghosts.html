9. The Three Ghosts 9. 三个幽灵

“A ghost story?” said Jack. “You mean we’ll make ghosts appear?” “一个鬼故事？”杰克说。 “你是说我们要让鬼出现吗？”

“Yep,” said Annie. “对。”安妮说。

“Um … I don’t know,” said Jack. Ghosts made him nervous. “嗯……我不知道，”杰克说。 鬼魂让他感到紧张。

“I promise this will work,” said Annie. “我保证这会奏效，”安妮说。

“But I don’t get it,” said Jack. “A ghost story. How can we change Charles with that?” “但是我不明白，”杰克说。 “一个鬼故事。 我们怎么用这个来改变查尔斯呢？”

“We won’t change him. The ghosts will change him, just like they changed Scrooge,” said Annie. “Our job is just to make the right ghosts appear.” “我们不会改变他。 鬼魂会改变他，就像他们改变了斯克鲁奇一样，”安妮说。 “我们的任务只是让正确的鬼魂出现。”

“But—” started Jack. “但是——”杰克开始说。

“It’s magic, Jack,” said Annie. “We have to trust the magic.” “这是魔法，杰克，”安妮说。 “我们必须相信魔法。”

“But Charles doesn’t even want us around,” said Jack. “但是查尔斯甚至都不想让我们靠近，”杰克说。

“That’s okay,” said Annie. “He doesn’t have to see us. He can believe it’s all happening in his imagination. He has a great imagination, you know. Come on, before he leaves.” “没关系，”安妮说。 “他不需要看到我们。 他可以相信这一切都是发生在他的想象中。 你知道，他有很棒的想象力。 快点，不然他要离开了。”

Jack sighed. “Okay,” he said. “But I hope we don’t give him a heart attack.” He unbuckled the green velvet bag and pulled out the violin and the bow. 杰克叹了口气。 “好吧，”他说。 “但是希望我们不要把他吓出心脏病。”他解开了绿色天鹅绒袋子，拿出小提琴和琴弓。

Jack rested the bow against the strings of the violin. Then he began moving the bow back and forth. The violin’s music was soft at first, but as it grew louder, it seemed to come from every direction. 杰克把琴弓放在小提琴的琴弦上。 然后他开始来回移动琴弓。 小提琴的音乐一开始很轻柔，但随着声音变大，似乎从四面八方传来。

At the bottom of the hill, Charles lifted his head. 在山脚下的查尔斯抬起头来。

Annie started singing in a soft, whispery voice: 安妮用轻柔、细声细气的声音开始唱歌：

Come, three ghosts,in a dreamlike swirl. Help our friend give his gifts to the world. 来吧，三个幽灵，在梦幻般的旋涡中出现。帮助我们的朋友把礼物送给世界。

The streetlamp flickered and went out. Then a hazy glow surrounded Charles. He stood up and looked around. In the haze, a ghostly figure swirled into shape. 街灯闪烁了一下，然后熄灭了。 接着，一团模糊的光芒包围了查尔斯。 他站了起来，环顾四周。 在朦胧中，一个幽灵般的身影逐渐成形。

Jack’s heart pounded as he kept playing. 杰克的心跳加快，他继续玩着。

Charles cried out and stumbled back. 查尔斯喊了出来，然后往后退了一步。

The ghost looked like both a child and an old man. He had long white hair, but there was not a wrinkle on his face. He wore a white tunic with a silver belt and carried a holly branch in his hand. 这个幽灵看起来既像一个孩子，又像一个老人。 他有一头长长的白发，但脸上却没有一丝皱纹。 他穿着一件白色长袍，腰间系着一条银色腰带，手里拿着一根冬青树枝。

Annie sang on: 安妮开始唱道：

Do not fear him, Charles,stand fast. He’s come to share a Christmas Past. 不要害怕他，查尔斯，站稳了。他来和你分享过去的圣诞节。

The ghost waved his hand, and a scene slowly appeared in the glowing fog: a small, frail boy was lying on a heap of rags. 鬼魂挥了挥手，发光的雾气中慢慢出现了一幕场景：一个瘦小的男孩正躺在一堆破布上。

“Who are you? And why do you show me this poor child?” cried Charles. “你是谁？ 安妮，你为什么带我去看这个可怜的孩子？”查尔斯喊道。

The boy rolled over and sat up. He had big eyes and wavy brown hair. 男孩翻了个身，坐了起来。 他有一双大眼睛，还有波浪形的棕色头发。

Charles gasped. “Oh, my!” he said. “Is that … is that me?” 查尔斯惊呼起来。 “哦，天哪！”他说。 “那是……那是我吗？”

The boy reached under the rags and pulled out a book. On the cover was a picture of a flying carpet. The boy opened the book and smiled. 男孩在破布下伸手，拿出了一本书。 封面上画着一张飞毯的图案。 男孩打开书，笑了。

“I remember that book! It is me!” cried Charles. “I loved to read The Arabian Nights! I felt as if I could ride on a flying carpet myself! Books gave me hope when I had no hope. But why do you show me this now?” “我记得这本书！” “是我！”查尔斯喊道。 “我特别喜欢读《一千零一夜》！ 我感觉我好像自己也能骑上飞毯！ 当我没有希望的时候，书给了我希望。 但是，为什么你现在要给我看这些呢？”

The ghost did not answer. He raised his pale hand in farewell. 鬼魂没有回答。 他抬起苍白的手，挥手告别。

“Wait!” cried Charles. “等等！”查尔斯喊道。

But the ghost faded into the haze. And the vision of young Charles Dickens faded with him. 但鬼魂渐渐消失在雾中。 年轻查尔斯·狄更斯的幻影也随着他一起消失了。

The glow still surrounded Charles. Jack’s violin music swelled with deep tones. Annie sang on. 光仍然环绕着查尔斯。 杰克的小提琴音乐响起，低沉的音调回荡。安妮继续唱着歌。

Come to Charles,O second ghost. It’s your turn now to be the host.… 来吧，查尔斯，第二个幽灵。现在轮到你当主人了……

The haze became rose-colored. It swirled in the shape of a small cyclone. The funnel of vapor spun wider and wider, until out from its center stepped a giant in a green robe. He had a bushy brown beard and wore a crown of icicles. He held a flaming torch high into the air. 雾气变成了玫瑰色。 它旋转成一个小旋风。 一团蒸汽形成的漏斗越转越大，最后从中心走出了一位身穿绿色长袍的巨人。 他有一把浓密的棕色胡须，头上戴着冰凌做的王冠。 他高高举起一支燃烧的火把。

“Who are you? What do you want with me?” cried Charles. “你是谁？ 你想要什么？”查尔斯喊道。

The ghost pointed into the mist and bellowed: 鬼魂指着迷雾大喊：

You say you’ll write not one more book? Stop your weeping, man,and look! “你说你要写完最后一本书？别再哭了，快看！”

Charles stared as human shapes began to form in the mist: a young Victorian couple stood at a bookstall. “I’m getting the new book by Charles Dickens! I love Mr. Dickens!” 查尔斯目瞪口呆地看着迷雾中逐渐显现出人影：一对维多利亚时代的年轻夫妇站在一个书摊前。 “我要买查尔斯·狄更斯的新书！我超爱狄更斯！”， “狄更斯！”

“I know you do, my dear! And so do I!” “我知道你这么做，亲爱的！我也一样！”

The couple laughed joyfully, and another scene appeared beside them: a teacher stood in front of schoolchildren. “So what did we learn today from Mr. Dickens’s book?” she asked. 这对夫妇开心地笑了，又一个场景出现在他们身边：一位老师站在一群小学生面前。 “所以今天我们从先生那里学到了什么” “狄更斯的书？”她问。

“We learned to be more generous and kind!” a small girl answered. “我们学会了更加慷慨和善良！”一个小女孩回答。

“Yes!” the class shouted. “是！”同学们喊道。

As the children cheered, another scene took shape: Queen Victoria was sitting on her throne! 孩子们欢呼时，又出现了一幕场景：维多利亚女王正坐在她的王座上！

“I say,” the queen said to a lady-in-waiting. “This Oliver Twist book is exceedingly interesting. Poor Oliver. I had no idea children in our kingdom lived such dreadful lives.” “我说，”女王对一位侍女说道。 “这本《雾都孤儿》书非常有趣。可怜的奥利弗。 我完全不知道我们王国里的孩子们过着这么悲惨的生活。”

The queen faded away. The couple faded away—and the teacher and the children. Everything faded away except the giant ghost. 女王渐渐消失了。 那对夫妇也渐渐消失了——老师和孩子们也都消失了。 除了那个巨大的幽灵，一切都不见了。

“They were all speaking about my books,” Charles said in a voice filled with wonder. “他们都在谈论我的书，”查尔斯用充满惊奇的声音说道。

The giant ghost boomed: 巨大的幽灵大声说道：

Yet writing is nothing but paper and ink! A foolish task! Is that not what you think? 然而写作不过就是纸和墨水！一项愚蠢的任务！难道你不是这么认为的吗？

“Yes! No! Yes, but …,” said Charles. “是！不是！ “是的，但是……”查尔斯说道。

The ghost shook his head sadly. Then he, too, vanished into the fog. 鬼魂伤心地摇了摇头。 接着，他也消失在了雾中。

Annie sang on: 安妮唱道：

Think about all you’ve been shown by this ghost,and get ready to meet your last ghostly host. 想想这个鬼魂向你展示的一切，准备好去见最后一个幽灵主人吧。

The rosy glow faded to an eerie silver light. The air grew very cold. Then, like a dark shadow, a third ghost silently appeared. This ghost wore a black cape with the hood pulled over its head. 玫瑰色的光芒渐渐褪去，变成了一种诡异的银色光线。 空气变得非常寒冷。 然后，就像一团黑暗的影子，第三个鬼魂悄无声息地出现了。 这个幽灵穿着一件黑色斗篷，兜帽盖住了它的头。

Charles raised his walking stick to protect himself. Jack trembled with cold and fear, but he kept playing the violin. 查尔斯举起他的拐杖，保护自己。 杰克因为寒冷和害怕发抖，但他仍然继续拉小提琴。

The ghost glided slowly toward Charles. 幽灵缓缓地朝查尔斯滑去。

“Who are you? What do you want?” Charles cried. “你是谁？ 你想要什么？”查尔斯喊道。

A scene swirled into view beside the ghost: a group of people stood in a graveyard. A young woman was weeping near a tombstone. 一个场景在幽灵旁边旋转着出现：一群人站在墓地里。 一个年轻女子在墓碑旁哭泣。

“Why—why do you show me this scene of grief?” asked Charles. “为什么——为什么你要给我看这个悲伤的场景？”查尔斯问道。

The ghost pointed a bony finger at the tombstone. 幽灵用一根骨头手指指向墓碑。

Charles lowered his walking stick and crept closer. He squinted at the writing on the tombstone. He gasped. “It’s my name!” he cried. “My name is on that stone!” 查尔斯放下了他的拐杖，悄悄地靠近了。 他眯着眼看墓碑上的字。他惊呼了一声。 “这是我的名字！”他喊道。 “我的名字刻在那块石头上！”，

“Poor Papa,” the young woman said to the other mourners. “How sad that he stopped writing when he was so young. So many beautiful words might have been written. So many wonderful characters might have lived. If only he’d given more stories to the world, he might have touched the hearts of millions.” The woman broke down in tears again. “可怜的爸爸，”年轻的女人对其他哀悼者说。 “真遗憾，他年轻时就停止写作了。 他本可以写出那么多美丽的文字。 他本可以让那么多精彩的角色活下来。 如果他能给世界留下更多的故事，他也许能打动数百万人的心。”女人又哭了起来。

“Stop! Stop!” cried Charles. “I can’t bear it!” “住手！ “停！”查尔斯喊道。 “我受不了了！”

The scene began to fade. 场景开始变淡了。

“Wait!” Charles shouted to the ghost. “I’ve changed my mind! Tell her I will keep writing! Wait!” “等等！”查尔斯对鬼魂喊道。 “我改变了主意！ 告诉她我会继续写的！等一下！“

But the ghost and the mourners and the tombstone had already disappeared into the fog. Charles Dickens stood alone in the cold silver light. 但是那个鬼魂、哀悼的人和墓碑早已消失在雾中了。 查尔斯·狄更斯独自站在寒冷的银色灯光下。

Jack played one long, low note on the magic violin. 杰克用魔法小提琴拉出一个长长的、低沉的音符。

Then there was silence. 然后，一切归于寂静。
