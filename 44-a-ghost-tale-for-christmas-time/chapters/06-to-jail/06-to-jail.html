6. To Jail 6. 进监狱

Jack handed the policeman his green bag. “But it’s mine, sir!” he tried to explain. “I promise it’s mine!” 杰克把他的绿色背包交给了警察。 “但是这是我的，先生！”他试图解释。 “我保证这是我的！”

“No, it’s mine!” said the kid who’d stolen the bag. “It belonged to my dear, departed father.” “不，这是我的！”偷了包的那个孩子说。 “这个包是我已故的父亲的。”

“It is not yours!” Jack said, furious. “这不是你的！”杰克生气地说。

“It is!” the boy shouted. “Take him to jail, Officer!” “是的！”那个男孩喊道。 “把他送去监狱，警官！”

“Stand back!” the policeman shouted at the kid. “Don’t worry, I will be puttin’ him in jail. But I might be puttin’ you there, too!” “退后！”警察对那个孩子喊道。 “别担心，我会把他送去监狱的。 但我也可能会把你送去那儿！”

“Jail?” said Jack. “监狱？”杰克说。

“But, sir, it really is our bag,” said Annie. “Those two kids stole it from us, and my brother just grabbed it back and—” “但是，先生，这真的是我们的包，”安妮说。 “那两个孩子从我们这儿偷走了它，我弟弟刚刚把它抢了回来，然后——”

“Quiet!” the policeman said. “Someone stole it—whether it’s him or him, or maybe you, we’ll soon find out.” “安静！”警察说。 “有人偷了它——不管是他还是他，或者也许是你，我们很快就会知道。”

“But I can tell you what’s in it!” said Jack. “I can prove—” “但我可以告诉你里面有什么！”杰克说。 “我可以证明——”

“Quiet! You can make your case to the chief inspector at Scotland Yard!” said the policeman. “Step aside! Let us through!” he yelled at the crowd. “安静！”， “你可以去苏格兰场向首席侦探提出你的证据！”警察说。 “让开！”， “让我们过去！”他对人群喊道。

As the crowd parted to make a path, Jack saw the tough kid and his friend slip away down the alley. “They’re running away, sir!” he said. 当人群分开让出一条路时，杰克看见那个顽皮的孩子和他的朋友溜进了小巷。 “他们在逃跑，先生！”他说。

But the policeman ignored him. “March!” he ordered. 但警察无视了他。 “出发！”他下令道。

Annie grabbed Jack’s hand and walked close to him. “I’m sorry,” she said, near tears. “I’m so sorry I made us give up our nice clothes.” 安妮抓住杰克的手，走到他身边。 “对不起，”她说着，眼眶都红了。 “真的很对不起，我让我们丢掉了漂亮的衣服。”

“Don’t worry,” said Jack. “You didn’t know this would happen.” He wasn’t mad at Annie. He was mad at the boys who had tried to steal his bag. And he was mad at Merlin, Teddy, and Kathleen! Why had they sent him and Annie to such a terrible place? He didn’t care at all about helping crazy Mr. Dickens anymore. He just wished they were back home in Frog Creek. “别担心，”杰克说。 “你根本不知道会发生这样的事。”他并没有生安妮的气。 他生那些试图偷他包的男孩的气。 他还生梅林、泰迪和凯瑟琳的气！ 为什么他们要把他和安妮送到这么糟糕的地方？ 他现在完全不想再去帮那个疯疯癫癫的狄更斯先生了。 他只是希望他们能回到蛙溪镇的家。

“This way!” the policeman ordered. He gave Jack a little push. Jack and Annie turned out of the alley and headed down the street. They walked through the smothering coal smoke and grimy rain. “这边走！”警察命令道。 他轻轻推了推杰克。 杰克和安妮从小巷里转出来，沿着街道走下去。 他们穿过浓重的煤烟和肮脏的雨水。

“Left!” the policeman shouted. “左！”警察喊道。

Jack and Annie turned left. The crowd still followed them. Jack kept his head down. He couldn’t bear to look at the gawkers. 杰克和安妮向左转。 人群仍然跟着他们。 杰克低着头。 他实在受不了看那些围观的人。

“Hello!” Annie suddenly shouted. “It’s us! Remember us?” “你好！”安妮突然喊道。 “是我们！还记得我们吗？”

Jack looked up. Who was Annie shouting to? 杰克抬头看了看。 安妮是在对谁喊呢？

Standing on the other side of the street was Mr. Dickens. 街道另一边站着狄更斯先生。

“It’s us!” Annie called again. “The chimney sweeps in your study! Remember?” “是我们！”安妮又喊了一遍。 “你书房里的扫烟囱工人！还记得吗？”

Mr. Dickens scowled, but he started to follow them, walking behind the crowd. 先生。 狄更斯皱了皱眉，但开始跟着他们，走在人群后面。

“Help us!” Annie shouted. “Please help us!” “帮帮我们！”安妮喊道。 “请帮帮我们！”

“Quiet, boy!” the policeman barked at Annie. “安静，男孩！”警察对安妮吼道。

Mr. Dickens moved briskly toward Jack and Annie, pushing his way through the crowd. “Excuse me, Officer!” he said. 先生。 狄更斯迅速朝杰克和安妮走去，穿过人群。 “打扰一下，警官！”他说。

The policeman stopped. He squinted at the small, well-dressed man. “Mr. Dickens? Mr. Charles Dickens?” he said with wonder. 警察停下了。 他眯着眼睛看着那个穿着得体的小个子男人。 “狄更斯先生？ 先生。 查尔斯·狄更斯？”，他惊讶地说。

Gasps and whispers went up from the crowd. “It’s him. It’s Charles Dickens, the writer!” the baker said. 人群里响起了一阵惊呼和窃窃私语。 “是他。 是查尔斯·狄更斯，这位作家！”面包师说。

“Yes, it is I,” said Mr. Dickens. “I know these sweeps. What seems to be the problem, Officer?” “没错，是我，”狄更斯先生说。 “我知道这些扫烟囱的人。 “杰克，怎么了，警官？”

“The boy stole this bag, sir.” The policeman held up the green velvet bag. “这个男孩偷了这个包，先生。”警察举起了那个绿色的天鹅绒包。

“When? When did he steal that bag?” asked Mr. Dickens. “什么时候？ “他什么时候偷的那只包？”狄更斯先生问。

“Just now, sir. I caught him trying to run off with it,” said the policeman. “就刚才，先生。” “我抓住他时，他正试图带着它逃跑，”警察说。

“Well, I’m afraid you’re mistaken, Officer,” said Mr. Dickens. “These lads were working at my house earlier today. They had that green bag with them then.” “噢，恐怕你搞错了，警官，”狄更斯先生说。 “今天早些时候，这几个孩子在我家干活。 他们那时带着那个绿色的包。”

“Ah, did they now?” said the policeman, looking at Jack. “啊，是吗？”警察看着杰克说。

Jack nodded. 杰克点了点头。

“This lad is innocent,” said Mr. Dickens. He turned and spoke to the crowd. “Do you see what has happened here? If I had not come along, the courts might have thrown this boy in jail for years. Why? Because he has soot on his face and holes in his shoes. How do you think this lad got so dirty? I ask all of you!” “这个男孩是无辜的，”狄更斯先生说。 他转身对人群说话。 “你们看到这里发生了什么吗？ 如果我没来，法院可能会让这个男孩坐牢很多年。为什么？ 因为他的脸上沾满了煤灰，鞋子上还有破洞。 你们觉得这个男孩怎么会弄这么脏呢？ 我问你们所有人！”，

No one in the crowd answered. 人群中没有人回答。

“I’ll tell you how,” said Mr. Dickens. “From honest work. And now all of you want to put this innocent, hardworking lad into prison?” “我来告诉你们是怎么回事，”狄更斯先生说。 “来自诚实的劳动。 现在你们还想把这位无辜、勤劳的男孩送进监狱吗？

The butcher, the baker, and the cigar seller lowered their gazes. The policeman looked ashamed, too. “Mr. Dickens, forgive me. I shall release him at once,” he said. 肉贩、面包师和雪茄商人都低下了头。 警察也显得很羞愧。 “先生 狄更斯，原谅我。 我立刻放了他，”他说。

“Yes, Officer, release this lad. Return his bag, and think twice before you arrest another child just because he is ragged and poor,” said Mr. Dickens. “是的，警官，放了这个男孩。 把他的包还给他，并且在下次因为孩子又穷又破旧就抓他之前，好好想一想，”狄更斯先生说。

The policeman handed the green velvet bag to Jack. “Your bag, boy,” he said. “Good luck on your journey through life. God bless you. And God bless you, Mr. Dickens. Good day.” 警察把那绿色的天鹅绒包递给了杰克。 “你的包，男孩，”他说。 “祝你一生的旅程好运。上帝保佑你。 上帝也保佑你，狄更斯先生。 祝你今天愉快。”

“Thank you, Officer. And one last word to all of you,” Mr. Dickens said to the crowd. “Remember, goodness dresses in rags and patches as often as it dresses in velvet and silk.” “谢谢您，警官。 “还有最后一件事要对你们所有人说，”先生说。 狄更斯对人群说道。 “记住，善良常常穿着破旧的补丁衣服，就像它有时穿着华丽的绸缎一样。”

The crowd was silent for a moment. Then some people broke into applause. 人群沉默了一会儿。 然后有些人开始鼓起掌来。

Mr. Dickens tipped his hat. He put his hands on Jack’s and Annie’s shoulders. “Come, lads. I will walk with you a ways, so no one else will prey upon you,” he said. 先生 狄更斯摘下帽子。 他把手放在杰克和安妮的肩膀上。 “来吧，孩子们。 我会陪你们走一段路，这样就不会有人欺负你们了，”他说。

“Thanks,” Jack said hoarsely. “谢谢，”杰克沙哑地说。

Mr. Dickens guided Jack and Annie away from the crowd. He steered them across the street. 先生。 狄更斯带杰克和安妮离开了人群。 他领着他们穿过街道。

“Thanks for helping us,” said Annie. “We’re sorry we ruined your work today. We didn’t mean to.” “谢谢你们帮助我们，”安妮说。 “对不起，今天我们毁了你的工作。 我们不是故意的。”

“Oh, no, you didn’t ruin my work,” said Mr. Dickens. “It was just that the sudden sight of you reminded me of all the children who sweep our chimneys and work in our mines and factories. You suffer.…” He shook his head. Then he looked at them and tried to smile. “Forgive me, lads. I needn’t tell you about your own hard lives. Instead, you both must tell me about yourselves.” “哦，不，你们没有毁了我的工作，”狄更斯先生说。 “只是突然看到你们，让我想起了所有扫烟囱的孩子，还有在矿井和工厂里工作的孩子们。 你们受苦了……”他摇了摇头。 然后他看着他们，努力笑了笑。 “原谅我，孩子们。” 我用不着告诉你你们自己有多辛苦的生活。 相反，你们两个必须告诉我你们自己是谁。

“Well, first, I’m not a lad,” said Annie. “I’m a girl. My name is Annie.” She took off her cap, and her pigtails fell to her shoulders. “好吧，首先，我不是个男孩，”安妮说。 “我是个女孩。” 我的名字是安妮。”她摘下帽子，她的两个辫子垂到了肩膀上。

Mr. Dickens’s big eyes grew bigger. “Why, I am speechless!” he sputtered. 先生。 狄更斯的大眼睛变得更大了。 “天哪，我简直说不出话了！”，他结结巴巴地说。

Annie shrugged. “Sorry, Mr. Dickens, but this is the real me.” 安妮耸了耸肩。 “对不起，先生。 “狄更斯，但这才是真正的我。”

“Well—well, Annie, I’m delighted to meet the real you!” Mr. Dickens looked at Jack. “And are you also a little girl in disguise?” he asked. “嗯——嗯，安妮，我很高兴见到真正的你！”先生。 狄更斯看着杰克。 “你也是个假扮成小女孩的人吗？”他问。

“No!” said Jack. “I’m her brother, Jack.” “不是！”杰克说。 “我是她的哥哥，杰克。”

“I see,” said Mr. Dickens. “So you are Jack and Annie. Well! I would like it if you both called me Charles.” “我明白了，”狄更斯先生说。 “所以你就是杰克和安妮。哇！ 我希望你们都叫我查尔斯。

“Thank you, Charles,” said Annie. “谢谢你，查尔斯，”安妮说。

“So now, Jack and Annie, what exactly happened today with the policeman and the mob?” asked Charles. “那么，杰克和安妮，今天到底发生了什么，和警察以及那群人有关吗？”查尔斯问。

“Tell him, Jack,” said Annie. “你告诉他，杰克，”安妮说。

“No, you tell him,” said Jack. He felt too upset to talk about it. “不，你告诉他，”杰克说。 他太难过了，说不出话来。

“Okay, Charles, I’ll tell you,” said Annie. “好吧，查尔斯，我会告诉你的，”安妮说。

As Annie told the story of how the boys had stolen the bag, Charles listened carefully, nodding and frowning. His eyebrows jumped up and down. His mouth twitched. He seemed to be feeling everything Annie was describing. 当安妮讲述那两个男孩是如何偷走袋子的时候，查尔斯认真地听着，点头又皱眉。 他的眉毛上下跳动着。 他的嘴巴抽动了一下。 他似乎能感受到安妮描述的一切。

Finally Annie finished, saying, “And then you came, and you know the rest.” 终于，安妮讲完了，说：“然后你就来了，你知道后面发生的事。”

“Remarkable!” said Charles. “太神奇了！”查尔斯说。

“Yes, it was remarkable,” Jack said bitterly. “是啊，这确实很神奇，”杰克苦笑着说。

“No, it is remarkable because it is exactly like a scene in my book Oliver Twist!” said Charles. “Oliver is wrongly accused of picking the pocket of an old man. And the real thieves lead the chase, yelling, ‘Stop, thief!’” “不，这很神奇，因为它和我书里的《雾都孤儿》里的一幕一模一样！”查尔斯说。 “奥利弗被错误地指控偷了老人的钱包。 真正的贼在后面追赶，大喊：‘抓住小偷！’”

“Really?” said Jack. “真的吗？”杰克说。

“Are you serious?” said Annie. “That happens in your book?” “你是认真的吗？”安妮说。 “这发生在你的书里吗？”

“Indeed,” said Charles. “确实如此，”查尔斯说。

“Cool,” said Jack with a little smile. “真酷，”杰克微笑着说道。

“Does that make you feel better?” Annie asked Jack. “这让你感觉好些了吗？”安妮问杰克。

“Yeah, it does, actually,” he said. “It makes me feel like I’m not alone.” “是的，确实感觉好些了，”他说。 “这让我感觉我不再孤单了。”

“Good!” said Charles. “Now, tell me, Jack and Annie, are you hungry?” “很好！”查尔斯说。 “现在，告诉我，杰克和安妮，你们饿吗？”

Jack and Annie nodded. 杰克和安妮点了点头。

“Of course you are!” said Charles. “I imagine you’ve had nothing but watery gruel for days! Let me treat you to a meal that you will never forget! Steak and gravy! Pork pie! Gooseberry jam! Come, we will dine like kings!” He looked at Annie. “And a queen! ” he added with a wink. “当然你们饿了！”查尔斯说。 “我想你们这些天吃的根本就是稀薄的粥！” 让我请你们吃一顿你们永远忘不了的饭！牛排和肉汁！ 猪肉派！醋栗果酱！ 来吧，我们要像国王一样吃饭！“他看着安妮。 “还要像女王一样！“他眨了眨眼。
