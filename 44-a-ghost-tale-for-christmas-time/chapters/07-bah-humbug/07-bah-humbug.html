7. Bah, Humbug! 7. 哎呀，圣诞老人！

Night was falling as Charles led Jack and Annie across the street to an old inn. Candles twinkled in the paned windows. Charles ushered them through the door and into a warm dining room. The room had low ceilings with dark wooden beams. A fire crackled in a huge fireplace at one end. 夜幕降临，查尔斯带领杰克和安妮穿过街道，来到一家老旅馆。 窗子上的玻璃窗里，蜡烛闪烁着微光。 查尔斯让他们走进门，来到一个温暖的餐厅里。 这个房间的天花板很低，上面有深色的木梁。 一端的巨大壁炉里，火苗噼啪作响。

“Ah, Mr. Dickens, welcome! Welcome!” said a man with a pointed nose and small eyes. He bowed low and rubbed his hands together. “啊，狄更斯先生，欢迎！” “欢迎！”一个鼻子尖尖、眼睛小小的男人说道。 他弯下腰，搓了搓手。

“Thank you, Mr. Pinch,” said Charles. “谢谢您，皮奇先生，”查尔斯说。 “您救了我们的命，”安妮说。

“What brings you to my humble inn today?” Mr. Pinch asked. “今天是什么风把你吹到我这间简陋的旅馆来了？”皮奇先生问道。

“I’ve come to dine with my two friends, Mr. Pinch,” said Charles. “我是来和我的两个朋友一起吃饭的，先生。 皮奇，”查尔斯说。

The innkeeper looked down at Jack and Annie. He frowned at their ragged, muddy clothes. “ These are your friends, Mr. Dickens?” he said. He wrinkled his nose as if he smelled something bad. 旅馆老板低头看着杰克和安妮。 他皱着眉头看着他们破旧、沾满泥巴的衣服。 “这些是你的朋友，先生。 狄更斯？”他说道。 他皱起鼻子，好像闻到了什么难闻的东西。

“Yes. They are hardworking children,” said Charles. “And they are quite hungry.” “是的。 他们是勤奋的孩子，”查尔斯说道。 “而且他们非常饿。”

“I see.…” Mr. Pinch looked fretfully around the room. “Well, what about that table in the corner, Mr. Dickens?” “我明白了……先生。 平奇烦躁地环顾了房间一圈。 “那么，狄更斯先生，角落里的那张桌子怎么样？”

“Yes, Mr. Pinch, that will be fine,” said Charles. “是的，先生。 “ pinch，这没问题，”查尔斯说。

Mr. Pinch ushered them to the table. A waiter brought them silverware and lit a candle. Some of the diners began to notice Charles Dickens and whispered to one another. 先生。 皮奇带他们走到桌边。 一位服务员给他们递上银制餐具，还点了一根蜡烛。 一些用餐的客人开始注意到查尔斯·狄更斯，互相小声议论起来。

An elegantly dressed couple came over to the table. “Excuse me, Mr. Dickens,” the woman said shyly. “But I want you to know how much my husband and I love your stories.” 一对穿着优雅的夫妇走到了桌边。 “对不起，先生。 狄更斯，”那位女士害羞地说。 “但我想让您知道，我和我丈夫有多么喜欢您的故事。”

“Why, thank you!” said Charles, grinning. “Tell me, what do you love most about them?” “哦，谢谢您！”查尔斯笑着说。 “告诉我，你最喜欢他们什么？”

As the couple began talking about their favorite scenes from his books, more people gathered around Charles Dickens. 当这对夫妇开始谈论他书中最喜欢的场景时，越来越多的人围到了查尔斯·狄更斯身边。

At the same time, serving people delivered heaping plates of food to the table: baked apples, turkey drumsticks, steak pie, mashed potatoes, brown bread, buttery cheese, dark jam, and steaming cups of tea. Jack and Annie started to eat at once. Gobbling his mashed potatoes, Jack noticed that Charles Dickens didn’t even look at his food. He was too busy laughing and talking with his fans. 与此同时，服务员端着满满一盘食物放到桌子上：烤苹果、火鸡腿、牛排派、土豆泥、全麦面包、黄油奶酪、深色果酱，还有冒着热气的茶杯。 杰克和安妮立刻开始吃起来。 一边大口吃着土豆泥，杰克注意到查尔斯·狄更斯甚至都没看一眼他的食物。 他太忙于和粉丝们笑啊、聊天了。

People all over London love Charles, Jack thought. So why did Merlin send us here? So far Charles was only helping them. They weren’t helping him at all. 杰克想，整个伦敦的人都喜欢查尔斯。 那为什么梅林要派我们来这里呢？ 到目前为止，查尔斯只是在帮助他们。 他们根本没有帮助他。

“Jack, look,” Annie whispered. She pointed toward a window. “杰克，看，”安妮小声说。 她指着一扇窗户。

A man and a small boy were staring through the glass, their faces lit by candlelight. The man was leaning on a crutch. He and the boy were both thin and sad-looking. 一个男人和一个小男孩正透过玻璃盯着看，他们的脸被烛光映照着。 那个男人正倚着一根拐杖。 他和小男孩都很瘦，看起来很伤心。

“They’re staring at Charles,” Jack mumbled, his mouth full of potatoes. “他们在盯着查尔斯看，”杰克嘴里含着土豆，小声嘟囔着。

“No, I think they’re staring at our food,” said Annie. “不，我觉得他们在盯着我们的食物，”安妮说。

While Charles kept talking with his fans, Annie took a drumstick, two pieces of bread, and a chunk of cheese from her plate. She wrapped them in a napkin. Then she slipped away from the table and out the door of the inn. 当查尔斯继续和他的粉丝说话时，安妮从自己的盘子里拿了一块鸡腿、两片面包和一块奶酪。 她用餐巾把它们包起来。 然后她悄悄离开桌子，走出了旅馆的门。

Mr. Pinch cried out. He waved his hands and charged to the door. He yelled in a shrill voice, “Come back here, urchin! Where are you going with that?” 皮奇先生喊了起来。 他挥舞着双手，冲向了门。 他用尖利的声音喊道：“回来这里，小叫花子！ 你拿那个要去哪儿？”

Oh, no, not again! thought Jack. He leapt up and ran to the doorway. He pushed past Mr. Pinch and stepped outside into the chilly air. 哦，不，又来了！杰克心想。 他跳起来，跑到门口。 他推开了皮奇先生。 然后走进了寒冷的空气里。

Annie was standing with the little boy and the man with the crutch. She was offering them her bundle of food. 安妮正和那个小男孩以及拄拐杖的男人站在一起。 她正把一包食物递给他们。

“Don’t you dare give that food away!” cried Mr. Pinch. “你敢把食物给别人就完蛋了！”皮奇先生喊道。

“Why?” said Jack. “She’s not stealing anything. She’s giving them some of her own dinner.” “为什么？”杰克问。 “她什么也没偷。 她只是给了他们一些她自己的晚餐。”

“What is happening here?” said Charles, stepping outside. “这里发生了什么？”查尔斯走出来说道。

The little boy grabbed the bundle of food from Annie. “Thank you,” he said softly, and he and the man took off. The man’s crutch thumped on the pavement as he hobbled away with the boy. 那个小男孩从安妮手里抢走了食物包。 “谢谢，”他轻声说，然后他和那个男人离开了。 那个男人拄着拐杖，一瘸一拐地走开，拐杖敲打着路面。

“That’s right! Get out of here!” Mr. Pinch yelled after them. “No greedy mice begging at my inn! And you, urchin, you had no right to do that!” “没错！ 滚开！”先生 皮奇在后面喊道。 “不许贪心的耗子在我的旅馆门口乞讨！ 至于你，小鬼，你根本没有权利这么做！”

“Mr. Pinch, from what I could tell, my young friend was only showing compassion,” said Charles. “先生。 皮奇，据我所知，我年轻的朋友只是在表达同情心，”查尔斯说。

“Bah! Foolishness!” said Mr. Pinch. “Rumors will spread now that I give away food!” 愚蠢！“皮奇先生说道。 “现在我开始分发食物，谣言就会传开！”

“And what harm would come of that?” Charles asked the innkeeper. “You’re rich enough. You can afford to share a bit with those less fortunate.” “那又会有什么坏处呢？”查尔斯问旅馆老板。 “你已经很富有啦。 你完全可以和那些不幸的人分享一点点。”

“Bah, humbug!” said Mr. Pinch. “Are there no poorhouses to feed them? No workhouses? Let them eat in debtors’ prisons! The father should put the boy to work! There are plenty of factories that would hire him!” “呸，无聊！”皮奇先生说道。 “难道没有贫民院可以养活他们吗？没有劳工所吗？ 让他们在债务监狱里吃东西吧！ 父亲应该让男孩去干活！ 有很多工厂会雇用他！”，

Charles seemed unable to speak as he stared at the stingy man. 查尔斯似乎无法说话，他盯着那个吝啬的男人。

“Charles?” said Annie. “查尔斯？”安妮说道。

Jack didn’t know what was wrong with Charles. But he knew he should get him away from the miserable innkeeper. “Let’s leave,” he said, tugging on Charles’s sleeve. “Let’s go.” 杰克不知道查尔斯怎么了。 但他知道他应该带他离开那个悲惨的旅馆老板。 “我们走吧，”他说着，拽了拽查尔斯的袖子。 “我们走吧。”

Charles looked at Jack. “Yes … yes …,” he said absently. “Indeed.” 查尔斯看着杰克。 “是……是……，”他心不在焉地说。 “确实如此。”

“Wait a second,” said Jack. He dashed back into the warm dining room and grabbed his green velvet bag. On his way out, he noticed that everyone had gone back to their dinners. They were eating and talking, their lives untouched by the sad events outside. “等一下，”杰克说。 他冲回温暖的餐厅，抓起了他的绿色天鹅绒袋子。 在出门的时候，他注意到每个人都回到了自己的晚餐中。 他们一边吃一边聊天，他们的生活没有受到外面悲伤事件的影响。

Jack joined Charles and Annie on the street. “All set,” he said. 杰克加入了查尔斯和安妮，一起走上街道。 “准备好了，”他说。

“Mr. Dickens? Please!” Mr. Pinch whined from the doorway. “Pay me before you leave? Please, sir?” “狄更斯先生？“ 请！” 杰克先生 皮奇从门口抱怨起来。 “你走之前能给我点钱吗？求您了，先生？”

“Yes. Yes … of course.” Charles pulled out his wallet and paid the innkeeper. “好。” “是的……当然。”查尔斯拿出钱包，付给旅馆老板钱。

Mr. Pinch smiled. “You understand, don’t you, sir?” he said. “It’s not my job to feed all of England.” 皮奇先生笑了。 “您明白的，对吧，先生？”他说。 “这不是我的工作，要养活整个英格兰。”

“No. Of course it isn’t,” said Charles. Then he walked away from the inn. Jack and Annie followed him. “不。” “当然不是，”查尔斯说。 然后他离开了旅馆。 杰克和安妮跟着他。

It was colder and darker now. Brownish fog shrouded the street. A lamplighter was lighting the streetlamps. 现在天气更冷，也更暗了。 棕色的雾笼罩着街道。 一个点灯人正在点亮街灯。

“Are you okay, Charles?” asked Annie. “你还好吗，查尔斯？”安妮问。

Charles barely nodded. “Yes—uh—did you children get enough to eat?” he said. 查尔斯勉强点了点头。 “是的——呃——你们两个孩子吃饱了吗？”他说。

“Yes, thank you,” said Annie. “We’re fine. But are you all right?” “是的，谢谢你，”安妮说。 “我们很好。” 但你没事吧？

“Me?” He let out a long, shaky sigh. “Sorry, but I—I must leave you now,” he said. “Here.” Charles pulled out his wallet again. “Take this. I want you to buy new boots and a week’s worth of food. I’m sorry to leave you, but I must.” His hands shook as he held out the leather wallet. “我？”他长长地、颤抖地叹了口气。 “对不起，但我——我必须现在离开你了，”他说。 “拿着。”查尔斯又拿出了他的钱包。 “拿去吧。 我想让你买双新靴子和一周的粮食。 对不起要离开你，但我必须这么做。”他颤抖着递出皮质钱包。

“No, we can’t,” said Jack. “不，我们不能拿，”杰克说。

“I insist,” said Charles, giving him the wallet. “Thank you for your company. And bless you both.” Charles Dickens then walked away from them, disappearing into the brown fog. “我坚持，”查尔斯说，把钱包给了他。 “谢谢你们的陪伴。” “祝你们好运。”查尔斯·狄更斯然后走开了，消失在棕色的雾气中。
