4. Out! Out! Out! 4. 出去！出去！出去！

Jack and Annie hurried around to the back door and slipped inside the house. They walked through a small, dark mudroom, then stepped into the large front hallway. Sun slanted through the tall windows. Everything seemed to be made of carved wood or marble. A wide staircase curved up to the second floor. 杰克和安妮赶紧绕到后门，溜进了房子。 他们穿过一个又小又暗的泥房，然后走进了宽敞的前厅。 阳光从高高的窗户斜射进来。 看起来这里的一切都是用雕刻的木头或大理石做的。 一座宽阔的楼梯蜿蜒向上，通往二楼。

“Get to work, sweeps!” barked the housekeeper. “Do the regular!” “干活去，扫地的！”管家吼道。 “做常规的！”

Mrs. Tibbs left them and clomped down the back stairs. Jack heard pots clattering below. He realized the kitchen must be in the cellar. The rest of the house was quiet, as if it were waiting for the mother and children to return. 女士 蒂布斯离开了他们，重重地走下后楼梯。 杰克听到下面传来锅碗瓢盆碰撞的声音。 他意识到厨房一定在地下室里。 房子的其他地方都很安静，好像在等待母亲和孩子们回来。

“I wonder where Mr. Dickens is writing,” Annie whispered. “我想知道先生 狄更斯在哪里写作，”安妮小声说。

“He must be working quietly somewhere in the house,” said Jack. “他一定在房子里某个地方安静地写作，”杰克说。

Mrs. Tibbs came bustling up the back stairs and burst into the front hallway. “I told you to get to work!” she said. “If I don’t see you working in two minutes, I’ll throw you out on your ears!” The housekeeper then disappeared up the wide staircase to the second floor. 女士 蒂布斯匆匆忙忙地走上后楼梯，冲进了前厅。 “我告诉过你要开始干活了！”她说。 “如果两分钟后我还看不到你在干活，我就把你从耳朵上扔出去！”管家说完，便消失在宽大的楼梯上，前往二楼。

“I guess we’d better get to work,” said Jack. “我想我们最好开始干活了，”杰克说。

“So where do we start?” said Annie. “那我们从哪里开始呢？”安妮说。

“Let’s check out the fireplaces on this floor,” said Jack. “我们先检查一下这层楼的壁炉吧，”杰克说。

Jack and Annie crept into a dining room that overlooked a garden. A fire crackled in the hearth. 杰克和安妮蹑手蹑脚地走进了一间可以俯瞰花园的餐厅。 壁炉里有一团火在噼啪作响。

“She can’t expect us to clean that chimney,” said Jack, “unless she wants us to burn to death.” “她不可能指望我们去打扫那个烟囱，”杰克说，“除非她想让我们被烧死。”

Jack and Annie went back through the front hall and into a room filled with leather-bound books. 杰克和安妮又从前厅走进了一间摆满皮面书籍的房间。

There was no fire in the large fireplace, but the room was bright and warm. It had big windows, a rich-colored plush carpet, and mirrors that reflected the light from outside. A vase of fresh flowers sat on a desk facing one of the windows. 壁炉里没有火，但房间很明亮温暖。 它有大窗户、色彩丰富的地毯，还有能反射外面光线的镜子。 一个插着新鲜花朵的花瓶放在一张面对窗户的书桌上面。

“Oh, man, I love this room,” said Jack, staring at all the books. “哦，天哪，我太喜欢这个房间了，”杰克盯着所有的书说道。

“Yeah, and look—there’s a desk with a feather pen and paper,” said Annie. “I’ll bet this is where Mr. Dickens does his writing. I wonder where he is.” “是啊，你看——这儿有一张书桌，上面有羽毛笔和纸，”安妮说道。 “我敢打赌，这就是梅林先生…… 狄更斯在写他的作品。 我想知道他去哪儿了。

“Maybe he’s taking a break,” said Jack. “也许他正在休息，”杰克说。

“Maybe he’ll come in here soon,” said Annie. “Let’s start cleaning the chimney.” “也许他很快就进来，”安妮说。 “让我们开始打扫烟囱吧。

“Okay,” said Jack. “But first we have to figure out how.” He unbuckled the green bag and pulled out their research book. He looked in the index and found chimney sweeps. He turned to the right page and read aloud: “好，”杰克说。 “但首先我们得想办法。”他解开了绿色的包，拿出他们的研究书。 他翻到目录，找到了烟囱清扫工。 他翻到正确的那一页，大声读起来：

In Victorian England, young boys worked as chimney sweeps, cleaning the soot made by coal fires. It was not only a dirty job, but a dangerous one as well. 在维多利亚时代的英格兰，年轻男孩们做烟囱清扫工，清理煤火产生的烟灰。 这不仅是一项脏活，还是一项危险的工作。

“Great.” Jack closed the book. “Good work, Annie. You made us give up our nice clothes and all our money, and you landed us a dirty, dangerous job.” “太棒了。”杰克合上了书。 “干得好，安妮。 你让我们放弃了漂亮的衣服和所有的钱，还让我们得到了这份又脏又危险的工作。”

“Don’t worry,” said Annie. “We shoveled coal, washed dishes, and hauled bananas with Louis Armstrong. We can do this.” “别担心，”安妮说。 “我们和路易斯·阿姆斯特朗一起铲煤、洗碗、搬运香蕉。我们能行的。”

“Yeah, but how do we do it? The book doesn’t tell us,” said Jack. “是啊，但我们怎么做到呢？ 书里没告诉我们，”杰克说。

“Well, we’ve got a brush, a broom, and rags,” said Annie. “Let’s start by using them. Remember, we’re just waiting to run into Charles Dickens. Then we’ll chat with him and play our music and—” “那我们有刷子、扫帚和抹布，”安妮说。 “我们先用这些开始吧。 记住，我们只是在等遇到查尔斯·狄更斯。 然后我们会和他聊天，演奏我们的音乐，——”

“Okay, okay,” said Jack. He put the book away. “好吧，好吧，”杰克说。 他把书收了起来。

Jack and Annie stepped into the hearth. Annie got down on her hands and knees and began scrubbing the stones with the brush. Jack pushed his broom up the chimney and tried to sweep the soot off the bricks. A pile of soft black powder fell on his head. It got in his eyes and mouth. 杰克和安妮走进壁炉里。 安妮跪在地上，用刷子开始用力擦洗石头。 杰克把扫帚往烟囱里一推，试着把砖块上的烟灰扫掉。 一堆黑色的软粉落在了他的头上。 烟灰进了他的眼睛和嘴巴里。

“Oh, no!” whispered Jack, puffing and blowing. As he squeezed his eyes shut, he felt Annie yank his sleeve. “哦，天哪！”杰克一边喘气一边吹气，小声说道。 当他闭上眼睛时，他感觉到安妮拽了他的袖子。

“Don’t …,” he said. “I—” “别……”，他说。 “我—”

“Shh!” whispered Annie. “He’s here!” “嘘！”安妮小声说。 “他来了！”

Jack opened his watery eyes. He saw a small, slender man standing in the hallway outside the door. The man had wavy brown hair. He wore a dark coat and pants. He was reading some papers and muttering to himself. 杰克睁开了他湿润的眼睛。 他看见一个又小又瘦的男士站在门外的走廊里。 这位男士有一头波浪状的棕色头发。 他穿着一件深色的外套和裤子。 他正在看一些文件，还自言自语着。

“Mr. Dickens,” whispered Annie. “先生。 狄更斯，”安妮小声说。

Before Jack could say anything, the man shouted, “Let no one enter my study, Mrs. Tibbs! Under penalty of death!” And he came into his study and slammed the door. 杰克还没来得及说话，那位男士就大喊道：“不准任何人进入我的书房，蒂布斯太太！ 否则将处以死刑！”然后他走进了自己的书房，砰地一声关上了门。

Penalty of death? thought Jack. He’s got to be kidding! But he and Annie crouched down in the fireplace. 死刑？杰克心想。 他肯定在开玩笑！ 但是杰克和安妮蹲在壁炉里了。

Mr. Dickens didn’t notice them or Jack’s green velvet bag sitting on the carpet. As he crossed the room, he kept looking at his papers and muttering to himself. He sat down at the desk, facing the window. He picked up the feather pen, dipped it into an ink pot, and began to write. 先生， 狄更斯没有注意到他们，也没有注意到杰克放在地毯上的绿色天鹅绒袋子。 当他穿过房间时，他一直看着自己的文件，自言自语。 他坐在书桌前，面对着窗户。 他拿起羽毛笔，把它浸入墨水瓶里，开始写字。

Suddenly Mr. Dickens leapt out of his chair and rushed to one of the mirrors. He put his hands around his neck and cried, “AGHHH!” He struggled and made a horrible face as if someone were choking him. 突然，先生， 狄更斯从椅子上跳起来，冲向一面镜子。 他用手环住脖子，喊道：“啊——！”他挣扎着，做出一副可怕的鬼脸，好像有人在掐他的脖子。

Then the writer hurried back to his desk and scrawled a few more lines. He stopped and read what he’d written. “Good, good!” he said. 然后，作家赶紧回到他的书桌前，又潦草地写了几行字。 他停下来，读了自己写的内容。 “好，好！”他说。

Then Mr. Dickens leapt up again and rushed back to the mirror. This time he rapped his head with his knuckles. He looked furious. “Bah-bah-bah!” he shouted. 然后是先生。 狄更斯又跳了起来，冲向镜子。 这次他用拳头敲了自己的头。 他看起来非常生气。 “呸——呸——呸——！”他喊道。

Jack and Annie watched, fascinated. 杰克和安妮目不转睛地盯着看。

Again Mr. Dickens hurried back to his desk and wrote. He stopped and read what he’d written. Then he crumpled the paper and threw it across the room. He covered his face with his hands and murmured, “I can’t, I can’t!” He was still for a long moment. 再次，先生。 狄更斯赶紧跑回他的书桌，开始写作。 他停了下来，读了自己写的内容。 然后他把纸揉成一团，扔到了房间的另一边。 他用手捂住脸，小声说：“我做不到，我做不到！”他静静地站了一会儿。

“Excuse me. Are you okay?” Annie asked in a soft voice. “对不起。 你还好吗？”安妮用温柔的声音问道。

Mr. Dickens gasped and whirled around. He saw Jack’s green velvet bag on the carpet. “What’s that? Who’s here?” Then he saw Jack and Annie huddled in the fireplace. He jumped out of his chair. 先生。 狄更斯惊呼一声，猛地转过身。 他看见杰克的绿色天鹅绒袋子躺在地毯上。 “那是什么？ 谁在这儿？”然后他看见杰克和安妮挤在壁炉边。 他从椅子上跳了起来。

“Chimney sweeps?” he cried. “Why—why are you in my study?” “烟囱清洁工？”他喊道。 “为什么——为什么你们在我的书房里？”

“Sorry, we’re just working on the chimney,” said Annie. “对不起，我们只是在清理烟囱，”安妮说。

Mr. Dickens groaned. “I—I can’t bear it,” he said. “I have to get out. I have to leave.…” He rushed across the room and threw open the door. 狄更斯先生呻吟了一声。 “我……我受不了了，”他说。 “我必须出去。 我必须离开。……”他冲到房间另一边，猛地推开房门。

The housekeeper was sweeping in the hallway. “What’s wrong, Mr. Dickens?” she asked. 管家正在门厅里扫地。 “杰克，你怎么了，先生？ “狄更斯？”她问。

“I’m finished for today, Mrs. Tibbs,” said Mr. Dickens. “They—they …” He pointed back into his study. “我今天已经完成了，蒂布斯太太，”先生说。 狄更斯。 “他们……他们……”他指着自己的书房。

Mrs. Tibbs saw Jack and Annie. “ Oh! What are you doing in there?” she cried. “Mr. Dickens, I’m sorry! They—” 女士。 蒂布斯看见杰克和安妮。 你们在里面干什么？“她喊道。 “先生。 狄更斯，对不起！他们—”

“Never—never mind. I’m going out,” said Mr. Dickens. “Tell Mrs. Dickens I don’t know when I’ll be back.” He grabbed his hat and walking stick. Then he hurried out the front door. “从—从来都不。” “我要出去，”狄更斯先生说。 “告诉Mrs. 狄更斯我还不知道什么时候能回来。”他抓起帽子和拐杖。 然后他赶紧从正门走出去。

“Mr. Dickens, don’t go!” cried Mrs. Tibbs. But the door closed before she could stop him. “先生， 狄更斯，别走！”蒂布斯太太喊道。 但门在她能阻止他之前就关上了。

Mrs. Tibbs whirled around and charged into the room. “What are you doing in here, filthy brats?” she shrieked. “You know you’re to start with the back rooms! Not his study! Never his study!” She waved her broom at them, as if she was trying to sweep them away. “Out! Out! Out!” 蒂布斯太太 猛地转身，冲进了房间。 “你们这些脏兮兮的小鬼，怎么跑这儿来了？”她尖叫道。 “你们知道你们应该从后面的房间开始！不是他的书房！ 绝不能是他的书房！”她挥舞着扫帚，好像要把他们扫走一样。 “出去！出去！ 出去！”

Jack grabbed his green bag. Then he and Annie fled from Mrs. Tibbs and her broom. They hurried out the front door. 杰克抓起了他的绿色袋子。 然后他和安妮从Mrs那里逃走了。 蒂布斯和她的扫帚。 他们赶紧从正门跑出去。
