10. They’re All Here 10. 它们都在这儿

Jack opened his eyes. 杰克睁开眼睛。

It was so dark he couldn’t see anything. 太黑了，他什么都看不见。

He felt his clothes. He was wearing his T-shirt and jeans again. The leather bag had turned back into his backpack. 他摸了摸自己的衣服。 他又穿着T恤和牛仔裤了。 皮袋子又变回了他的背包。

“Hello,” said Morgan le Fay. Her voice came from the corner of the tree house. “你好，”摩根·勒菲说。 她的声音从树屋角落传来。

“Hi!” said Annie. “嗨！”安妮说。

“Did you have a good journey?” asked Morgan. “旅程愉快吗？”摩根问。

“I did,” said Jack, “but girls can’t do anything fun in ancient Greece.” “挺好的，”杰克说，“可在古希腊，女孩什么好玩的都不能做。”

“I did one fun thing,” Annie said wistfully. “I rode in a chariot pulled by a flying horse.” “我倒是做了一件好玩的事，”安妮留恋地说。 “我坐过飞马拉的战车。”

“That must have been wonderful,” said Morgan. “You were very lucky to be bringing me the story of Pegasus.” “那一定很美妙，”摩根说。 “你们能把珀伽索斯的故事带给我，真是太幸运了。”

“Who?” said Jack. “谁？”杰克说。

“Pegasus,” said Morgan. “He’s the great white winged horse in Greek mythology.” “珀伽索斯，”摩根说。 “他是希腊神话里那匹巨大的白色飞马。”

“Oh, yeah,” said Jack. “I think I’ve heard of him.” “哦，对，”杰克说。 “我想我听说过他。”

He felt in his backpack and found the scroll. He gave it to Morgan. He could still barely see her in the dark. 他在背包里摸到了卷轴。 他把它递给摩根。 黑暗里他几乎还看不见她。

“It was written by Anonymous,” said Annie. “作者是匿名，”安妮说。

“I know,” said Morgan. “Many talented women used that name in the past. Her story will be a great addition to my Camelot library.” “我知道，”摩根说。 “过去许多有才华的女人都用过这个名字。 她的故事会成为我卡米洛特图书馆里很重要的补充。”

“Plato helped us find it,” said Jack. “柏拉图帮我们找到的，”杰克说。

“Ah, my good friend Plato,” said Morgan. “He was one of the greatest thinkers who ever lived.” “啊，我的好朋友柏拉图，”摩根说。 “他是有史以来最伟大的思想家之一。”

“And Pegasus was the greatest horse,” said Annie. She sighed. “I just wish I could see him again.” “珀伽索斯是最棒的马，”安妮说。她叹了口气。 “我真希望能再见到它。”

“You can,” Morgan said softly. “He’s here right now.” “你可以，”摩根轻声说。 “它现在就在这儿。”

“Pegasus?” cried Annie. “Oh, boy!” “珀伽索斯？”安妮喊道。 “太好了！”

Annie turned on the flashlight and used it to find her way down the rope ladder. 安妮打开手电筒，借着光顺着绳梯往下爬。

Jack grabbed his backpack and followed her. 杰克抓起背包跟上她。

When they were both standing on the ground, Annie shined her flashlight at the dark trees. 两人站在地上后，安妮把手电照向黑暗的树林。

“Pegasus?” she said. “Where are you? Pegasus?” “珀伽索斯？”她说。 “你在哪儿？珀伽索斯？”

“Turn off your flashlight, Annie,” said Morgan. She was looking down from the tree house window. “把手电关掉，安妮，”摩根说。 她正从树屋窗口往下看。

Annie switched off the light. 安妮关掉了灯。

“In the night, you can see all the story characters that saved you on your last four missions,” said Morgan. “They are all here—Hercules and the silk weaver; Sarph, the serpent monster; and Pegasus.” “夜里，你能看见在你们上四次任务里救过你们的所有故事人物，”摩根说。 “它们都在这儿——赫拉克勒斯和织女；蛇怪萨尔夫；还有珀伽索斯。”

Jack pushed his glasses into place and studied the dark woods. 杰克把眼镜推正，仔细打量黑暗的树林。

“Where are they, Morgan?” cried Annie. “Where’s Pegasus?” “它们在哪儿，摩根？”安妮喊道。 “珀伽索斯在哪儿？”

“Look hard,” said Morgan. “仔细看，”摩根说。

“I can’t see him!” said Annie. “我看不见它！”安妮说。

“Yes, you can,” said Morgan. “The old stories are always with us. We are never alone.” “你能看见，”摩根说。 “古老的故事永远与我们同在。 我们从不孤单。”

Has Morgan gone crazy? wondered Jack. 摩根是不是疯了？杰克心想。

“Look up,” said Morgan. “Your friends are in the night sky. They are stars.” “抬头看，”摩根说。 “你们的朋友在夜空里。它们是星星。”

“Stars?” whispered Jack. “星星？”杰克低声说。

He stared at the shimmering field of tiny stars overhead. 他盯着头顶那片闪烁的小星星。

“Hercules is a constellation,” said Morgan. “The Romans imagined him kneeling in the sky, holding a club over his head.” “赫拉克勒斯是一个星座，”摩根说。 “罗马人想象他跪在天上，头上举着一根大棒。”

Morgan waved her finger at the sky. For a moment Jack saw a living, breathing Hercules outlined by stars. 摩根用手指朝天空一指。 有那么一瞬间，杰克看见一个活生生、在呼吸的赫拉克勒斯，由星星勾出轮廓。

“And there’s the silk weaver, with her beloved cowherd,” said Morgan. “The ancient Chinese believed that they were two stars on either side of the Milky Way.” “还有那位织女，和她心爱的牛郎，”摩根说。 “古代中国人相信，他们是银河两侧的两颗星星。”

Morgan waved her hand again. The lovely silk weaver was outlined in the heavens. 摩根又挥了挥手。 美丽的织女在天际勾勒出轮廓。

“And long ago, the Irish believed the Milky Way itself was the serpent monster Sarph,” said Morgan. “很久以前，爱尔兰人相信银河本身就是蛇怪萨尔夫，”摩根说。

Morgan waved her hand. A giant serpent glittered through the sky. 摩根挥了挥手。 一条巨大的蛇在天空中闪闪发光。

“And the ancient Greeks named one of their constellations Pegasus,” said Morgan. “古希腊人把他们的一个星座命名为珀伽索斯，”摩根说。

She waved her hand again and the white horse’s head, wings, and galloping legs glowed in the sky. 她再一挥手，白马的头、翅膀和奔驰的腿在天空中发光。

“I see him!” said Annie. Then she whispered, “I love you, Pegasus.” “我看见它了！”安妮说。 然后她轻声说：“我爱你，珀伽索斯。”

Jack thought he saw the stars move as if Pegasus was rearing back in the sky. 杰克觉得星星在动，好像珀伽索斯在空中扬起前蹄。

After a breathless moment of silence, Morgan lowered her arm. The night sky became a field of tiny glittering stars again. 在一阵令人屏息的寂静之后，摩根放下了手臂。 夜空又变成一片细碎闪烁的星海。

“You have done amazing work as Master Librarians,” said Morgan. “I would trust you with any important mission.” “你们作为首席图书管理员做得非常出色，”摩根说。 “任何重要的任务，我都愿意托付给你们。”

“Does that mean we’re going on more trips?” asked Jack. “那是说我们还会有更多旅行？”杰克问。

“Indeed it does,” said Morgan. “Many more.” “确实如此，”摩根说。 “还有很多次。”

Jack smiled with relief. 杰克松了一口气，笑了。

“When is our next mission?” asked Annie. “我们下次任务是什么时候？”安妮问。

“As soon as I need your help, I will send for you,” said Morgan. “Go home now and rest.” “等我需要你们帮忙时，我会叫你们，”摩根说。 “现在回家休息吧。”

“Good-bye,” said Annie. “再见，”安妮说。

“Good-bye,” said Jack. “再见，”杰克说。

“Farewell,” said Morgan. “再见，”摩根说。

There was a sudden rushing of wind, then a blur of blinding light. And Morgan le Fay and the magic tree house vanished. 突然一阵风呼啸而过，接着是一道刺眼的光影。 摩根·勒菲和魔法树屋忽然消失了。

The night was still. 夜晚又安静了。

“Home?” asked Annie. “回家？”安妮问。

“Sure,” said Jack. “当然，”杰克说。

As they walked between the trees, the Frog Creek woods were pitch-black. 他们走在树间时，蛙溪树林漆黑一片。

Jack couldn’t see a thing. 杰克什么都看不见。

But he didn’t ask Annie to turn on the flashlight. For once, he wasn’t worried about finding his way home. 可他没让安妮打开手电筒。 难得这一次，他并不担心找不到回家的路。

He felt as if someone—or something—was leading them through the woods. 他觉得好像有什么人——或什么东西——正领着他们穿过树林。

Morgan’s words came back to him: The old stories are always with us. We are never alone. 摩根的话又回到他耳边：古老的故事永远与我们同在。 我们从不孤单。

Jack looked up at the stars again. They were beginning to fade in the growing light of dawn. 杰克再次抬头看星星。 它们在渐渐亮起的晨光里开始变淡。

But he thought he could hear the beating of giant wings, somewhere high above. 可他觉得自己听见了巨大翅膀拍打的声音，就在高高的上空某处。
