4. Not Fair! 4. 不公平！

The young woman smiled at Jack and Annie. 年轻女子对杰克和安妮微笑。

“How did you learn to read and write?” Annie asked. “你是怎么学会读写的？”安妮问。

“I taught myself,” the woman answered. “我自学的，”那女人回答。

“She wrote a poem and brought it to me,” said Plato, “because I have written and told people that I think Greek girls should go to school and learn things.” “她写了一首诗带给我，”柏拉图说，“因为我写过也跟人们说过，我觉得希腊女孩应该上学、学习东西。”

“Is that the poem?” said Jack. He pointed to the poet’s scroll. “那就是那首诗吗？”杰克说。 他指着诗人的卷轴。

“Yes,” said the young woman. “是的，”年轻女子说。

“It’s a wonderful story,” said Plato. “But she will get in trouble if it is read in our land. You must take it back to your faraway home, where it will be safe.” “这是个精彩的故事，”柏拉图说。 “可要是这首诗在我们国家被人读到，她就会有麻烦。 你们必须把它带回遥远的家乡，那儿才安全。”

The poet handed Jack her scroll. He put it into his bag. 诗人把卷轴递给杰克。 他把它放进袋子里。

“Tell us your name,” said Annie. “So we can tell people who wrote the story.” “告诉我们你的名字，”安妮说。 “好让我们告诉别人是谁写的这个故事。”

The young woman shook her head. 年轻女子摇了摇头。

“I cannot,” she said. When she saw Annie’s sad face, she added, “You can tell people it was written by Anonymous.” “我不能，”她说。 看见安妮难过的脸，她补充说：“你们可以告诉别人，这是匿名者写的。”

“That’s your name?” asked Annie. “那是你的名字？”安妮问。

“No, anonymous means that no one knows who wrote it,” said Plato. “不，匿名的意思是没有人知道是谁写的，”柏拉图说。

“But that’s not true!” said Annie. “可那不是真的！”安妮说。

“I’m afraid the risk is too great,” said Plato. “恐怕风险太大了，”柏拉图说。

Annie looked back at the woman. 安妮回头看着那个女人。

“I’m sorry,” said Annie. “It’s not fair—not at all.” “对不起，”安妮说。 “这不公平——一点也不公平。”

The poet smiled at her. “I am happy that you will take my story to your country,” she said. “Perhaps someday women everywhere will write books just like men.” 诗人对她微笑。 “我很高兴你们会把我的故事带到你们的国家，”她说。 “也许有一天，世界各地的女人也能像男人一样写书。”

“They will,” said Jack. “I promise.” “他们会的，”杰克说。 “我保证。”

The young woman looked at him, puzzled. 年轻女子困惑地看着他。

“It’s true!” said Annie. “是真的！”安妮说。

“Thank you, Annie,” the young woman said. “And thank you, Jack.” She bowed, then hurried out of the courtyard. “谢谢你，安妮，”年轻女子说。 “也谢谢你，杰克。”她鞠了一躬，然后匆匆走出院子。

“Wait!” said Annie. “等等！”安妮说。

She started to go after the poet, but Plato stopped her. 她要去追那位诗人，可柏拉图拦住了她。

“Come along,” he said. “The games will start soon.” “走吧，”他说。 “比赛很快就要开始了。”

Plato then led Jack and Annie out of the Greek house back onto the dirt road. 柏拉图随后领着杰克和安妮走出希腊房子，回到土路上。

“Girls can’t write stories,” grumbled Annie. “They can’t go to school. They can’t be in plays. I’ve had enough of ancient Greece. Let’s get out of here.” “女孩不能写故事，”安妮嘟囔着。 “她们不能上学。 “她们不能演戏。 我已经受够古希腊了。 我们离开这儿吧。”

“Wait,” said Jack. “What about the Olympics?” “等等，”杰克说。 “那奥运会呢？”

“Oh, yeah,” said Annie. Her eyes got brighter. “I almost forgot.” “哦，对，”安妮说。 她的眼睛亮了起来。 “我差点忘了。”

“Well,” said Plato slowly. “I would like to take you both to the games. I have special seats in the viewing box. However … ” He looked at Annie. “嗯，”柏拉图慢慢地说。 “我想带你们俩去看比赛。 我在观礼席有特别座位。 不过……”他看向安妮。

“Don’t tell me,” she said. “Girls can’t go to the Olympics either.” “别告诉我，”她说。 “女孩也不能去看奥运会。”

Plato shook his head. 柏拉图摇了摇头。

“A girl will get in terrible trouble if she goes to the games,” he said. “女孩要是去看比赛，会惹上大麻烦，”他说。

Annie sighed. “It’s really, really not fair,” she said. 安妮叹了口气。 “这真的、真的不公平，”她说。

“I’m sorry,” said Plato. “My country is a democracy. We believe in freedom for our citizens. But I’m afraid right now that only means men.” “对不起，”柏拉图说。 “我的国家是一个民主国家。 我们相信公民应有自由。 可恐怕现在那只意味着男人。”

“Annie’s right. It’s not fair,” said Jack. “I think we should go home now.” “安妮说得对。 这不公平，”杰克说。 “我觉得我们该回家了。”

“No, Jack. You go to the Olympics,” said Annie. “At least you can tell me about it. Take notes.” “不，杰克。 你去看奥运会，”安妮说。 “至少你可以跟我讲讲。记笔记。”

“What about you?” Jack said. “那你呢？”杰克说。

“I’ll go back to that play at the outdoor theater,” Annie said. “Meet me there when you’re done.” “我回露天剧场看那场戏，”安妮说。 “你看完后来那儿找我。”

Jack didn’t want to leave Annie alone. But he also didn’t want to miss the Olympics. 杰克不想让安妮独自留下。 可他也不想错过奥运会。

“Go! Have fun!” Annie said. She began walking away. “I’ll see you later! Bye, Plato!” “去吧！ 好好玩！”安妮说。 她开始走开。 “待会儿见！再见，柏拉图！”

“Bye, Annie,” said Plato. “再见，安妮，”柏拉图说。

Annie turned back again and waved. 安妮又转过身来挥了挥手。

“I’ll tell you all about it!” Jack called. “我会全讲给你听的！”杰克喊道。

“This way,” said Plato. “这边走，”柏拉图说。

He and Jack turned and joined the crowd heading toward the Olympic grounds. 他和杰克转过身，加入朝奥林匹克场地走去的人群。
