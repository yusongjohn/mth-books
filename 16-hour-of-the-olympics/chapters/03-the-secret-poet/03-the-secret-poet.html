3. The Secret Poet 3. 秘密诗人

The bearded man smiled at Annie. 留胡子的男人对安妮微笑。

“My name is Plato,” he said. “我叫柏拉图，”他说。

“Plato?” said Jack. That name sounded familiar. “柏拉图？”杰克说。 这名字听着很熟。

“You may have heard of me,” the man said. “I am a philosopher.” “你也许听说过我，”那人说。 “我是个哲学家。”

“What’s that mean?” said Annie. “那是什么意思？”安妮说。

“A lover of wisdom,” said Plato. “就是智慧的爱好者，”柏拉图说。

“Wow,” said Annie. “哇，”安妮说。

Plato smiled at her. 柏拉图对她微笑。

“It’s odd to see a girl walking so bravely through Olympia,” he said. “You must be from far away.” “看见一个女孩这么大胆地走在奥林匹亚，真是少见，”他说。 “你们一定是从很远的地方来的。”

“We’re Jack and Annie,” said Annie. “And we come from Frog Creek, Pennsylvania. It’s very far away.” “我们是杰克和安妮，”安妮说。 “我们来自宾夕法尼亚州的蛙溪。 非常远。”

Plato looked puzzled. 柏拉图显得很困惑。

Annie turned to Jack. 安妮转向杰克。

“I think we should show him our cards,” she said in a low voice. “He’s a lover of wisdom.” “我觉得该把卡片给他看，”她低声说。 “他是智慧的爱好者。”

Jack nodded. He reached into his bag and took out the secret library cards. He showed them to Plato. 杰克点点头。 他伸手进袋子，取出秘密图书管理员卡。 他把卡片给柏拉图看。

The letters M and L that stood for Master Librarian glittered on the cards. 卡片上闪着代表“图书管理员大师”的字母M和L。

“Amazing!” said Plato. “I’ve never met such young Master Librarians. Why have you come to Olympia?” “太了不起了！”柏拉图说。 “我从未见过这么年轻的首席图书管理员。 你们为什么来奥林匹亚？”

Jack pulled out the piece of paper with the title of the story. 杰克抽出写着故事标题的那张纸。

“We’re looking for this story,” he said. “我们在找这个故事，”他说。

“Oh, yes,” said Plato softly. “This was written by a brilliant poet—a friend of mine, in fact.” “哦，是的，”柏拉图轻声说。 “这是一位才华出众的诗人写的——其实还是我的一个朋友。”

“Do you know where the poet lives?” asked Jack. “你知道那位诗人住在哪里吗？”杰克问。

“Very near here,” said Plato. “就在附近，”柏拉图说。

“Will you take us there?” asked Annie. “你能带我们去吗？”安妮问。

“Yes, but I must warn you—never tell anyone who the poet is,” said Plato. “It’s a secret.” “好，可我必须警告你们——千万别告诉任何人这位诗人是谁，”柏拉图说。 “这是个秘密。”

“We won’t,” whispered Annie. “我们不会说的，”安妮低声说。

Plato led them away from the outdoor theater. 柏拉图领着他们离开露天剧场。

They started down a dirt road. It was crowded with people heading to the games. 他们走上一条土路。 路上挤满了赶去看比赛的人。

Plato stopped at the door of a sand-colored house with a brick roof. 柏拉图在一栋沙色房子的门口停下来，那房子有砖砌的屋顶。

He opened the door and led Jack and Annie into an empty courtyard. 他打开门，带杰克和安妮走进一座空荡荡的院子。

“Wait here,” he said. He disappeared through a doorway. “在这儿等着，”他说。 他穿过一道门消失了。

Jack and Annie looked around. 杰克和安妮四处看了看。

Rooms opened onto the sunny courtyard. Everything was quiet. 房间都朝向洒满阳光的院子。 一切都很安静。

“The people who live here must have gone to the games,” said Annie. “住在这儿的人一定是去看比赛了，”安妮说。

“I bet you’re right,” said Jack. “我猜你说得对，”杰克说。

He pulled out the Greek book and found a picture of a house. He read aloud: 他抽出希腊书，找到一幅房子的图画。他大声读道：

Men and women lived in separate parts of a Greek house. Women spent most of their time spinning and weaving and taking care of the kitchen. Boys were sent away to school when they were seven. Girls were not allowed to go to school.

“Girls can’t go to school?” said Annie. “How do they learn to read and write?” “女孩不能上学？”安妮说。 “那她们怎么学读写？”

At that moment Plato returned. With him was a young woman dressed in a long tunic with a colored border. She was holding a scroll. 就在这时，柏拉图回来了。 和他一起来的是一位年轻女子，穿着镶彩边的长袍。 她手里拿着一卷轴。

Annie smiled a big smile. 安妮露出大大的笑容。

“Finally, ” she said. “Another girl.” “终于，”她说。 “又有一个女孩了。”

“Jack and Annie, meet our secret poet,” said Plato. “杰克和安妮，来认识我们的秘密诗人，”柏拉图说。
