2. Any Girls Here? 2. 这里有女孩吗？

Jack opened his eyes. Warm sunshine streamed into the tree house. 杰克睁开眼睛。 温暖的阳光泻进树屋。

“We sure don’t need the flashlight here,” he said. “这里根本用不着手电筒，”他说。

“Look, Morgan gave us clothes like the ones we wore in Pompeii,” said Annie. “看，摩根给我们的衣服跟在庞贝穿的差不多，”安妮说。

Jack looked down. 杰克低头一看。

His clothes were similar to the ones he’d worn in the Roman town of Pompeii: a tunic and sandals. He also had a leather bag in place of his backpack again. 他的衣服跟他在罗马城镇庞贝穿过的差不多：一件束腰外衣和一双凉鞋。 他的背包又变成了皮袋子。

Annie looked out the window. 安妮朝窗外望去。

“And we landed in an olive tree—just like Pompeii!” said Annie. “我们还落在一棵橄榄树上——跟庞贝一模一样！”安妮说。

Jack looked out the window. He caught his breath. 杰克也朝窗外望去。 他倒吸一口气。

“Are we in the wrong place?” he asked. “我们是不是走错地方了？”他问。

“I don’t know,” said Annie. “Look past the trees. Doesn’t it look like a big fair?” “我不知道，”安妮说。 “看树那边。 那边看起来不像个大集市吗？

Jack looked. Annie was right. Past the olive grove was a field filled with white tents. Beyond the field were red-brick buildings with columns and huge crowds of people. 杰克看了看。安妮说得对。 橄榄树林过去，是一片支满白色帐篷的场地。 场地再过去，是带石柱的红砖建筑，还有大群的人。

“What’s going on?” Jack asked. “这是怎么回事？”杰克问。

He pulled the research book out of his leather bag. He found a picture of the scene outside. Below the picture were these words: 他从皮袋子里抽出研究书。 他找到一幅和外面景色一样的图画。 图画下面写着这些话：

The Olympic Games began in ancient Greece over 2,500 years ago. Every four years, more than 40,000 people traveled to Olympia, the town where the festival of athletic games took place.

“Oh, man,” Jack whispered. “We’re at the ancient Olympics!” “天哪，”杰克低声说。 “我们在古代奥运会！”

“Cool,” said Annie. “太酷了，”安妮说。

Jack wrote in his notebook: 杰克在笔记本上写道：

图中文字：first Olympics take place 图中文字：第一届奥运会举行

“Come on, let’s go watch!” Annie said. She started down the rope ladder. “来吧，我们去看比赛！”安妮说。 她开始顺着绳梯往下爬。

Jack threw his notebook and the research book into his leather bag. 杰克把笔记本和研究书扔进皮袋子。

“Don’t forget we have to get Morgan’s story, too,” he said as he followed Annie. “别忘了我们还得拿到摩根要的故事，”他一边跟着安妮一边说。

Annie waited as Jack stepped to the ground. Then they walked through the olive grove to where the tents were. 安妮等杰克落到地面。 然后他们穿过橄榄树林，走向帐篷那边。

Jack heard pipe music and smelled food roasting over fires. Groups of men talked excitedly to one another. 杰克听见管乐声，还闻到火上烤肉的香味。 一群群男人兴奋地彼此交谈着。

“That’s funny,” said Annie. “I don’t see any girls here.” “真奇怪，”安妮说。 “我怎么没看见女孩。”

“Oh, sure, there’re girls,” said Jack. “当然有女孩，”杰克说。

“Where?” said Annie. “Show me.” “在哪儿？”安妮说。 “指给我看。”

Jack looked around. But he only saw men and boys—no women or girls at all. 杰克四处张望。 可他只看见男人和男孩——一个女人或女孩都没有。

Then he saw an outdoor theater. A woman was standing on the stage. She had yellow hair and a purple tunic. 接着他看见一座露天剧场。 一个女人正站在舞台上。 她有黄色的头发，穿着紫色束腰外衣。

“There,” said Jack, pointing. “那儿，”杰克指着说。

“What’s she doing?” asked Annie. “她在干什么？”安妮问。

A soldier was on the stage with her. He wore a long cape. A helmet with a red crest hid his face. 一个士兵和她一起在舞台上。 他穿着一件长斗篷。 一顶带着红色羽饰的头盔遮住了他的脸。

The woman and the soldier were waving their arms and talking loudly to each other. 那个女人和士兵挥舞着手臂，大声对彼此说话。

“I think they’re doing a play,” said Jack. “I’ll look.” “我觉得他们在演戏，”杰克说。 “我查查。”

He pulled out the Greek book and found a picture of the theater. 他抽出希腊书，找到一幅剧院的图画。

“Listen,” he said. He read aloud: “听着，”他说。他大声读道：

The Greeks were the first to write plays. Many English words for the theater come from Greek words, such as drama, scenery, and chorus. Many Greek plays are still performed today.

“Hey, Jack,” said Annie. “You’re wrong.” “嘿，杰克，”安妮说。 “你错了。”

When Jack looked up, he saw the woman had pulled off her wig. It was a boy dressed up as a woman! 杰克抬起头，看见那个女人已经摘掉了假发。 那其实是个装扮成女人的男孩！

“See, even she’ s a boy,” said Annie. “That’s weird.” “看，连‘她’也是男孩，”安妮说。 “真奇怪。”

“Hmm,” said Jack. He went on reading: “嗯，”杰克说。 他继续读：

A few actors would play many different parts in the same play. Women were not allowed to act, so men played the female roles, too.

“That’s not fair,” said Annie. “What if a woman wanted to be in a play?” “这不公平，”安妮说。 “要是有女人想演戏呢？”

“Don’t worry about it,” said Jack. He put the book away. “Let’s just take a peek at the Olympics, then find our story.” “别想那么多了，”杰克说。 他把书收起来。 “我们先去奥运会看一眼，然后再找故事。”

He nudged Annie to move along. 他推了推安妮，让她继续往前走。

Just then he heard a voice. 就在这时，他听到一个声音。

“Wait!” “等等！”

They turned around. A man with a short white beard was walking toward them. 他们转过身。 一个留着短白胡子的男人朝他们走来。

“Hello,” said the man. He was looking right at Annie. “Who are you?” “你好，”那人说。 他正看着安妮。 “你是谁？”

“Who are you? ” Annie asked boldly. “你是谁？”安妮大胆地问。
