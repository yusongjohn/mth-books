2. Lots of Ice 2. 大量的冰

“Welcome to Antarctica,” said Annie. “欢迎来到南极洲，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie were bundled in cold-weather gear. They both wore snow pants, gloves, boots with spikes, and thick red parkas with hoods. Goggles covered their eyes, and wool masks covered their mouths and noses. A hiker’s backpack had replaced Jack’s own pack. 杰克和安妮都穿上了防寒装备。 他们两个人都穿着雪裤、手套、带钉子的靴子，还有厚实的红色带兜帽的羽绒服。 护目镜盖住了他们的眼睛，羊毛面罩盖住了他们的嘴巴和鼻子。 一个徒步旅行者的背包取代了杰克自己的背包。

Jack felt trapped by all the gear. He pulled his mask down under his chin and pushed his goggles onto his forehead. Annie did the same. Their breath made clouds of mist in the freezing air. 杰克觉得被这么多装备束缚住了。 他把面罩拉到下巴下面，把护目镜推到额头上面。 安妮也做了同样的事情。 他们在寒冷的空气中呼出的气变成了白色的雾气。

“It’s really cold here!” said Jack. The wind stung his bare face and made his eyes water. But he kept his mask and goggles off as he looked out the window with Annie. “这里真的好冷啊！”杰克说。 风刮在他们光秃秃的脸上，让他们的眼睛流泪。 但他把面具和护目镜摘了下来，和安妮一起从窗户往外看。

The tree house was on the ground. It was tucked under the overhang of an ice cliff near the ocean. Icy seawater sparkled in the sunlight. The shore was silent and deserted. 树屋在地面上。 它藏在靠近海洋的一座冰崖的遮蔽处。 冰冷的海水在阳光下闪闪发光。 海岸边一片寂静，空无一人。

“It looks totally empty out there,” said Jack. “I still don’t get it—how do we find the Cave of the Ancient Crown here? No king or queen ever ruled Antarctica. In fact, no people ever lived here at all until explorers came in recent times.” “看起来外面空空荡荡的，”杰克说。 “我还是不明白——我们怎么在这里找到古代王冠洞穴呢？ 没有国王或女王曾经统治过南极洲。 事实上，直到最近才有探险者来到这里，之前这里根本没有人类居住过。”

“Let’s get started and try to figure it all out,” said Annie. “我们开始吧，试着弄清楚一切，”安妮说。

“Not so fast,” said Jack. He pulled off his glove and opened their research book to the first chapter. He read aloud: “别急，”杰克说。 他摘下手套，翻开他们的研究书，翻到第一章。他大声读起来：

The continent of Antarctica is the coldest, driest, and windiest place on earth. Larger in area than the United States, it is a land filled with ice—ice cliffs, icebergs, ice sheets, ice shelves— 南极洲是地球上最冷、最干燥、风最大的地方。它的面积比美国还大，是一片充满冰雪的地方——冰崖、冰山、冰盖、冰架——

“Okay, lots of ice,” said Annie. “Got it. Let’s go.” “好的，有很多冰，”安妮说。 “明白了。我们走吧。”

“In a minute.” Jack kept reading: “等一下。”杰克继续读：

But Antarctica was not always a land of ice. Eons ago, it was part of a supercontinent scientists have namedGondwana. It had forests, flowers, and many animals, including dinosaurs. But no people ever lived there. 但是南极洲并不是一直都有冰。很久很久以前，它曾是科学家称为冈瓦纳的超级大陆的一部分。那里有森林、花朵和许多动物，包括恐龙。但从来没有人在那里生活过。

“See?” said Jack. “No kings, no queens, no crowns.” “看到了吗？”杰克说。 “没有国王，没有王后，没有王冠。”

“Yep, let’s go,” said Annie. “嗯，走吧，”安妮说。

But Jack kept reading: 但杰克继续读着：

Over millions of years, Antarctica broke off from Gondwana and drifted south. 数百万年来，南极洲从冈瓦纳大陆分离出来，向南漂移。

“Okay, I’m drifting now myself, Jack,” said Annie. “Byeee—” She climbed out the window and disappeared from the tree house. “好吧，我自己也开始飘起来了，杰克，”安妮说。 “拜拜—”她从窗户爬出去，消失在了树屋里。

Jack looked back at the book, but before he could read further, he heard Annie whoop with laughter. “Oh, wow! Jack, come look!” she yelled. 杰克回头看向那本书，但就在他准备继续读的时候，他听见安妮兴奋地大笑起来。 “哦，哇！ 杰克，快来看！”她喊道。

“What is it?” said Jack, closing the book. “怎么了？”杰克说着，合上了书。

“You won’t believe this!” said Annie. “You have to come see!” “你绝对想不到！”安妮说。 “你一定要来看！”

Jack put his glove back on and pulled on his backpack. He folded the paper with Morgan’s rhyme and put it in his pocket. Then, clutching the research book to his chest, he climbed out of the window. 杰克把他的手套重新戴上，然后背上了背包。 他把写有摩根韵文的纸折好，放进了口袋里。 接着，他紧紧抱着研究书，爬出了窗户。

Jack followed the sound of Annie’s laughter beyond the tree house. On the frozen seashore was a group of penguins, grown-ups and babies. The grown-ups had orange streaks on their cheeks, puffy white chests, and black wings they held straight by their sides. The babies were fluffy balls of gray fuzz. All the penguins were waddling toward Annie. They rocked from side to side, taking funny little steps. 杰克跟着安妮的笑声，走到了树屋外面。 在结冰的海边，有一群企鹅，有大人，也有小宝宝。 那些大人脸颊上有橙色的条纹，胸部是蓬松的白色，翅膀是黑色的，他们把翅膀紧紧贴在身体两边。 小宝宝们就像一团团灰色的毛球。 所有的企鹅都摇摇晃晃地朝安妮走来。 他们左右摇晃，走着滑稽的小步子。

Jack burst out laughing. The big penguins looked like a committee of little men in black suits. 杰克忍不住笑出声来。 那些大企鹅看起来就像一群穿着黑色西装的小矮人。

The group stopped in front of Annie and squawked at her. 这群企鹅在安妮面前停了下来，大声地叫着她。

“Hi, guys,” said Annie. “Glad to meet you!” “嗨，大家好，”安妮说。 “很高兴见到你！”

The penguins stared back at her with friendly curiosity. 企鹅们用友好的好奇目光看着她。

“They’re so cool,” said Jack. He opened the book and found a picture that looked like the group of penguins around Annie. He read: “它们太酷了，”杰克说。 他打开书，发现了一张看起来像安妮身边的那群企鹅的图片。他读道：

Emperor penguins are the tallest and heaviest of the penguin species and are also the most ancient. Over three feet tall, grown penguins can weigh up to ninety pounds. Researchers say the penguin’s closest ancestors lived 40 million years ago. 帝企鹅是企鹅中最高、最重的，也是最古老的。成年帝企鹅身高超过三英尺，体重可达九十磅。研究人员说，帝企鹅最近的祖先生活在四千万年前。

“Forty million years!” said Jack. “So what time have we gone back to? A million years ago? A thousand years ago?” “四千万年！”杰克说。 “那我们回到了什么时代？ 四百万年前？ 一千年以前？”

“I don’t know,” said Annie. “But I’ll take a picture of them for my family project. They look like a little family, don’t they?” She pulled her camera out of her pocket and aimed it at the penguins. “Smile, everyone.” “我不知道，”安妮说。 “不过我要给它们拍张照片，作为我家庭作业的一部分。 它们看起来像一个小家庭，对不对？”她从口袋里拿出相机，对准了企鹅。 “大家笑一笑。”

As Annie took a picture, a shadow moved over the ice. The penguins squawked loudly and clustered together. 当安妮拍照时，一个影子掠过了冰面。 企鹅们大声地叫着，挤在一起。

Jack and Annie looked up. The shadow belonged to a giant bird circling above them. The bird was brownish gray with a long beak. It let out an eerie cry. 杰克和安妮抬头看了看。 影子属于一只在他们头顶盘旋的巨大鸟。 这只鸟是灰褐色的，有着长长的喙。 它发出了一声令人毛骨悚然的叫声。

“What is that?” Annie asked with alarm. “那是什么？”安妮惊慌地问。

“I’ll look it up,” said Jack. He flipped through the pages of their book to find a photo. “Here!” He read quickly: “我去查查，”杰克说。 他翻着书页，想找一张照片。 “在这儿！”他快速读道：

Giant petrels are the vultures of Antarctica. They eat dead birds and animals. Sometimes they even attack young seals and pen— 巨鹱是南极的秃鹫。它们吃死掉的鸟和动物。有时候它们甚至会攻击小海豹和企—

“Oh, no!” cried Annie. “哦，不！”安妮喊道。

Jack looked up. The petrel was diving toward the penguins. It hit the smallest one with its wing and swooped back into the air. 杰克抬头看了看。 那只海燕朝企鹅俯冲下来。 它用翅膀撞到了最小的那只企鹅，然后又飞回空中。

The penguin squawked and waddled away from the group. The petrel spread its wide wings and dove again. 那只企鹅叫了一声，摇摇晃晃地离开了队伍。 海燕展开宽大的翅膀，再次俯冲下来。

“NO!” Annie cried. “不！”安妮喊道。

The petrel swooped away but circled back toward them. 海燕飞走了，但又绕回来朝他们飞来。

Jack dropped the book and scooped up a handful of snow to make a snowball. Before he could throw it, the petrel attacked again. Jack leapt toward the small penguin. He fell on his knees and threw his arms around the fluffy little body. 杰克把书扔到一边，抓了一把雪团成一个雪球。 他还没来得及扔出去，那只海燕又扑过来了。 杰克朝那只小企鹅跳了过去。 他跪倒在地，张开双臂紧紧抱住了那毛茸茸的小身体。

Annie charged at the petrel. “Go! Get out of here!” she yelled, waving her arms. 安妮冲向那只海燕。 “走！ 快离开这里！”她一边大喊，一边挥舞着双臂。

The petrel cawed, then rose high into the sky and disappeared over the ice cliff. 海燕叫了一声，然后飞得高高的，消失在冰崖后面。

Jack let go of the small penguin and stood up. The penguin peeped and waggled its head at Jack. 杰克松开小企鹅，站了起来。 那只企鹅“叽叽”叫了两声，还朝杰克晃了晃脑袋。

Jack laughed. “You’re welcome,” he said. “Now go back to your family. Go—” 杰克笑了。 “谢谢，”他说。 “现在回去找你们的家人。走—”

He shooed the penguin back to the group. “All of you guys, back in the water now. You’ll be safer there. Go, go.” 他把企鹅赶回了队伍里。 “你们都回去水里吧。 在那里会更安全。走，快点。”

The penguins squawked and flapped their wings as if saying good-bye. Then they waddled across the icy shore, taking quick, tiny steps toward the sea. One after another, they dove between cracks in the ice until they all disappeared. 企鹅们嘎嘎叫着，拍打着翅膀，好像在说再见。 然后它们摇摇晃晃地穿过结冰的海岸，快速地、小心翼翼地朝大海走去。 一个接一个，它们从冰缝中跳入水中，直到全都消失不见了。

“Bye, guys,” said Annie. “再见了，伙计们，”安妮说。

HONK! 喔喔喔！

“What’s that?” said Jack. “那是什么？”杰克说。

HONK! 喔喔喔！

“It sounds like some kind of horn,” said Annie. “听起来像是某种号角，”安妮说。

“That’s weird,” said Jack. “这太奇怪了，”杰克说。

“It’s coming from over there,” said Annie. “声音是从那边传来的，”安妮说。

Jack picked up their book and followed Annie up the side of an icy slope. The metal spikes on his boots dug into the ice and kept him from slipping. When he and Annie reached the top, they looked down. 杰克拿起他们的书，跟着安妮沿着一条结冰的斜坡爬上去。 他靴子上的金属尖钉扎进冰里，防止他滑倒。 当杰克和安妮到达顶部时，他们向下看去。

“Oh, man,” said Jack. “We haven’t gone back in time at all!” “天啊，”杰克说。 “我们根本没有回到过去！”
