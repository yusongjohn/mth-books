3. Short Grown-ups 3. 短个子大人

In the icy field below the slope were yellow, brown, and green buildings; telephone poles; metal pipes; and storage tanks. Tractors and bulldozers moved along gravel roads. It looked like a small town. 在斜坡下方的冰冻田野上，有黄色、棕色和绿色的建筑；电线杆；金属管道；还有储油罐。 拖拉机和推土机沿着碎石路行驶。 看起来像一个小镇。

HONK! The sound came from a red bus with gigantic wheels that was parked nearby. honk！ 声音来自一辆红色的公交车，它停在附近，车轮非常大。

“What is this place?” asked Annie. “这是什么地方？”安妮问。

Jack looked in their book and found a photo that looked like the scene below. The caption read: McMurdo Station. Jack read the paragraph under the photo: 杰克翻看他们的书，找到了一张看起来和下面场景一样的照片。 照片上的说明写着：麦克默多站。 杰克读了照片下面的段落：

Antarctica has many scientific research stations, representing countries from all over the world. The largest is named McMurdo Station. Researchers live there for weeks or even months at a time. 南极洲有许多科学考察站，代表了来自世界各地的国家。最大的一个叫做麦克默多站。研究人员在那里生活几周甚至几个月。

HONK! 嘎嘎！

Jack looked up. He saw four people come out of a yellow building and start toward the bus. They were all bundled in red parkas with hoods, goggles, and masks. They carried backpacks and camera bags. 杰克抬头看了看。 他看见四个人从一座黄色的建筑里走出来，朝公交车走去。 他们全都穿着红色的带兜帽的防寒服，戴着护目镜和面具。 他们背着背包和相机包。

“They must be researchers,” said Jack. “他们一定是研究人员，”杰克说。

“Let’s go talk to them,” said Annie. “我们去跟他们聊聊吧，”安妮说。

“We can’t,” said Jack. “They’ll ask why two kids are traveling alone in Antarctica.” “我们不能去，”杰克说。 “他们会问为什么两个孩子独自在南极旅行。”

“Maybe they won’t know we’re kids,” said Annie. “If we put our goggles and masks back on, we’ll look just like them, only shorter. They’ll think we’re short grown-ups.” “也许他们不会知道我们是小孩，”安妮说。 “如果我们把护目镜和面具重新戴上，我们看起来就会和他们一样，只是更矮一点。 他们会以为我们是矮个子大人。”

“Uh … I don’t think so,” said Jack. “呃……我不这么认为，”杰克说。

Just then someone jumped off the bus. “Hi, folks!” a woman shouted to the four researchers. “I’m Nancy—your bus driver and guide today!” 就在这时，有人从公交车上跳了下来。 “大家好！”一位女士朝四位研究人员喊道。 “我是南希——今天你们的公交车司机和导游！”

Nancy caught sight of Jack and Annie. She waved her arms at them. “Hello!” she called. “Are you two part of the group going up to the volcano?” She pointed toward a mountain looming in the distance. 南希看见了杰克和安妮。 她朝他们挥了挥手。 “你好！”她喊道。 “你们两个是去火山那边的那群人吗？”她指着远处一座高耸的山峰说道。

“Did you hear that?” Jack said to Annie. “A volcano!” He cupped his gloved hands around his mouth. “Yes!” he shouted back in his deepest voice. “We’re coming!” “你听到吗？”杰克对安妮说。 “是一座火山！”他用戴着手套的手捂住嘴喊道。 “是的！”他用最深的声音喊回去。 “我们来了！”

“We are?” Annie asked with surprise. “我们真的要去了吗？”安妮惊讶地问。

“A volcano— get it?” said Jack. “A volcano is ‘a burning mountain’!” “火山——明白了吗？”杰克说。 “火山就是‘燃烧的山’！”

“Oh, right!” said Annie. “Like in the rhyme—the burning mountain! Got it!” “哦，对啊！”安妮说。 “就像那首童谣里的——燃烧的山！明白了！”

“Quick, cover up your face!” said Jack. “快，把脸遮住！”杰克说。

Jack and Annie covered their eyes with their goggles and pulled up their face masks. Then they started walking toward the bus. 杰克和安妮用护目镜遮住眼睛，然后拉上面罩。 接着，他们朝公交车走去。

“Try not to talk to anyone unless we have to,” Jack said quickly. “And if we do, talk in a really deep voice.” “除非万不得已，否则不要和任何人说话，”杰克赶紧说道。 “如果必须说话，就用非常低的声音说。”

“No problem,” Annie croaked in a really deep voice. “没问题，”安妮用非常低沉的声音说道。

“Um … maybe you shouldn’t talk at all,” said Jack. “呃……也许你最好不要说话，”杰克说。

“Hurry!” Nancy called to them. “快点！”南希朝他们喊道。

“Coming!” Jack shouted in his deepest voice, and they started running across the snow. “来了！”杰克用他最深的声音喊道，他们开始在雪地上奔跑。

By the time Jack and Annie arrived at the bus, everyone but Nancy had climbed aboard. “Good. You made it just in time!” Nancy said. “Follow me!” She bounded up the steep steps of the red bus and sat in the driver’s seat. 杰克和安妮到达公交车时，除了南希，其他人都已经上车了。 “很好。” “你们刚好及时赶到了！”南希说。 “跟我来！”她蹦蹦跳跳地爬上红色巴士的陡峭台阶，坐在了司机座位上。

Without a word, Jack and Annie climbed on after Nancy. Walking down the aisle, Jack glanced at the others in the group. A couple of them nodded and he nodded back. Everyone was hidden behind goggles, ski masks, and bulky parkas. Jack couldn’t tell anyone’s age, or even whether they were male or female. Jack和Annie没有说话，爬上了巴士，坐在南希后面。 Jack沿着过道走过去，看了眼小组里的其他人。 其中有几个人点了点头，他也点了点头回应。 每个人都藏在护目镜、滑雪面具和厚重的羽绒服后面。 Jack看不出来他们的年龄，甚至分不清他们是男是女。

Jack and Annie sat a few rows behind the others. Jack took off his backpack and put it at his feet. Jack和Annie坐在其他人后面几排的位置上。 Jack取下背包，放在了自己的脚边。

“All set?” Nancy asked, looking in the bus mirror. “准备好了吗？”南希一边问，一边看着公交车的后视镜。

Jack and Annie nodded. 杰克和安妮点了点头。

Nancy closed the door and started the engine. As the bus’s giant wheels moved slowly over the gravel road, Jack looked out the window. 南希关上门，启动了引擎。 公交车巨大的车轮慢慢碾过碎石路时，杰克望向窗外。

The sun shone brightly on the wide, snowy fields. Glittering ice crystals blew in the wind. All the world around them seemed to be sparkling. 阳光明媚地照在广阔而雪白的田野上。 闪闪发光的冰晶被风吹着飘荡。 他们周围的世界似乎都在闪闪发亮。

“How’s everyone doing?” Nancy called over her shoulder. “Are you all happy campers?” “大家好吗？”南希回头喊道。 “你们都是快乐的露营者吗？”

Everyone, including Jack and Annie, nodded. 每个人都点了点头，包括杰克和安妮。

“Good. I like travelers who don’t complain!” Nancy joked. “很好。” “我喜欢那些不抱怨的旅行者！”南希开玩笑说。

So far, so good, Jack thought. No one seemed to suspect they were kids. 到目前为止，一切顺利，杰克想。 看起来没人怀疑他们是孩子。

“We have a short ride,” said Nancy. “But enough time for you all to introduce yourselves to me. I’m Nancy Tyler, and I work here in Antarctica as a guide, a bus driver, and a flight mechanic.” “我们很快就要出发了，”南希说。 “但你们有足够的时间向我自我介绍一下。” “我是南希·泰勒，我在南极这里做导游、公交车司机和飞行机械师。”

“Cool,” whispered Annie. “真酷，”安妮小声说。

“I know you all come from different countries as researchers and journalists,” said Nancy. “Start from the front and tell me who you are.” “我知道你们都来自不同的国家，是研究人员和记者，”南希说。 “从前面开始，告诉我你们是谁。”

The woman in front pulled down her face mask. “I’m Lucy Banks,” she said. “I’m an American, and I’m a space scientist. I’m writing a paper on the use of robots on the crater of Mount Erebus. Hopefully this will help our work on Mars someday.” 前面的女士摘下了她的面罩。 “我叫露西·班克斯，”她说。 “我是个美国人，也是个太空科学家。 我正在写一篇关于在埃里伯斯火山的火山口使用机器人的论文。 希望这有一天能帮助我们在火星上的工作。”

Oh, brother, thought Jack. What can we say? That we’re Jack and Annie from Frog Creek and we’ve come to Antarctica to find the fourth secret of happiness to save Merlin the magician in Camelot? 哦，天哪，杰克心想。我们该说什么呢？ 说我们是来自蛙溪镇的杰克和安妮，我们来到南极洲是为了找到幸福的第四个秘密，以拯救卡梅洛特的梅林魔法师吗？

“Wonderful, Lucy!” Nancy said. “Antarctica is as similar to Mars as any place you can find on earth. Next?” “太棒了，露西！”南希说。 “南极洲和火星在地球上是任何地方都最相似的。下一个？”

“Ali Khan, biologist from Turkey,” said the man sitting behind Lucy Banks. “I’m researching heat-resistant bacteria in the crater of Mount Erebus.” “阿里·汗，来自土耳其的生物学家，”坐在露西·班克斯后面的男人说。 “我正在研究埃里伯斯火山火山口里的耐热细菌。”

Quick, think! thought Jack. 快点，想啊！杰克心想。

“Very good!” said Nancy. “Next?” “很好！”南希说。 “下一个？”

“Tony Sars from Sydney, Australia,” another man said. “I’m a travel writer for the Sydney Morning Herald.” He held up a notebook. “托尼·萨尔斯，来自澳大利亚悉尼。”另一个人说。 “我是《悉尼晨邮报》的旅行作家。”他举起了一本笔记本。

“Good!” said Nancy. “很好！”南希说。

Yes! thought Jack. He yanked off a glove and pulled his notebook and a pencil out of his backpack. 对！杰克心想。 他摘下手套，从背包里拿出笔记本和一支铅笔。

“Kim Lee,” said the woman sitting behind Tony. “I’m a photographer for a Korean magazine.” “金·李，”坐在托尼后面的女人说。 “我为一家韩国杂志做摄影师。”

“Great,” said Nancy. “And my friends in the back?” “太好了，”南希说。 “那我后面的朋友呢？”

Without taking off his mask, Jack shouted in a deep voice, “Frog Creek Times, USA!” He held up his notebook. “Story about Antarctica. She’s … uh …” 杰克没有摘下他的面具，用低沉的声音喊道：“《蛙溪镇时报》，美国！”他举起自己的笔记本。 “关于南极洲的故事。她……呃……”

Annie held up her camera. “His photographer!” she called in a deep voice. 安妮举起了她的相机。 “他的摄影师！”，她用低沉的声音喊道。

“Excellent!” said Nancy. “A great group! We’ll hear more introductions later. Now, I know you’ve all heard this before, but I have to tell you again. It is very important to remember the rules here in Antarctica.” “太棒了！”，南希说道。 “一个很棒的团队！ 我们之后还会听到更多的介绍。 现在，我知道你们都听过这个，但我要再告诉你们一次。 记住这里的规则非常重要，尤其是在南极洲。

Jack opened his notebook and got ready to write down the rules. 杰克打开他的笔记本，准备写下这些规则。

“Never rush,” said Nancy. “You should always think about where you’re going and what you’re doing.” “不要着急，”南希说。 “你总是要想想自己要去哪儿，正在做什么。」

Jack scribbled: 杰克涂鸦着：





图中文字：Go slow! 图中文字：慢慢来！





“Never walk on snow and ice fields alone,” said Nancy. “In many places beneath the snow, there are deep, hidden cracks in the ice.” “不要独自在雪地和冰面上行走，”南希说。 “在雪下面的很多地方，冰层里都有很深的、隐藏的裂缝。”

Jack wrote: 杰克写道：





图中文字：Stay with others! 图中文字：和别人在一起！

图中文字：Cracks in ice! 图中文字：冰裂了！





“And remember, all of Antarctica is a nature preserve,” said Nancy. “Never, ever touch or disturb the wildlife.” “记住，整个南极洲都是一个自然保护区，”南希说道。 “永远、永远不要触碰或打扰野生动物。”

“Oops,” said Annie. “哎呀，”安妮说道。

Jack frowned. “We really broke the rules with those penguins,” he whispered. 杰克皱起了眉头。 “我们真的违反了规则，”他小声说，“那些企鹅。”

“I know, but we won’t do it again,” Annie whispered back. “我知道，但我们不会再这样做了，”安妮小声回答。

“Right,” said Jack. He wrote down: “对，”杰克说。他写下来：





图中文字：Never touch wildlife! 图中文字：千万别碰野生动物！





“Got all that?” Nancy asked the group. “都记住了吗？”南希问大家。

Everyone nodded. 大家都点了点头。

“Good,” said Nancy. “I look forward to sharing Antarctica with you today. I know you’ll all find some great information and stories!” “很好，”南希说。 “我很期待今天和你们一起分享南极洲。” 我知道你们都会找到一些很棒的信息和故事！

As the bus rolled along, no one gave Jack and Annie a second look. “Nancy called us ‘friends,’ ” Jack whispered to Annie. “The others must think she knows us.” 当公交车缓缓行驶时，没有人再次看向杰克和安妮。 “南希叫我们‘朋友’，”杰克对安妮小声说。 “其他人一定以为她认识我们。”

“Yeah, and she thinks we’re friends with them,” said Annie. “是啊，她还觉得我们和他们是朋友，”安妮说。

“We’re getting away with this,” said Jack. He could hardly believe it. “我们这次成功了，”杰克说。 他几乎不敢相信。

“It reminds me of our last mission,” Annie whispered, “on the ship with the ocean scientists.” “这让我想起了上次的任务，”安妮小声说，“在那艘装着海洋科学家的船上。”

“This is better,” said Jack. “Here everyone’s treating us like grown-ups, and I don’t feel like throwing up.” “这次更好，”杰克说。 “这里每个人都把我们当大人对待，我一点都不想吐。”

“And nowadays women get to do really cool stuff, too, like men do,” said Annie. “现在女性也能做很多很酷的事情，就像男性一样，”安妮说。

“Good point,” said Jack. “But there’s still stuff about our rhyme I don’t get.” He pulled the rhyme out of his pocket, and he and Annie read it silently: “说得对，”杰克说。 “但是还有一些关于我们的咒语我不太明白。”他从口袋里掏出咒语，他和安妮默默地读起来：

For the final secret, you must go

To a burning mountain of ice and snow

On wheels, by air, then all fall down,

Till you come to the Cave of the Ancient Crown.

Then speed to Camelot by close of day,

Lest grief take Merlin forever away. 最后一个秘密，你必须去一座燃烧的冰雪山，坐轮子、乘飞机，然后全都掉下来，直到你来到古代王冠洞穴。然后赶快在天黑前赶到卡美洛，否则悲伤会永远带走梅林。

“See, it sounds like it’s talking about a magical world,” said Jack. “But Antarctica is a hundred percent real. It’s filled with scientists!” “你看，听起来就像是在说一个魔法世界，”杰克说。 “但是南极洲是百分之百真实存在的。 那里有科学家！”

“I know, but some of the rhyme fits,” said Annie. “Like you said, the ‘burning mountain of ice and snow’ is the volcano, Mount Erebus.” She pointed out the window of the bus. “And there it is.” “我知道，但有些咒语是符合的，”安妮说。 “就像你说的，‘燃烧的冰与雪的山’就是火山，埃瑞博斯山。”她指着巴士的窗户。 “就在那儿。”

A white mountain loomed in the distance. Ice and snow covered its slopes, and puffs of smoke rose from its peak, drifting into the blue sky. 一座白色的山出现在远处。 冰雪覆盖着它的山坡，烟雾从山顶升起，飘向蓝天。

“It’s burning, all right,” said Jack. “它真的在燃烧，”杰克说。

“And we’re on wheels,” said Annie. “而且我们还在轮子上，”安妮说。

“Yep,” said Jack. He looked at the rhyme again. “Okay, ‘burning mountain of ice and snow,’ ‘on wheels’—but then what about ‘by air’? What’s—” “对，”杰克说。 他又看了看那首诗。 “好吧，‘燃烧的冰和雪的山’，‘在轮子上’——那‘通过空气’又是什么意思呢？——”

“Oh, my gosh!” said Annie, craning her neck. “天啊！”安妮喊道，伸长了脖子。

“What?” said Jack. “怎么了？”杰克说。

“Look over there!” said Annie. “看那边！”安妮说。
