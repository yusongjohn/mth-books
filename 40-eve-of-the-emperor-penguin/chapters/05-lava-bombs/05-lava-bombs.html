5. Lava Bombs 5. 熔岩炸弹

Jack didn’t feel like a happy camper anymore. Walking through the cold, thin air, he looked at the snowmobiles parked near the hut. 杰克不再觉得当个快乐的露营者是一件好事了。 他走在寒冷稀薄的空气中，看着停在小屋旁边的雪地摩托。

“We don’t know how to drive snowmobiles!” Jack whispered to Annie. “We haven’t trained for anything, including altitude sickness!” “我们不知道怎么开雪地摩托！”杰克低声对安妮说。 “我们连高原反应都没训练过！”

“That’s okay. If we get in trouble, we can use the wand,” said Annie. “没关系。 如果我们遇到麻烦，可以用魔杖，”安妮说。

“No, we can’t,” said Jack. “We can’t use the wand just for ourselves. Plus, we haven’t tried our hardest yet.” “不，我们不能这么做，”杰克说。 “我们不能只为了自己使用魔杖。 而且，我们还没尽全力去尝试呢。”

“This way, gang,” called Nancy. She was ushering everyone into the small orange building. “这边走，大家，”南希喊道。 她正把大家领进那座小橙色建筑里。

Jack and Annie followed the group inside. The one-room hut had plastic chairs, a small heater, axes, jugs of water, and shelves with boxes of trail mix. 杰克和安妮跟着大家走了进去。 这个只有一间房间的小屋里有塑料椅子、一个小暖气器、斧头、水罐，还有架子上装着 trail mix 的盒子。

“Have a seat and help yourself to some of the best water in the world,” Nancy said. “It comes from melted glacier ice.” “请坐下来，大家可以随便喝一些世界上最好的水，”南希说。 “这些水是从融化的冰川冰里来的。”

Everyone sat on the plastic chairs. Lucy, Kim, Tony, and Ali poured water into tin cups and lowered their masks to drink. Jack was thirsty, but he shook his head no at Annie. He was afraid for them to show their faces. 大家都坐在了塑料椅子上。 露西、金、托尼和阿里把水倒进铁杯里，然后把面具往下拉喝水。 杰克口渴了，但他摇摇头，对安妮说不。 他担心他们露出脸会被发现。

“Before we take off, I want to warn you again about the snowmobiles,” said Nancy. “No matter how much training you’ve had, you must be very careful. On your ride up to the crater, remember to drive sideways so if the snowmobile slips and rolls over, your leg won’t get crushed.” “在我们出发之前，我想再提醒你们一次关于雪地摩托的事情，”南希说。 “不管你接受过多少训练，你都必须非常小心。” 坐雪地摩托上 crater 的时候，记得要侧着开，这样如果雪地摩托打滑翻倒了，你的腿就不会被压到。”

Everyone nodded. 大家都点了点头。

How do you drive sideways? Jack wondered in a panic. 怎么侧着开呀？ 杰克慌张地问。

“Don’t be afraid to go fast, and don’t lock up the brakes,” said Nancy. “Locking your brakes can spell disaster.” “不要害怕开快一点，刹车也不要踩死，”南希说。 “刹车没锁好可能会带来灾难。”

“And watch out for lava bombs,” said Ali, the biologist. “还要小心火山弹，”生物学家阿里说。

“Lava bombs?” Jack piped. He quickly cleared his throat and deepened his voice. “Excuse me. Lava bombs?” “火山弹？”杰克插话道。 他赶紧清了清嗓子，把声音压低了。 “不好意思，火山弹？”

“Lava that spits from the crater,” said Ali. “是从火山口喷出来的熔岩，”阿里说。

“Spits?” repeated Jack. “喷出来？”杰克重复道。

“Like oatmeal that bubbles and spatters out of the pot,” said Lucy, the space scientist. “就像燕麦粥在锅里冒泡溅出来一样，”太空科学家露西说。

“Except these bombs aren’t made of oatmeal,” said Ali. “They’re blobs of fiery hot liquid rock. Some of them can be as big as a car. They burn deep holes in the ice and snow.” “不过这些火山弹可不是用燕麦做的，”阿里说。 “它们是滚烫的液态岩石块。” 有些它们可以大得像一辆汽车。 它们会在冰和雪上烧出深深的洞。

“If they hit you …,” said Tony, chuckling. “Well, just think about it.” “如果它们打到你……，”托尼说着，笑了起来。 “好吧，你想想看。”

Jack didn’t want to think about it. 杰克不想去想这件事。

“Seriously, it’s pretty neat,” said Nancy. “For millions of years, hot gas and lava have carved deep, hidden places beneath the surface ice of these slopes—caverns and towers. No one knows all the secrets of Mount Erebus.” “真的，这还挺酷的，”南希说。 “数百万年来，热气和熔岩一直在这些斜坡表面的冰层下雕刻出深藏的洞穴和塔楼。 没有人知道埃里伯斯山的所有秘密。

Nancy took a last sip of water and put her cup down. “Okay, guys, you have a few hours to do your experiments and get your stories. Then Pete will come back for us. Let’s go!” 南希最后喝了一口水，把杯子放下了。 “好了，大家，你们有几小时的时间做实验和写故事。” 然后皮特会回来接我们。我们走吧！“

As Jack stood up to go, he almost fell over. The room was spinning. He closed his eyes, but that only made it worse. After the others had filed out the door, Jack sat back down on his chair. I’ll just stay here for a second, he thought, his heart pounding. 杰克站起来要走的时候，差点摔倒了。 房间在旋转。 他闭上眼睛，但这让情况更糟了。 其他人走出去后，杰克又坐回了椅子上。 他心想，我在这儿待一会儿，心跳得可快了。

“You okay?” said Annie, coming back into the hut. “你还好吗？”安妮走进小屋，问道。

“I’m dizzy,” said Jack, trying to catch his breath. “I think I’ve got altitude sickness.” “我有点晕，”杰克努力喘着气说。 “我想我得了高原反应。”

“I don’t feel so great, either,” said Annie. “Try taking off your mask and goggles so you can breathe better.” She helped Jack lift off his mask and goggles. “Does that help?” “我也不太舒服，”安妮说。 “试试摘下你的面具和护目镜，这样你就能呼吸得更顺畅一些。”她帮杰克摘下了面具和护目镜。 “这样好些了吗？”

“Yeah, a little…” Jack took a deep breath. “But we need lots of help.” “嗯，好一点……”杰克深吸了一口气。 “但我们还需要很多帮助。”

“What do you mean?” said Annie. “你是说啥？”安妮问。

“Help getting over altitude sickness,” said Jack, “help driving snowmobiles, help dodging lava bombs, help finding an ancient crown … and what is that, anyway—an ancient crown?” “比如帮助我们克服高原反应，帮助我们驾驶雪地摩托，帮助我们躲避熔岩炸弹，帮助我们找到古代的王冠……那个是什么， anyway——古代的王冠？”

Nancy poked her head back into the hut. “What’s up, guys? You coming?” 南希把头又探进小屋里。 “怎么了，大家？你们要来吗？”

“Yikes,” said Annie. “天哪，”安妮说。

Jack quickly tried to put his goggles and mask back on. But it was too late. 杰克赶紧试着把护目镜和面具重新戴上。 但是已经太晚了。

“What—what are you—who are you?” Nancy sputtered. “You’re not a journalist! You’re a kid!” “你——你是什么人——你是谁？”南希结结巴巴地说。 “你不是记者！你是个小孩！”

“Don’t worry,” said Annie in a deep voice. “He’s my son.” “别担心，”安妮用低沉的声音说。 “他是我的儿子。”

“What?” said Nancy. “什么？”杰克说。

“He is,” said Annie. “I often take him on my assignments.” “他是，”安妮说。 “我经常带他去执行我的任务。”

“You what?” said Nancy. “你说什么？”纳西说。

“Annie, give it up,” said Jack. “We’re caught.” “安妮，放弃吧，”杰克说。 “我们被困住了。”

“Oh, okay,” said Annie. She pushed up her goggles and pulled down her mask. “I’m Annie, and he’s Jack. We’re sister and brother.” “哦，好吧，”安妮说。 她推了推护目镜，拉下口罩。 “我是安妮，他是杰克。 我们是兄妹。”

“I’m going to have a heart attack!” said Nancy. “What are you kids doing here?” “我要心脏病发作了！”南希说。 “你们这些孩子在这里干什么？”

“We were looking for …” Annie stopped. “Um …” Jack knew even Annie couldn’t explain their mission to save Merlin to Nancy. “我们在找……”安妮停了下来。 “呃……”杰克知道，即使安妮也无法向南希解释他们拯救梅林的使命。

“This is unbelievable!” said Nancy. “I’ve got to get you two back to the station! Your parents must be frantic. I can’t believe this!” “这太难以置信了！”南希说。 “我得赶快把你们俩送回车站！ 你爸妈一定急坏了。 我简直不敢相信！”

“No, no, it’s our fault. No one will blame you,” said Jack. “不，不，这是我们的错。 没人会怪你的，”杰克说。

But Nancy was pulling out a small radio. “Come in, Pete. Come in.” 但是南希拿出了一台小收音机。 “请回答，皮特。请回答。”

The radio crackled and popped. Then Pete’s voice came through. “Hear you, Nancy,” he said. 收音机里发出噼里啪啦的声音。 然后皮特的声音传了过来。 “收到，南希，”他说。

“Pete, you need to get the chopper back here quick and pick up two of my group. They’re just little kids!” “皮特，你得赶快把直升机开回来，接上我组里的两个孩子。” 他们只是两个小孩子！“

Not so little, thought Jack. 杰克心想，他们可不小。

“Repeat that, Nancy,” said Pete. “再说一遍，南希，”皮特说。

“Two little kids came with the group!” Nancy shouted. “I didn’t know they were kids—oh, it’s too hard to explain! Can you come back right away, please?” “有两个小孩子和队伍一起来了！”南希喊道。 “我不知道他们原来是孩子——哦，这太难解释了！ 你能马上回来吗？”

“Sure thing,” said Pete. “You head up with the others. I’ll pick up the kids.” “当然可以，”皮特说。 “你先和其他人一起走吧。 我去接那两个孩子。”

“Thanks. They’ll be here at the hut, waiting for you to take them back,” said Nancy. “Over and out.” “谢谢。” “它们会在小屋等着你，等着你带它们回去，”南希说。 “完毕，再见。”
