4. Hands Up! 4. 举起手来！

“It’s the colt!” said Annie. “He’s looking for his mother!” “是那匹小马驹！”安妮说。 “它在找妈妈！”

She ran toward the wild-eyed little horse. 她朝那匹惊慌失措的小马跑去。

“Wait!” called Jack. “Oh, brother.” He pulled the book out of his pack. “等等！”杰克喊道。 “哦，天哪。”他从背包里抽出书。

He found a chapter titled “Horses of the Wild West.” He started reading. 他找到一章，题目是“蛮荒西部的马”。他开始读起来。

At the end of the 1800s, over a million wild horses, called mustangs, wandered the West. These tough, fast horses were descendants of runaway Spanish horses. Mustang herders captured them and sold them to ranchers. Breaking a wild mustang took great skill. 19世纪末，超过一百万匹野马（叫做mustangs）在西部游荡。这些强壮又快的马是逃跑的西班牙马的后代。野马牧人把它们抓起来，卖给牧场主。驯服一匹野马需要很高的技巧。

Jack turned the page. There was a picture of a herd of horses. Two of them even looked like the beautiful mare and her colt. 杰克翻过一页。 有一幅马群的图画。 其中两匹甚至看起来就像那匹美丽的母马和她的小马驹。

“Hey, Annie,” Jack called. “You should see this picture.” “嘿，安妮，”杰克喊道。 “你该看看这幅图。”

Annie didn’t answer. 安妮没有回答。

Jack looked up. 杰克抬起头。

Annie was trying to get close to the young mustang, but he kept darting away. 安妮想靠近那匹小野马，可它不停地跑开。

“Watch it! He’s wild!” said Jack. “当心！ 它是野的！”杰克说。

Annie was speaking softly to the colt. 安妮正轻声对小马驹说话。

She slowly reached out and grabbed the end of his rope. Still talking to him, she led him to a broad wooden post. 她慢慢伸出手，抓住了绳子的末端。 她一边跟它说话，一边把它牵到一根粗木桩前。

“Stop! Don’t do anything!” said Jack. “停下！ 什么都别做！”杰克说。

He flipped the pages of his book. He found a section called “How to Treat a Horse.” 他飞快地翻着书页。 他找到一节，叫“怎样对待马”。

The basic rules on how to treat a horse are simple: a soft hand, a firm voice, a sunny attitude, praise, and reward. 对待马的基本规则很简单：手要轻柔，声音要坚定，态度要阳光，还要多表扬和奖励。

“I’ve got the rules!” shouted Jack. “Don’t do anything before I write them down!” “我找到规则了！”杰克喊道。 “等我写下来之前什么都别做！”

Jack pulled out his notebook and pencil. He wrote: 杰克拿出笔记本和铅笔，写道：

图中文字：Horse Rules

1. soft hand

2. firm voice

3. Sunny attitude

4. praise

5. reward 图中文字：马的规则 1. 轻柔的手 2. 坚定的声音 3. 愉快的态度 4. 表扬 5. 奖励

“Okay, listen—” Jack looked up. “好，听着——”杰克抬起头。

But Annie was already sitting on the colt’s back! 可安妮已经坐在小马驹背上了！

Jack froze. He held his breath. 杰克愣住了。 他屏住呼吸。

The mustang whinnied and pawed the ground. He snorted and tossed his head. 野马嘶叫着，用蹄子刨着地。 他喷着鼻息，甩着头。

Annie kept patting his neck and talking softly. 安妮不停地拍着他的脖子，轻声说话。

Finally the young horse grew still. 终于，这匹小马安静下来。

Annie smiled at Jack. “I named him Sunset,” she said. 安妮朝杰克笑了笑。 “我给他取名叫日落，”她说。

Jack let out his breath. 杰克松了口气。

“Let’s go,” said Annie. “We have to take him to his mom.” “我们走吧，”安妮说。 “我们得带他去找妈妈。”

“Are you nuts?” said Jack. “We have to solve our riddle. It’ll be dark soon. And those guys were real bad guys, I could tell.” “你疯了吗？”杰克说。 “我们得解开谜语。 天很快就黑了。 而且那些家伙是真正的坏蛋，我看得出来。”

“We don’t have any choice,” said Annie. “我们别无选择，”安妮说。

“Oh, brother.” Jack knew she wouldn’t change her mind. “Let’s see what the book says.” He read more about mustangs:

Wild mustangs live together in families. The bond between a mare and her young is very strong. His sounds of distress or hunger will always bring her to him. A mustang cannot bear to wander alone. 野马群以家庭为单位生活在一起。母马和小马之间的纽带非常牢固。小马发出痛苦或饥饿的声音时，母马总会立刻跑来。野马无法忍受独自流浪。

Jack groaned. He looked at Sunset. The young mustang did have a sad look in his eyes. 杰克哼了一声。 他看着日落。 那匹小野马眼里果然带着悲伤的神情。

“Okay, we’ll make a plan,” he said. “But first I have to get out of these boots.” “好吧，我们想个办法，”他说。 “可我得先脱掉这些靴子。”

Jack grabbed one of his boots and pulled. 杰克抓住一只靴子往外拉。

“Hurry!” said Annie. “快点！”安妮说。

“I can’t even think in these things!” said Jack. “穿着这些东西我连思考都不能！”杰克说。

He huffed and puffed and pulled. Then a deep voice stopped him cold: “Hands up—or I’ll shoot!” 他喘着粗气，使劲拉。 接着一个低沉的声音让他愣住了：“举起手——不然我就开枪！”

Jack let go of his boot. He raised his hands in the air. So did Annie. 杰克松开靴子。 他把双手举到空中。安妮也一样。

A cowboy rode out of an alley. His face was bony and tanned. He was riding a gray horse and pointing a six-shooter. 一个牛仔从小巷里骑了出来。 他的脸瘦削而晒成了棕色。 他骑着一匹灰马，手里指着一把六响手枪。

“I reckon you’re the smallest horse thieves I’ve ever come across,” he said. “我看你们是我见过最小的盗马贼，”他说。
