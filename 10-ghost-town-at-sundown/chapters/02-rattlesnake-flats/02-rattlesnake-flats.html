2. Rattlesnake Flats 2. 响尾蛇平原

The air was hot and dry. 空气又热又干。

Jack and Annie peeked outside. 杰克和安妮向外面偷看。

The tree house had landed in a lone tree on a prairie. The sun was low in the sky. 树屋落在大草原上一棵孤零零的树上。 太阳低垂在天空。

Right in front of them was the town from the cover of the book. In real life it looked empty and spooky. 就在他们面前，是书封面上的那座镇子。 现实里，它看上去空荡荡的，阴森可怕。

To one side of the town was a patch of ground with several tombstones. 镇子一侧有一块地，上面立着几块墓碑。

“That’s creepy,” said Annie. “真诡异，”安妮说。

“Yeah,” said Jack. He took a deep breath. “What’s our riddle say?” “是啊，”杰克说。 他深吸一口气。 “我们的谜语怎么说？”

Annie held up the ancient scroll. She unrolled it. Then she and Jack read together: 安妮举起那卷古老的卷轴，把它展开。 然后她和杰克一起念道：

Out of the blue,

my lonely voice

calls out to you.

Who am I? Am I? 突然间，我孤独的声音朝你喊了出来。我是谁？我是谁？

Jack pushed his glasses into place and read the riddle again to himself. 杰克推了推眼镜，又把谜语默读了一遍。

“There must be a mistake,” he said. “ ‘ Am I?’ is written twice.” “肯定写错了，”他说。 “‘是我吗？’写了两遍。”

“Well, I don’t hear any voices now,” Annie said as she looked out the window. “嗯，我现在可没听见什么声音，”安妮一边看着窗外一边说。

There were no human sounds at all—only the buzzing of flies and the whistling of the dry wind. 完全没有人的声音——只有苍蝇的嗡嗡声和干风的呼啸。

“Let’s look at the book,” Jack said. “我们看看这本书吧，”杰克说。

He opened the book. The pages were yellow with age. He found a picture of the town and read the words beneath it out loud: 他打开书。 书页因年代久远而发黄。 他找到镇子的图画，大声读出图下方的文字：

In the 1870s, Rattlesnake Flats was a rest stop for the stagecoach that carried passengers from Santa Fe, New Mexico, to Fort Worth, Texas. When the creek dried up, everyone left. By 1880, it was a “ghost town.” 19世纪70年代，Rattlesnake Flats是连接新墨西哥州圣达菲和得克萨斯州沃斯堡的驿车停靠站。当溪流干涸后，所有人都离开了。到1880年，这里变成了一座“鬼城”。

“Wow, a ghost town,” said Annie, her eyes wide. “Let’s take a quick look around,” said Jack. “So we can leave before dark.” “我们先很快四处看看，”杰克说。 “这样我们能在天黑前离开。”

“Right,” said Annie. “Let’s hurry.” She started down the rope ladder. “好，”安妮说。 “我们快点。”她开始顺着绳梯往下爬。

Jack put the old book into his pack. Then he followed Annie down the ladder. 杰克把那本旧书放进背包。 然后他跟着安妮下了梯子。

They stood by the tree and looked about. Tumbleweeds blew across the dry ground. 他们站在树旁，环顾四周。 风滚草在干燥的地面上翻滚。

Suddenly something jumped past them. 突然有什么东西从他们身边跳过去。

“Yikes!” they both said. “呀！”他们一起叫道。

But it was just a rabbit—a lone, long-legged rabbit hopping past them. 可那只是一只兔子——一只孤单的、腿很长的兔子从他们身边跳过。

“Hey, he’s just like that rabbit we saw at home,” said Jack. “嘿，他跟我们在家看见的那只兔子一模一样，”杰克说。

“Yeah, that rabbit must have been a sign of things to come,” said Annie. “是啊，那只兔子一定是后来要发生的事的预兆，”安妮说。

The rabbit hopped across the prairie and out of sight. 兔子跳过草原，消失在视线之外。

“I’d better take notes,” said Jack. “我最好记点笔记，”杰克说。

He reached into his backpack and took out his notebook and pencil. 他伸手进背包，拿出笔记本和铅笔。

He wrote: 他写道：

图中文字：rabbits with long legs 图中文字：长腿的兔子

“What’s that sound?” said Annie. “那是什么声音？”安妮说。

“What sound?” asked Jack. “什么声音？”杰克问。

“That rattling sound!” said Annie. “就是那种嗒嗒的声音！”安妮说。

Jack looked up. “What?” he said. 杰克抬起头。 “什么？”他说。

“There!” Annie pointed to a rattlesnake. It was about a hundred feet away. It was coiled up and rattling. “那儿！”安妮指向一条响尾蛇。 它大约在一百英尺外。 它盘成一团，正在发出响声。

Jack took one look at the snake and ran. Annie ran, too. They ran past the graveyard and right into the ghost town. 杰克看了一眼那条蛇就跑了。安妮也跑了。 他们跑过墓地，径直跑进了鬼镇。

“I guess that’s why this town is called Rattlesnake Flats,” said Annie when they stopped. “我猜这就是为什么这镇子叫响尾蛇平原，”他们停下来时安妮说。

Jack looked around. The town was hardly big enough to call a town. There was one unpaved street and a few old buildings. 杰克四处看了看。 这镇子小得几乎称不上镇子。 只有一条没有铺路的街道和几栋旧房子。

It was quiet, too quiet. 这里很安静，安静得过分。

“Look, a store,” said Annie. “看，一家商店，”安妮说。

She pointed to a building. The faded sign said GENERAL STORE. “Let’s look inside. Maybe the answer to the riddle is in there.” 她指着一座建筑物。 那块褪了色的招牌上写着“杂货店”。 “我们进去看看。 也许谜语的答案就在里面。”

Jack and Annie stepped onto the porch. The wooden boards creaked loudly. The door had fallen off its hinges. They peeked inside. 杰克和安妮踏上了门廊。 木板嘎吱嘎吱响得很厉害。 门已经从合页上掉了下来。 他们向里面偷看。

The air was thick with dust. Spider webs hung from the ceiling. 空气里满是厚厚的灰尘。 蜘蛛网从天花板上垂下来。

“Maybe we shouldn’t go in,” said Jack. “也许我们不该进去，”杰克说。

“But what if the answer’s here?” said Annie. “Let’s just take a quick look.” “可要是答案就在这儿呢？”安妮说。 “我们就很快看一眼。”

Jack took a deep breath. “Okay.” 杰克深吸一口气。 “好吧。”

He and Annie tiptoed into the store. 他和安妮踮着脚走进商店。

“Look,” said Annie. She picked up a pair of rusty spurs. “看，”安妮说。 她捡起一对生锈的马刺。

“Careful,” said Jack. He poked at other stuff in the store—an old feed sack, a rusted tin cup, a faded calendar dated 1878. “小心，”杰克说。 他戳了戳店里别的东西——一只旧饲料袋、一只生锈的锡杯、一本写着1878年的褪色日历。

“Oh, wow,” said Annie. She held up two cowboy hats. She put one on and handed the other one to Jack. “For you.” “哦，哇，”安妮说。 她举起两顶牛仔帽。 她戴上一顶，把另一顶递给杰克。 “给你。”

“It’s too dusty,” said Jack. “太脏了，”杰克说。

“Just blow on it,” said Annie. “吹一吹就行，”安妮说。

Jack blew on his hat. A cloud of dust rose up. Jack sneezed. 杰克朝帽子上吹了吹。 一团尘土扬了起来。

“Just try it on!” said Annie. “试试看嘛！”安妮说。

Jack put the hat on. It nearly covered his eyes. 杰克戴上帽子。 帽子几乎遮住了他的眼睛。

“Boots!” said Annie. She pointed to a row of cowboy boots on a shelf. “There are even some small ones, like our sizes. Here’s a pair for you.” She handed the boots to Jack. “靴子！”安妮说。 她指着架子上一排牛仔靴。 “居然还有小号的，跟我们的尺码差不多。 这儿有一双给你。”她把靴子递给杰克。

“They’re not ours,” he said. “这不是我们的，”他说。

“I know, but just try them on,” said Annie. “我知道，可你就试试吧，”安妮说。

Jack turned his boots upside down and shook them as hard as he could. 杰克把靴子倒过来，用尽力气摇。

“What are you doing?” Annie asked, pulling on another pair of boots. “你在干什么？”安妮一边问，一边又拉上一双靴子。

“Checking for scorpions,” Jack said. “看看有没有蝎子，”杰克说。

“Ja-ack.” Annie laughed. “Try them on!” “杰——克。”安妮笑了。 “试试看！”

Jack sighed. He pulled off his sneakers. He pushed his feet into the boots. He pushed and pushed. The boots were really stiff. Finally he got his feet inside. Then he tried to walk. 杰克叹了口气。 他脱掉运动鞋。 他把脚往靴子里塞。 他塞啊塞。 这双靴子硬极了。 终于把脚塞进去了。 然后他试着走路。

“Owww!” he said. “Forget it.” He started to pull off the boots. “哎哟！”他说。 “算了。”他开始往下脱靴子。

“What’s that?” Annie said. “那是什么？”安妮说。

Jack froze. 杰克愣住了。

“Piano music,” said Annie. “Maybe it’s the voice in the riddle! Come on!” “钢琴声，”安妮说。 “也许就是谜语里的那个声音！快点！”

Jack threw his sneakers into his backpack and hobbled after Annie. 杰克把运动鞋扔进背包，一瘸一拐地跟着安妮。
