3. Player Piano 3. 自动钢琴

Outside, the sad tune played on. 外面，那支悲伤的曲子还在继续。

“It’s coming from there,” said Annie. “是从那边传来的，”安妮说。

She crept toward a building that had a sign with the word HOTEL on it. Jack limped after her. 她悄悄走向一座挂着“旅馆”牌子的房子。 杰克一瘸一拐地跟在她后面。

Annie slowly pushed open a swinging door. They peeked inside. 安妮慢慢推开一扇摇门。 他们向里面偷看。

The fading daylight lit a piano in the corner of the room. The keys were moving up and down. But no one was there! 渐渐暗下去的日光，照着房间角落里的一架钢琴。 琴键自己上下动着。 可里面一个人也没有！

“Yikes,” whispered Annie. “A ghost playing the piano!” “呀，”安妮低声说。 “有鬼在弹钢琴！”

Suddenly the keys were still. The air got very cold. 突然琴键停住了。 空气变得非常冷。

“No. No way,” said Jack. “There’s no such thing as a ghost.” “不。 才不要，”杰克说。 “世界上根本没有鬼。”

“We saw one in ancient Egypt,” whispered Annie. “我们在古埃及见过一个，”安妮小声说。

“Yeah, but that was ancient Egypt,” said Jack. Even so, his heart raced. “是啊，可那是古埃及，”杰克说。 尽管如此，他的心还是狂跳起来。

“I’ll look it up.” Jack pulled out the Wild West book. He found a picture of a piano. He read aloud: “我查查书。”杰克拿出《狂野西部》这本书。 他找到一幅钢琴的图，大声念道：

Player pianos were popular in the Old West. The piano played automatically when someone pumped its floor pedals. Later, with the help of electricity, the piano played all by itself. 自动钢琴在旧西部很受欢迎。当有人踩踏它的踏板时，钢琴会自动演奏。后来，有了电的帮助，钢琴可以自己演奏了。

“Whew.” Jack closed the book. “I knew there was an answer,” he said. “It must be electric, and somehow it came on.” “呼。”杰克合上书。 “我就知道会有解释，”他说。 “一定是电动的，不知怎么就自己响起来了。”

“I didn’t know they had electricity in the Wild West,” said Annie. “我不知道蛮荒西部还有电，”安妮说。

“They didn’t,” said Jack. “他们没有，”杰克说。

He looked at Annie. “Oh, man, let’s get out of here!” he said. 他看着安妮。 “天哪，我们快走吧！”他说。

Jack and Annie backed out of the hotel. 杰克和安妮从旅馆里退了出来。

When they got outside, they heard another sound: horse hooves thumping against the hard ground. 他们走到外面，又听见另一种声音：马蹄砰砰敲打着坚硬的地面。

A cloud of dust seemed to be moving toward the town. As it got closer, Jack saw three riders. They were herding a small band of horses. 一团尘土似乎正朝镇子移过来。 走近后，杰克看见三个骑马的人。 他们正赶着一小群马。

“Hide!” Jack said. “躲起来！”杰克说。

“Where?” said Annie. “躲哪儿？”安妮说。

Jack looked around wildly. He saw two barrels outside the hotel. 杰克慌忙四处看。 他看见旅馆外有两个木桶。

“There!” he said. “那儿！”他说。

Jack and Annie hurried to the empty barrels. Jack climbed inside one and tried to scrunch down. His hat wouldn’t fit! He jumped out of the barrel and threw his hat into the hotel. 杰克和安妮赶紧钻进空木桶。 杰克爬进一只桶里，想把自己缩下去。 他的帽子塞不进去！ 他跳出木桶，把帽子扔进旅馆。

“Mine, too!” said Annie. “我的也是！”安妮说。

Jack grabbed hers and threw it. Then he scrambled back into the barrel. Just in time. 杰克抓过她的帽子也扔了进去。 然后他连忙爬回桶里。刚好来得及。

Jack heard the horses thunder into town. He peeked through a crack in the barrel and saw a blur of cowboys and horses go by. 杰克听见马群轰隆隆冲进镇子。他从桶缝里往外看，只见牛仔和马影一晃而过。 他从桶缝里往外看，只见牛仔和马影一晃而过。

“Whoa!” “Whoa!” “Whoa!” men shouted. “吁！”“吁！”“吁！”男人们喊着。

Jack heard the horses come to a halt. They stamped and snorted. All he could see were shadows through the crack. 杰克听见马群停了下来。 它们跺着蹄，喷着鼻息。 透过缝隙，他只能看见影子。

Dust covered Jack. He had to sneeze. He pinched his nose. 灰尘落满了杰克全身。他忍不住要打喷嚏。 他捏住鼻子。

“The creek bed must have dried up!” a cowboy yelled. “This town’s a ghost!” “河床一定干了！”一个牛仔喊道。 “这镇子成鬼镇了！”

“Yup, it gives me the shivers,” said another. “Let’s camp over the rise.” “对，怪吓人的，”另一个说。 “咱们到土丘那边去露营吧。”

Jack really had to sneeze now. He pinched his nose tighter. But he couldn’t stop the sneeze. He let out a choked “Ah-choo!” 杰克现在真的很想打喷嚏。 他更用力地捏住鼻子。 可他忍不住打了个喷嚏。 他憋出一声闷闷的“阿嚏！”

“What was that?” someone said. “那是什么？”有人说。

Just then a loud whinny split the air. Jack saw a beautiful horse rear up. 就在这时，一声响亮的马嘶划破空气。 杰克看见一匹漂亮的马扬起前蹄。

She had no rider or saddle, just a rope around her neck. She was as red as the sunset. She had a wild black mane and a white star above her eyes. 她没有骑手，也没有马鞍，脖子上只套着一根绳子。 她红得像夕阳一样。 她有一头蓬乱的黑鬃，眼睛上方有一颗白星。

“We can’t keep fighting this one, boss!” a cowboy yelled. “老板，这匹马我们斗不过了！”一个牛仔喊道。

“Yup. She wants her colt,” another said. “We shouldn’t have left him behind.” “没错。 她想要她的小马驹，”另一个说。 “我们不该把他丢下的。”

“He was too slow,” a growly voice said. “We’ll sell her when we cross the border.” “它太慢了，”一个低沉咆哮的声音说。 “等过了边境，我们就把它卖掉。”

That’s terrible! thought Jack. He knew Annie must be upset, too. He just hoped that she wouldn’t jump out of her barrel. 太糟糕了！杰克想。 他知道安妮一定也很难过。 他只希望安妮别从木桶里跳出来。

But the cowboys pulled the red horse away. The ground rang from the pounding of hooves as they galloped off. 可牛仔们把那匹红马拉走了。 他们疾驰而去，马蹄声震得地面嗡嗡响。

Jack and Annie stood up. They watched the riders disappear into the dust. 杰克和安妮站了起来。 他们看着骑马的人消失在尘土里。

The pounding faded away. All was quiet again, except for the lazy buzzing of flies. 马蹄声渐渐远去。 一切又安静下来，只剩下苍蝇懒洋洋的嗡嗡声。

“They were mean to that horse,” Annie said in a low, angry voice. “他们对那匹马太狠了，”安妮压着嗓子、生气地说。

“I know. But there was nothing we could do,” said Jack. His boots were killing him. He climbed out of his barrel. “我知道。 可我们无能为力，”杰克说。 他的靴子把他夹得受不了。 他从木桶里爬出来。

“Man, I have to get these off,” he said. “伙计，我得把这些脱掉，”他说。

Jack sat down on the porch of the hotel. He grabbed the foot of one boot and pulled. 杰克在旅馆的门廊上坐下。 他抓住一只靴子的脚尖往外拉。

“Jack,” said Annie, “I think there is something we can do.” “杰克，”安妮说，“我觉得我们能做点什么。”

“What?” Jack looked up. “什么？”杰克抬起头。

A small horse was running down the road. He was as red as the wild mother horse. He had the same black mane and white star above his eyes. 一匹小马正沿着路跑来。 他红得像那匹野生的母马。 他也有同样的黑鬃毛，眼睛上方还有一颗白色星斑。

A rope was around his neck. He looked very lost. 脖子上套着一根绳子。 它看上去非常迷惘。
