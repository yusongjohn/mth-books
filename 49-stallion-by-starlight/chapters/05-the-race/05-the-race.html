5. The Race 5. 比赛

As they followed Alexander, Jack wished he hadn’t shown off. What if the prince challenged him to mount a moving chariot? Or hunt a lion? As he followed Alexander out of the room, Annie hurried along with him. 当他们跟着亚历山大时，杰克希望自己没有炫耀。 万一王子挑战他去驾驶一辆移动的战车？或者去狩猎狮子？ 当他跟着亚历山大走出房间时，安妮赶紧和他一起走。

Prince Alexander strode ahead through the courtyard. He led Jack and Annie to a covered porch behind the house. 亚历山大王子大步走在庭院里。 他带杰克和安妮来到房子后面的一个有顶棚的门廊。

“So, what would you like us to do for you?” asked Annie, smiling. “那么，你们想为我们做些什么呢？”安妮微笑着问。

Alexander gazed down at her. “Well, I would like a girl to do nothing for me,” he said. “You should be with the other females, cooking and spinning.” 亚历山大低头看着她。 “嗯，我想让一个女孩什么都不为我做。”他说。 “你应该和其他女性在一起，做饭和纺纱。”

The smile left Annie’s face. “Fine,” she said. “I’ll go back in and hang out with Aristotle.” She started back through the door. 安妮的脸上的笑容消失了。 “好吧，”她说。 “我回去了，和亚里士多德待在一起。”她开始往回走。

“I’ll go, too,” said Jack, eager to get away from the arrogant prince. “我也去，”杰克说，迫不及待地想离开那个傲慢的王子。

“Halt!” barked Alexander. He looked at Annie. “I will endure you awhile longer. You boast that your brother likes to write things down? Well, now he has something very important to write about: me! Do you have something to write with?” he asked Jack. “站住！”亚历山大吼道。 他看着安妮。 “我还会忍受你一会儿。 你吹嘘你哥哥喜欢把事情写下来？ 那现在他有非常重要的事情要写了：我！ 他问杰克：‘你有东西可以写字吗？’

“Sure.” Jack pulled out his notebook and pencil. ‘当然。’杰克拿出他的笔记本和铅笔。

“When you return to your own country, your writing could bring me lasting fame there,” said Alexander. ‘当你回到自己的国家时，你的写作可能会让我在那里获得长久的名声，’亚历山大说。

“You bet,” said Jack. Writing was easy, he thought. Mounting a moving chariot wasn’t. ‘肯定。’杰克说。 他觉得写作很容易。 但骑上一辆移动的战车就不容易了。

“We will walk and talk,” the prince ordered. “Come.” Jack and Annie followed Alexander off the porch, stepping into the hot sunlight. ‘我们将走路并交谈，’王子下令。 ‘走吧。’杰克和安妮跟着亚历山大走下台阶，走进火热的阳光中。

The prince led them through a garden overlooking the hillside. On a plateau near the bottom of the slope was a long barn with a riding ring. 王子带他们穿过一个俯瞰山坡的花园。 在山坡底部附近的一个高地上，有一座长长的谷仓，旁边还有一个骑马场。

“You said Hercules is a favorite hero of yours,” the prince said. “Did you know he is my great-great-grandfather?” “你刚才说赫拉克勒斯是你最喜欢的英雄之一，”王子说。 “你知道吗，他其实是我的曾曾祖父？”

“Who? Hercules?” asked Annie. “谁？ “赫拉克勒斯？”安妮问。

“Yes. And since he is a son of Zeus, I am a living Greek god myself,” said Alexander. “是的。” “既然他是宙斯的儿子，我自己就是一位活着的希腊神，”亚历山大说道。

Whoa, thought Jack. He rolled his eyes. 哇，杰克心想。 他翻了个白眼。

“You mock me?” Alexander asked. “你是在嘲笑我吗？”亚历山大问。

“No, no, I just got some dust in my eyes,” said Jack. He rubbed his eyes. “不，不，我只是眼睛里进了点灰尘，”杰克说。 他揉了揉眼睛。

“A living Greek god,” said Annie. “Write that down, Jack.” “一个活着的希腊神，”安妮说。 “记下来，杰克。”

“Of course,” said Jack. But he wrote: “当然，”杰克说。但他是这样写的：

This guy’s a raving nut.

Annie read Jack’s notes and snickered. Alexander turned and looked at her. 安妮读了杰克的笔记，忍不住笑了出来。 亚历山大转过身，看着她。

Annie tried to change her snicker into a snort. “Sorry, got some dust up my nose,” she said, scratching her nose. 安妮试着把笑声变成一个喷鼻音。 “对不起，我鼻子有点痒，”她说，挠了挠鼻子。

The prince kept walking and talking. “This means, of course, that when I go to war, I will never lose a battle. Soon I will be master of the universe.” 王子继续往前走，继续说话。 “当然，这意味着当我去打仗的时候，我永远不会输掉一场战斗。” “很快我就会成为宇宙的主人了。”

“Cool,” said Jack. But he wrote: “太酷了，”杰克说。但他写下了：

This guy should be locked up.

Annie glanced at Jack’s notes and stifled a laugh. Alexander looked at her again. 安妮瞥了一眼杰克的笔记，忍住笑。 亚历山大又看了她一眼。

“Dust in my throat,” she said. She coughed. “我喉咙里有灰尘，”她说。她咳嗽了一下。

Alexander kept walking. “You said you went to the Olympics in Greece?” he said. “Well, so have I.” 亚历山大继续往前走。 “你不是说你去希腊参加过奥运会吗？”他说。 “嗯，我也参加过。”

“Were you competing?” said Jack. “你是去比赛吗？”杰克问。

“Of course not. I never compete with other athletes,” the prince said wearily. “I would win every time. I am the greatest living athlete in the world.” “当然不是。” “我从不和其他运动员比赛，”王子疲惫地说。 “我每次都会赢。 我是世界上最伟大的运动员。”

“Amazing,” said Jack. But he wrote: “真厉害，”杰克说。但他在纸上写道：

What a show-off! He could never have friends.

Jack looked up. “More?” he said. 杰克抬头看了看。 “还有吗？”他说。

“First I would like to see all you have written so far,” said the prince. “首先，我想看看你到目前为止写了什么，”王子说。

“You would?” said Jack, gulping. “你真的想看？”杰克结结巴巴地说。

“Yes.” “是的。”

“Uh—but I’m not finished,” said Jack. “I don’t like to show my writing until I’ve gotten all my notes together.” He closed his notebook and started to put it in his bag. “呃——但是我还没写完，”杰克说。 “我还没把所有笔记整理好，就不喜欢展示我的写作。”他合上笔记本，开始把它放进包里。

“I want to read it now, ” said the prince, snatching the notebook. He opened it and looked at Jack’s notes. “ ‘ A raving nut’? What is that?” “我想现在就读它，”王子说着，一把抢过笔记本。 他打开它，看了看杰克的笔记。 “‘发疯的坚果’？这是什么？”

“Um … in our land, a ‘raving nut’ is a very rare and valuable kind of nut,” said Jack. “呃……在我们那里，‘发疯的坚果’是一种非常稀有且珍贵的坚果，”杰克说。

“Right, by comparing you to a raving nut, Jack’s saying you are very valuable,” added Annie. “对，杰克是说你非常有价值，因为你就像一个疯疯癫癫的天才，”安妮补充道。

Alexander looked at Annie suspiciously, then back at the notebook. “ ‘ This guy should be locked up,’ ” he read. 亚历山大怀疑地看着安妮，然后又低头看向笔记本。 “‘这个人应该被关起来，’”他读道。

“For protection!” said Annie. “Someone as great as you should be locked up for your own safety. You’re a living Greek god, for goodness’ sake!” “为了保护你！”安妮说道。 “像你这样伟大的人，为了你自己的安全，应该被关起来。” 你竟然是一位活生生的希腊神，天哪！”

Alexander read more: “ ‘What a show-off!’ ” He looked puzzled. “Show-off?” he asked. 亚历山大继续读下去：“‘真炫耀！’”他看起来很困惑。 “炫耀？”他问。

“That means you can’t help showing the world how great you are,” said Jack. “那就是说你忍不住要向全世界展示你有多棒，”杰克说。

“Ah, that is true,” said Alexander. The prince then read Jack’s last note: “ ‘He could never have friends.’ ” For a second, the prince looked hurt. “啊，这倒是真的，”亚历山大说。 王子接着读了杰克最后的便条：“‘他永远交不到朋友。’” 王子看起来有点受伤。

“That—uh—I mean it must be really hard for you to find people who are as great as you to hang out with…,” Jack said lamely. “那个……我的意思是，对你来说，找到和你一样厉害的人一起玩，一定很难吧……，”杰克结结巴巴地说。

“Hmm,” said Alexander. He closed the notebook and handed it back to Jack. “Perhaps we should compare athletic skills now,” he said. “嗯，”亚历山大说。 他合上笔记本，把它还给了杰克。 “也许我们现在该比比运动技能了，”他说。

“Your skills with mine?” said Jack. “你的技能加上我的？”杰克说。

“Don’t you want to tell us more about yourself?” Annie asked the prince.

“No, I want to learn more about Jack,” said Alexander. “Let us race one another,” he said to Jack. He pointed down the hill. “To the stable fence and back.”

“I’d rather not,” said Jack.

“You must race me! Now!” the prince ordered.

“Okay, okay,” said Jack. He was a pretty good runner. Maybe he had a chance to win. He put away his pencil and notebook and handed his bag to Annie. Then he knelt down on one knee.

“GO!” shouted Alexander.

The prince sprinted down the hill toward the stable, his purple cloak flying behind him. Jack ran as fast as he could, but Prince Alexander ran much faster.

By the time Jack touched the fence of the stable, Alexander was already running back up the hill. In the searing heat of the late afternoon, Jack struggled to keep going. Halfway up the hill, he felt like he might pass out.

“Come on, Jack! Come on!” yelled Annie.

Alexander stood at the top of the hill, grinning down at him.

When Jack finally reached Alexander and Annie, he wobbled a few steps and collapsed.

“Jack!” Annie cried. She knelt beside him. “Are you okay?”

Alexander walked over to them. Instead of helping Jack up, he just stared down at him. “I think being a winner is much better than being a friend,” the prince said. “Don’t you?”

“No!” said Annie. “You’re mean.”

“All right. I’ll give your brother another chance,” said Alexander. “Would you like to race again, Jack? Or perhaps wrestle?”

Jack didn’t answer. He was just trying to breathe.

“I didn’t think so,” said Alexander. “Perhaps you should compete against girls in the future.”

“Very funny,” Annie said. “Hold on, Prince.” She reached into Jack’s bag and pulled out the tiny bottle. Then she leaned close to Jack and whispered in his ear, “Here, sniff the magic mist. Race him again. Show him!” She slipped the tiny bottle into Jack’s hand.

Jack wrapped his fingers around the bottle. All he had to do was sniff and make a wish to be the greatest athlete in the world. He clutched the bottle tighter.

Then a strange calm came over Jack. He could use Merlin’s magic. But he didn’t really want to. Or need to. Why waste it on a mean, arrogant bully?

“No, I don’t want to race you again,” Jack said. Even though he felt sick, he forced himself to sit up. “You’re clearly a much better athlete than I am.”

The sound of hoofbeats came from the stable below. Men were herding horses into the fenced-in riding ring. Alexander let out a whoop. Without glancing back, he ran down the hill.

Jack and Annie watched the prince climb the fence and join the men and horses in the ring. In the distance, accompanied by his bodyguards and companions, King Philip was heading down to the stable, too.

“You sure you’re okay?” Annie asked Jack.

“I’m fine,” he said, though he still felt woozy from the heat. “Here, put this back.” He handed the bottle to Annie. “He’s not worth it.”

“You’re right,” said Annie.

“I’m totally done with him,” said Jack.

“What about our mission?” Annie asked. “There’s a truth about greatness we’re supposed to learn from him. And we haven’t learned it yet.”

“Yes, we have,” said Jack. “The truth is: he’s not that great.”

Annie held up her finger to show Jack the ring. “I don’t think that’s it. The Ring of Truth isn’t glowing.”

“I don’t care,” Jack said slowly. “I never want to see him again. I don’t trust him. I don’t like him. And I certainly don’t need to learn anything from him.”
