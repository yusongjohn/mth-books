3. Big Thinkers 3. 大脑活跃者

“Ha-ha,” Jack laughed, as if Annie were crazy. “Don’t pay attention to her. We’re leaving now.” “哈哈哈，”杰克笑着说，好像安妮疯了一样。 “别理她。 我们现在要离开了。”

“Who are you to defy our teacher?” a boy shouted. “你是谁，敢违抗我们的老师？”一个男孩喊道。

“How dare a girl insult him!” another yelled. He shook his fist at Annie. “怎么敢让一个女孩侮辱他！”另一个人大声喊道。 他朝安妮挥了挥拳头。

“She wasn’t insulting anyone,” said Jack. “But don’t worry, we’re going.” He took Annie’s arm and started to pull her along. “她并没有侮辱任何人，”杰克说道。 “别担心，我们要去。”他抓住安妮的手臂，开始拉她往前走。

“Stay!” the teacher commanded. “留下！”老师命令道。

Jack and Annie froze. 杰克和安妮僵在了那里。

“Tell me more,” the man said. “It is rare that I am surprised—and you have surprised me. What do you mean, the earth is not the center of the universe?” “告诉我更多，”那人说。 “很少有让我惊讶的时候——而你让我惊讶了。 你是说，地球不是宇宙的中心吗？

“Well,” said Annie, “the earth is a planet, and all the planets in our solar system travel around the sun.” “嗯，”安妮说，“地球是一颗行星，我们太阳系里的所有行星都绕着太阳转。”

The teacher smiled. “Is that what you believe?” he said. 老师微笑了。 “这就是你相信的吗？”他说。

“It’s not just what I believe. It’s what I know,” said Annie. “A trip around the sun takes a year.” “这不仅仅是我的信仰。 “这就是我所知道的，”安妮说。 “绕太阳一圈要花一年时间。”

The students laughed. “You are speaking nonsense,” one said. “And wasting our precious time with Aristotle!” 学生们笑了起来。 “你在胡说八道，”一个人说。 “还浪费我们宝贵的时间去研究亚里士多德！”

Aristotle? thought Jack. He knew that name. His mind raced, trying to remember who Aristotle was. 亚里士多德？杰克心想。 他知道这个名字。 他的脑海飞速运转，努力回忆亚里士多德是谁。

“I’m just telling you the facts,” said Annie. “While the earth is circling the sun, it rotates. Rotate means it spins around. One rotation of the earth is one day.” “我只是在告诉你事实，”安妮说。 “当地球绕着太阳转的时候，它也在自转。” 旋转的意思就是绕着转圈。 地球转一圈就是一天。

The boys snickered, but Aristotle was quiet. “What a novel idea,” he said softly. Then he turned to the boys. “Our class is over for the day. I would like to speak to these two visitors alone.” 男孩们偷偷笑了，但亚里士多德却一声不吭。 “真是个新点子，”他轻声说道。 然后他转向那两个男孩。 “今天上课就到这里了。 我想单独和这两位访客谈谈。”

The boys grumbled, but they tucked their tablets under their arms and headed out into the bright square. 男孩们嘟囔着，但还是把平板电脑夹在腋下，走进了明亮的广场。

Aristotle stared at Jack and Annie. “Who are you? Where are you from?” he asked. 亚里士多德盯着杰克和安妮。 “你们是谁？ 你来自哪里？”他问。

“I’m Annie. This is Jack, my brother. And, um, we’re from Frog Creek.” “我是安妮。 这是杰克，我的哥哥。 还有，呃，我们来自蛙溪镇。”

“Frog Creek …?” said Aristotle. “蛙溪镇……？”亚里士多德说。

“It’s west of Greece,” Jack said. “它在希腊的西边，”杰克说。

“And who are you? ” asked Annie. “你是谁？”安妮问。

“My name is Aristotle. I have come from Athens, Greece, to teach philosophy and science in Macedonia.” “我叫亚里士多德。 我从希腊的雅典来到这里，在马其顿教哲学和科学。”

Jack gasped. Now he remembered! Aristotle was a great philosopher and scientist in ancient Greece! On a past mission, they had delivered his writings to the ruler of Baghdad. 杰克惊呼。 现在他想起来啦！ 亚里士多德是古希腊一位伟大的哲学家和科学家！ 在一次过去的任务中，他们曾把他的著作送给了巴格达的统治者。

“We’ve heard of you,” said Annie. “You’re a big thinker. We saved your writings once, but a camel ate them.” She laughed. “It wasn’t funny at the time. But—” “我们听说过你，”安妮说。 “你是个爱思考的人。 我们曾经救过你的著作，但一头骆驼把它们吃掉了。”她笑了。 “当时一点都不好笑。但是——”

“Annie,” said Jack. He shook his head. It would be impossible to explain their trip to Baghdad. That trip had happened more than a thousand years after this time in history. “My sister has a big imagination,” he said. “安妮，”杰克说。 他摇了摇头。 要解释他们去巴格达的旅行是不可能的。 那次旅行发生在一千多年前的这个历史时期。 “我姐姐想象力很丰富，”他说。

“So it would seem,” said Aristotle. “Her ideas about the universe are completely wrong, of course, but I am astonished that she has a theory.” “看来是这样，”亚里士多德说。 “她对宇宙的想法当然完全错误，但让我惊讶的是，她竟然有一个理论。”

“Why?” asked Annie. “为什么？”安妮问。

“I did not think that girls had the ability to think about such things,” said Aristotle. “我从没想过女孩能想这些事，”亚里士多德说。

Annie looked at Jack. “He’s kidding, right?” she said. 安妮看着杰克。 “他是在开玩笑吧？”她说。

Jack laughed nervously. “Well, no,” he said. “That’s what people thought a long time ago.” 杰克紧张地笑了。 “嗯，不是，”他说。 “很久以前，人们就是这样想的。”

Scowling, Annie started to say something, but Aristotle smiled at her. “You must be a very special kind of girl. Come. Let us walk and talk, and you can show me what big thinkers you both are.” 安妮皱着眉头，正想说什么，但亚里士多德对她笑了笑。 “你一定是个非常特别的女孩。来吧。 让我们边走边聊，你可以向我展示你们两个都是怎么思考的。”

Jack and Annie fell into step with the philosopher as he started across the square. “Besides contemplating the universe, what else do you think about?” Aristotle asked Annie. 杰克和安妮跟在哲学家后面，一起走进了广场。 “除了思考宇宙，你还想些什么呢？”亚里士多德问安妮。

“Um … I think a lot about animals,” she said. “嗯……我经常想动物的事情，”她说。

“Wonderful. Animals always reveal to us something natural and beautiful,” said Aristotle. “So you study them?” “太棒了。 动物总是向我们展现出自然和美丽的东西，”亚里士多德说。 “你是研究它们吗？”

“I do study them,” Annie said. “But more than that, I fall in love with them. I think that’s the way I really learn.” “我确实研究它们，”安妮说。 “但更重要的是，我爱上了它们。 我觉得这才是我真正学习的方式。”

“Ah, very good,” said Aristotle. “To truly educate your mind, you must also educate your heart. And where does your heart lead you, Jack? Do you prefer a life of sports? Military training?” “啊，非常好，”亚里士多德说。 “要真正教育你的头脑，你也必须教育你的心。 那么，你的心带你去往哪里，杰克？ 你更喜欢充满运动的生活吗？ 还是军事训练？”

Jack shook his head. “I’m not super-great at sports,” he said, “or military training. But I’m good at doing research. I take notes on everything.” Jack found it surprisingly easy to talk with the philosopher. “I love learning about the rain forests and the deep sea and the moon. I love learning about everything, really.” 杰克摇了摇头。 “我在运动方面并不是特别厉害，”他说，“也不是特别擅长军事训练。” 但是我很擅长做研究。 我什么都记笔记。” 杰克发现和这位哲学家聊天居然出奇地容易。 “我喜欢学习关于雨林、深海和月亮的知识。 我真的喜欢学习所有东西。”

“Me too!” said Annie. “我也是！”安妮说。

“Indeed?” said Aristotle. “You both are remarkable!” “真的吗？”亚里士多德说道。 “你们俩真的很了不起！”，

Jack shrugged. “Not really. I guess we just kind of know ourselves.” 杰克耸了耸肩。 “其实也没那么厉害。 我想我们只是比较了解自己罢了。”

“So it would seem,” Aristotle said. “Knowing yourself is the beginning of all wisdom.” “看来是这样，”亚里士多德说。 “认识自己是智慧的开始。”

“The more Jack and I learn about the world, the more we learn about ourselves,” said Annie. “We’re always trying new stuff.” “杰克和我越了解这个世界，就越了解自己，”安妮说。 “我们一直在尝试新东西。”

“Yeah, even if we make fools of ourselves sometimes,” said Jack. “Especially me.” “是啊，即使有时候我们会出丑，”杰克说。 “尤其是我。”

Aristotle chuckled. “I think we should all dare to make fools of ourselves again and again,” he said. “Anyone who fears looking like a fool must say nothing, do nothing, and be nothing.” 亚里士多德笑了笑。 “我想我们都应该一次次地勇敢地让自己出丑，”他说。 “害怕看起来像傻瓜的人，什么都不敢说，什么都不敢做，什么都不是。”

“So it would seem,” said Annie. “看来是这样，”安妮说。

“May I ask: why have you come to Macedonia?” said Aristotle. “Does your visit have a purpose?” “我可以问一下：你为什么来到马其顿？”亚里士多德说。 “你的来访有什么目的吗？”

“It does,” said Jack with a laugh. He was enjoying talking to Aristotle so much that he’d forgotten their mission. “Actually, we’re looking for Alexander the Great.” “有，”杰克笑着说。 他和亚里士多德聊得太开心了，都忘了他们的任务。 “其实，我们在找亚历山大大帝。”

“Do you know him?” asked Annie. “你知道他吗？”安妮问。

“I know a prince named Alexander, the son of King Philip,” said Aristotle. “我知道一位叫亚历山大的王子，他是国王菲利普的儿子，”亚里士多德说。

“That’s him!” said Annie. “那就是他！”安妮说。

“But I must say, I would not call him great yet,” said Aristotle. “He is only twelve years old.” “不过我得说，我还不敢称他为伟大的人，”亚里士多德说。 “他才十二岁呢。”

“Twelve?” said Jack. “十二？”杰克说。

“Yes. Alexander is the reason I, too, have come to Macedonia,” said Aristotle. “When the prince turns thirteen in a few weeks, I will become his tutor. Why are you looking for him?” “是的。 亚历山大就是我来到马其顿的原因，”亚里士多德说。 “当王子几周后满十三岁时，我将成为他的导师。 你为什么在找他？

“We’d love to spend some time with him,” said Annie. “We heard he was … you know, great.” “我们很想和他待一会儿，”安妮说。 “我们听说他……你知道的，很了不起。”

Aristotle sighed. “The prince would certainly wish you to think so,” he said. “Well. If you want to meet him, King Philip is having a gathering at the Royal House this afternoon. It is close by.” Aristotle pointed to the mansion on the hill above the square. “The prince will be in attendance. Would you like to go with me?” 亚里士多德叹了口气。 “王子肯定希望你们这么认为，”他说。 “好吧。” 如果你想见他，今天下午国王菲利普会在皇家府邸举行聚会。 它就在附近。”亚里士多德指着广场上方山上的那座豪宅。 “王子会来参加。” 你想和我一起去吗？

“Yes!” Jack and Annie said together. “好！”杰克和安妮异口同声地说。

“Good. Then let us climb the hill,” said the philosopher, and he started up a pebble path that led to the Royal House. “很好。” “那么，让我们爬上山吧，”哲学家说，然后他沿着一条通往皇家房屋的小石子路开始往上走。

Jack and Annie followed, grinning at each other. “This is fantastic!” Jack said softly. “Maybe our mission will be easier than I thought!” 杰克和安妮跟在后面，互相笑着。 “这太棒了！”杰克小声说。 “也许我们的任务会比我想象的更容易！”

When they reached the top of the hill, Jack was surprised by the plainness of the Royal House. It looked like a big white box with a tiled roof and simple columns. Two guards in crested helmets stood like statues by the entrance. Each held a giant shield decorated with a star. 他们到达山顶时，杰克对皇家房子的普通样子感到惊讶。 它看起来像一个大白盒子，有瓦片屋顶和简单的柱子。 两个戴着有羽毛装饰头盔的卫兵站在入口处，像雕像一样一动不动。 每个人手里都拿着一个巨大的盾牌，上面装饰着一颗星星。

“Please wait outside,” said Aristotle. “I must alert the king that I have brought guests to his gathering—and that one of them is a girl.” “请在外面等着，”亚里士多德说。 “我必须告诉国王，我带客人来参加他的聚会了——而且其中有一个是女孩。”

“Why?” asked Annie. “No girls allowed?” “为什么？”安妮问。 “不允许女孩参加吗？”

“I fear females are never allowed to attend such events,” said Aristotle. “But I imagine the king has never met a girl like you before.” “我担心女性从来都不被允许参加这样的活动，”亚里士多德说。 “不过，我想国王以前从没见过像你这样出色的女孩。”

“Thanks,” said Annie. “I think.” After Aristotle left, she turned to Jack. “What is wrong with all the men in history? Nearly everywhere we go in the tree house, girls aren’t allowed to do any of the fun stuff.” “谢谢，”安妮说。 “我想是吧。”亚里士多德离开后，她转向杰克。 “历史上所有男人都怎么了？ 几乎每次我们去树屋，女孩都不被允许做任何有趣的事情。

“I know, it’s crazy,” said Jack. “But stay calm. Remember, the king’s a ferocious fighter.” “我知道，这太疯狂了，”杰克说。 “但要冷静。 记住，国王是个可怕的战士。

“Yeah, well …” Annie held up her fists. “We have magic that could make me a great fighter, too,” she said. “是啊，不过……”安妮举起拳头。 “我们有魔法，也能让我变成一个了不起的战士，”她说。

“Don’t even think about it,” said Jack, glancing at the guards. “你千万别想这个主意，”杰克一边说，一边看了看守卫。

Annie lowered her fists. “Aren’t you surprised that Alexander’s only twelve?” she said. “At home he’d be just a sixth or seventh grader.” 安妮放下了她的拳头。 “你难道不惊讶亚历山大才十二岁吗？”她说。 “在家里，他只是一个六年级或七年级的学生。”

“I know. How great can he be?” said Jack. He pulled out their book and looked up Alexander, childhood in the index. With his back to the guards, Jack read in a soft voice: “我知道。” “他能有多厉害呢？”杰克说。 他拿出他们的书，在索引里找到了亚历山大童年的部分。 背对着卫兵，杰克用轻柔的声音读道：

Young Alexander was raised in the manner of noble youths. From an early age, he received military training and became an excellent swordsman, spear thrower, and chariot driver. He was a champion athlete and excelled in all sports. 年幼的亚历山大是按照贵族子弟的方式被抚养长大的。 从很小的时候起，他就接受了军事训练，成为了一名出色的剑客、标枪投掷者和战车驾驶员。 他是一名冠军运动员，在所有体育项目上都表现优异。

“Oh, man,” said Jack. “He sounds like Superman.” “哦，天哪，”杰克说。 “他听起来像超人。”

“Aristotle’s back,” whispered Annie. “亚里士多德回来了，”安妮小声说。

Jack slipped the book into his bag. 杰克把书放进他的包里。

“The king has granted his permission,” Aristotle said, looking at Annie. “You may both come inside.” “国王已经同意了，”亚里士多德看着安妮说。 “你们两个都可以进去了。”

“Hurray!” said Annie. Then she and Jack followed the philosopher into the Royal House. “太棒了！”安妮说。 然后她和杰克跟着这位哲学家走进了王宫。
