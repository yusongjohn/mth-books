4. The One-Eyed King 4. 独眼国王

The front hallway was dark and cool. The flickering flames of oil lamps cast shadows on wall murals. The murals showed figures from Greek myths. 前厅又黑又凉。 油灯的火光闪烁着，在墙上的壁画上投下影子。 壁画上画的是希腊神话中的人物。

“Zeus,” Annie whispered to Jack, pointing to a painting of the ruler of all the Greek gods. “宙斯，”安妮低声对杰克说，指着一幅画，画中是所有希腊神的统治者。

“Centaurs,” whispered Jack, pointing to creatures that were half man and half horse. “半人半马的怪物，”杰克低声说，指着一些一半是人一半是马的生物。

“This way,” said Aristotle. “这边走，”亚里士多德说。

Jack and Annie followed the philosopher through the hall to an open courtyard. Women in long white dresses were grilling meat over a fire and pulling bread from a clay oven. They glanced at Annie with curiosity. 杰克和安妮跟着这位哲学家穿过大厅，来到一个开放的庭院。 一些穿着长白裙子的妇女正在火上烤肉，还从陶土烤炉里拿出面包。 她们好奇地看了安妮一眼。

Aristotle led them past the cooks to a doorway off the courtyard. The sounds of loud conversation came from the room beyond. 亚里士多德带他们走过厨师们，来到庭院边的一扇门旁。 房间里传来了大声说话的声音。

“King Philip has gathered the men of his most elite cavalry,” said Aristotle. “They are known as the King’s Companions. Do not be startled when you look upon the king. Years ago, he lost an eye in battle.” “菲利普国王召集了他的精锐骑兵，”亚里士多德说。 “他们被称为国王的亲随。 当你看到国王时，不要感到惊讶。 多年前，他在战斗中失去了一只眼睛。”

“Oww,” said Annie, wincing. “哎哟，”安妮皱着眉头说。

“Even so, he is still the greatest military leader in the known world,” said Aristotle. “即使如此，他仍然是已知世界最伟大的军事领袖，”亚里士多德说。

Jack nodded. He took a deep breath. 杰克点了点头。 他深吸了一口气。

“Prince Alexander will be arriving soon,” said the philosopher. “Come.” Then he led Jack and Annie into the spacious, lamp-lit room. “亚历山大王子很快就到，”哲学家说。 “来吧。”然后他带领杰克和安妮走进了宽敞、灯光明亮的房间。

The King’s Companions lay on couches, propped up on their elbows, or on pillows. They were talking and eating. King Philip was on a couch draped in purple silk. He had a black patch over one eye. Two bodyguards with curved swords hanging from their belts stood nearby. 国王的随从们躺在沙发上，用手肘撑着，或者靠在枕头上看书。 他们一边说话，一边吃东西。 菲利普国王坐在一张铺着紫色丝绸的沙发上。 他的一只眼睛上蒙着一块黑布。 两名持着弯剑的卫兵站在附近，剑挂在腰带上。

When the king and his men caught sight of Jack and Annie and Aristotle, they fell silent. 当国王和他的随从们看到杰克、安妮和亚里士多德时，他们都安静下来了。

“King Philip the Second of Macedonia,” Aristotle said, bowing. “这位是马其顿的菲利普二世国王，”亚里士多德说，然后鞠了一躬。

Jack and Annie bowed also. 杰克和安妮也鞠了一躬。

“I have just met these two very learned young people today,” said Aristotle. “They are Jack and his sister, Annie, of Frog Creek, a land west of Greece.” “我今天刚遇到这两位非常有学问的年轻人，”亚里士多德说。 “他们是来自蛙溪镇的杰克和他的妹妹安妮，蛙溪镇在希腊的西边。”

“Hi,” said Annie with a smile. “你好，”安妮微笑着说。

Jack smiled, too, as he looked around at the warriors. 杰克也笑了，他环顾四周，看着那些战士们。

No one smiled back, including King Philip II of Macedonia. “Sit,” the king ordered. 没有人回以微笑，包括马其顿的菲利普二世国王。 “坐下来，”国王命令道。

Jack and Annie sat together on an empty couch. Aristotle sat nearby. Three servants quickly appeared. The King’s Companions resumed eating and talking as a servant removed Jack’s sandals. She rinsed his dust-covered feet in a tub of warm water. Jack kept his eyes down, not sure what to do or say. 杰克和安妮一起坐在一张空着的沙发上。 亚里士多德坐在附近。 三个仆人迅速出现了。 国王的随从们继续吃饭和交谈，一个仆人取下了杰克的鞋子。 她用一桶温水冲洗他沾满灰尘的脚。 杰克低着头，不知道该做什么或说什么。

Next, servants delivered small dishes of food to them. Jack identified olives, cheese, purple grapes, nuts, figs … and then there was something that looked like dead bugs. 接下来，仆人们给他们端来了小盘食物。 杰克认出了橄榄、奶酪、紫色的葡萄、坚果、无花果……然后他看到一些看起来像死掉的虫子的东西。

Annie caught Jack’s eye. “Grasshoppers?” she whispered, wrinkling her nose. 安妮注意到了杰克的眼神。 “蚱蜢吗？”她小声说，皱起了鼻子。

“Eat the grapes,” he whispered back. “吃葡萄吧，”他小声回答。

Jack and Annie silently ate grapes as the King’s Companions feasted and talked with Aristotle and King Philip. The king was telling a story about a stallion that had been taken captive. 杰克和安妮默默地吃着葡萄，而国王的随从们则一边享用美食，一边和亚里士多德和菲利普国王聊天。 国王正在讲一个关于一匹被俘虏的骏马的故事。

Not until the dishes were cleared away did King Philip turn his gaze on Jack and Annie. “Silence!” the king ordered his men. “It is time now to hear from our esteemed visitors. Whomever Aristotle admires, I admire also.” 直到盘子被端走后，菲利普国王才把目光转向杰克和安妮。 “安静！”国王命令他的手下。 “现在是时候听听我们尊贵的客人的话了。 “无论亚里士多德欣赏谁，我也欣赏他。”

Jack nearly choked on a grape. 杰克差点被一颗葡萄呛到。

“Aristotle tells us you have studied and learned much—both of you,” the king said, looking at Annie. “Is that true?” “亚里士多德告诉我们，你们两个都学了很多东西，”国王看着安妮说。 “这是真的吗？”，

Before Annie could answer, a boy burst into the room. He was fair-haired and muscular. He wore a purple cloak over his tunic. He strode to the center of the room, tossed back his cloak, and bowed. 安妮还没来得及回答，一个男孩冲进了房间。 他有一头金发，身材健壮。 他穿着一件紫色斗篷，盖在衬衣外面。 他走到房间中央，甩开斗篷，鞠了一躬。

“I greet you all!” the boy declared. “At last I have arrived!” “我向你们所有人问好！”男孩宣布道。 “终于我到啦！”

“Hail, Prince Alexander!” the men said in unison. “向亚历山大大帝致敬！”男人们齐声说道。

Alexander the Great! Jack thought. 亚历山大大帝！杰克心想。

Prince Alexander started to speak, but to Jack’s surprise, the king snapped at him, “Quiet, boy! Sit down!” 亚历山大王子开始说话，但让杰克惊讶的是，国王冲他吼道：“安静，孩子！坐下！”

The smile left the prince’s face, but he obeyed and sat on a couch near his father. 王子脸上的笑容消失了，但他服从了，坐在他父亲旁边的沙发上。

Aristotle leaned forward and spoke kindly to Alexander. “My prince,” he said, “when you entered, we were about to hear from two learned young people from Frog Creek, a land west of Greece. They have come here expressly to meet you. This is Jack and his sister, Annie.” 亚里士多德向前倾身，温和地对亚历山大说话。 “我的王子，”他说，“当你进来时，我们正准备听来自蛙溪镇的两位有学问的年轻人讲话，蛙溪镇在希腊的西边。 他们特地来到这里，就是为了见你。 这位是杰克和他的姐姐安妮。”

“I see,” said Alexander, puffing out his chest. He gave Jack and Annie a superior smile. “Well then, small visitors, please share with us your great knowledge.” “我明白了，”亚历山大说道，挺起胸膛。 他给了杰克和安妮一个高高在上的微笑。 “那么，小朋友们，请和我们分享你们丰富的知识吧。”

Isthiskidserious? Jack wondered. Whodoeshethinkheis?

“I’ll share,” Annie piped up. “Recently we learned a lot about a rare kind of bear called a panda bear.” “我来分享。”安妮插话道。 “最近我们学到了很多关于一种稀有熊类的知识，叫做熊猫。”

“Panda bear?” said the prince with a smirk. “熊猫？”王子带着讥笑的语气说道。

“Yes, but don’t confuse pandas with other bears, like polar bears or grizzly bears,” said Annie. “Pandas live in China. Polar bears live in the Arctic.” “是的，但不要把熊猫和其他熊搞混了，比如北极熊或者灰熊。”安妮说道。 “熊猫住在中国。 北极熊住在北极。”

“That’s right,” said Jack, clearing his throat. “They’re very heavy, polar bears, but they can move over thin sheets of ice. They balance their weight and slide on their paws.” “没错，”杰克清了清嗓子说道。 “北极熊非常重，但它们可以在薄薄的冰面上移动。” 它们会平衡体重，用爪子在冰面上滑行。”

“Like this,” said Annie. She held out her arms and moved as if she were sliding over ice. She laughed—and the king and his companions laughed with her. “就像这样，”安妮说。 她张开双臂，好像在冰面上滑行一样动来动去。 她笑了——国王和他的随从们也和她一起笑了。

“How wondrous!” the king exclaimed to Aristotle. “多么神奇啊！”国王对亚里士多德说道。

“Yes, indeed,” said the philosopher. “是啊，确实如此，”哲学家说。

The prince, though, looked bored. 可是，王子看起来很无聊。

“And then there are koala bears,” said Jack. “还有考拉熊呢，”杰克说。

“Enough about bears!” Alexander said rudely. “Let us talk about the lion hunt I recently went on.” “够了，别再说熊了！”亚历山大粗鲁地说。 “让我们谈谈我最近去的狮子狩猎吧。”

“Not now!” the king snapped. He looked at Jack. “I want to hear more about the bears.”

Jack cleared his throat. “Actually, koala bears are not bears at all,” he said. He was eager to show off his knowledge in front of the prince. “They’re marsupials. A kangaroo is a marsupial, too.”

“Kang-a-roos?” Alexander said in a mocking voice. “Mar-soop-eels?”

“Kangaroos are as tall as a person,” said Annie, “but they hop like frogs. They can box, too. Like this …” Facing Alexander, she punched the air with her fists.

The prince automatically ducked, making the King’s Companions laugh. The king laughed loudest.

“How foolish she is!” the prince growled.

“Quiet, Alexander, do not be angry,” said his father. “Go on, tell us more,” he said to Jack and Annie. “Do you honor the Greek gods as we do?”

“I’m not sure what you mean by honor,” said Annie. “But we actually met Hercules in Pompeii. Pegasus saved us at the Greek Olympics. They’re favorites of ours.”

The men looked confused. “What?” said the king.

“What I mean,” Annie said quickly, “is that we believe in the power of the imagination and the power of ancient stories. By reading about Hercules and Pegasus, we feel like we know them.”

“Right. And they’re constellations,” said Jack. “So if you look at the stars in the night sky, you’ll see that the old stories are always with us. We are never alone.”

Jack glanced around the room. The king and his companions were smiling and nodding. Only the prince looked unhappy.

“Thank you, Jack. Those are wise words,” said Aristotle.

“Yes, indeed,” said the king. “Aristotle, your young friends are truly amazing. Alexander, you would do well to learn from these two.”

“Learn what?” Alexander asked. “Can this boy mount a chariot moving at full speed? Can he throw a spear farther than a grown man? Can he hunt a lion?”

“No, and he never will,” Annie said. She smiled at the prince. “But Jack’s great at writing. He takes notes and he writes his own stories.”

“Ah … ah!” said Alexander. He sprang to his feet. “Forgive my rudeness,” he said to Jack. “I would like to talk with you alone. Perhaps you can help me.”

Uh-oh, thought Jack.

The prince looked at his father. “May we go?”

Pleasesayno, thought Jack. Alexander made him nervous.

But King Philip nodded. “We hate to lose your company,” he said to Jack, “but it would be good for you to counsel my son.”

“Yes, indeed. Come with me, small visitors from Frog Creek,” said Alexander, beckoning to Jack and Annie.

Jack glanced at Aristotle.

The philosopher looked nervous, too, but he smiled encouragingly and nodded, as if to say, Bebrave. Dare again to make fools of yourselves.
