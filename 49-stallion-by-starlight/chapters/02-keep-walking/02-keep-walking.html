2. Keep Walking 2. 继续走

Jack and Annie were wearing tunics with soft belts and lace-up sandals. A cloth bag had replaced Jack’s backpack. He looked into the bag and saw his notebook, his pencil, and the bottle with the magic mist from the Isle of Avalon. 杰克和安妮穿着有软腰带和系带凉鞋的长袍。 一个布袋取代了杰克的背包。 他看了看袋子里，看到了他的笔记本、铅笔，还有从阿瓦隆岛带来的魔法雾瓶。

“We wore clothes like these when we went to Pompeii,” said Annie. “我们去庞贝时穿的就是这样的衣服，”安妮说。

“Yeah, and when we went to the Greek Olympic Games,” said Jack. “对，还有去参加古希腊奥运会时，”杰克说。

“When you went to the games,” said Annie. “They didn’t let girls in, remember?” “你去参加比赛时，”安妮说。 “记得吗？那时候不让女孩子参加，”

“Oh, right,” said Jack. “You nearly caused a riot when you tried to sneak in.” “哦，对啊，”杰克说。 “你偷偷溜进来的时候差点引起了一场骚乱。”

“It wasn’t my fault,” said Annie. “这不是我的错，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie looked out the window together. The air felt dry and hot. The sun was directly overhead in a cloudless sky. The tree house was low to the ground, tucked into the spreading branches of an olive tree. Donkeys and horses ambled up a winding dirt road. 杰克和安妮一起看向窗外。 空气又干又热。 太阳高高挂在没有云的天空中。 树屋离地面很近，藏在橄榄树茂密的枝叶间。 驴子和马沿着一条蜿蜒的土路慢慢走来。

“This looks like a quiet, sleepy place,” said Annie, “not fierce or warlike at all.” “这里看起来像是一个安静、沉睡的地方，”安妮说，“一点都不凶猛或好战。”

“I wonder what Merlin meant,” said Jack. He opened their book to the first page and read out loud: “我想知道梅林是什么意思，”杰克说。 他翻开他们的书，读出声来：

More than 2,300 years ago, Macedonia was a kingdom north of Greece. It was ruled by King Philip II, the father of Alexander the Great. The brilliant and ferocious king was known far and wide for his military skills. 2300多年前，马其顿是希腊北部的一个王国。 它由腓力二世国王统治，他是亚历山大大帝的父亲。 这位聪明又勇猛的国王因为军事才能而远近闻名。

“Ferocious king,” Jack repeated. “Well, I guess that fits.” “勇猛的国王，”杰克重复道。 “嗯，我想这确实很合适。”

“Don’t worry, we have magic that gives us a great talent,” said Annie. “So we can wish to have military skills, too.” “别担心，我们有魔法，能赋予我们非凡的才能，”安妮说道。 “所以，我们也可以许愿拥有军事才能。”

“I can’t picture us having military skills,” said Jack. “我很难想象我们会有军事才能，”杰克说道。

“Come on, let’s go look for Alexander the Great!” said Annie. She started down the rope ladder. Jack packed up the book and followed her. “来吧，我们去找亚历山大大帝吧！”安妮说道。 她开始顺着绳梯往下爬。 杰克收拾好书，跟了上去。

Several men on donkeys were riding by on the dirt road. Annie started to call out to them. 几个人骑着驴子从泥路上经过。 安妮开始朝他们喊话。

“Don’t,” Jack whispered, stopping her. “People might ask us questions we can’t answer.” “别喊，”杰克小声说，拦住了她。 “人们可能会问一些我们答不出来的问题。”

“But I only wanted to ask them where Alexander the Great lives,” said Annie. “但是我想问问他们亚历山大大帝住在哪里，”安妮说。

“Let’s just try to blend in first,” said Jack, “until we know our way around.” “我们先试着融入进去吧，”杰克说，“直到我们熟悉这里的环境。”

Annie sighed, but she kept quiet as she and Jack followed the donkeys up the dusty road. They passed rocky meadows dotted with cows, and small farmhouses with tiled roofs, chickens, and vineyards. They saw a shepherd tending sheep, a goat herder herding goats, and a farmer with a plow pulled by an ox. 安妮叹了口气，但她保持沉默，和杰克一起跟着驴子沿着尘土飞扬的路走上去。 他们经过了长满岩石的草地，草地上有牛，还有屋顶铺着瓦片的小农舍、鸡和葡萄园。 他们看到一个牧羊人在照看羊群，一个牧羊人正在赶山羊，还有一个农夫牵着一头牛在犁地。

“Can we ask someone now?” Annie said. “我们现在可以问别人吗？”安妮说。

“Let’s wait,” said Jack. “We don’t want to draw attention to ourselves unless we have to.” “我们等等吧，”杰克说。 “除非万不得已，我们不想引起别人的注意。”

They kept walking. Soon they rounded some tall rocks. Beyond a stone wall was a huge field. Thousands of warriors were marching. 他们继续往前走。 不久，他们绕过了几块大石头。 石墙后面是一片大大的田野。 成千上万的战士正在行进。

“Yikes,” said Annie. She and Jack stopped and stared at the warriors. “天哪，”安妮说。 她和杰克停下脚步，盯着那些战士看。

“That must be the king’s army!” said Jack. “那一定是国王的军队！”杰克说。

The foot soldiers wore armor and helmets with tall crests. In one hand, each man carried an oval shield. In the other hand, each held a long, pointed spear. Behind the foot soldiers were rows of soldiers on horses. 步兵们穿着盔甲，戴着有高高装饰的头盔。 每个人的一只手里拿着一个椭圆形的盾牌。 另一只手里拿着一根又长又尖的长矛。 步兵们的后面，一排排骑马的士兵正在等待。

“They’re just practicing, right?” said Annie. “他们只是在练习，对吧？”安妮说。

“I hope so,” said Jack. “I don’t see any enemies.” He pulled out the research book and found a chapter called “The King’s Army.” He read aloud: “希望如此，”杰克说。 “我看不到任何敌人。”他拿出研究书，找到了一个叫“国王的军队”的章节。他大声读道：

At the time of King Philip II, Macedonia was threatened on all sides, by frontier tribes as well as the Persian army. King Philip II drilled his men night and day, until his army became the best fighting machine in the known world. 在腓力二世国王的时代，马其顿受到四面八方的威胁，既有边境部落，也有波斯军队。 腓力二世国王日夜训练他的士兵，直到他的军队成为当时世界上最强大的战斗机器。

Jack looked at the soldiers again. They marched to the right and then the left. The front row of soldiers pointed their spears forward. The other rows pointed their spears up in the air. All the men moved in perfect unison, their helmets, shields, and spears flashing in the sunlight. 杰克再次看了看那些士兵。 他们向右走，然后又向左走。 第一排的士兵把矛向前指着。 其他几排的士兵则把矛举到空中。 所有士兵动作整齐划一，他们的头盔、盾牌和矛在阳光下闪闪发光。

“They do look like a fighting machine,” said Annie. “他们看起来真像一台战斗机器，”安妮说。

Jack thought they looked more like a monstrous insect with thousands of legs and spikes sticking out of its body. He shivered. “Let’s get away from this place,” he said. 杰克觉得他们看起来更像是一个有成千上万条腿和尖刺的怪物。他打了个寒战。 “我们得离开这儿，”他说。

Jack and Annie headed farther up the dusty road. Finally, beyond a grove of olive trees, they came to a walled town. Above the town a white mansion sat on a hilltop. 杰克和安妮沿着尘土飞扬的小路往更远的地方走去。 终于，在一片橄榄树林后面，他们来到了一座有围墙的城镇。 城镇上方，一座白色的大房子坐落在山顶上。

“Maybe that’s where King Philip the Second of Macedonia and Alexander the Great live,” said Annie, pointing at the mansion. “也许那里住着马其顿的菲利普二世国王和亚历山大大帝，”安妮指着那座豪宅说。

Jack and Annie walked through the town gate and headed toward a market square. On the street leading to the square, a group of boys were sword-fighting with sticks. “Hello—” Annie called. 杰克和安妮穿过城门，朝市场广场走去。 通往广场的街上，一群男孩正用木棍比划着剑术。 “你好——”安妮喊道。

“Don’t,” said Jack. “别喊，”杰克说。

It was too late. The boys stopped fighting and stared at them. 但已经太晚了。 男孩们停止了打斗，盯着他们看。

“Does King Philip of Macedonia live on that hill?” Annie asked, pointing to the columned house. “马其顿的菲利普国王住在那座山上吗？”安妮指着那栋有柱子的房子问。

The boys scowled at Annie. 男孩们皱着眉头看着安妮。

Jack grabbed Annie’s arm and pulled her along. “Keep walking,” he said under his breath. “Turn right.” 杰克抓住安妮的手臂，把她拉走了。 “继续往前走，”他小声说。 “向右转。”

“Thanks anyway!” Annie called. She and Jack kept going and turned right at the next corner. “They weren’t very friendly,” said Annie. “谢谢了！”安妮喊道。 她和杰克继续往前走，在下一个拐角处向右转。 “他们不太友好，”安妮说。

“No kidding,” said Jack. “Listen, do not draw attention to yourself. These are fierce and warlike people with a ferocious king.” “别开玩笑了，”杰克说。 “听着，不要引起别人的注意。 这些都是凶猛好战的人，还有一个可怕的国王。

“They were just kids,” Annie protested. “他们只是孩子，”安妮抗议道。

“Doesn’t matter. Keep your head down. Act cool,” said Jack. “没关系。” 低下头。 “装作冷静点，”杰克说。

“I am cool!” said Annie. “ You act cool.” “我真的很冷静！”安妮说。 “你表现得很冷静。”

Jack and Annie walked toward the bright market square, which was filled with carts and tents. Bordering the square was a covered walkway. 杰克和安妮朝明亮的市场广场走去，那里摆满了手推车和帐篷。 广场旁边有一条有顶棚的走道。

“Want to walk over there so we can be in the shade?” said Jack, pointing. The hot sunlight was starting to get to him. “我们过去走走吧，这样就能在阴凉处了，”杰克指着说。 炎热的阳光开始让他感到不舒服了。

“Sure,” said Annie. “好啊，”安妮说。

They headed over to the covered walkway. It was lined with shops, where men made and sold pottery, jewelry, and weapons. 他们朝有顶棚的走道走了过去。 这里有很多店铺，男人们在这里制作并出售陶器、珠宝和武器。

Jack stopped to watch some blacksmiths. “I wonder what they’re making,” he said. One of the blacksmiths used tongs to lift a piece of red-hot metal from a fire. Another hammered the metal into a curved shape. 杰克停下来看一些铁匠。 “我想知道他们在做什么，”他说。 其中一个铁匠用钳子从火中夹起一块烧红的金属。 另一个铁匠把金属敲打成弯曲的形状。

“Whoa, it’s a sword,” said Jack. He pulled out his notebook and pencil. He opened the notebook and wrote: “哇，这是一把剑，”杰克说。 他拿出他的笔记本和铅笔。 他打开笔记本，开始写：

sword makers heat iron, hammer with

Before Jack could finish his thought, Annie poked him. “Watch it, you’re drawing attention to yourself!” she whispered. 杰克还没说完他的想法，安妮就戳了他一下。 “小心点，你在引起别人的注意！”，她小声说。

“What?” Jack looked up. “什么？”杰克抬头看了看。

The blacksmiths were glaring at him. 铁匠们正瞪着他。

“Maybe they think you’re stealing military secrets,” said Annie. “也许他们以为你在偷军事机密，”安妮说。

“Uh-oh,” said Jack. “Let’s go.” Clutching his notebook, he hurried down the covered walkway. Annie followed him. “糟了，”杰克说。 “我们走。”他紧紧抓着自己的笔记本，赶紧沿着有顶的走道跑下去。 安妮跟了上去。

Jack looked over her shoulder. “They’re following!” 杰克从她肩膀上看过去。 “他们在跟着我们！”

“That way!” said Annie. She grabbed Jack’s arm and pulled him back into the crowded sunlit square. The two of them walked faster and faster, weaving around food stalls that sold stuffed grape leaves, eggs, fish, cheese, and bread. “那边！”安妮说。 她抓住杰克的手臂，把他拉回那个拥挤的阳光广场里。 他们俩越走越快，绕着卖着填馅葡萄叶、鸡蛋、鱼、奶酪和面包的小摊穿行。

Annie glanced back. “They’re still searching for us!” 安妮回头看了一眼。 “他们还在找我们！”

The blacksmiths were standing in the crowd, looking around. “We’ve got to hide!” Jack said. 铁匠们站在人群中，四处张望。 “我们必须躲起来！”杰克说。

“Where?” said Annie. “在哪里？” said 安妮。

“I don’t know! Duck down!” said Jack. “我不知道！ “蹲下！”杰克说。

Ducking their heads, they passed a group of teenage boys gathered under a canopy. The boys were listening quietly to a man with a curly beard. 他们低下头，经过一群在树荫下聚集的少年男孩。 男孩们安静地听着一位留着卷胡子的男人。

“Here! Here!” said Jack. He pulled Annie under the canopy, and they stood with the group. “在这儿！在这儿！”杰克说。 他把安妮拉到树冠下，他们和大家一起站着。

The boys were all taking notes on wooden tablets covered with wax. They used pieces of bone to carve words into the wax. 男孩们都在用覆盖着蜡的木板做笔记。 他们用骨头碎片在蜡上刻字。

“Act like you’re a student,” Jack whispered to Annie. “装作你是个学生，”杰克对安妮小声说。

As the teacher lectured to the group, Jack pretended to take notes in his notebook. 当老师给小组讲课时，杰克假装在笔记本上做笔记。

“As you all know, the earth is the center of the universe,” the man said in an easy, calm voice. “正如你们都知道的，地球是宇宙的中心，”那人用轻松、平静的声音说。

“He’s wrong,” Annie whispered to Jack. “他错了，”安妮对杰克小声说。

“Shh,” Jack whispered. “In ancient times, everyone thought that.” He glanced out at the square and saw the blacksmiths passing by. When they were out of sight, he whispered to Annie, “Let’s go, quick, before they come back.” “嘘——”杰克小声说。 “在古代，大家都这么认为。”他朝广场看了一眼，看见铁匠们正从那儿经过。 他们看不见的时候，他低声对安妮说：“快走，赶紧，别让他们回来。”

Annie didn’t budge. She was listening carefully to the teacher. “The sun and planets revolve around the earth,” the man said. 安妮一动不动。 她正仔细听着老师讲课。 “太阳和行星围绕地球转，”那个人说。

“That’s totally wrong,” Annie whispered to Jack. “这完全错了，”安妮低声对杰克说。

“Who cares?” said Jack. “We have to go. We have to—” “谁在乎啊？”杰克说。 “我们必须走。我们必须——”

“But we can’t let him teach something wrong, ” said Annie. “但我们不能让他教错的东西，”安妮说。

“Forget it,” said Jack. “ We—” “算了吧，”杰克说。

Before Jack could finish, Annie raised her hand. “Excuse me!” she called out. “The earth is not the center of the universe!” 杰克还没说完，安妮就举起了手。 “对不起！”她喊道。 “地球并不是宇宙的中心！”
