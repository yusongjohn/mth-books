6. Warhorses 6. 战马

Annie sighed. “I agree with you. But what are we going to do?” 安妮叹了口气。 “我同意你的看法。 但我们该怎么办呢？”

“I don’t know.” Jack dropped his head into his hands. His skull was pounding. He felt like he might throw up. “我不知道。”杰克把头埋进手里。 他的头骨在剧烈疼痛。 他感觉好像要吐了。

“Do you want to leave Macedonia?” asked Annie. “你想离开马其顿吗？”安妮问。

“No,” said Jack, shaking his head. “不，”杰克摇着头说。

“You want to stay?” Annie said. “你想留下来吗？”安妮说。

“No,” said Jack. “不，”杰克说。

“Jack! Annie!” someone shouted. “杰克！ 安妮！”，有人喊道。

Jack lifted his head. Aristotle was striding toward them. When he reached them, he looked at Jack and said, “You need water. Come with me.” 杰克抬起了头。 亚里士多德正朝他们走来。 他走到他们面前，看着杰克说：“你需要水。跟我来。”

Annie helped Jack to his feet. Jack still felt wobbly as they followed Aristotle to a well near the Royal House. He and Annie splashed water from a bucket onto their faces and drank from their cupped hands. The cool, clear water made Jack feel better. “Thank you,” he said. 安妮扶起杰克。 杰克跟着亚里士多德走向皇家房子附近的一口井时，仍然感觉摇摇晃晃。 他和安妮用桶舀水泼在脸上，又用手捧着水喝。 清凉的水让杰克感觉好多了。 “谢谢你，”他说。

“Did the prince make a proper fool of you?” Aristotle asked Jack. “王子是不是让你出尽了丑？”亚里士多德问杰克。

“More or less,” said Jack. “差不多吧，”杰克说。

“Ah, you are not the only one,” Aristotle said. “It’s because he so greatly fears looking like a fool himself. He is desperate to equal his father’s power and strength.” “啊，你不是唯一一个这样的人，”亚里士多德说。 “这是因为他自己非常害怕看起来像个傻瓜。 他拼命想和他父亲的权力和力量一样。”

“Well, someone needs to remind him he’s only twelve,” said Annie. “好吧，有人得提醒他，他才十二岁，”安妮说。

Aristotle smiled. “I agree. Where is he now?” 亚里士多德笑了。 “我同意。他现在在哪里？”

“He ran to see the horses,” said Annie. “他跑过去看马，”安妮说。

“Ah, yes,” said Aristotle, peering down the hill. “The King’s Companions are all eager to see the warhorses a seller has brought from Thessaly. Would you like to see them, too?” “啊，是的，”亚里士多德说，朝山下望去。 “国王的随从们都迫不及待地想看看一个商人从色雷斯带来的战马。 你也想看看吗？”

“I love horses,” said Annie. “But …” She looked at Jack. “我喜欢马，”安妮说。 “但是……”她看着杰克。

“You guys go. I’ll wait here,” he said. He didn’t want to be anywhere near Prince Alexander. “你们去吧。 我就在这里等，”他说。 他不想离亚历山大王子太近。

“I don’t want to leave you,” said Annie. “Please come with us. Please.” “我不想离开你，”安妮说。 “请和我们一起去吧。求你了。”

Jack stared at her and then sighed. “Okay,” he said. 杰克盯着她看了好一会儿，然后叹了口气。 “好吧，”他说。

As Aristotle, Jack, and Annie started down the hill, the sun was low in the sky. Jack was silent as Annie chatted with Aristotle. “So do warhorses go to war?” she asked. 当亚里士多德、杰克和安妮开始往山下走时，太阳已经低低地挂在天空中了。 杰克一声不吭，而安妮则和亚里士多德聊着天。 “那战马是不是去打仗呢？”她问。

Aristotle nodded. “They carry soldiers to war in lands near and far,” he said. “Their speed and strength can help save a man’s life or win a battle.” 亚里士多德点了点头。 “它们把士兵带到远近不同的地方去打仗，”他说。 “它们的速度和力量可以帮助救一个人的命，或者赢得一场战斗。”

“What a hard job,” said Annie. “真是个辛苦的工作，”安妮说。

“Yes, but they are the most honored horses in the kingdom,” said Aristotle. “是的，但它们是王国里最受尊敬的马，”亚里士多德说。

When Jack, Annie, and Aristotle reached the riding ring, the horses were all standing in a row with grooms attending them. As the King’s Companions watched from outside the fence, King Philip and the small, wiry horse seller walked together down the line, discussing each animal. Alexander walked behind them, looking in the horses’ mouths and feeling their legs. 当杰克、安妮和亚里士多德到达骑马场时，马匹排成一行，马夫在它们身边照料。 国王的随从们站在围栏外观看，国王菲利普和那个瘦小的马贩子一起沿着马匹走过去，讨论每匹马。 亚历山大在他们后面跟着，查看马匹的嘴巴，摸它们的腿。

“The prince loves horses, too,” Aristotle said. “王子也喜欢马，”亚里士多德说。

Too bad for the horses, Jack thought. “这对马来说可不太妙，”杰克心想。

“What are they looking for?” Annie asked. “他们在找什么？”安妮问。

“Thick bones, strong teeth, and a good bend in the knees,” said Aristotle, “a broad chest and high-set neck, bony head, large nostrils, a thick mane, small ears.” “厚实的骨头、强壮的牙齿、膝盖要有好的弯曲度，”亚里士多德说，“宽阔的胸部、高高隆起的脖子、骨骼分明的头部、大的鼻孔、浓密的鬃毛、小耳朵。”

“That’s a lot,” Annie said. “太多了，”安妮说。

The king came to the end of the line and began to haggle with the horse seller. Alexander tried to be part of the conversation, but his father silenced him. Jack almost felt sorry for the prince. King Philip pointed to a couple of horses. The horse seller nodded, and then the grooms led all the horses from the riding ring back into the barn. 国王走到队列的尽头，开始和马贩子讨价还价。 亚历山大想加入谈话，但他的父亲让他闭嘴。 杰克差点为那个王子感到难过。 菲利普国王指着几匹马。 马贩子点点头，然后马夫们把所有马从骑马场赶回了马厩。

The setting sun flashed over the hillside. “The horse trading is over for today,” said Aristotle. “It will resume at daylight tomorrow.” 落日的余晖照在山坡上。 “今天的马交易到此结束，”亚里士多德说。 “明天天亮时再继续。”

Suddenly a black stallion bolted from the barn. Several grooms ran after him. The stallion was stunningly beautiful. As he trotted around the ring, the light of the setting sun seemed to set his dark coat and mane on fire. He held his head high and arched his neck. He had small ears and wide eyes. 突然，一匹黑马从马厩里冲了出来。 几个马夫追了上去。 这匹马非常漂亮。 他绕着场地走的时候，夕阳的光线好像把他的黑色外套和鬃毛点燃了一样。 他高昂着头，脖子弯成了一道弧线。 他的耳朵小小的，眼睛又大又亮。

“That must be the magnificent horse I have heard so much about,” said Aristotle. “那一定就是我听别人说过的那匹 magnificent 的马，”亚里士多德说。

“Who is he?” asked Annie. “他是谁呀？”安妮问。

“His name is Bucephalus,” said Aristotle. “He once belonged to the Royal Horse Master of Thessaly.” “他的名字叫布西法勒斯，”亚里士多德说。 “他曾经属于色萨利的皇家马术大师。”

When the stallion slowed to a walk, a groom approached him and tried to grab his bridle. But as the man’s shadow fell over Bucephalus, the stallion neighed and bucked. He broke away from the man and backed against the fence. His ears were flat against his head. His eyes were wild. 当这匹公马慢下来走路时，一个马夫走过来，试图抓住他的缰绳。 但是当那个人的影子落在布西法勒斯身上时，这匹公马嘶叫起来，猛地一跃。 他挣脱了那个人，往后退到围栏边。 他的耳朵紧紧贴在头上。 他的眼睛看起来很狂野。

“What’s going on?” said Annie. “发生了什么事？”安妮说。

“He was once a noble warhorse,” said Aristotle. “But ever since he was captured in battle, he has not let any rider mount him.” “他曾经是一匹高贵的战马，”亚里士多德说。 “但从他被俘虏以来，他就再也没有让任何骑手骑上他。”

“Why?” asked Annie. “为什么？”安妮问。

“No one knows,” said Aristotle. “But the seller insists he can be tamed again and wants a bag of gold for him.” “没人知道，”亚里士多德说。 “但卖家坚持说他可以再次被驯服，并且想要一袋金子作为报酬。”

A groom threw a rope around the horse’s neck. Another brought out a whip. 一个马夫把一根绳子绕在马的脖子上。 另一个拿出了鞭子。

“What’s that for?” asked Annie. “那是什么？”安妮问。

The groom cracked the whip on the ground. groom 在地上抽了一下鞭子。

“No!” shouted Prince Alexander. “Put the whip down!” “不！”亚历山大王子喊道。 “把鞭子放下！”

The groom cracked the whip again. The horse reared up. Arching his neck, he kicked his hooves, almost trampling the man who held the whip. The grooms jumped back to avoid being crushed. groom 再次抽了一下鞭子。 马猛地扬起了前蹄。 它昂起头，用蹄子踢了一下，差点踩到拿着鞭子的人。 groom 们跳开，以免被踩到。

“No!” shouted Alexander again, rushing forward. “Do not hit him!” He grabbed the whip from the groom and threw it to the ground. “不！”亚历山大再次喊道，冲了过去。 “不要打他！”他从 groom 手中抢过鞭子，扔到了地上。

“Do not be a fool!” the king roared at the prince. “Let them do their job!” “不要做傻瓜！”国王对着王子吼道。 “让他们做好自己的工作！”

Alexander ignored his father. He walked calmly toward the stallion. The horse stared at him for a moment, then took off across the ring. Alexander started running after him. 亚历山大无视了他的父亲。 他平静地走向那匹骏马。 那匹马盯着他看了片刻，然后冲着场地跑开了。 亚历山大开始追着它跑。

“Stop him!” the king ordered his bodyguards. Two of the guards rushed forward and grabbed Alexander before he reached the horse. They held the prince as more grooms approached Bucephalus. The stallion reared up again. “抓住他！”国王命令他的卫兵们。 两名卫兵冲上前去，在亚历山大靠近马之前抓住了他。 他们抓住王子，更多的马夫走向布塞法鲁斯。 那匹骏马再次扬起了前蹄。

“This horse cannot be tamed!” the king shouted at the horse seller. “这匹马没法被驯服！”国王对着马贩子喊道。

“Yes he can!” shouted Alexander. He tried to break free from the guards, but they held him tightly. “And I am the one who can tame him!” “可以的！”亚历山大喊道。 他试图挣脱卫兵的束缚，但卫兵紧紧抓住了他。 “我会驯服这匹马的！”

“Take him from my sight! He is worthless!” shouted the king. Jack couldn’t tell if the king meant the stallion or the prince. “把他赶走！” 他一无是处！”国王喊道。 杰克不知道国王说的是那匹黑马还是王子。

The grooms drove the black stallion back into the stable. And the guards held on to Alexander as they followed the king and his companions back up the hill. Several times, the king turned and shouted at his son: “You fool!” “So reckless!” “Arrogant!” 马夫们把那匹黑马赶回了马厩。 卫兵们抓住亚历山大，跟着国王和他的随从们一起走回山上。 几次三番，国王转身对着儿子喊道：“你这个傻瓜！”“太鲁莽了！”“真傲慢！”

“Excuse me,” said Aristotle. “I must try to calm the king.” He strode away, leaving Jack and Annie alone. “请问一下，”亚里士多德说。 “我得试着让国王冷静下来。”他说着走开了，留下杰克和安妮独自一人。

“Boy, Alexander’s dad is really mean to him,” said Annie. “天啊，亚历山大的爸爸真的对他很凶。”安妮说。

“I noticed that at the house,” said Jack. “He kept telling Alexander to be quiet.” “我在他家的时候注意到了。”杰克说。 “他一直让亚历山大安静点。”

Jack and Annie were quiet themselves as they watched King Philip yell at the prince all the way back to the Royal House. By the time the king and his party had gone inside, the sun had slipped behind the hill. Night was falling fast. The air had grown cooler. 杰克和安妮自己也安静下来，看着菲利普国王一路回到王宫，对着王子大喊大叫。 等国王和他的随从都走进去后，太阳已经悄悄溜到山后面去了。 夜色来得很快。 空气也变得凉了一些。

“I know we think Alexander’s a mean kid and a show-off,” said Annie, “but just now he was trying to protect the black stallion.” “我知道我们觉得亚历山大是个坏孩子，还爱炫耀，”安妮说，“但是刚才他是在试着保护那匹黑马。”

Jack didn’t say anything. 杰克什么也没说。

“And we were kind of mean to him, too, actually,” said Annie. “We made fun of him and stuff. We did.” “其实，我们也对他有点刻薄，”安妮说。 “我们嘲笑他，还做了很多类似的事，确实如此，”

“He deserved it,” Jack mumbled. “他活该，”杰克小声嘟囔着。

“But the truth is we were showing off a little, too,” said Annie, “to the king and his men.” “但说实话，我们也在向国王和他的手下炫耀，”安妮说，“向国王和他的手下炫耀。”

“Fine. So what’s your point?” Jack said, sighing. “好吧。 那你到底想说什么？”杰克叹了口气说。

“Probably we’re even,” said Annie. “Maybe we should start over with him.” “也许我们扯平了，”安妮说。 “也许我们应该重新开始，和他做朋友。”

“Oh, brother,” said Jack, but he actually agreed with her. Strangely, his anger at the prince had faded. He even felt sorry for him now. “哦，天哪，”杰克说，但他其实同意她的看法。 奇怪的是，他对王子的愤怒已经消失了。 现在，他甚至有点同情他了。

“I think his life is pretty sad,” said Annie. “Maybe we could help him somehow.” “我觉得他的生活挺悲伤的，”安妮说。 “也许我们可以想办法帮助他。”

“How do you help a kid like that?” said Jack. “我们怎么帮助这样一个孩子呢？”杰克说。

“I don’t know,” said Annie. “我不知道，”安妮说。

“Well, let’s start with a question,” said Jack. “What do we know about him so far?” “那我们先从一个问题开始吧，”杰克说。 “到目前为止，我们对他了解些什么呢？”

“We know he craves excellence and glory,” said Annie. “That’s what Aristotle told us.” “我们知道他渴望卓越和荣耀，”安妮说。 “这是亚里士多德告诉我们的。”

“We know he’s desperate to equal his father’s power and strength,” said Annie. “我们知道他非常渴望能和他爸爸一样强大有力，”安妮说。

“And he thinks he’s the great-great-grandson of Hercules,” said Jack. “他认为自己是赫拉克勒斯的曾曾孙子，”杰克说。

“Right. He’s a living Greek god,” Annie said. “对的。 他是个活着的希腊神，”安妮说。

“And the world’s greatest athlete,” said Jack. “而且是世界上最厉害的运动员，”杰克说。

“And soon he’ll be master of the universe,” said Annie. “很快他就会成为宇宙的主宰了，”安妮说。

Annie laughed, and Jack laughed with her. “He’s ridiculous,” Jack said, shaking his head. 安妮笑了，杰克也跟着她笑了。 “他太可笑了，”杰克说，摇了摇头。

“I’ve never met a kid like him before,” said Annie. “我以前从没见过像他这样的孩子，”安妮说。

“Thank goodness!” said Jack. “谢天谢地！”，杰克说。

“But we also know something else about him,” said Annie. “但我们还知道他的另一件事，”安妮说。

“What?” said Jack. “什么？”杰克说。

“We know he loves Bucephalus,” Annie said. “我们知道他喜欢布塞法勒斯，”安妮说。

“Yeah … so?” said Jack. “是啊……然后呢？”杰克说。

“So let’s start there,” said Annie. “Love is always a good place to start. Don’t you think?” “那我们就从这里开始，”安妮说。 “爱总是个不错的起点，你不觉得吗？”
