11. Lady Gregory 11. 莱迪·格雷戈里

“A hh, sunshine,” said Jack. He closed his eyes and felt the sunshine streaming through the tree house window. “啊，阳光，”杰克说。 他闭上眼睛，感觉到阳光从树屋的窗户透进来。

“And clean, dry clothes,” murmured Annie. She placed the Pennsylvania book back in the corner. “还有干净、干燥的衣服，”安妮小声说。 她把宾夕法尼亚的书放回角落。

Jack took the magic whistle out of his pocket and placed it next to the book. “There. Let’s go home now,” he said. “I want to look on the Internet for information about Augusta.” He started down the rope ladder. 杰克从口袋里拿出魔法口哨，把它放在书旁边。 “那里。 “我们现在回家吧，”他说。 “我想上网查一下关于奥古斯塔的信息。”他开始沿着绳梯往下爬。

“Great idea,” said Annie, following Jack. “We can find out what happened to her.” “好主意，”安妮说，跟着杰克走。 “我们可以查查她到底发生了什么事。”

Jack and Annie ran through the chilly Frog Creek woods. They crossed the street and hurried up the sparkling sidewalk to their yard. They tramped over old snow up to their porch. Annie opened the front door and led the way inside. 杰克和安妮跑过了寒冷的蛙溪镇树林。 他们穿过街道，快步沿着闪闪发亮的人行道跑回他们的院子。 他们踩着旧雪走到门廊前。 安妮打开前门，带头走了进去。

“Hi!” Jack called. “We’re back!” “你好！”杰克喊道。 “我们回来了！”

“Hi!” their mom called from the kitchen. “Did you have a nice break?” “你好！”他们的妈妈从厨房里喊道。 “你们休息得愉快吗？”

“Yes, we did!” said Annie. “是的，我们做到了！”安妮说。

“Good. Get back to your homework now,” said their mom. “So you can finish in time to go to the theater.” “很好。 现在回去做作业，”他们的妈妈说。 “这样你就有时间去剧院了。”

“Okay!” called Annie. She went to the computer desk and sat down. “What should I type?” she asked Jack. “好的！”安妮喊道。 她走到电脑桌前坐了下来。 “我应该打什么？”她问杰克。

Jack pulled up a chair and sat beside her. “Well, we don’t know her last name,” he said. “So try Galway … Augusta … and Irish stories.” 杰克拉过一把椅子坐在她旁边。 “嗯，我们不知道她的姓氏，”他说。 “那试试加里韦……奥古斯塔……还有爱尔兰的故事。”

Annie typed these words on the keyboard, then hit enter. There were lots of choices for different Web sites. Annie clicked on the first one. 安妮在键盘上输入了这些词，然后按了回车。 有很多不同网站的选择。 安妮点击了第一个。

On the screen was a black-and-white photograph of a woman. The caption under it said: 屏幕上是一张黑白照片，照片里是一个女人。 照片下面写着：

Lady Augusta Gregory 奥古斯塔·格雷戈里女士

“Look! It’s her!” said Annie. “看！ 是她！”安妮说。

The woman on the screen was middle-aged, but she still looked like Augusta. Her hair was parted neatly down the middle. 屏幕上那位女士年纪不小了，但看起来还是像奥古斯塔。 她的头发中间整齐地分成了两半。

Jack read aloud from the screen: 杰克从屏幕上大声读出来：

Lady Augusta Gregory was born into a wealthy family in Galway, Ireland, in 1852. She wrote over forty plays and many poems and essays. She was a co-founder of the Abbey Theatre, the national theater of Ireland. Lady Gregory also learned the old language of Ireland and became well known for collecting Irish stories and legends and sharing them with the world. 奥古斯塔·格里高利小姐1852年出生在爱尔兰加里韦伊的一个富裕家庭。她写过四十多部戏剧，还有许多诗歌和散文。她是爱尔兰国家剧院——阿比剧院的共同创始人。奥古斯塔·格里高利小姐还学会了爱尔兰的古老语言，并因收集爱尔兰的故事和传说并分享给全世界而闻名。

“Wow!” said Annie. “Augusta did have a brave heart and a fine mind! And she must have liked our play, since she wrote forty of her own and started her own theater.” “哇！”安妮说。 “奥古斯塔确实有一颗勇敢的心和聪明的头脑！ 她一定喜欢我们的表演，因为她写了四十部自己的剧本，还创办了自己的剧院。”

“Yeah,” said Jack. “She really turned out great.” This reminded him of a question he’d asked himself earlier. “I wonder what I’m good for? I didn’t know how to do anything on that Irish farm.” “是啊，”杰克说。 “她真的做得很好。”这让他想起了自己之前问过的一个问题。 “我想知道自己擅长什么？ 在那个爱尔兰农场上，我不知道自己能做什么。”

“Me neither,” said Annie. “But hardly any kids today know how to do that kind of stuff.” “我也是，”安妮说。 “但是现在几乎没有孩子知道怎么做这些事情了。”

“So what would we do if all our machines and computers broke down?” said Jack. “那如果我们所有的机器和电脑都坏了，我们该怎么办呢？”杰克说。

“We’d have to figure out how to grow potatoes and make our own clothes and milk cows,” said Annie. “我们得想办法种土豆、自己做衣服、挤牛奶，”安妮说。

“I’d probably read some kind of instructions first, then give it a try,” said Jack. “我大概会先看看一些说明，然后再试试看，”杰克说。

“I’d probably give it a try first,” said Annie, “ then read the instructions.” “我大概会先试试看，”安妮说，“然后再看看说明。”

Jack laughed. 杰克笑了。

“I know some stuff we’re good for,” said Annie. “我知道一些我们擅长的事情，”安妮说。

“What?” said Jack. “什么？”杰克说。

“First, we’re good for helping each other,” said Annie. “首先，我们擅长互相帮助，”安妮说。

“Yeah, but—” said Jack. “是的，但是—”杰克说。

“No, really. We help each other all the time,” said Annie. “不，真的， 我们一直互相帮助，”安妮说。

“That’s true,” said Jack. “这倒是真的，”杰克说。

“And we’re good for helping Augusta,” said Annie, “and helping Louis Armstrong, Mozart, and Leonardo da Vinci. We put the smile on the Mona Lisa’s face, remember?” “而且我们很擅长帮助奥古斯塔，”安妮说，“还有帮助路易斯·阿姆斯特朗、莫扎特和达·芬奇。 我们给蒙娜丽莎的脸上加上了笑容，还记得吗？

Jack nodded. “Yep,” he said. 杰克点了点头。 “记得，”他说。

“And we’re good for saving an orphan penguin, a huge octopus, and the cities of Tokyo, Venice, and New York,” said Annie. “We’re good for rescuing a baby gorilla from a leopard, and schoolkids from a twister. We’re good for helping Shakespeare, Clara Barton, and George Washington. We’re good for rescuing two kids from a tsunami, a Lakota boy from a buffalo stampede, and a baby kangaroo and a koala from a forest fire. We’re good for—” “我们已经成功救出了孤儿企鹅、一只巨大的章鱼，还有东京、威尼斯和纽约这些城市，”安妮说。 “我们还成功救出了被豹子追赶的小山 gorilla，以及被龙卷风困住的学校孩子们。 我们帮助过莎士比亚、克拉拉·巴顿和乔治·华盛顿，做得很好。 我们救过两个孩子免受海啸伤害，救过一个拉科塔男孩免受野牛群踩踏，还救过一只小袋鼠和一只考拉免受森林大火的伤害。我们做得很好——

“Wait, stop,” said Jack. “Stop.” “等等，停下，”杰克说。 “停下。”

“But that’s not even half of it,” said Annie. “但那还不到一半呢，”安妮说。

“I know,” said Jack. “But that’s plenty. I’m inspired. I’m ready to write that story for homework. I’ll use my own experience. I have a little more than I thought.” “我知道，”杰克说。 “但已经够多了。我受到启发了。 我准备写那篇家庭作业的故事了。 我会用自己的经历。 我比想象中要多一些经验。”

“Cool,” said Annie. She went back to reading about Lady Gregory on the computer. “酷，”安妮说。 她又回到电脑上，继续阅读关于格里高里的女士的内容。

Jack grabbed a pencil and pulled out his notebook. He moved to the couch and sat down. As late-winter light slanted into the living room, he began to write. 杰克抓起一支铅笔，拿出他的笔记本。 他走到沙发旁，坐了下来。 随着冬末的光线斜斜照进客厅，他开始写作。
