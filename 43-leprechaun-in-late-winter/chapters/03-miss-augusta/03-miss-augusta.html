3. Miss Augusta 3. 奥古斯塔小姐

Jack and Annie looked at each other. Augusta! 杰克和安妮互相看了看。奥古斯塔！

The girl put down her baskets and took off her wet cape. 女孩放下她的篮子，脱下了她湿漉漉的斗篷。

“Did you deliver your cakes to the poor, Miss Augusta?” asked Molly. “你给贫困的人送蛋糕了吗，奥古斯塔小姐？”莫莉问道。

“Yes, Molly,” said Augusta. “I visited seven cottages today.” “是的，莫莉，”奥古斯塔说道。 “我今天参观了七座小屋。”

“Seven? In this weather? You’re an angel, Miss Augusta!” said Molly. “Always so kind to the poor.” “七？在这种天气？” “你是个天使，奥古斯塔小姐！”莫莉说道。 “总是这么善良，对穷人来说太好了。”

“It is my duty, Molly,” the girl said, “to help those less fortunate than myself.” Her gaze rested on Jack and Annie. “And who are these poor children?” “这是我的责任，莫莉，”女孩说，“我要帮助那些不如我幸运的人。”她的目光落在杰克和安妮身上。 “这些可怜的孩子是谁？”

“They’re looking for work, miss,” said the cook. “But I’m afraid they’re sorry creatures, not good for anything. I was just sending them away.” “他们在找工作，小姐，”厨师说。 “但恐怕他们是一些可怜的家伙，什么也做不了。” “我刚刚才让他们离开。”

“Oh, surely we must not turn them out so quickly, Cook,” said Augusta. “How tired and miserable they look.” “哦， cook，我们一定不能这么快就把他们赶走。”奥古斯塔说。 “他们看起来又累又难受。”

Jack didn’t think they looked that bad. 杰克觉得他们看起来没那么糟。

“We are tired and miserable,” Annie said. Her voice sounded sad. Her shoulders sagged. “我们确实又累又难受，”安妮说。 她的声音听起来很伤心。 她的肩膀垂了下来。

Oh, brother, Jack thought. Annie was really acting her part. 哦，哥哥，杰克心想。 安妮真的在认真扮演这个角色。

“My poor dears, you must both come into the parlor and rest a bit,” said Augusta. “我可怜的朋友们，你们两个必须到客厅里休息一会儿，”奥古斯塔说。

“We would like that,” Annie said pitifully. “我们想要那个，”安妮无助地说。

“Follow me,” said Augusta. “跟我来，”奥古斯塔说。

“Miss Augusta, surely you’re not taking those dirty children into the parlor!” said the cook. “奥古斯塔小姐，你肯定不会带这些脏兮兮的孩子去客厅吧！”厨师说。

“We must always be kind to the poor, Cook, no matter how dirty they are,” said Augusta. “We should give them something to drink if they are thirsty, and something to eat if they are hungry.” “我们必须对穷人始终善良，厨子，不管他们有多脏，”奥古斯塔说。 “如果他们口渴了，我们就该给他们点喝的；如果他们饿了，就该给他们点吃的。”

“You are too kind, Miss Augusta,” said Molly, shaking her head. “你太客气了，奥古斯塔小姐，”莫莉摇着头说。

“Well, at least make them take off their filthy boots,” said the cook. “好吧，至少让他们把脏兮兮的靴子脱掉，”厨师说。

Jack and Annie pulled off their boots and socks and set them by the door. Their feet were red and raw-looking. 杰克和安妮脱下靴子和袜子，把它们放在门边。 他们的脚又红又看起来很粗糙。

Augusta took two peeled potatoes from a bowl and put them in her pocket. Then she picked up a lit candle from the hearth. “Come, let me take you to the parlor,” she said to Jack and Annie. 奥古斯塔从碗里拿了两个去皮的土豆，放进她的口袋里。 然后她从壁炉上拿起一支点燃的蜡烛。 “来，我带你去客厅，”她对杰克和安妮说。

“Thanks, Augusta,” said Annie. “谢谢您，奥古斯塔，”安妮说。

“Show some respect!” the cook called after Annie. “Call her ‘ Miss Augusta’!” “要有礼貌！”厨师在安妮身后喊道。 “叫她‘奥古斯塔小姐’！”

“Sorry!” said Annie. “Thanks, Miss Augusta.” “对不起！”安妮说。 “谢谢您，奥古斯塔小姐。”

Jack rolled his eyes. Why should he call her ‘miss’? Augusta didn’t look like she was any older than he was! 杰克翻了翻眼睛。 为什么他要叫她‘小姐’呢？ 奥古斯塔看起来和他差不多大！

Holding her flickering candle, Augusta led Jack and Annie out of the kitchen. The wooden floor creaked as they walked barefoot through a narrow hallway. 奥古斯塔举着摇晃的蜡烛，领着杰克和安妮走出了厨房。 他们光着脚走过狭窄的走廊，木头地板发出吱呀声。

How are we ever going to inspire this strange, serious girl? wondered Jack. She acts as if she’s already a grown-up and treats Annie and me like babies. 杰克想知道，我们怎么能激励这个奇怪又严肃的女孩呢？ 她表现得好像已经是个大人了，还把安妮和我当成小宝宝对待。

“We’ll sit in here, children,” said Augusta. She directed Jack and Annie into a large room with heavy curtains and dark furniture. The pale teenage girl who’d answered the front door sat on a sofa, knitting. Another teenage girl knitted beside her. They scowled when they saw Jack and Annie. “孩子们，我们坐这儿吧，”奥古斯塔说。 她把杰克和安妮带进了一间大房间，那里挂着厚重的窗帘，家具都很暗。 之前开门的那个苍白的女中学生正坐在沙发上织毛衣。 另一个女中学生坐在她旁边也织毛衣。 她们一看到杰克和安妮，就皱起了眉头。

“What are you doing, Augusta?” asked the pale girl. “Why are you bringing those two into the parlor?” “奥古斯塔，你这是在干什么？”苍白的女孩问。 “你为什么要把那两个人带到客厅里？”

“I invited them to tea, Gertrude,” said Augusta. She turned to Jack and Annie. “Pay no attention to my sisters,” she said. “Please, sit down.” “我邀请他们来喝茶，杰特鲁德，”奥古斯塔说。 她转向杰克和安妮。 “别理会我姐姐们，”她说。 “请坐下来。”

“Augusta, have you gone mad?” said Gertrude. “You cannot invite these two ragamuffins to sit in here!” “奥古斯塔，你疯了吗？”杰特鲁德说。 “你不能邀请这两个脏兮兮的孩子进来！”

“Mother will be furious,” said Augusta’s other sister. “They’re filthy! They’re not even wearing shoes!” “妈妈会生气的，”奥古斯塔的另一个姐姐说。 “他们身上好脏！ 他们连鞋子都没穿！”

Jack looked down at his muddy clothes and cold red feet. 杰克低头看了看自己脏兮兮的衣服和冰冷的红脚丫。

“Cook made them remove their muddy shoes in the kitchen, Eliza,” Augusta said. “I only wish I had nice, dry shoes to give them. Sit down, children,” she said to Jack and Annie again. “库克让他们在厨房里脱掉脏鞋子，伊丽莎，”奥古斯塔说。 “我真希望我能给他们一双又干净又干燥的鞋子。 孩子们，坐下来，”她又对杰克和安妮说了一遍。

Jack and Annie slowly sat down. 杰克和安妮慢慢地坐了下来。

“You’re going to get into trouble, Augusta …,” said Gertrude. “你这样做会惹上麻烦的，奥古斯塔……，”杰里杜说。

“Mother will never approve,” said Eliza. Both sisters shook their heads as they went back to their knitting. “妈妈永远不会同意的，”伊丽莎说。 两个姐姐摇了摇头，然后又回到织毛衣上。

Augusta ignored her sisters and walked to a silver teapot on a sideboard. “Would you like some hot tea, my poor dears?” she asked Jack and Annie. 奥古斯塔无视她的姐妹们，走到旁边柜子上的一个银茶壶前。 “你们要来点热茶吗，我可怜的孩子们？”她问杰克和安妮。

“Yes, Miss Augusta,” said Annie. “是的，奥古斯塔小姐，”安妮说。

Jack nodded. Hot tea sounded good. He still felt chilled from the cold wind and rain. There was a fireplace in the dreary parlor, but no fire was lit. Everything in the room seemed dark and gloomy, except for a few books on a table. 杰克点了点头。 热茶听起来不错。 他仍然觉得冷风和雨水让他发冷。 阴暗的客厅里有个壁炉，但没有生火。 房间里的一切似乎都又暗又阴郁，除了桌子上几本书。

As Augusta poured tea into fancy china cups, Jack leaned closer to get a look at the books. One was titled The Plays of William Shakespeare. Another was called The Tales of King Arthur. Jack smiled to himself. Seeing those book titles made him feel a little more comfortable. 当奥古斯塔把茶倒进精致的瓷杯里时，杰克凑得更近，想看看那些书。 其中一本的标题是《威廉·莎士比亚的戏剧》。 另一本叫做《亚瑟王的故事》。 杰克自己笑了笑。 看到那些书名，他感觉舒服了一点。

Augusta carried cups of tea to Jack and Annie. Then she pulled the potatoes out of her pocket and gave one to each of them. 奥古斯塔给杰克和安妮端来了茶杯。 然后她从口袋里拿出土豆，每人给了一个。

“Thank you, Miss Augusta,” said Annie. “谢谢您，奥古斯塔小姐，”安妮说。

Jack took a sip of tea, but it was too bitter and hot to drink. He took a bite of his cold potato, but it was too hard to chew. 杰克喝了一口茶，但太苦太烫了，喝不下去。 他咬了一口冷土豆，但太硬了，嚼不动。

“So, Miss Augusta, what do you like to do around here?” Annie asked. “What inspires you?” “那么，奥古斯塔小姐，你在这里喜欢做什么呢？”安妮问。 “什么启发了你？”

Augusta looked puzzled. “I do not know what you mean,” she said. 奥古斯塔看起来很困惑。 “我不知道你是什么意思，”她说。

“What about reading books?” said Jack. “Have you read those books?” He pointed to The Tales of King Arthur and The Plays of William Shakespeare. “那我们来读书吧？”杰克说。 “你读过那些书吗？”他指着《亚瑟王传说》和《莎士比亚戏剧》说。

“Those books belong to my brothers,” said Augusta. “那些书是我哥哥的，”奥古斯塔说。

“The time has not come for Augusta to read such books,” said her sister Gertrude. “现在还不是奥古斯塔读这种书的时候，”她的姐姐杰特鲁德说。

“Not until she is older,” said her sister Eliza. “等她长大了再说，”她的姐姐伊丽莎说。

“Why?” asked Jack. “为什么？”杰克问。

“Mother says The Tales of King Arthur and the plays of Shakespeare are not for young ladies,” said Augusta. “妈妈说《亚瑟王传说》和莎士比亚的戏剧不适合小女孩，”奥古斯塔说。

“Really?” said Jack. “真的吗？”杰克说。

“Yes. But I’m afraid I sometimes peek at my brothers’ books,” Augusta said to Jack and Annie in a low voice. “I love stories. I remember every story I read or hear.” “是的。 不过我有时候会偷偷看我哥哥的书，”奥古斯塔低声对杰克和安妮说。 “我超爱故事。” “我读过的每本书和听过的每个故事都记得清清楚楚。”

“I love stories, too,” said Annie. “And I love books.” “我也超爱故事，”安妮说。 “我还超爱看书。”

The two older sisters smiled. “Keep striving, my dear,” said Eliza. “Perhaps one day you will learn to read.” 两个姐姐微笑着。 “继续努力，亲爱的，”伊丽莎说。 “也许有一天你就能学会读书了。”

“I already know how to read,” said Annie. “Jack and I read lots of books.” She pointed to the books on the table. “In fact, we know tons about King Arthur, and we go see plays by Shakespeare with our parents. And one time, we even acted in a play by Shakespeare—A Midsummer Night’s Dream.” “我已经会读书了，”安妮说。 “杰克和我读了很多书。”她指着桌上的书。 “事实上，我们对亚瑟王知道很多，我们还经常和爸爸妈妈一起去看莎士比亚的戏剧。” 有一次，我们甚至还演过莎士比亚的戏剧——《仲夏夜之梦》。

“It was at our school,” Jack broke in, before Annie could tell them that she and Jack had actually met Shakespeare himself! “是在我们学校，”杰克插话道，还没等安妮告诉他们，其实她和杰克真的见过莎士比亚本人！

“A Midsummer Night’s Dream?” said Augusta. She looked surprised. “《仲夏夜之梦》？”奥古斯塔说。 她看起来很惊讶。

“Don’t listen to them, Augusta,” said Gertrude. “I doubt these children have ever been to school—much less acted in a play by William Shakespeare.” “别听他们的，奥古斯塔，”格特鲁德说。 “我怀疑这些孩子从来没上过学——更别说演过莎士比亚的戏剧了。”

“I suspect you are quite right, Gertrude,” someone said. “我想你说得对，杰特鲁德，”有人说道。

A tall woman was standing in the doorway of the parlor. She wore a long black velvet dress and stood very straight. There was an icy look on her face as she stared at Jack and Annie. 一位高个子女人正站在客厅的门口。 她穿着一件长长的黑色天鹅绒连衣裙，站得笔直。 她脸上带着冰冷的表情，盯着杰克和安妮看。

“Oh! Mother!” said Eliza. 妈妈！”伊丽莎说。
