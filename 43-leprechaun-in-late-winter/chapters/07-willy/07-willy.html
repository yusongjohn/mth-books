7. Willy 7. 威利

Hundreds of Shee swarmed over the bright bridge. Behind the galloping riders more Shee came fluttering on wings like butterflies. 成百上千的谢伊涌上了那座明亮的桥。 骑手们飞驰而过，更多的Shee像蝴蝶一样扇动着翅膀飞来。

Augusta stood up as if in a trance, her hands clasped over her heart. 奥古斯塔仿佛陷入幻觉般站了起来，双手紧紧按在胸口。

“Oh, wow!” breathed Annie. “哦，太棒了！”安妮惊叹道。

“Keep playing!” shouted Jack. “继续演奏！”杰克喊道。

Annie blew into the whistle again. 安妮又对着哨子吹了一口气。

The Shee flew and galloped along the wide, grassy bank. Their horses, fleet as the wind, had arched necks and flaming eyes. They moved faster and faster, swirling into a blinding circle of light and color. Shee们一边飞翔一边沿着宽阔的草地飞驰。 他们的马匹轻盈如风，脖子优雅地弯曲着，眼睛闪烁着火焰般的光芒。 它们越跑越快，旋转着形成了一圈耀眼的光和色彩。

The soaring sounds of the music inspired Jack to soar with his words, too. He looked at his notebook and sang: 音乐的声音高亢激昂，也激发了杰克用语言飞翔的灵感。 他看着自己的笔记本，唱了起来：

In a swirl they leave,

so wild, so free,

with a lonely girl

to the hill of the Shee! 他们旋风般地离开，如此狂野，如此自由，留下一个孤独的女孩走向Shee山！

“Jack, no!” shouted Annie. “杰克，别！”安妮喊道。

A mighty blast of wind nearly blew Jack and Annie off their rock. They crouched down and covered their heads. When the wind grew calm, they stood up—just in time to see the swirling cloud of light vanish back into the old forest. 一阵猛烈的风吹得杰克和安妮几乎从岩石上被吹下去。 他们蹲下身子，用手护住头部。 当风渐渐平息时，他们站了起来——刚好看到那团旋转的光雾消失在古老的森林里。

The Shee were gone. Once again, sky blended with water and earth in a veil of silver mist. 谢伊人不见了。 又一次，天空、水和大地在银色的薄雾中融为一体。

“Whew,” Jack said breathlessly. “That was amazing!” “哇——”杰克喘着气说。 “太棒了！”

“Jack! Do you know what you just did?” “杰克！ 你知道你刚才做了什么吗？

“Yeah, I made the Shee appear and disappear,” Jack said. “I used my notes from Mary’s story, just like we planned.” “是的，我让谢伊出现又消失，”杰克说。 “我用了玛丽故事里的笔记，就像我们计划的一样。”

“Yes, you did that,” said Annie. “And you made Augusta disappear, too!” “是的，你做到了，”安妮说。 “你还让奥古斯塔也消失了！”

“What?” said Jack. “什么？”杰克说。

“The Shee took Augusta!” said Annie. “I tried to stop you, but it was too late! You’d already sung those words—” “谢伊带走了奥古斯塔！”安妮说。 “我试图阻止你，但已经太晚了！ 你已经唱过那些词了——”

“What words?” said Jack. “什么词？”杰克说。

“You sang: In a swirl they leave, so wild, so free, with a lonely girl to the hill of the Shee!” said Annie. “你唱的是：在旋风中他们离开，如此狂野，如此自由，和一个孤独的女孩一起前往谢伊的山丘！”安妮说。

“I was just reading from my notes!” said Jack. “They took Augusta? Are you sure?” “我刚才只是在看我的笔记！”杰克说。 “他们带走了奥古斯塔？你确定吗？”，

“Yes! She’s gone!” said Annie. “是的！”， “她不见了！”安妮说。

“Oh, no!” said Jack. He and Annie ran to the ridge above the river. “Augusta!” he shouted. “哦，不！”杰克说。 他和安妮跑到河对岸的山脊上。 “奥古斯塔！”，他喊道。

There was no sign of Augusta’s red cape anywhere. 到处都看不到奥古斯塔那条红色的斗篷。

“See? She’s gone,” said Annie. “看？ 她不见了，”安妮说。

“It’s all my fault!” said Jack. “都是我的错！”杰克说。

“You couldn’t help yourself,” said Annie. “你已经尽力了，”安妮说。

“But I’m responsible!” said Jack. “We have to get her back!” “但我得负责！”杰克说。 “我们必须把她救回来！”

“Maybe Mary can help us,” said Annie. “She—” “也许玛丽能帮我们，”安妮说。 “她—”

“Wait, listen,” said Jack. “What’s that sound?” “等等，听我说，”杰克说。 “你听到什么声音了吗？”

Squeaky noises were coming from behind the pile of small boulders. It sounded as if someone was trying to play the magic whistle. But the sound was definitely not magical. “吱吱”的声音是从一堆小石头后面传来的。 听起来好像有人在试着吹魔法口哨。 但这个声音肯定不是魔法发出的。

“The whistle! I must’ve dropped it!” said Annie. “是口哨！ 我肯定把它弄丢了！“安妮说道。

“Maybe it’s Augusta!” said Jack. “也许是奥古斯塔！“杰克说道。

Jack and Annie ran back and looked over the rocks. 杰克和安妮跑回去，看了看那些岩石。

A man no higher than Jack’s knee was blowing into the whistle. The man wore a green jacket and a three-cornered red cap with a white feather. He had big ears, a bushy red beard, skinny little legs, and silver buckles on his shoes. 一个比杰克膝盖还矮的男人正在吹口哨。 他穿着一件绿色夹克，戴着一顶三边的红色帽子，帽子上有一根白色羽毛。 他有着大耳朵、浓密的红色胡须、瘦小的腿，鞋子上有银色的扣子。

“Oh, man,” said Jack. “天啊，”杰克说道。

“A leprechaun!” whispered Annie. “是个小精灵！“安妮小声说道。

Jack and Annie just stared at the leprechaun as he blew into the whistle. His small, bony fingers danced over its six holes. But only squeaks, chirps, and hollow tweeting sounds came out. 杰克和安妮看着那个小矮人，看他对着哨子吹气。 他细小、骨节分明的手指在哨子的六个孔上跳来跳去。 但出来的只是吱吱声、叽叽声和空洞的鸟叫声。

The leprechaun blew harder and harder. Then he stopped. He turned the whistle over and looked at it closely. He shook his head and frowned. 小矮人吹得越来越用力，然后停了下来。 他把哨子翻过来，仔细地看了看。 他摇了摇头，皱起了眉头。

“Hello!” said Annie. “你好！”安妮说。

The leprechaun jumped and looked up. “Well, hello yourself!” he said, grinning. “You startled me! Here, take this back. It’s no use to me, as you can plainly hear.” 小矮人吓了一跳，抬起头来看。 “嗯，你好啊！”他说，咧嘴笑了。 “你吓到我了！” 这里，把这个拿回去。 对你来说没用，你听得清清楚楚。

The little man held up the whistle. Jack reached over the rock and took it. He slipped it back into the pocket of his coat. 那个小人举起了哨子。 杰克越过岩石，把它拿了起来。 他把它又放回了外套的口袋里。

“Listen, we need your help,” said Jack. “Our friend—” “听着，我们需要你的帮助，”杰克说。 “我们的朋友—”

“Oh, you humans! Always in such a hurry!” the leprechaun said. “哦，你们这些人类！ 总是这么着急！”小精灵说。

“Sorry,” said Jack, “but we really need you to help us. You see—” “对不起，”杰克说，“但我们真的需要你帮我们。你瞧—”

“First of all, who are you?” the leprechaun asked. “首先，你是谁？”小精灵问。

“I’m Jack. She’s my sister, Annie,” said Jack. “Our friend—” “我是杰克。 她是我的妹妹，安妮，”杰克说。 “我们的朋友——”

“Ah, well then, Jack and sister Annie, I’m Willy,” said the leprechaun. “Now, let’s get a few things straight right away—never call me Little Willy or Tiny Willy. I don’t like it. And never, ever call me Wee Willy. That’s the one I hate the most.” “啊，那好吧，杰克和妹妹安妮，我叫威尔利，”小精灵说。 “现在，我们得先说清楚几件事——千万别叫我小威尔利或者小不点威尔利。我不喜欢这样。 也千万别叫我小小威尔利。 那是我最讨厌的一个称呼。”

“Okay, fine!” said Jack. “But—” “好吧，知道了！”杰克说。 “但是——”

“Jack, let me handle this,” said Annie. She turned to the leprechaun. “Willy, why didn’t you go back over the river with the Shee?” “杰克，让我来处理这件事，”安妮说。 她转向了那个精灵。 “威利，你为什么没有和希一起过河回去呢？”

“Ask me why I came over the river in the first place, and I’ll tell you the answers to both questions,” said Willy. “你问我为什么一开始要过河，我就能告诉你这两个问题的答案，”威利说。

“Okay, why did you come over the river in the first place, Willy? And why didn’t you go back across with the Shee?” said Annie. “好吧，威利，你一开始为什么要过河呢？” “安妮问：‘那你为什么没有和希一起过桥回去呢？’

“Answer number one: I was having a bit of a nap in the reeds when I heard your whistle playing,” said Willy. “Before I knew it, I was moving with the Shee across the bridge. Probably you didn’t see me. Lost in the swirl of things, I was.” “答案一：我正在芦苇丛里小睡，听到你吹口哨时才醒过来，”威利说。 “我还没反应过来，就已经跟着希一起过桥了。 可能你没看到我。 我被那些东西卷走了，完全迷失了。”

“Excuse me—” started Jack. “打扰一下——”杰克开口说。

“I’ve heard some good whistle playing,” Willy said. “But yours, missy, was like none I’ve ever heard from a human—not in the nine hundred years of my life. So! Answer number two: I didn’t go back over the river because I wanted to find out the secret of your playing. Well?” “我听过一些不错的口哨声，”威尔利说。 “但小姐你的口哨声，是我九百年来从没听过的，不是人类能吹出来的——所以！ 答案二：我没回去过河，因为我想要找出你口哨声的秘密。怎么样？

“That’s easy,” said Annie. “The whistle played itself. It wasn’t me.” “这很简单，”安妮说。 “是口哨自己在吹，不是我。”

“Ah, you’re a modest girl,” said Willy. “And you like to keep the secrets of your talents to yourself.” “啊，你真是个谦虚的姑娘，”威尔利说。 “你还喜欢把你的才能秘密藏起来。”

“Not really—” said Annie. “其实不是——”安妮说。

“Listen, Willy!” Jack broke in. “Can you help us? We lost our friend, Augusta. It was my fault. I sang about a lonely girl. Then she was taken by the Shee—” “听着，威尔利！”杰克插话道。 “你能帮帮我们吗？” 我们失去了我们的朋友，奥古斯塔。这是我的错。 我唱了一首关于一个孤独女孩的歌。 然后她被谢伊带走了—”

“Yes, I saw that,” said Willy. “Whisked away, she was. So I’m guessing now you want me to help you find her. Is that it?” “是的，我看到了，”威尔利说。 “被带走了，她就这样。” 所以现在我想你一定是想要我帮你找到她。是这样吗？

“Yes! That’s it!” said Jack. “是的！ “就是这里！”杰克说。

“We were going to ask Mary Sheridan to help us,” said Annie. “But you probably know the way better. And—” “我们本来是想请玛丽·谢里登帮我们的，”安妮说。 “不过你可能更熟悉路。而且——”

“Wait a minute,” said Willy. “Did you say Mary Sheridan?” “等一下，”威尔利说。 “你刚刚说玛丽·谢里登？”

“You know Mary?” said Annie. “你知道玛丽吗？”安妮说。

“Know her?” said Willy, grinning from ear to ear. “Why, if I were four feet taller, I’d have married Mary Sheridan years ago.” “认识她？”威尔利咧嘴笑着说道。 “哦，如果我再高四英尺，早就和玛丽·谢里登结婚了。”

“Really?” said Annie. “真的吗？”安妮说。

“Oh, yes, indeed, we’re very good friends,” said Willy. “You see, I live in what you might call the In- between. I have one foot in the magical world of the Shee. And I have one foot in the mortal world of humans, like Mary. Ah, lovely Mary …” “哦，是的，我们是非常好的朋友，”威尔利说。 “你看，我住在所谓的中间地带。 我一只脚踏在谢伊的魔法世界里。 而且我有一只脚在人类的凡间，就像玛丽一样。 啊，可爱的玛丽……”

“Yeah, Mary’s great,” said Jack. “But now—” “Yeah, Mary’s great,” said Jack. “But now—”

“You want me to guide you to your missing friend,” said Willy. “You want me to guide you to your missing friend,” said Willy.

“Right, right!” said Jack. “对，对！”杰克说。

“I can do that, but what will you give me for it?” said Willy. “我可以帮你，但你要给我什么作为回报呢？”威尔说。

“What do you mean?” asked Jack. “你什么意思？”杰克问。

“What will you give me for helping you find your friend? My time’s very valuable, you know,” said Willy. “你要给我什么作为帮助你找到你朋友的回报？ 我的时间非常宝贵，你知道的，”威尔说。

“We don’t really have anything,” said Jack. “我们真的没什么，”杰克说。

“We’re poor and miserable,” said Annie. “我们又穷又苦，”安妮说。

“I can see that,” said Willy. “All right then, how about this: I’ll lead you to your friend, and you’ll teach me how to play the whistle the way you play it. Fair enough?” “我看出来了，”威尔利说。 “那这样吧，我带你去找你的朋友，你教我怎么像你那样吹口哨。怎么样？”

“Sorry, I—” said Annie. “对不起，我—”安妮说。

“Deal!” Jack broke in. “成交！”杰克插话道。

“Jack?” said Annie. “杰克？”安妮说。

“Annie, it’s a deal,” Jack said. He was ready to promise anything to save Augusta! “安妮，这是成交了，”杰克说。 他愿意承诺任何事来拯救奥古斯塔！

“Good!” said Willy. “I’d love to play like that for Mary someday. Now, the path I’m about to show you is very, very secret. You must never show another living soul.” “太好了！”威尔利说。 “我 someday 想要像这样为玛丽玩一次。” 现在，我要给你们看的这条路非常、非常秘密。 你们绝不能告诉任何活人！”，

“Of course not,” said Jack. “当然不会，”杰克说。

“Then come with me,” said Willy, “and I’ll lead you across the water to the home of the Shee!” “那跟我来，”威尔利说，“我会带你们穿过水面，去到希伊的家！”
