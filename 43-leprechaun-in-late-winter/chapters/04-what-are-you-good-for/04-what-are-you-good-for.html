4. What Are You Good For? 4. 你有什么用处？

“Hi there!” Annie said cheerfully. “你好啊！”安妮开心地说。

Augusta’s mother did not reply. She was staring at Jack’s bare feet. Her expression made him sink down in his chair. 奥古斯塔的母亲没有回答。 她正盯着杰克光着的脚。 她的表情让他不由自主地坐低了身子。

“Do not blame Eliza or me, Mother,” said Gertrude. “These are Augusta’s friends, not ours.” “别怪伊丽莎或我，妈妈，”格特鲁德说。 “这些都是奥古斯塔的朋友，不是我们的。”

“I took pity on them, Mother,” said Augusta. “They were wet and miserable.” “妈妈，我可怜他们，”奥古斯塔说。 “他们又湿又难受。”

Her mother finally smiled. “Yes, daughter, I imagine they were. It’s very nice to have pity for the poor, but dirty children should not be sitting in our parlor.” 她的妈妈终于笑了。 “是的，女儿，我想他们确实如此。 对穷人怀有同情心非常好，但脏兮兮的孩子不应该坐在我们的客厅里。

“They were hungry, Mother,” said Augusta. “他们饿了，妈妈，”奥古斯塔说。

“Yes, and I see you have given them food,” said her mother. “So it is time to take them out of the house now.” “是的，我看到你已经给他们食物了，”她的妈妈说。 “那么现在是时候让他们离开房子了。

Jack and Annie stood up. Jack was happy to leave. He felt like Augusta and her mother were talking about stray dogs or cats. 杰克和安妮站了起来。 杰克很高兴离开。 他觉得奥古斯塔和她的妈妈在谈论流浪狗或猫。

But Augusta sat very still and just stared at her mother. 但奥古斯塔坐得非常安静，只是盯着她的妈妈看。

“Go on—get them out of here, Augusta,” said Gertrude. “They are not clean! They might even have bugs in their hair.” “快点——把它们带出去，奥古斯塔，”杰克说。 “它们不干净！ 它们的头发里甚至可能有虫子。”

The mere mention of bugs made Jack’s scalp itch. He and Annie both scratched their heads. 一提到虫子，杰克的头皮就开始发痒了。 杰克和安妮都挠了挠头。

“See!” said Gertrude. “看到了吧！”杰克说。

“Augusta …,” her mother said in a stern voice. “奥古斯塔……，”她妈妈用严厉的语气说。

“Oh, all right! All right!” said Augusta, standing up. “I was trying to be kind! Come with me, please,” she said to Jack and Annie. “I’ll lead you down the lane a bit, at least past the sheepdogs.” “哦，好吧！ 好吧！”奥古斯塔站起来说。 “我只是想做个好心人！” 请跟我来，”她对杰克和安妮说。 “我会带你们沿着小路走一段，至少走到牧羊犬那边。”

As Augusta started out of the room, her mother stopped her and pinched her shoulders. “Carry yourself straight, daughter,” she said. 当奥古斯塔准备走出房间时，她妈妈拦住了她，捏了捏她的肩膀。 “要挺直腰板，孩子，”她说道。

Jack couldn’t imagine how the girl could carry herself any straighter. 杰克简直无法想象这个女孩还能把腰板挺得更直。

Augusta led Jack and Annie back down the dark hallway, through the fish-smelling kitchen, past the ancient butler sleeping by the fire and the three kitchen maids and the cook. Without a word, she grabbed her red cape and pulled it around her. 奥古斯塔带着杰克和安妮沿着昏暗的走廊走回，穿过有鱼腥味的厨房，经过在火边睡觉的年迈管家，还有三个厨房女仆和厨师。 她一句话也没说，抓起她的红色斗篷披在身上。

“Where are you going, Miss Augusta?” asked Molly. “奥古斯塔小姐，你去哪儿？”莫莉问道。

“I have been ordered to send these poor children back out into the storm,” Augusta said. “我被命令送这两个可怜的孩子回到暴风雨中，”奥古斯塔说。

Jack and Annie forced their feet into their stiff, wet socks and boots. Augusta held the door for them, then followed them outside, slamming the door shut behind her. 杰克和安妮努力把他们的脚塞进又硬又湿的袜子和靴子里。 奥古斯塔为他们开门，然后跟着他们走出去，砰地一声把门关上了。

Even though it was still rainy and windy, Jack felt much happier outside the Big House than inside it. He and Annie followed Augusta past the gates and out to the lane. Augusta walked stiffly, leading them like a mother duck. 虽然外面还在下雨和刮风，但杰克觉得在大房子外面比在里面开心多了。 他和安妮跟着奥古斯塔走过大门，来到小路上。 奥古斯塔走路时很僵硬，像一只母鸭子一样领着他们走。

“What are we going to do about her?” Annie whispered to Jack. “我们该怎么办呢？”安妮低声对杰克说。

“I don’t know,” whispered Jack. “She doesn’t seem very creative or imaginative to me.” “我不知道，”杰克小声说。 “在我看来，她似乎不太有创造力和想象力。”

“Well, we have to inspire her! Come on!” said Annie. She and Jack hurried to catch up with Augusta. “好吧，我们必须激励她！ 快点！”安妮说。 她和杰克赶紧去追上奥古斯塔。

“Miss Augusta!” said Annie, walking alongside her. “Do you like to sing? Dance? Paint? Play a musical instrument? Anything creative like that?” “奥古斯塔小姐！”安妮边走边说，走在她旁边。 “你喜欢唱歌？跳舞？ 画画？ 演奏乐器？ 做些有创意的事情吗？”

“No,” said Augusta. She sounded angry. Jack figured she must be mad at her mother for kicking them out of the Big House. “不喜欢，”奥古斯塔说。 她听起来很生气。 杰克猜她一定是生她妈妈的气，因为她把他们赶出了大房子。

“Well, what about nature?” said Annie. “那自然呢？”安妮问。

“What about it?” asked Augusta. “怎么了？”奥古斯塔问。

“Walking in the woods?” said Annie. “Trees? Birds? Does anything like that inspire you?” “在树林里散步？”安妮说。 “树木？鸟儿？ 这些能激发你的灵感吗？

“Not anymore. I was close to nature once,” Augusta said. “I used to roam the woods with my younger brothers. They said I was like a robin with the eye of a hawk. I knew where to find the caves of the otters. I knew where to find the nests of wild birds.” “现在不会了。 我以前离自然很近，”奥古斯塔说。 “我以前常和我年幼的弟弟们在树林里玩耍。 他们说我像一只知更鸟，眼睛却像鹰一样锐利。 我知道在哪里能找到水獭的洞穴。 我知道在哪里能找到野鸟的巢穴。”

“That’s so cool,” said Annie. “这太酷了，”安妮说。

“I knew where the deer lay down to sleep,” said Augusta. “I knew the names of every tree: oak, beech, elm, hazel, larch, pine.…” Augusta’s voice grew a little wobbly, as if she might cry. “But I’m not allowed to roam the woods with my brothers anymore. Mother says it’s not proper for a young lady.” “我知道鹿在哪里睡觉，”奥古斯塔说。 “我知道每棵树的名字：橡树、山毛榉、榆树、榛树、冷杉、松树……”奥古斯塔的声音有点颤抖，好像她可能会哭。 “但我不能再和我的兄弟们一起在树林里乱跑了。 妈妈说，一个年轻女士这样做是不合适的。”

“That’s so sad!” said Annie. “这太让人难过了！”安妮说。

“Never mind,” said Augusta, lifting her chin. “Let us not talk about me anymore. Let us try instead to help the two of you. Cook said you were not good for anything. Why would she say that?” “别在意，”奥古斯塔抬起了下巴。 “我们不要再谈论我了。 让我们试着帮助你们两个吧。 厨子说你们什么用处都没有。 她为什么这么说？”

“They asked us if we were good for cleaning chimneys, plucking chickens, or catching rats,” said Annie, “and we said no.” “他们问我们会不会打扫烟囱、捡鸡蛋或者抓老鼠，”安妮说，“我们说不会。”

“Then you must find other ways to make yourselves useful,” said Augusta, “or you will never find your way in the world. Can you shear sheep?” “那么你们必须找到其他让自己有用的方法，”奥古斯塔说，“否则你们永远无法在这个世界上立足。” 你们会剪羊毛吗？

“We’ve never tried it,” said Jack. “我们从来没试过，”杰克说。

“Milk cows? Churn butter? Weave a shawl?” Augusta asked impatiently. “Hunt rabbits with hounds?” “挤牛奶？搅拌黄油？ “织一条披肩？”奥古斯塔不耐烦地问。 “用猎犬去追兔子？”

“Oh, never that!” said Annie. Jack laughed. “哦，千万别那样！”安妮说。杰克笑了。

Augusta frowned. “This is nothing to laugh about. Every day, you must ask yourselves: what am I good for?” 奥古斯塔皱起了眉头。 “这可不是笑的事情。” 每天，你们都要问自己：我有什么用？”

Actually, that was a good question, Jack thought. What was he good for? 其实，这是一个很好的问题，杰克想。 他有什么用呢？

“And I would like to give you a further piece of advice,” said Augusta. “Never make up stories about yourselves that aren’t true.” “我还想给你们再提一条建议，”奥古斯塔说。 “不要编造一些不真实的故事来描述自己。”

“What do you mean?” said Jack. “你是说啥？”杰克说。

“You never acted in a play of Shakespeare’s, did you? Tell the truth now,” said Augusta. “你们从来没演过莎士比亚的戏剧，对吧？ 现在说实话，”奥古斯塔说。

“We did,” said Jack. “My sister was telling the truth. We were both in A Midsummer Night’s Dream.” “我们演过，”杰克说。 “我姐姐说的是实话。” 我们俩都在《仲夏夜之梦》里。”

“We played wood fairies,” said Annie. “We had green costumes, and Jack gave a little speech and I danced and sang.” “我们扮演了木精灵，”安妮说。 “我们有绿色的服装，杰克还做了一个小演讲，我跳舞唱歌了。”

Augusta shook her head. “You poor dears,” she said. “I know you only make up these wild stories because your real lives are so miserable, but—” 奥古斯塔摇了摇头。 “你们这两个可怜的孩子，”她说。 “我知道你只编这些离奇的故事，是因为你真实的生活太悲惨了，但是——”

“Wait a minute. Stop,” said Jack. “What’s wrong with you? Why do you act so snobby?” “等一下。停住，”杰克说。 “你怎么了？ 你为什么表现得这么傲慢？”

“Snobby? Me?” Augusta looked confused. “傲慢？” “我？”奥古斯塔看起来很困惑。

“Jack—” said Annie. “杰克——”安妮说。

“No, I’m serious,” Jack said to Annie. “She thinks she’s better than us.” “不，我是认真的，”杰克对安妮说。 “她以为她比我们厉害。”

“No, I don’t!” Augusta said, stunned. “I’m not like that at all! Each day I walk several miles to town to give cakes and clothes to poor children like yourselves.” “不，我没有！”奥古斯塔震惊地说。 “我一点都不像那样！ 我每天都要走好几英里去镇上，给像你们这样的穷孩子送蛋糕和衣服。

“That’s nice,” said Jack. “But you think you’re better than those poor children, don’t you? You’d never want to be real friends with them, would you?” “那很好，”杰克说。 “但你是不是觉得你比那些穷孩子更优秀？ 你根本不想和他们做真正的朋友，对吗？”

“What you say about me is not true!” Augusta said to Jack. “I love the poor! Why, my favorite friend in all the world is quite poor and has never been to school. Some say she’s even a little cracked in the head, but I love her dearly!” “你对我说的话不真实！”奥古斯塔对杰克说道。 “我爱穷人！ 哦，我在这个世界上最好的朋友非常穷，而且从未上过学。 有些人甚至说她有点疯疯癫癫的，但我非常爱她！”，

“Who’s that?” asked Annie. “那是谁？”安妮问道。

“Mary! Mary Sheridan, our old nursemaid,” said Augusta. “I’ll take you to meet her. Mary will tell you the truth about me! Come along!” “玛丽！ 玛丽·谢里登，我们的老保姆，”奥古斯塔说道。 “我要带你去见她。 玛丽会告诉你关于我的真相！快过来！”，

Augusta ran from the lane and across the muddy grass, her red cape flying in the wind. 奥古斯塔从小路上跑开，穿过泥泞的草地，她的红色斗篷在风中飘扬。

“Um … I don’t think you inspired her,” Annie said. “嗯……我觉得你并没有激励她，”安妮说。

“I know, I’m sorry,” said Jack. “I just couldn’t take her attitude any longer.” “我知道，对不起，”杰克说。 “我只是再也受不了她的态度了。”

“Well, get over it,” said Annie. “We’re supposed to help her, not annoy her.” “那你就好好反省一下吧，”安妮说。 “我们是来帮助她的，不是来烦她的。”

“She was annoying me! ” said Jack. “她烦我！”杰克说。

“Yeah, I know,” said Annie. “Me too. But we’ve got a mission. Come on.” “我知道，”安妮说。 “我也是。 但我们有一个任务。快走吧。”

Jack and Annie followed Augusta across the grass to a small white cottage with a straw roof. Augusta banged on the door, scaring away birds eating crumbs by the front steps. “Mary! Mary! It’s me, Augusta!” she called. 杰克和安妮跟着奥古斯塔穿过草地，来到一幢有稻草屋顶的小白房子前。 奥古斯塔砰砰地敲门，把正在前台阶吃面包屑的鸟儿吓跑了。 “玛丽！玛丽！ 是我，奥古斯塔！”她喊道。

“Come in, my dear,” a voice answered. “进来吧，亲爱的，”一个声音回答道。

Augusta lifted the latch and led Jack and Annie inside. 奥古斯塔抬起门闩，领着杰克和安妮进去了。

Wrapped in a brown shawl, Mary Sheridan was stroking an orange cat by an open fire. She had ragged white hair and bright blue eyes. Her crooked smile revealed a few missing teeth. 玛丽·谢里登裹着一条棕色的披肩，正靠在敞开的火炉旁抚摸着一只橘猫。 她有一头凌乱的白发和明亮的蓝眼睛。 她歪着嘴笑，露出几颗缺失的牙齿。

“One and twenty welcomes on this wonderful winter day!” said the old woman. “二十一个欢迎，在这个美妙的冬日里！”老妇人说道。
