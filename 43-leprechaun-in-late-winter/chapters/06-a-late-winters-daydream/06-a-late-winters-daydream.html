6. A Late-Winter’s Daydream 6. 冬末的白日梦

Jack sighed. Their mission really seemed hopeless now. 杰克叹了口气。 现在，他们的任务看起来真的毫无希望了。

“We’d better go find her,” said Annie. “我们最好去找到她，”安妮说。

“She won’t go far,” said Mary. “My poor Augusta … she has a fine mind and a brave heart. But she is so unhappy.” “她不会走太远，”玛丽说。 “可怜的奥古斯塔……她有聪明的头脑和勇敢的心。 但她现在非常不开心。”

“Why is she so unhappy, Mary?” asked Annie. “玛丽，她为什么这么不开心？”安妮问。

“Yeah, what is her problem?” said Jack. “是啊，她到底怎么了？”杰克说。

“More than any of her brothers and sisters, Miss Augusta loved my stories,” said Mary. “Remembered every one of them, she did.” “比起她的所有兄弟姐妹，奥古斯塔小姐最喜爱我的故事，”玛丽说。 “她记得每一个故事，真的。”

“Really?” said Jack. “真的吗？”杰克说。

“Yes, she would repeat them back to me, word for word,” said Mary. “是的，她会一字不差地把故事重复给我听，”玛丽说。

“That’s amazing,” said Annie. “太棒了，”安妮说。

“She loved the stories so much that she grew desperate to see the Shee for herself,” said Mary. “At night she would carry a lantern across the fields, calling for them. By day, she poked and prodded every part of the farm. Why, she even used a magnifying glass, scouring the earth for tiny footprints! But I’m afraid she never found them.” “她那么爱这些故事，以至于她非常渴望亲眼见到希伊，”玛丽说。 “晚上，她会提着灯笼穿过田野，呼唤他们。 白天，她则会仔细检查农场的每一个角落。 咦，她竟然用放大镜，仔细地在地面寻找微小的脚印！ 但恐怕她从来没有找到它们。

“Why?” said Annie. “为什么？”安妮说。

Mary sighed. “Because she looked for them with her head and not her heart,” she said. “Eventually she gave up and stopped searching. She didn’t even want to hear the stories anymore. She’s been a dutiful—but sorrowful—child ever since.” 玛丽叹了口气。 “因为她用的是脑袋，而不是用心，”她说。 “最后她放弃了，不再继续寻找。 她甚至不想再听那些故事了。 从那以后，她一直是个尽责但悲伤的孩子。

“That’s terrible,” said Annie. “What can we do to help her?” “这太糟糕了，”安妮说。 “我们能做些什么来帮助她呢？”

“There is only one thing you can do,” said Mary. “你只能做一件事，”玛丽说。

“What?” breathed Jack. “什么？”杰克屏住呼吸问。

Mary leaned forward in her chair. Her blue eyes seemed to stare right through Jack and Annie. “You must show her the magic,” she said. 玛丽向前倾了倾身子。 她蓝色的眼睛似乎直直地盯着杰克和安妮。 “你必须让她看到魔法，”她说。

What? thought Jack. Does Mary know about the magic tree house? “ What do you mean?” he asked. 什么？杰克心想。 玛丽知道那座有魔法的树屋吗？ “你是什么意思？”他问。

“I know that you children are like me—you see things that others don’t,” said Mary. “Help Augusta see them, too. Help her find the magic in the fields and in the forest.” “我知道你们这些孩子和我一样——你们能看到别人看不到的东西，”玛丽说。 “帮奥古斯塔也看到它们吧。” 帮她找到田野和森林里的魔法。

For a moment, Jack and Annie didn’t say anything. The wind blew through the open door. The fire crackled. 有一会儿，杰克和安妮什么也没说。 风吹进了敞开的门。 火堆噼啪作响。

Then Annie took a deep breath. “Okay,” she said. “We know exactly what to do.” 然后安妮深吸了一口气。 “好，”她说。 “我们知道该怎么做。”

“We do?” said Jack. “我们真的知道吗？”杰克说。

“Yep, we’ll talk about it outside,” said Annie. “Thanks, Mary. We’ll find Augusta and take care of everything.” “对，我们到外面去说，”安妮说。 “谢谢，玛丽。 我们会找到奥古斯塔，把一切都处理好。”

Jack and Annie stood up to go. 杰克和安妮站起来准备离开。

“One and twenty fare-thee-wells on this wonderful winter day,” said Mary. “在这美好的冬日里，我向你道别二十一次，”玛丽说道。

“One and twenty to you, too,” said Jack. Then he and Annie left the cozy cottage, scattering the winter birds by the front door. “我也向你道别二十一次，”杰克说。 然后他和安妮离开了温暖的小屋，走到门口时惊飞了冬天的鸟儿。

It had warmed up a little outside. The rain had stopped, but fog hung heavily over the sheep fields. The ground was soggy with mud. 外面的温度稍微上升了一点。 雨停了，但羊群的田野上弥漫着浓重的雾气。 地面湿漉漉的，满是泥巴。

Jack could barely make out Augusta’s red cape through the fog. She was across the lane, sitting on a stone wall at the edge of the sheep meadow. “Mary was right, she didn’t go far,” Jack said. “So how do we show her the magic, Annie?” 杰克在雾中勉强看清了奥古斯塔红色的斗篷。 她正在小路对面，坐在羊群草地边缘的石墙上。 “玛丽说得对，她没走远，”杰克说。 “那我们怎么向安妮展示魔法呢？”

“Easy,” said Annie. “We play our magic whistle.” “容易，”安妮说。 “我们来吹我们的魔法口哨。”

“No, we can’t do that,” said Jack. “We’re supposed to save the whistle for a moment of great danger.” “不，我们不能那样做，”杰克说。 “我们要留着哨子，等到遇到大危险的时候再用。”

“That moment is now! ” said Annie. “Come on.” “现在就是那个时刻！”安妮说。 “快点。”

“Hold on,” said Jack. “What great danger are we facing right now?” “抓紧了，”杰克说。 “我们现在正面对着什么大危险？”

“Not us. Augusta,” said Annie. “She faces the great danger of losing all hope and happiness and being bored and sad for the rest of her life—and never being inspired and never sharing her gifts with the world! It’s almost too late already!” “不是我们。” “奥古斯塔，”安妮说。 “她正面临着巨大的危险：失去所有的希望和快乐，余生都感到无聊和悲伤，永远无法受到启发，也永远无法与世界分享她的天赋！ 现在几乎已经太晚了！”

“Okay, okay,” said Jack. “But are we just going to go up to her and start blowing the whistle and singing? That seems pretty weird.” “好吧，好吧，”杰克说。 “但是我们就这样直接走到她面前，开始吹哨子、唱歌吗？ 这看起来有点奇怪。”

“Hmm … yeah, it does,” said Annie. “嗯……是啊，”安妮说。

“How about this?” said Jack. “We’ll tell Augusta that we want to put on a play for her.” “那我们这样怎么样？”杰克说。 “我们告诉奥古斯塔，我们想为她演一出戏。”

“A play?” said Annie. “演一出戏？”安妮说。

“Yep,” said Jack. “We can tell her we want to prove that we weren’t lying, that we really were in a play by Shakespeare.” “对，”杰克说。 “我们可以告诉她，我们想证明我们没有撒谎，我们真的在演一出莎士比亚的戏。”

“Oh, cool,” said Annie. “Then what?” “哦，太酷了，”安妮说。 “那接下来呢？”

“We play the magic whistle,” said Jack. “We sing about the Shee. We make them appear—like in Mary’s story—galloping and thundering! Augusta sees them. She gets inspired. Our mission’s done.” “我们吹魔法口哨，”杰克说。 “我们唱关于希伊的歌。 我们让它们出现——就像玛丽的故事里那样——奔跑着，轰隆隆地！ 奥古斯塔看到了它们。 她受到启发。 我们的任务完成了。

“Perfect!” said Annie. “Let’s go!” “太棒了！”安妮说。 “我们走吧！”

Jack and Annie hurried across the lane to the stone wall. “Excuse us, Miss Augusta,” said Annie. “We just had a really great idea! Want to hear it?” 杰克和安妮赶紧穿过小路，跑到石墙那里。 “打扰一下，奥古斯塔小姐，”安妮说道。 “我们刚想出了一个特别棒的主意！想听听吗？”

Augusta didn’t answer. She kept staring at the ground. 奥古斯塔没有回答。 她一直盯着地面。

“How would you like to see a play?” said Jack. “你想看一场戏剧吗？”杰克说。

Augusta looked up. “A play?” she said. 奥古斯塔抬起头。 “演一出戏？”她说道。

“We want to put on our own play for you,” said Annie. “我们想为我们表演一出属于自己的戏，”安妮说道。

“Why?” said Augusta. “为什么？”奥古斯塔说道。

“Because it’s really good,” said Jack. “And maybe it will prove to you that we really were in a play by Shakespeare.” “因为这真的很好看，”杰克说道。 “也许它能证明我们真的在看一出莎士比亚的戏。”

Augusta looked doubtful. 奥古斯塔看起来很怀疑。

“Come on, you’ll love it,” said Annie. “Do you know a quiet spot where no one can bother us?” “来吧，你会喜欢的，”安妮说道。 “你知道一个安静的地方，没人会打扰我们吗？”

Augusta bit her lip and looked around. Then she stood up. “All right,” she said. “The river near the old forest. I used to go there with my brothers.” 奥古斯塔咬了咬嘴唇，四处张望。 然后她站了起来。 “好吧，”她说。 “在老森林附近的那条河。 我以前常和我的兄弟们一起去那里。”

“Great!” said Annie. “太棒了！”安妮说。

Annie and Jack followed Augusta through the rain-soaked, misty meadow. They walked past grazing sheep, then down a slope toward a wide, rushing river. The river separated the sheep’s meadow from an old forest. Jack could barely see the trees through the ghostly fog. 安妮和杰克跟着奥古斯塔穿过被雨水打湿的、雾气弥漫的草地。 他们走过吃草的绵羊，然后沿着斜坡走向一条宽阔湍急的河流。 这条河把绵羊的草地和一片老森林分隔开了。 杰克几乎看不清树，因为鬼魅般的雾气遮住了视线。

Augusta stopped on a low ridge above the riverbank, near some large rocks. “Here,” she said. 奥古斯塔停在河岸上方的一处低矮山脊上，靠近一些大石头的地方。 “这里，”她说。

“Good, those rocks can be our stage,” said Jack. “好，这些石头可以当我们的舞台，”杰克说。

Jack and Annie climbed the pile of small boulders and stood on a large, flat rock. 杰克和安妮爬上了那堆小石头，站在了一块又大又平的石头上。

“Okay,” said Annie. “The name of this play is A Late-Winter’s Daydream.” “好吧，”安妮说。 “这部剧的名字叫《冬末的梦幻》。”

Not bad, thought Jack. 还不错，杰克心想。

“And this is what’s going to happen,” said Annie. “Jack will be the narrator. I’ll play the Irish whistle. And Jack will sing a song that tells the story.” “接下来会发生的事是这样的，”安妮说。 “杰克会当叙述者。 我会吹爱尔兰口哨。 杰克会唱一首讲述故事的歌。”

“What?” said Jack. “Excuse us a minute, Augusta.” Jack turned to Annie. “Why me sing?” he whispered. “Why not I play and you sing?” “什么？”杰克说。 “打扰一下，奥古斯塔。”杰克转向安妮。 “为什么是我唱歌？”他小声说。 “为什么不是我玩，你来唱歌？”

“No, I want to play,” said Annie. “You took notes at Mary’s, right? So just say a few words to describe the scene. Then use your notes about the Shee to make up a song. You can do that, can’t you?” “不，我想玩，”安妮说。 “你在玛丽家做笔记，对吧？ 所以只说几句话来描述眼前的场景。 然后用你关于希的笔记编一首歌。 你能做到，对吧？”

“I guess …,” said Jack. “我想……，”杰克说。

“Cool,” said Annie. “Give me the whistle.” “太酷了，”安妮说。 “把口哨给我。”

Jack reached into his pockets and pulled out his notebook and the Irish whistle. He gave the whistle to Annie. 杰克把手伸进口袋，拿出他的笔记本和爱尔兰口哨。 他把口哨给了安妮。

“Sorry, Miss Augusta,” said Annie. “We’re almost ready.” She whispered directions to Jack. “Okay. Say your introduction. I’ll start to play. Then you’ll start to sing. Then—” “对不起，奥古斯塔小姐，”安妮说。 “我们马上就要准备好了。”她小声对杰克说方向。 “好的。 说你的介绍。 我会开始演奏。 然后你就开始唱歌。然后—”

“I’ve got it,” said Jack. “Let’s just start.” “我明白了，”杰克说。 “我们开始吧。”

Jack and Annie turned to face Augusta. Jack cleared his throat. Then he spoke in a loud voice: 杰克和安妮转身面对奥古斯塔。 杰克清了清嗓子。 然后他大声说道：

All is still in an old forest—

until music sounds from a hidden world.… 一片古老的森林里一片寂静——直到从一个隐藏的世界传来音乐声。…

Jack nodded to Annie. 杰克朝安妮点了点头。

Annie raised the magic Irish whistle to her lips and began to play. 安妮把神奇的爱尔兰口哨举到嘴边，开始吹奏。

Strange, sweet music came from the whistle. The music was both sad and happy. It was full of beauty and hope, pain and sorrow. Like the fog over the river, the music seemed to blend everything together. 奇怪而甜美的音乐从口哨中传来。 音乐既悲伤又快乐。 它充满了美丽和希望，痛苦和悲伤。 就像河上的雾气一样，音乐似乎把一切融合在了一起。

For a moment, the whistle music was so powerful that Jack couldn’t sing. He felt like crying and laughing at the same time. Finally he looked down at his notes and began to sing: 有一瞬间，哨子发出的音乐太强大了，杰克都唱不出来了。 他既想哭又想笑。 最后，他低头看了看自己的笔记，开始唱起来：

In the spinning of light—

in a cloud, like a dream,

a bridge appeared over

a wide, flowing stream. 在光的旋转中——像云朵，像梦境，一座桥出现在一条宽广流淌的溪流上。

Jack surprised himself. He thought his words for the song sounded pretty good. 杰克自己都吓了一跳。 他觉得这首歌的歌词听起来还不错。

A long, fluttering high note burst from Annie’s whistle. Bright, dancing light flowed across the river. The light arched through the fog toward Jack, Annie, and Augusta. 安妮的哨子发出了一串长长的、飘忽的高音。 明亮的、跳动的光流过河面。 光穿过雾气，朝杰克、安妮和奥古斯塔飞去。

Augusta gasped. Jack looked back down at his notes and sang: 奥古斯塔惊叫了一声。 杰克又低头看了看自己的笔记，开始唱起来：

Some came on horses,

some came with wings.

From an enchanted world

little queens, little kings! 有些骑着马，有些长着翅膀。来自一个神奇世界的小女王、小国王！

The wind began to blow. Jack looked up. Leaves and grass and twigs were flying everywhere. 风开始吹起来了。杰克抬头看了看。 树叶、草和树枝到处飞舞。

The whistle music grew wilder. 口哨声变得越来越狂野。

Thundering and rumbling sounds came from the old forest. A herd of very small white horses galloped out of the mist. On their backs were proud and lovely riders—men in gold helmets, women with long hair floating on the wind. Their capes and gowns were the colors of nature—the pale rose of a spring dawn, the green of summer hummingbirds, the blue of winter twilight, and the gold of autumn oak leaves. 雷声和轰隆声从老森林里传来。 一群非常小的白色马从雾中奔跑出来。 它们的背上坐着骄傲又美丽的骑手——戴着金色头盔的男人，头发长长的女人，头发在风中飘扬。 他们的披风和长袍是大自然的颜色——春天黎明时淡淡的玫瑰色，夏天蜂鸟的绿色，冬天黄昏时的蓝色，还有秋天橡树叶的金色。

“The Shee!” cried Augusta. “是谢伊！”奥古斯塔喊道。
