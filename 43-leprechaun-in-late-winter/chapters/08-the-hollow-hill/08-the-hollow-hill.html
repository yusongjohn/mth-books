8. The Hollow Hill 8. 空心小山

The leprechaun scrambled down the steep bank of the river. 小精灵急忙沿着河岸陡峭的斜坡爬下来。

Jack started to follow, but Annie grabbed him. “Jack, the whistle won’t work for Willy!” she said. 杰克开始跟着，但安妮抓住了他。 “杰克，哨子对威尔利没用！”她说。

“I know,” said Jack. “We’ll worry about that later. Right now, we just have to save Augusta! Come on!” “我知道，”杰克说。 “我们以后再担心这个吧。” 现在，我们得赶紧救奥古斯塔！快点！”

Jack and Annie hurried after Willy to the wide, rushing river. 杰克和安妮赶紧跟着威尔跑到那条宽阔湍急的河边上。

“How do we get across?” Jack asked. “我们怎么过河呢？”杰克问。

“Follow me along the river,” said Willy, “and I’ll show you. Step lightly.” “沿着河走，”威尔说，“我会带你们走。要轻一点踩。”

Willy skipped ahead of them along the riverbank. Jack and Annie followed. Jack tried to step lightly, but it was impossible. Slopping through the mud, his boots filled with ooze and made squishing sounds with every step. 威尔蹦蹦跳跳地沿着河岸往前走。 杰克和安妮跟了上去。 杰克试着轻手轻脚地走，但根本不可能。 他踩着泥浆走过去，靴子里灌满了黏糊糊的泥，每走一步都发出“咕啾咕啾”的声音。

The river grew narrower and narrower, twisting and turning like a snake. The mist grew thicker, until Jack could hardly see a thing. He bumped into Willy, almost knocking him over. 河水越来越窄，弯弯曲曲地像一条蛇。 雾越来越浓，杰克几乎什么也看不见了。 他撞上了威尔利，差点把他撞倒。

“Careful, laddie!” said the leprechaun. “小心点，小家伙！”小精灵说。

“Sorry,” said Jack. “对不起，”杰克说。

“All right,” said Willy. “Here’s where we cross. Hop from stone to stone after me.” Willy disappeared into the mist, crossing the river. Annie followed him. “好吧，”威尔利说。 “我们在这里过河。 跟着我，从石头跳到石头上。”威尔利消失在雾中，过河去了。 安妮跟着他。

Jack started across, too. He tried to hop carefully from stone to stone, but the stones were slippery. On his third hop, his boot slid off a mossy rock, and he splashed into the freezing water! 杰克也开始过河了。 他试着小心地从一块石头跳到另一块石头上，但石头很滑。 第三次跳的时候，他的靴子从一块长满青苔的石头上滑了下去，他一下子掉进了冰冷的水里！

“Jack, are you okay?” Annie called in a loud whisper. “杰克，你没事吧？”安妮压低声音大声喊道。

“Yeah, yeah, I’m fine!” Jack said. He scrambled up. His clothes were soaked and heavy. He really was miserable now. He waded to the other side of the river and joined Annie and Willy. “没事，没事，我很好！”杰克说。他赶紧爬了上来。 他的衣服全都湿透了，沉甸甸的。 现在他真的很难过了。 他蹚过河，走到对岸，加入了安妮和威尔利。

“Ah, you fell in,” said Willy. “That happens sometimes. Come with me now, into the forest.” “啊，你掉进河里了，”威尔利说。 “这种事情有时会发生。 现在跟我来，去森林里。”

Jack, Annie, and Willy walked under the trees. They passed old oaks and maples that creaked in the wind. Jack shivered in his soaking wet clothes. 杰克、安妮和威尔利在树下走着。 他们经过了老橡树和枫树，风一吹，树就发出嘎吱嘎吱的声音。 杰克穿着湿透的衣服，忍不住打了个哆嗦。

A big black crow called from a high, bare branch. 一只大黑乌鸦从高高的光秃秃的树枝上叫了起来。

Willy jumped, then laughed. “Ah, ’tis probably Patrick Daly,” he said. “Rumor is the Shee changed him into a crow for stealing a pair of the High Queen’s shoes.” 威利吓了一跳，然后笑了起来。 “啊，这大概是帕特里克·达利，”他说。 “传闻说，因为偷了高王后的一双鞋子，Shee把他变成了一只乌鸦。”

“Changed him into a crow?” said Jack. “变成了一只乌鸦？”杰克说。

Willy nodded. “One of the Shee’s favorite tricks,” he said, “changing humans who displease them into animals.” 威利点了点头。 “这是Shee最喜欢用的把戏之一，”他说，“把让他们不高兴的人类变成动物。”

“Oh,” said Jack. “哦，”杰克说。

A white rabbit scampered through the forest. “That bunny over there is no doubt the former Mrs. Shan McCartie. She was rude to the High King,” said Willy. 一只白兔子在森林里蹦蹦跳跳地跑过。 “那边那只兔子毫无疑问就是以前的山·麦卡蒂夫人。 她对高王很不礼貌，”威尔利说。

“Rude to the High King?” said Jack. He was starting to feel like he was trapped in a nightmare. “对高王不礼貌？”杰克说。 他开始觉得自己好像被困在了一个噩梦里。

“The Shee cannot bear the rudeness of humans,” said Willy. “Look there.” He pointed to a fawn peeking out from behind a fir tree. “谢伊族人无法忍受人类的不礼貌，”威尔利说。 “看那儿。”他指着一只小鹿，它正从一棵冷杉树后探出头来。

“Aww, it’s so cute,” said Annie. “啊，它太可爱了，”安妮说。

“Cute? Maybe. Maybe not,” said Willy. “Could easily be old John Foley. I heard he was changed just for being a grouch! Come along.” “可爱？也许吧。 也许不是，”威尔利说。 “很可能是老约翰·弗利。” 我听说他只是因为脾气坏就被改变了！快走吧。”

Willy led Jack and Annie through the forest until they came to a tangle of briars and brambles. “The hollow hill of the Shee lies just beyond this thicket,” he said to them. “Do you still want to go and find your friend?” 威利带着杰克和安妮穿过森林，直到他们来到一片荆棘丛生的地方。 “通往希伊空山的路就在那片灌木丛后面，”他对他们说。 “你们还想去找你的朋友吗？”

“Yes,” Jack and Annie both whispered. “是的，”杰克和安妮都小声说。

“Then off you go, and good luck to you,” said Willy, tipping his hat. “那你们就出发吧，祝你们好运，”威尔利说着，摘下帽子鞠了一躬。

“What? Aren’t you coming with us?” said Jack. “什么？ “你不是要和我们一起去吗？”杰克说。

“Oh, my, no,” said Willy. “The Shee would be furious if they knew I’d shown humans the path to their secret hiding place. And I certainly don’t want to live the rest of my life as a weasel.” “哦，不，我不去，”威尔利说。 “如果他们知道我带人类去了他们的秘密藏身之处，那些希就会非常生气。” “我当然不想一辈子都当一只松鼠。”

“But won’t they be angry at us for finding their secret hiding place?” said Jack. “但他们不会因为我们找到了他们的秘密藏身之处而生我们的气吗？”杰克说。

“Possibly,” said Willy. “Here’s what I recommend you do: politely tell the High King and the High Queen that you’ve just come looking for your dear friend to take her back to her loving family. The Shee place very great value on friendship and family.” “有可能，”威尔利说。 “我建议你们这么做：礼貌地告诉高国王和高王后，你们刚刚来找你们亲爱的朋友，把她带回她亲爱的家人那里。” “Shee族非常重视友谊和家庭。”

“Okay,” said Jack. “Friendship and family …” “好的，”杰克说。 “友谊和家庭……”

“And remember: be simple, direct, and honest at all times,” said Willy. “记住：任何时候都要简单、直接、诚实，”威尔利说。

“Simple, direct, honest,” repeated Jack. “简单、直接、诚实，”杰克重复道。

“And polite, that’s the most important—very, very polite,” said Willy. “而且要讲礼貌，这是最重要的——非常、非常讲礼貌，”威尔利说。

“Very polite,” said Annie. “Got it.” “非常讲礼貌，”安妮说。 “明白了。”

“Another thing,” said Willy. “In the world of the Shee, the old tales still live. So do not be afraid if you see odd sights floating about. They’re just bits and pieces of the old stories. Now go. Be simple, direct, honest, and polite, and save your dear friend before she’s lost forever.” “还有件事，”威尔利说。 “在希伊的世界里，古老的故事仍然活着。 如果你看到一些奇怪的东西飘来飘去，不要害怕。 它们只是古老故事中的一小部分。现在去吧。 要简单、直接、诚实、有礼貌，救出你亲爱的朋友，否则她将永远失去。”

“Thanks, Willy,” said Annie. “谢谢你，威尔利，”安妮说。

“Best of luck,” said Willy. “祝你好运，”威尔利说。

“See you later,” said Jack. “再见了，”杰克说。

“Aye, I’ll be waiting for you,” said the leprechaun. “啊，我会等你的，”小精灵说。

Jack and Annie crouched down and started through the thicket. Thorns and briars pulled at Jack’s wet coat. They scratched his hands and got caught in his hair. 杰克和安妮蹲下身子，开始穿过灌木丛。 荆棘和刺藤拽着杰克湿漉漉的外套。 它们划伤了他的手，还缠在了他的头发里。

Jack battled his way through the tangled brush until he caught up with Annie. They both pushed their way out of the thicket and stepped into a glade. 杰克奋力穿过缠绕的灌木，直到追上安妮。 他们一起推开灌木丛，走进了一片空地。

“Wow, it’s like Mary said,” whispered Annie. “It is like summer here.” “哇，就像玛丽说的一样，”安妮小声说。 “这里就像夏天一样。”

No rain fell. No wind blew. Warm sunshine shone on the emerald-green glade. In the middle of the glade was a large grassy mound. At the foot of the mound was a small doorway framed by stones. 没有下雨，也没有风吹。 温暖的阳光照在翡翠色的空地上。 空地的中央有一个大大的草地土丘。 土丘脚下有一个小门，门框由石头围成。

“That must be it—the hollow hill,” said Jack, “the secret home of the Shee.” “那一定是它——空心山丘，”杰克说，“那是希伊的秘密家园。”

“And that must be someone from an old story,” whispered Annie. She pointed to a woman floating above the green mound. “那一定是来自一个古老故事里的人，”安妮小声说。 她指着空地上方漂浮着的一位女士。

The woman wore a wreath of flowers on her head and carried a branch with silver apples. She vanished in the sunny haze. 这位女士头上戴着花环，手里拿着一根挂着银苹果的树枝。 她消失在阳光的雾气中。

“Whoa,” whispered Jack. “哇，”杰克小声说。

A small sailing ship then appeared in the air. It had white sails and flags flying. The ship, too, vanished. Then an old woman spinning at a spinning wheel appeared … then a wispy dragon … then a knight with a sword. The parade of images faded into the sunshine like wisps of smoke. 然后一艘小帆船出现在空中。 它有白色的帆和飘扬的旗帜。 船也消失了。 接着，一个在纺车旁纺纱的老妇人出现了……接着是一条薄雾般的龙……接着是一个拿着剑的骑士。 这些画面像烟雾一样在阳光中渐渐淡去。

“Whew,” said Jack. “哇，”杰克说。

“Listen,” said Annie. “听，”安妮说。

The sound of drumming was coming from the doorway of the grassy mound. “Come on, let’s look inside,” said Annie. 鼓声是从草丘门口传来的。 “我们去看看里面吧，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie snuck close to the small doorway. It was no higher than Jack’s waist. He and Annie knelt down and peeked inside. 杰克和安妮悄悄靠近小门口。 门口的高度 barely 超过杰克的腰部。 他和安妮蹲下身子，往里面 peeked（窥视）了一下。

The hollow hill was filled with a pale-green light. Very small dancers, none more than eight inches tall, were bathed in the light. As drummers pounded tiny drums, the dancers danced together in rows. They held their arms straight by their sides and kicked their legs and turned around and around to the rhythm of the drums. 这个空心的小山丘里充满了淡绿色的光。 非常小的舞者们，没有一个超过八英寸高，被这光笼罩着。 当鼓手们敲着小鼓时，舞者们排着队一起跳舞。 他们把手臂直直地放在身体两侧，踢着腿，围着鼓声的节奏转圈圈。

A small shining couple sat on high golden chairs watching the dancers. They wore golden crowns. 一对小小的闪亮的人坐在高高的金色椅子上，看着舞者们。 他们戴着金色的王冠。

“They must be the High King and the High Queen,” whispered Annie. “他们一定是高王和高后，”安妮小声说。

Jack and Annie watched until the rows of dancers parted. Then they could see a third person watching the dance. She was no taller than the dancers. Draped around her shoulders was a red cape. 杰克和安妮一直看着，直到舞者们排成的队伍分开。 然后他们看见第三个人正在看着这场舞蹈。 她和那些舞者一样矮矮的。 一条红色的披风披在她的肩膀上。

“It’s Augusta!” said Annie. “是奥古斯塔！”安妮说。
