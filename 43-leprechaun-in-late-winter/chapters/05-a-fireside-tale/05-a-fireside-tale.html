5. A Fireside Tale 5. 火炉边的故事

“Hello, Mary!” said Augusta. She kissed the old woman on her wrinkled cheek. “你好，玛丽！”奥古斯塔说。 她亲了亲这位老妇人布满皱纹的脸颊。

With the firelight on her face, Mary seemed to glow. Her warm, snug cottage was the opposite of the Big House. It smelled of damp leaves and moss, bread and chocolate. Firelight danced on the earthen floor and stone walls. Rain dripped through the roof, pinging into a couple of tin buckets. 火光映在玛丽脸上，她看起来闪闪发亮。 她温暖舒适的 cottage 与大房子完全相反。 这里散发着潮湿的树叶、青苔、面包和巧克力的香味。 火光在泥土地板和石墙上跳动。 雨水从屋顶滴落，叮叮当当地掉进两个铁桶里。

“And who do we have here, Miss Augusta?” Mary asked. “那么，我们这儿有谁呢，奥古斯塔小姐？”玛丽问道。

“Two poor children from town,” said Augusta. “I want you to tell them about me—how I truly love the poor and try to help them.” “两个来自镇上的穷孩子，”奥古斯塔说。 “我想让你告诉他们关于我的事——我是多么真正地爱穷人，努力帮助他们。”

Mary smiled. “Please, sit down first,” she said. 玛丽微笑了。 “请先坐下，”她说。

Jack, Annie, and Augusta sat down on three rickety wooden chairs. 杰克、安妮和奥古斯塔坐在三条摇摇晃晃的木椅上。

“Would you children like some hot cocoa?” Mary asked. “孩子们，你们想要一些热可可吗？”，玛丽问。

Jack and Annie nodded eagerly. 杰克和安妮兴奋地点头。

“Yes, please, Mary,” said Augusta. “But would you tell them—” “是的，请给我一些，玛丽，”奥古斯塔说。 “但是你能告诉他们——”

“Yes, I will tell them all about you,” said Mary. She picked up a pot sitting on the hearth. She poured steaming cocoa into three mugs and handed them to Jack, Annie, and Augusta. “是的，我会告诉他们关于你的所有事情，”玛丽说。 她拿起放在壁炉上的一个壶。 她把冒着热气的可可倒进三个马克杯里，然后递给了杰克、安妮和奥古斯塔。

The cocoa smelled delicious. Jack took a sip and licked his lips. “Yum,” he said. His insides felt warm for the first time all day. 可可闻起来真香。 杰克喝了一口，舔了舔嘴唇。 “真好吃，”他说。 他第一次觉得肚子里面暖暖的。

“Now, Mary?” asked Augusta. “现在，玛丽？”奥古斯塔问。

“In time, my child,” Mary said to Augusta. “Tell me, what have you been doing today?” “慢慢来，我的孩子，”玛丽对奥古斯塔说。 “告诉我，你今天做了什么？”

“I delivered cakes in town,” Augusta said proudly. “Then I found these poor children in our kitchen looking for work. I’ve tried to help them. But they say I am snobby. I brought them here so you could tell them the truth about me.” “我在镇上送蛋糕，”奥古斯塔骄傲地说。 “然后我在我们厨房找到了这些可怜的孩子，他们在找活干。 我试着帮助他们。 但他们说我是个傲慢的人。 我带他们来这里，是让你告诉他们我真正的样子。

“Ah, I see, Miss Augusta. How did you try to help them?” said Mary. “啊，我明白了，奥古斯塔小姐。 玛丽问：‘你是怎么试着帮助他们的？’

“I’ve tried to discover what they are good for,” said Augusta. “But it appears they are good for nothing.” “我试着发现他们有什么用处，”奥古斯塔说。 “但看起来他们什么用处也没有。”

“Really?” Mary fixed her twinkling eyes on Jack and Annie. “Well, let us start with this, children: tell me something you like to do. No, wait—what do you love to do?” “真的吗？”玛丽用闪亮的眼睛盯着杰克和安妮。 “好吧，孩子们，让我们从这里开始：告诉我你们喜欢做的事情。 不，等等——你们最爱做的事情是什么？”

“Uh … well, I love to read,” said Jack. “呃……好吧，我喜欢读书，”杰克说。

“And write,” said Annie. “而且要写下来，”安妮说。

“Read and write?” said Augusta. “I don’t think so.” “读和写？”奥古斯塔说。 “我想不是吧。”

Mary ignored Augusta and kept looking at Jack and Annie. “What do you like to read and write?” asked Mary. 玛丽无视了奥古斯塔，继续看着杰克和安妮。 “你们喜欢读什么、写什么？”玛丽问。

“Facts mostly,” said Jack. “True stories.” “大多是事实，”杰克说。 “真实的故事。”

“Jack writes facts down all the time,” said Annie. “杰克一直都在写事实，”安妮说。

“Honestly, Mary,” said Augusta. “Soon they’ll be telling you that they are actors, too—and have performed in a play by William Shakespeare. Can we talk about me now?” “说实话，玛丽，”奥古斯塔说。 “很快他们就会告诉你，他们也是演员，还演过威廉·莎士比亚的戏剧。” 我们现在能谈谈我吗？

“Actually, that’s true, ” Jack said to Mary. “We were in A Midsummer Night’s Dream. Annie and I were fairies. I had stage fright, but Will—” “其实，这是真的，”杰克对玛丽说。 “我们当时在《仲夏夜之梦》里。 安妮和我都是仙女。 我有点紧张，但威尔——

“William Shakespeare himself, ” said Annie. “威廉·莎士比亚本人，”安妮说。

“He helped me get over it,” said Jack. He looked straight at Augusta. “他帮助我走出来了，”杰克说。 他直直地盯着奥古斯塔看。

Augusta rolled her eyes. 奥古斯塔翻了翻白眼。

“Will was so nice,” said Annie. “威尔真是太好了，”安妮说。

“And smart,” said Jack. “而且很聪明，”杰克说。

“Of course he was!” said Mary. “You can tell that from his stories.” “当然啦！”玛丽说。 “你可以从他的故事里看出来。”

“Oh, please stop. Don’t tell Mary those ridiculous things!” Augusta said. “What about me, Mary?” “哦，求你别说了。 别告诉玛丽那些荒唐的话！”奥古斯塔说。 “那我呢，玛丽？”

“Wait, child, I have a question for them,” Mary said. She leaned forward and spoke in a whisper. “Where is summer? Can you answer me that?” “等等，孩子，我有个问题要问他们，”玛丽说。 她向前倾身，小声说道。 “夏天去哪儿了？ 你能告诉我吗？”

Mary’s question doesn’t make sense, Jack thought. 玛丽的问题让杰克觉得不太对劲。

“I don’t know. Do you know where summer is, Mary?” asked Annie. “我不知道。 玛丽，你知道夏天在哪里吗？”安妮问。

“Summer is hiding with the Shee!” said Mary, laughing. “夏天正和希偷偷藏起来了！”玛丽笑着说。

“The Shee? What’s that?” said Annie. “希？ 那是什么？”安妮说。

“Surely, you must know the Shee,” said Mary. “That’s what we Irish call our fairies. In the winter, the Shee steal all the warmth and sunshine, leaving us to suffer with the cold and rain!” “你肯定知道希的，”玛丽说。 “那是我们爱尔兰人叫的精灵。 冬天的时候，希会偷走所有的温暖和阳光，让我们只能在寒冷和雨水中受苦！”

Annie laughed, too. 安妮也笑了。

“So you’ve played the parts of fairies in a play by Shakespeare?” said Mary. “They’re just like our Shee. Have you seen the Shee here in Ireland?” “你们在莎士比亚的戏剧里扮演过仙女吗？”玛丽说。 “她们就像我们的谢伊。 你在这里的爱尔兰见过谢伊吗？

“Mary!” Augusta said impatiently. “玛丽！”奥古斯塔不耐烦地说。

“Not yet,” said Annie. “还没呢，”安妮说。

“That’s a shame,” said Mary. “I have seen them. This is a true story”—she looked at Jack and smiled—“with facts. You might want to write them down.” “那太遗憾了，”玛丽说。 “我见过她们。 这是一个真实的故事”—她看着杰克，微笑着说—“有事实依据的。 你可能想把它们记下来。”

“Oh. Sure,” said Jack. He pulled his notebook and pencil out of his pocket. Augusta looked surprised. “哦，当然，”杰克说。 他从口袋里拿出笔记本和铅笔。 奥古斯塔看起来很惊讶。

Mary leaned close to Jack and Annie again. Her eyes were shining and her voice was hushed. “One day long ago, a lonely young girl took a walk in an old forest,” said Mary. “All was still, until joyful music began coming from a hidden world.…” 玛丽又靠近杰克和安妮。 她的眼睛闪着光，声音很轻。 “很久以前的一天，一个孤独的小女孩在一片古老的森林里散步，”玛丽说。 “一切都很安静，直到快乐的音乐从一个隐藏的世界传来……”

Jack loved Mary’s way of telling a story. He wrote down: 杰克喜欢玛丽讲故事的方式。他写下来：

old forest, all still

joyful music, hidden world 古老森林，到处都是欢快的音乐，隐藏着一个奇妙的世界

Augusta frowned. “So, I guess you can write,” she muttered. 奥古斯塔皱起了眉头。 “所以，我想你还会写，”她小声嘟囔着。

“Suddenly there came a spinning wind,” said Mary, “and a cloud so bright, and a beam of light poured over a river!” “突然间，一阵旋转的风来了，”玛丽说，“还有一朵非常明亮的云，一道光束照在河面上！”

Jack quickly wrote: 杰克快速地写到：

spinning wind, bright cloud beam of light, river

“Then they came, rumbling and thundering!” exclaimed Mary. “然后他们来了，轰隆轰隆地！”玛丽喊道。

“Mary,” said Augusta. She sounded impatient. “玛丽，”奥古斯塔说。 她听起来很不耐烦。

But Mary kept talking. “Some with wings, some on horses of white! Queens and kings! In robes and gowns the colors of summer, fall, winter, and spring!” Jack wrote: 但玛丽还在继续说。 “有些有翅膀，有些骑着白色的马！女王和国王！ 穿着夏天、秋天、冬天和春天颜色的长袍和礼服！”杰克写道：

some with wings

white horses

queens, kings 一些有翅膀的白马、女王、国王

“They galloped in a circle, a blinding swirl!” said Mary. “They swept up that lonely girl and carried her across the river to their secret hollow hill! Had she gone inside, she would have become very small and seen many wondrous sights!” “他们绕着圈奔跑，一片炫目的旋风！”玛丽说。 “他们卷起了那个孤独的女孩，把她带过河，送到他们秘密的山丘洞穴里！ 如果她进去了，她就会变得非常小，看到许多奇妙的景象！”

Jack wrote: 杰克写到：

take lonely girl

hollow hill of Shee

wondrous sights 带孤独的女孩去神奇的希山谷看奇妙的景色

Jack looked up from his notebook, waiting for Mary to go on. When she spoke again, her voice was very soft. “But the girl grew afraid and ran home instead.” 杰克从笔记本上抬起头，等着玛丽继续说下去。 她再次开口时，声音非常轻柔。 “但是女孩变得害怕，转身跑回家了。”

Mary sat back in her chair and closed her eyes. The only sound in the cottage was the crackling of the fire and the pinging of rain into the tin buckets. 玛丽靠回椅子上，闭上了眼睛。 小屋里唯一的声音就是火炉噼啪作响，还有雨水滴进铁桶的叮当声。

“Mary?” Annie said softly. “Are you the girl in the story?” “玛丽？”安妮轻声说道。 “你是故事里的那个女孩吗？”

Mary opened her eyes. “I will never tell,” she said. 玛丽睁开了眼睛。 “我永远不会说的，”她说。

“Oh, Mary,” said Augusta, “such tales!” She turned to Jack and Annie. “Mary still believes in the impossible.” “哦，玛丽，”奥古斯塔说，“这样的故事！”她转向杰克和安妮。 “玛丽仍然相信不可能的事情。”

“Aye, I do, I surely do,” said Mary. “Every night I leave a bit of milk on my windowsill for the Shee. I leave crumbs at my door. They eat them, too.” “啊，我确实这么做，我确实这么做，”玛丽说。 “我每晚都在窗台上放一点牛奶给希希吃。 我在门口放一些面包屑。 他们也吃这些。”

“Mary, the birds eat the crumbs!” said Augusta. “玛丽，鸟儿吃面包屑！”奥古斯塔说。

“Yes, the birds are hungry also,” said Mary. “But the Shee pick over the crumbs first! At twilight, they steal across the river from their hidden hollow hills. Just ask the old fishermen of County Galway. Ask the farmers and nursemaids.” “是的，鸟儿也饿了，”玛丽说。 “但是谢伊会先挑拣面包屑！ 天色渐暗时，他们偷偷从藏身的山谷跨过河流。 只要去问加里韦伊郡的老渔夫。 去问农民和护士吧。

Augusta shook her head sadly. “Mary, only simple-minded folk still believe in such things,” she said. “Educated people know what is true and what is not true.” 奥古斯塔伤心地摇了摇头。 “玛丽，只有头脑简单的人才还相信这些事，”她说。 “有知识的人知道什么是真的，什么是假的。”

“No, child,” said Mary. “They only know what they think is true.…” “不，孩子，”玛丽说。 “他们只知道他们认为是真实的……”

Augusta straightened her shoulders. “Well. We should be going now, Mary,” she said. “So could you please tell these children the truth about me now?” 奥古斯塔挺直了肩膀。 “嗯。 我们现在应该走了，玛丽，”她说。 “那你能现在告诉这些孩子关于我的真相吗？”

“Yes,” said Mary. She turned to Jack and Annie. “Do you children have names?” “好，”玛丽说。 她转向杰克和安妮。 “你们孩子有名字吗？”

Jack smiled. This was the first time today anyone had asked them their names. 杰克笑了。 这是今天第一次有人问他们的名字。

“Yes. Our names are Jack and Annie,” said Annie. “是的。 我们的名字是杰克和安妮，”安妮说。

“Well, Jack and Annie, thank you for coming to visit me today. I can tell that you are very special,” said Mary. “好吧，杰克和安妮，感谢你们今天来拜访我。 我能感觉到你们非常特别，”玛丽说。

“What about me, Mary?” Augusta asked. “Am I special?” “那我呢，玛丽？”奥古斯塔问。 “我特别吗？”

“Yes, child, you are,” said Mary. She turned to Jack and Annie. “Augusta is special, too, but in a different way.” “是的，孩子，你特别，”玛丽说。 她转向杰克和安妮。 “奥古斯塔也很特别，但方式不同。”

“How am I different, Mary?” asked Augusta. “我跟玛丽有什么不同呢？”奥古斯塔问。

“You try very, very hard to be good, and you are very smart. But you—” Mary stopped. “你非常努力地做一个好孩子，而且你非常聪明。” 但你——”玛丽停了下来。

“What, Mary? I—what?” said Augusta. “怎么了，玛丽？ 我——什么？”奥古斯塔说。

“You are not happy,” said Mary. “And that breaks my heart.” “你看起来不开心，”玛丽说。 “这让我很难过。”

Augusta’s eyes filled with tears. 奥古斯塔的眼睛里充满了泪水。

“Oh, Augusta, don’t cry,” said Annie. She reached out to take Augusta’s hand, but the girl stepped back. “哦，奥古斯塔，别哭，”安妮说。 她伸出手想握住奥古斯塔的手，但女孩退后了。

Augusta wiped her eyes. “That’s silly. I’m happy enough. I know I’ve never seen the Shee and I never will. But I don’t care anymore. And if you like these miserable children more than me, Mary—well, that’s fine!” 奥古斯塔擦了擦眼睛。 “这很傻。我已经很开心了。 我知道我从来没有见过希伊，以后也不会见到。 但我不再在乎了。 如果你更喜欢这些可怜的孩子而不是我，玛丽——那也没关系！”

Augusta ran to the door and opened it. The damp air swept inside as she rushed out of the cottage. Through the open doorway, Jack and Annie could see Augusta’s red cape flying behind her. 奥古斯塔跑向门口，然后打开了门。 潮湿的空气随着她冲出小屋而涌入进来。 透过敞开的门，杰克和安妮看到奥古斯塔的红色斗篷在她身后飘扬。
