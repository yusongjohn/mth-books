6. No Big Deal 6. 没什么大不了的

Annie turned and saw the jackals running toward her. “Oh, no!” she cried. 安妮转过身，看到豺狗正朝她跑来。 “哦，天哪！”她喊道。

Shrieking, Koku leapt from Annie’s arms and bounded across the dry, cracked ground. When the jackals saw the baby baboon, they stopped. Then, cackling and yelping, they took off after her. 尖叫着，科库从安妮的臂弯里跳了出来，飞快地跑过干裂的土地。 当豺狼看到小狒狒时，他们停了下来。 接着，他们一边咯咯笑一边尖叫着，朝她追了过去。

Koku screeched and bolted toward the mountain. The jackals were right behind her. 科库尖叫着，朝山的方向冲了过去。 豺狼紧随其后。

“NOOO!” screamed Annie. She tore after Koku and the jackals. “不——！”安妮尖叫起来。 她拼命追了上去，跟在科库和豺狼后面。

“Annie!” Jack yelled. He jumped off his donkey and ran after her. “安妮！”杰克喊道。 他从驴子上跳了下来，朝她跑去。

From a distance, Jack saw the baby baboon disappear into a crack between two cliffs. Howling and cackling, the jackals followed Koku. They slipped into the crack and vanished from sight. 从远处看，杰克看到小狒狒消失在两座悬崖之间的裂缝里。 嚎叫着，豺狼们跟着科库。 他们滑进了裂缝，从视线中消失了。

Annie was racing to catch up. Jack was far behind. When he finally reached her, Annie was desperately trying to squeeze through the narrow opening. 安妮正在拼命追赶。 杰克远远落在后面。 当他终于追上她时，安妮正拼命想挤过那个狭窄的入口。

“We can’t fit through there!” said Jack. “我们穿不过去！”杰克说。

“We have to save Koku!” Annie cried. “我们必须救科库！”安妮喊道。

They heard shrieking, yelping, and cackling above them. Then gradually the sounds faded away. 他们听到头顶传来尖叫声、惊叫和笑声。 然后，声音渐渐消失了。

“KOKU!” screamed Annie. “KOKU!” 安妮尖叫道。

Dead silence. 死一般的寂静。

“Oh, no! I think they got her!” Annie wailed. “I think they got her, Jack!” She burst into tears. “Koku, Koku,” she repeated, sobbing. “哦，不！ 我想他们把她抓走了！”安妮哭喊道。 “我想他们把她抓走了，杰克！”她突然哭了起来。 “Koku，Koku，”她一边重复着，一边抽泣。

Jack didn’t know what to say. Everything had happened so fast! Then suddenly he heard another sound. 杰克不知道该说什么。 一切都发生得太快了！ 然后，他突然听到了另一个声音。

WAHOO! WAHOO!

“Annie!” Jack grabbed her by the shoulders and shook her. “Listen!” “安妮！”杰克抓住她的肩膀，摇晃她。 “听！”

Annie put her hand over her mouth. Tears rolled down her cheeks as she listened. 安妮把她的手放在嘴上。 她听着，眼泪顺着脸颊流下来。

WAHOO! The sound came from high above them. 哇呼！ 声音来自他们头顶很高的地方。

Jack and Annie stepped back and looked up the steep cliff. Koku was at the very top, jumping up and down, screeching at them. 杰克和安妮往后退，抬头看向陡峭的悬崖。 库库在悬崖的最顶端，跳上跳下，朝他们尖叫。

“She made it!” cried Annie. “She got away! Koku, come down! Come down!” “她成功了！”安妮喊道。 “她逃走了！库库，下来！” “下来！”

“Where’re the jackals?” said Jack. “杰克在哪里？”安妮说。

“I’ll bet it’s too steep for them to climb up after her!” said Annie. “Koku, come down!” “我敢打赌，对他们来说，爬上来找她太陡了！”安妮说。 “库库，下来！”

The baby baboon paced back and forth along the edge of the cliff, crying. 小狒狒宝宝在悬崖边来回踱步，一边哭一边走。

“We have to go get her!” said Annie. “She’s afraid to come down.” “我们必须去把她救出来！”安妮说。 “她害怕下来。”

Koku screeched and waved her arms. 科库尖叫着，挥舞着她的手臂。

“We can’t climb up there,” said Jack. “It’s too steep.” “我们爬不上去，”杰克说。 “太陡了。”

“We have to try!” said Annie. “我们必须试试！”安妮说。

“We’re not rock climbers,” said Jack, looking up at the steep wall of limestone. “Only the best rock climbers in the world could get up there.” “我们不是攀岩高手，”杰克抬头看着陡峭的石灰岩墙说。 “世界上最好的攀岩高手才能爬上去。”

“We can do that!” said Annie. “We can be great rock climbers.” “我们能做到！”安妮说。 “我们可以成为很棒的攀岩高手。”

“You can’t just be one,” said Jack. “You have to go through lots of training and practice—” “你不能一下子就成为高手，”杰克说。 “你必须经过很多训练和练习——”

“The mist!” Annie said. “We can use the magic mist and make a wish to be great rock climbers!” “雾气！”安妮说。 “我们可以用魔法雾气，许愿成为很棒的攀岩高手！”

“Use the mist?” said Jack. They could only use the mist once, and it was supposed to help them with their mission. But when he looked up at the baby baboon crying and waving her arms, saving Koku seemed more important than anything. “用雾气？”杰克说。 他们只能用一次雾气，而且它本来是帮他们完成任务的。 但当他抬头看到小狒狒在哭，还挥舞着胳膊时，救库库似乎比任何事情都更重要。

“Okay!” said Jack. “We’ll use the mist to get her down. But we might have to walk back. I’ll bet both our donkeys have run far away by now.” “好吧！”杰克说。 “我们会用雾气把她救下来。 但我们可能得走回去。 我敢打赌，现在我们的两头驴子早就跑远了。”

“No problem! We can do that!” said Annie. “Hurry! Before the jackals come back!” “没问题！ 我们能做到！”安妮说。 “快点！ 在豺狼回来之前！”

Jack reached into his leather pouch and pulled out the tiny bottle. He uncorked the top. 杰克把手伸进他的皮口袋，拿出那小瓶儿。 他拔掉了瓶塞。

“Here goes!” Jack said. “I wish to be a great rock climber!” He slowly inhaled the magic mist. His nostrils filled with the scent of mountain air and cold, moving water. The fresh, clean smell filled him with strength and energy. “出发啦！”杰克说。 “我希望自己成为一名出色的攀岩者！”他慢慢地吸了一口魔法雾气。 他的鼻孔里充满了山间空气和冰冷流动的水的气味。 这清新干净的气味让他感到充满力量和活力。

Jack handed the bottle to Annie. “I wish to be a great rock climber!” she exclaimed. Annie inhaled the mist, too. Then she gave the bottle back to Jack. He corked it and put it away. 杰克把瓶子递给安妮。 “我希望自己成为一名出色的攀岩者！”她喊道。 安妮也吸了一口雾气。 然后她把瓶子又还给了杰克。 他把瓶子盖上，收了起来。

Jack and Annie looked up at the steep wall of the limestone cliff. 杰克和安妮抬头看着石灰岩悬崖陡峭的岩壁。

“No big deal,” said Jack. “没什么大不了的，”杰克说。

“Easy,” said Annie. She called up to the baby baboon, “We’re coming, Koku. Don’t worry. We’ll save you!” “很简单，”安妮说。 她朝小狒狒喊道：“我们这就来，库库。别担心。” 我们会救你！

“We have one hour to climb up and back down,” said Jack. “First we need to take off our boots, so our toes can fit in the cracks.” “我们有一小时的时间爬上树屋再下来，”杰克说。 “首先，我们需要脱掉鞋子，这样我们的脚趾才能卡进缝隙里。”

“Righto,” said Annie. She and Jack coolly slipped off their boots and stockings. “好的，”安妮说。 她和杰克冷静地脱掉了鞋子和袜子。

“Okay. Now we follow the three basic rules of rock climbing,” said Jack. “We look. We think. We move.” “好。” “现在我们要遵守攀岩的三个基本规则，”杰克说。 “我们观察。我们思考。 我们行动。”

“Got it,” said Annie. “明白了，”安妮说。

Jack and Annie moved about twenty feet apart. They each studied the cliff wall as if it were a giant map. 杰克和安妮各自移动了大约二十英尺远。 他们各自仔细研究着悬崖墙，仿佛它是一张巨大的地图。

Jack looked for cracks, ledges, bumps, and holes. After he’d charted his route in his mind, he took a long, deep breath. He lodged the toes of his left foot into a crack in the limestone. He reached high and gripped two tiny bumps on the wall with his fingers. Then he pulled himself up. Out of the corner of his eye, he saw Annie doing the same thing. 杰克寻找裂缝、平台、凸起和洞穴。 在他心里规划好路线后，他深吸了一口气。 他把左脚的脚趾插入石灰岩中的一个裂缝里。 他伸长手臂，用手指抓住墙上两个小小的凸起。 然后他用力一拉，把自己往上提。 他眼角一瞥，看到安妮正在做同样的事情。

Jack and Annie climbed higher and higher up the wall. Jack took his time to find new cracks for his toes. He slowly ran his hands over the rock face to find bumps and ledges to grab with his fingers. 杰克和安妮沿着墙往上爬得越来越高。 杰克花时间寻找新的裂缝，让他的脚趾踩上去。 他慢慢地用手掌在岩壁上摸索，寻找可以抓握的凸起和平台。

As an expert rock climber, Jack knew exactly how to shift his body weight and when to relax his grip. He knew exactly how to push with his legs and when to pull with his hands. 作为一名专业的攀岩者，杰克知道如何准确地调整身体的重量，也知道什么时候该放松手的抓握。 他知道什么时候该用腿发力，什么时候该用手拉。

Annie was silently climbing up the cliff at the same time, but Jack didn’t look at her or think of her. He completely ignored Koku’s screeching from above. He kept all his attention focused on finding new places to grip the face of the cliff with his fingers and toes. 安妮同时默默地沿着悬崖往上爬，但杰克没有看她，也没有想到她。 他完全忽略了上面库库的尖叫声。 他全神贯注地用手指和脚趾寻找可以抓握的新地方。

Slowly and calmly, Jack gripped, pulled, and pushed his way up the face of the cliff. He climbed and climbed until he was only a few feet from the top. He couldn’t see over the ledge, but he gripped tightly with his fingertips and hoisted himself higher. 杰克慢慢地、冷静地，用手指和脚趾抓紧、拉住、推着自己往上爬。 他一直爬啊爬啊，直到离山顶只有几英尺远了。 他看不到山顶，但他用指尖紧紧抓住，把自己抬得更高了。

WAHOO! 哇——！

Koku poked her head over the edge of the cliff, breaking Jack’s concentration. She reached down and grabbed his ear. 库库把头从悬崖边探出来，打断了杰克的注意力。 她伸手下来，抓住了他的耳朵。

“Oww!” Jack yelled. His left hand lost its grip. His right foot slipped. Then his left foot slipped! Jack was hanging on to the ledge with just one hand—and that hand was slipping! “Annieee!” he yelled. “啊——！”杰克喊道。 他的左手失去了抓握。 他的右脚滑了一下。 然后他的左脚滑了一下！ 杰克只用一只手抓着岩石边缘——那只手也开始打滑了！ “安妮！”，他喊道。

Just as Jack was about to lose his grip and fall, he felt a hand clamp down on his right wrist. 就在杰克快要松手掉下去的时候，他感觉到一只手下到了他的右腕上。

“Hold on!” Annie cried. She’d reached the top ahead of him! Jack’s fingers slipped from the ledge, but Annie held on to his wrist with both hands as he dangled high above the stony ground below. “抓紧！”安妮喊道。 她已经先一步到达了顶部！ 杰克的手指从岩石边缘滑落了，但安妮用两只手紧紧抓着他的手腕，他高高悬在下面布满石头的地面之上。

The baby baboon shrieked. The vultures cawed in the cloudless sky. 小狒狒宝宝尖叫起来。 秃鹫在晴朗的天空中发出叫声。

“You’re still a great rock climber, Jack!” Annie shouted. “You know how to do this!” “你还是个很棒的攀岩者，杰克！”安妮喊道。 “你知道怎么做这个！”

I know how to do this! Jack thought. He clicked back into thinking like an expert rock climber. He twisted himself toward the rock wall. He reached out with his free hand and secured his fingertips in a small crevice. He planted his bare toes onto two ridges. 我知道怎么做这个！杰克想。 他重新开始像一个专业的攀岩者那样思考。 他扭动身体朝岩石墙靠近。 他用空着的手伸出去，把手指尖卡进一个小小的裂缝里。 他光着脚踩在两个凸起上。

“Okay! I’m good,” called Jack. “You can let go.” “好了！ 我没事，”杰克喊道。 “你可以放手了。”

Annie released Jack’s hand. He quickly gripped a small bump on the rock face. He was very still for a moment. Blocking out all sounds and thoughts, he waited for his breathing to slow down. He waited for his legs to stop shaking and for his body to relax. 安妮松开了杰克的手。 他迅速抓住岩石上一个小凸起。 他停住不动了一会儿。 他屏蔽掉所有声音和想法，等着自己的呼吸慢慢变慢。 他等着自己的腿不再发抖，身体慢慢放松下来。

Using all his strength, Jack pulled himself upward. He lifted his leg over the rim of the limestone cliff. Then he gracefully pulled his whole body up and over the edge. 杰克用尽全力，把自己往上拉。 他抬起腿，跨过了石灰岩悬崖的边缘。 然后，他优雅地把全身拉了上去，翻过了边缘。

“Whew,” breathed Jack. He was safe. “哇，”杰克喘了口气。他安全了。
