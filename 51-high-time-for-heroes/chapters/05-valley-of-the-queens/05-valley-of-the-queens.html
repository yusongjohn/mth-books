5. Valley of the Queens 5. 女王谷

“Miss Nightingale, your donkey!” Ali called out. The small boy stood with a donkey and a larger packhorse. “南丁格尔小姐，你的驴子！”阿里喊道。 那个小男孩站在那里，身边是一头驴子和一匹更大的驮马。

“How lovely to see you, Ali,” said Florence. “很高兴见到你，阿里，”弗洛伦斯说道。

Mustafa stepped into the shallow water and pulled the rowboat onto the bank. He then helped everyone out of the boat. 穆斯塔法走进浅水区，把划艇拉上了岸。 他然后帮大家从船上下来了。

Jack and Annie stood with Koku and watched Florence mount the donkey. Mustafa climbed onto his packhorse. Jack felt helpless. He didn’t know how to stop Florence from leaving them. 杰克和安妮和科库站在一起，看着弗洛伦斯爬上驴子。 穆斯塔法爬上了他的驮马。 杰克感到无能为力。 他不知道怎么才能阻止弗洛伦斯离开他们。

“Have a nice afternoon,” said Annie. “祝你们下午愉快，”安妮说。

Florence looked back at them. “I hope you have a nice afternoon, too,” she said. “I would ask you to join me, but I am … well, I’m afraid I need a bit of quiet and solitude right now.” 弗洛伦斯回头看了他们一眼。 “我也希望你们下午愉快，”她说。 “我想邀请你们和我一起去，但我……唉，我现在恐怕需要一点安静和独处的时间。”

“We understand,” said Annie. “我们理解，”安妮说。

“Thank you,” said Florence. She turned back to the guide. “Lead the way, Mustafa.” “谢谢，”弗洛伦斯说。 她转身面对向导。 “带路吧，穆斯塔法。”

Mustafa and his horse headed away from the river. Florence followed on her donkey. Jack, Annie, and Koku watched as they headed up the dirt road toward the distant mountains. Soon they disappeared from view. 穆斯塔法和他的马朝着远离河流的方向走去。 弗洛伦斯骑着她的驴子跟在后面。 杰克、安妮和科库看着他们沿着泥路朝远处的山脉走去。 不久后，他们就从视线中消失了。

“Darn,” breathed Annie. “真倒霉，”安妮叹气道。

“Yeah,” said Jack. “是啊，”杰克说。

“Donkeys for you, too?” Ali asked, coming up behind them. “You can travel to the ruins without a guide, you know.” “你们也要驴子吗？”阿里从他们身后走过来，问道。 “你知道吗，不用向导也能去废墟。”

Annie looked at Jack. “Want to?” she asked. 安妮看着杰克。 “想去吗？”她问。

“How do we get to the Valley of the Queens?” Jack asked Ali. “我们怎么去女王谷？”杰克问阿里。

“Very easy.” The boy pointed to the road. “Travel the same way they went,” he said. “Follow that road and never turn.” “非常容易。”男孩指着那条路。 “沿着他们走的路走，”他说。 “沿着这条路一直走，不要转弯。”

“That sounds simple,” said Annie. “Let’s go.” “听起来很简单，”安妮说。 “我们走吧。”

“Wait,” said Jack. He took all of his Egyptian coins from his pouch and showed them to Ali. “Do we have enough to rent two donkeys?” “等等，”杰克说。 他从口袋里拿出所有埃及的硬币，展示给阿里看。 “我们有足够的钱租两头驴吗？”，

“Oh, yes!” Ali took only one coin. “Good for two donkeys all afternoon,” he said. “哦，够了！”，阿里只拿了一枚硬币。 “这枚硬币可以租两头驴一整个下午，”，他说。

“Okay, we’ll do it,” said Jack. “好吧，我们这么做，”，杰克说。

“Yay!” said Annie. “太棒了！”，安妮说。

“Good. I will get them for you,” said Ali. He headed to the donkeys standing under the trees. “很好， 我会帮你们去租，”，阿里说。 他朝树下站着的驴子走去。

“I wonder if there’s anything we need to know about riding donkeys,” said Jack. He opened their travelers’ handbook again and looked up donkey. He read aloud: “我想知道我们是否需要了解一些关于骑驴的事情，”，杰克说。 他又翻开他们的旅行手册，查了一下“驴子”。他大声读道：

Donkeys were first trained to carry travelers over 6,000 years ago in Egypt. The animals are easy to ride. But the traveler must approach them slowly and treat them with kindness and affection. 驴子早在6000多年前就在埃及被训练用来载人旅行。 这些动物很容易骑。 但旅行者必须慢慢地接近它们，并以友善和关爱对待它们。

“Easy!” said Annie. “That’s the way I always treat animals.” “容易！”安妮说道。 “我对待动物一直就是这样。”

HEE-HAW!

“Here they are!” said Ali, leading two donkeys toward Jack and Annie. “你们在这儿呢！”阿里说道，带着两头驴子朝杰克和安妮走来。

Both donkeys were sandy brown with sweet faces. Their saddles were blankets made of heavy cloth. Their long, furry ears twitched to keep the flies away. 两头驴子都是沙黄色的，长着可爱的脸蛋。 它们的鞍子其实是用厚重的布料做成的毯子。 它们长长的、毛茸茸的耳朵不停抖动，赶走苍蝇。

“Hello there!” Annie said to one of the donkeys. “You have ears like a rabbit. Can I call you Bunny?” “你好呀！”安妮对其中一头驴子说。 “你有兔子一样的耳朵。 我可以叫你 Bunny 吗？”

“Oh, brother,” said Jack. “哦，天哪，”杰克说。

“Talk to yours, too,” Annie coached Jack. “Call her a sweet name. Kindness and affection, remember?” “也跟你那头说话，”安妮指导杰克。 “给她起个甜蜜的名字。 记得要善良和有爱心哦？”

Jack rolled his eyes and scratched the ears of the other donkey. “Hi … um … Honey. Want to go for a ride?” he said. The donkey nuzzled her head against Jack’s hand. 杰克翻了翻白眼，然后挠了挠另一头驴子的耳朵。 “嗨……嗯……Honey。 你想去兜风吗？”他说。 驴子把头蹭了蹭杰克的手心。

Ali laughed. “I think my donkeys like you very much,” he said. 阿里笑了。 “我觉得我的驴子非常喜欢你，”他说。

Holding Koku, Annie climbed onto the back of Bunny, while Jack climbed onto Honey. 安妮抱着科库，爬上了 Bunny 的背，而杰克则爬上了 Honey。

“You must take water,” said Ali. He tied tin canteens to the donkeys’ saddles. “Come back by sundown. There is danger in the hills after dark.” “你们必须带水，”阿里说。 他把锡水壶绑在驴子的鞍子上。 “日落前必须回来。 天黑后山里有危险。”

“Like what kind of danger?” asked Jack. “什么危险？”杰克问。

“Wild jackals, tomb raiders,” said Ali. “野豺狼，盗墓贼，”阿里说。

“Got it,” said Jack. “明白了，”杰克说。

“We’ll be back before dark,” said Annie. “我们在天黑前就会回来，”安妮说。

“Way before dark,” said Jack. “天黑前很久就会回来，”杰克说。

“Good-bye, Ali. Thank you!” said Annie. “Giddyup, Bunny!” She led the way with Koku on her shoulder. “再见了，阿里。 谢谢你！”安妮说。 “出发吧， Bunny！”她肩上扛着Koku，带头走了。

“Follow them, Honey, please,” said Jack. “ honey，麻烦你跟着他们，”杰克说。

The two donkeys left the lush riverbank and trotted up the same road that Mustafa and Florence had traveled. 两只驴子离开了茂盛的河岸，沿着穆斯塔法和弗洛伦斯走过的同一条路小跑上去。

“Our donkeys remind me of the camels we rode to Baghdad!” Annie called back to Jack. “Remember Beauty and Cutie?” “我们的驴子让我想起了我们去巴格达时骑的骆驼！”安妮回头对杰克喊道。 “还记得 Beauty 和 Cutie 吗？”

“Oh, brother,” said Jack. The camels had nearly ruined their mission. “哦，天哪，”杰克说。 骆驼差点毁了他们的任务。

Bunny and Honey trotted past cornfields, palm trees, sheep, goats, mud huts, and children playing in the dirt. When the children saw Jack and Annie, they laughed and pointed at the baboon riding on Annie’s shoulder. 兔子和蜂蜜 trotted 路过玉米田、棕榈树、绵羊、山羊、泥巴小屋，还有在泥地上玩耍的孩子们。 当孩子们看到杰克和安妮时，他们笑了，指着骑在安妮肩膀上的狒狒。

Soon the dirt road opened onto a wider, rougher road that crossed a flat plain. The plain was bordered by the sand-colored mountains. Hot breezes blew clouds of dust as the donkeys clomped side by side over the dry, cracked earth. 很快，泥路变成了更宽但更粗糙的路，穿过一片平坦的平原。 平原被沙黄色的山脉环绕着。 热风掀起尘土，驴子们并排踏着干裂的土地前进。

Through the gritty haze, Jack could see ruins scattered about the plain—broken columns and chunks of ancient walls half buried in sand. 在尘土飞扬的迷雾中，杰克能看到平原上散落着废墟——断裂的石柱和半埋在沙中的古老墙壁碎片。

“Look!” said Annie. “看啊！”安妮说。

Two gigantic statues loomed ahead against the blue sky: enormous pharaohs on thrones, as tall as five-story buildings. 前方的蓝天之下，两座巨大的雕像矗立着：巨大的法老坐在宝座上，有五层楼那么高。

“Let’s stop for a minute,” Jack called. He pulled on his donkey’s reins and took out the travelers’ handbook. He found a picture of the statues and read aloud: “我们停一下吧，”杰克喊道。 他拉了拉驴子的缰绳，拿出了旅行者手册。 他找到了雕像的图片，大声读起来：

More than 3,000 years old, the Colossi of Memnon once guarded the temple of a pharaoh. MemnonmeansRuler of the Dawn. Ancient Egyptians believed one of the giant statues sang every day at sunrise. 这些门农巨像已经有3000多年的历史了，曾经守护着一位法老的神庙。 门农的意思是黎明之主。 古埃及人相信，这些巨大的雕像每天清晨都会唱歌。

Jack took out his notebook and pencil and wrote: 杰克拿出他的笔记本和铅笔，写下了：

Memnon=Ruler of the Dawn 门农=黎明之主

He looked at the ruins again. The pharaoh’s temple had vanished with time. The statues were faceless, their bodies broken and worn away by thousands of years of wind and sand. The wounded giants still looked amazingly powerful, though. Through the dry heat, Jack could imagine their ancient song. 他又一次看了看废墟。 法老的神庙已经随着时间消失了。 这些雕像没有脸，他们的身体被几千年的风沙磨得破烂不堪。 虽然如此，那些受伤的巨人看起来依然非常有力量。 在干燥的热浪中，杰克仿佛能听到他们古老的歌声。

HEE-HAW!

Jack jumped. “What’s that?” 杰克跳了起来。 “那是什么？”

“A donkey! Up ahead somewhere,” said Annie, “past those temple ruins. I’ll bet it’s Florence’s donkey. Hurry! Let’s go!” “是一只驴！ “在前面的某个地方，”安妮说，“在那些神庙废墟的后面。 我敢打赌那是佛罗伦萨的驴。快点！ 我们走吧！

Jack put away his notebook and pencil and the handbook. “Giddyup!” he said, and his donkey started walking again. “Annie, when we find Florence, try to act surprised, so she won’t think we’re following her. Be cool.” 杰克把他的笔记本、铅笔和手册收了起来。 “一、二、三！”他说，他的驴子又开始走了。 “安妮，当我们找到弗洛伦斯时，试着表现得惊讶一点，这样她就不会觉得我们在跟踪她了。要冷静。”

“Don’t worry, I’m always cool,” said Annie. “But we’ll have to find a way to stick close to her, so we can find out her secret of greatness.” “别担心，我一向都很冷静，”安妮说。 “但我们得想办法一直靠近她，这样我们才能发现她伟大的秘密。”

“Yeah,” Jack sighed. “The only problem is I’m having trouble seeing what makes her so great,” he said. He thought Florence seemed unhappy and unfriendly. “是啊，”杰克叹了口气。 “唯一的问题是我很难看出是什么让她变得这么伟大，”他说。 他觉得弗洛伦斯看起来似乎很不开心，也不友善。

“But she is great,” said Annie. “I know she is!” “但她确实很伟大，”安妮说。 “我知道她很伟大！”

Jack and Annie kept riding their donkeys through the heat and dust. When they stopped at the ruins of an ancient temple, there were no signs of Florence or Mustafa among the broken columns and crumbling walls. There were no signs of any living things. 杰克和安妮继续骑着他们的驴子，穿过炎热和尘土。 当他们停在一座古老神庙的废墟时，在倒塌的石柱和斑驳的墙壁间，看不到弗洛伦斯或穆斯塔法的踪影。 这里没有任何生命的迹象。

“I don’t see them,” said Jack. “We must have lost them somewhere.” He shivered, despite the heat. The quiet felt spooky to him. He grabbed his canteen and took a long drink of water. Annie did the same. Then she poured a little bit of water into her cupped hand and offered it to Koku. “我看不到他们，”杰克说。 “我们一定是在某个地方把他们弄丢了。”他打了个寒战，尽管天气很热。 他觉得这里的安静让人毛骨悚然。 他抓起水壶，喝了一大口。 安妮也做了同样的事。 然后她倒了一点水在手心里，递给科库。

“Do you think we should keep going to the Valley of the Queens?” asked Annie. “你觉得我们应该继续前往女王谷吗？”安妮问。

“I guess so,” said Jack. “我想是的，”杰克说。

“Florence and Mustafa are probably already there,” said Annie. “Giddyup, Bunny.” “弗洛伦斯和穆斯塔法可能已经到那里了，”安妮说。 “出发吧，小兔子。”

As the donkeys walked toward the cliffs, Jack couldn’t shake his uneasy feeling. Everything was the color of mud and sand. The only other living creatures were some vultures circling in the sky. As Jack stared up at the birds, Annie brought her donkey to a halt. 当驴子们朝悬崖走去时，杰克怎么也摆脱不了那种不安的感觉。 一切的颜色都像泥巴和沙子一样。 唯一其他的活物就是一些秃鹫在天空中盘旋。 当杰克抬头看着那些鸟时，安妮让她的驴子停了下来。

“Oh, wow! Those must be the tombs of the pharaons’ wives!” said Annie. She pointed to several huge square holes carved into the limestone walls of the cliffs. “哦，哇！ 那些一定是法老王后们的墓穴！”安妮说。 她指着悬崖上石灰岩墙壁上几个巨大的方形洞口。

“Yeah.” Jack shuddered. “You know, I think I’d like to go back. This place gives me the creeps.” “是啊。”杰克打了个寒颤。 “你知道吗，我想我可能想回去。” “这个地方让我觉得毛骨悚然。”

“Are you afraid of ghosts?” said Annie. “你怕鬼吗？”安妮说。

Jack shook his head. “I don’t know. Maybe. Let’s just go,” he said. “We can wait for Florence back at Charles and Selina’s boat.” 杰克摇了摇头。 “我不知道。也许。” “我们干脆走吧，”他说。 “我们可以去查尔斯和塞尔ina的船那里等弗洛伦斯回来。”

“Don’t you at least want to peek inside one of the tombs?” asked Annie. “你至少不想看看其中一个墓穴里面吗？”安妮问。

“Nope,” Jack said. “不想，”杰克说。

“Well, okay, you don’t have to,” said Annie. “But if I don’t take a look, I’ll always wonder what I missed. Koku and I will be right back.” “好吧，你不用的话也没关系，”安妮说。 “但是，如果我不去看看，我就会一直想知道我错过了什么。” 科库和我马上回来。”

“No, Annie, let’s leave this place,” said Jack. “不，安妮，我们得离开这儿，”杰克说。

“Just one teeny minute,” said Annie. Holding on to the baby baboon, she climbed off her donkey. “We’ll just take a quick look in that one.” She pointed to the nearest tomb cut into the mountain. “I promise we won’t go inside. We’ll hurry back.” With Koku sitting on her shoulder, Annie took off toward the tomb. “再等一小会儿，”安妮说。 她抱着小狒狒，从驴背上爬了下来。 “我们只是去看看那个。”她指着最近的墓穴，那墓穴凿在山里。 “我保证我们不会进去的。 我们会很快就回来。”凯库坐在她肩膀上，安妮朝墓穴跑去。

Jack’s donkey shook her head and kicked her hind legs. “What’s wrong, Honey?” said Jack. 杰克的驴子摇了摇头，踢了踢后腿。 “怎么了，亲爱的？”杰克说。

Annie’s donkey started acting strangely, too. She brayed loudly and turned away from the cliffs. “Calm down, Bunny,” said Jack, trying to speak in a soothing voice. “It’s okay, it’s okay.” 安妮的驴子也开始表现得奇怪了。 它大声地叫着，然后转身离开了悬崖。 “冷静点，小兔子，”杰克用温柔的声音说。 “没关系，没关系。”

Something was clearly bothering both animals. Jack’s donkey tried to turn around, and Annie’s donkey began trotting back the way they’d come! 显然，这两只动物都被什么东西困扰着。 杰克的驴子想转身，安妮的驴子却开始往他们来的路上小跑！

“Wait, Bunny!” Jack called as Annie’s donkey sped away. “Annie!” he shouted. “Come back now! Bunny’s running off!” “等等，小兔子！”杰克喊道，安妮的驴子飞快地跑开了。 “安妮！”他喊道。 “现在就回来！ 小兔子要跑掉了！”

Annie was standing at the entrance of the tomb. She turned around. “What?” she called. 安妮站在墓穴的入口处。 她转过身。 “什么？”她喊道。

Koku let out a bone-chilling shriek. From behind a pile of rocks near the tomb came two dirty, gray, wolf-like animals. The two creatures looked at Annie, then loped in her direction. 科库发出一声令人毛骨悚然的尖叫声。 从墓穴附近的一堆岩石后，出现了两只又脏又灰、像狼一样的动物。 这两只生物看了看安妮，然后朝她跑来。

“Oh, man!” said Jack. “ Jackals!” “天哪！”杰克说。 “豺狗！”
