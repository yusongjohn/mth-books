10. Good-Bye, Thebes! 10. 再见，底比斯！

“I’m afraid we should leave, too,” said Annie. “It’s almost time for us to meet up with our parents.” “恐怕我们也该离开了，”安妮说道。 “差不多该去和我们的父母会合了。”

“Oh, yes, of course,” said Florence. “Where do you need to go?” “哦，对，当然可以，”弗洛伦斯说。 “你需要去哪儿？”

“Across the Nile,” said Jack, “by the sycamore. We’ll rendezvous there.” Jack put on his shirt, his belt, and his hat. “穿过尼罗河，”杰克说，“在悬铃木旁边。 我们在那儿碰头。”杰克穿上衬衫、腰带和帽子。

“Good,” said Florence. “No doubt Mustafa and Ali are already sitting by the river, waiting to help travelers. We will shout for him to row across and pick you up.” “很好，”弗洛伦斯说。 “毫无疑问，穆斯塔法和阿里已经坐在河边，等着帮助旅行者了。 我们会大声喊他来划船，接你们过去。”

“Thanks,” said Jack. “谢谢，”杰克说。

“And you will need this, Annie,” said Florence. She went to a small closet and took out a wooden crutch. “I always travel with a few medical supplies. It’s not much, but it should help you a bit.” “你还需要这个，安妮，”弗洛伦斯说。 她走到一个小衣柜旁，拿出一根木拐杖。 “我总是随身带着一些医疗用品。 虽然不多，但应该能帮到你一点。”

“Great!” said Annie. She took the crutch from Florence and used it to help herself down from the bed. “太棒了！”安妮说。 她从弗洛伦斯那里拿过拐杖，用它帮助自己从床上下来。

Jack climbed down, too, careful not to jostle his right arm. As they followed Florence out of her cabin, dawn was spreading its rosy glow across the Temple of Luxor, the anchored sailboats, and the waters of the Nile. 杰克也爬了下来，小心不要碰到他的右臂。 他们跟着弗洛伦斯走出小屋时，黎明的粉红色光芒正洒在卢克索神庙、停泊的帆船和尼罗河的水面上。

Charles and Selina were already on the deck. Selina was sketching the temple, while Charles sat in a deck chair, reading a book. “Good morning!” they both exclaimed when they saw Jack, Annie, and Florence. 查尔斯和塞尔ina已经在甲板上了。 塞尔ina正在画神庙，而查尔斯坐在甲板椅上，看书。 “早上好！”他们看到杰克、安妮和弗洛伦斯时，都大声喊道。

“Say good morning and good-bye to our new friends,” said Florence. “Jack and Annie are leaving Thebes soon.” “向我们的新朋友说早上好，也说再见，”弗洛伦斯说道。 “杰克和安妮很快就离开底比斯了。”

“Oh, dear, we will miss these brave American children,” said Selina. She put down her sketching tools and rushed over to give Jack and Annie careful hugs. “Good luck with your injuries, my dears.” “哦，天哪，我们会想念这些勇敢的美国孩子的，”塞莉娜说。 她放下画具，赶紧跑过去给杰克和安妮一个温暖的拥抱。 “祝你们的伤势早日康复，我亲爱的。”

“Thanks,” said Jack. “谢谢，”杰克说。

“Thanks for helping us,” said Annie. “谢谢你们的帮助，”安妮说。

“Good-bye, old chap,” said Charles, shaking Jack’s left hand. “And, young lady, I’m sure you’ll be leaping over barrels soon.” “再见了，老伙计，”查尔斯一边说，一边握了握杰克的左手。 “还有，小姐，我相信你很快就会像跳过桶一样灵活了。”

“Righto,” said Annie. “没错，”安妮说。

Donkeys brayed across the river. 驴子们在河对岸大声叫着。

“Look!” said Jack, pointing to Mustafa and Ali on the western bank. “看！”杰克指着西部河岸上的穆斯塔法和阿里喊道。

“They are already up and ready for work,” said Charles. The Englishman stepped across the boat deck, faced the Nile, and shouted, “Mustafa!” He waved for the guide to come across the river. “他们已经起床，准备开始工作了，”查尔斯说。 那个英国人跨过船甲板，面对尼罗河，大声喊道：“穆斯塔法！”他挥手示意向导过河来。

Mustafa climbed into his rowboat, pushed off the bank, and started rowing across. 穆斯塔法爬进他的划艇，离开河岸，开始划船过河。

“Come,” said Florence. “I’ll accompany you to the water’s edge.” “来吧，”弗洛伦斯说。 “我会陪你走到水边。”

“Thanks again!” said Annie, waving good-bye to Charles and Selina. “谢谢你们再次帮助我们！”安妮挥手向查尔斯和塞莉娜告别。

Charles tipped his hat as Selina waved back. Then Jack and Annie followed Florence down the landing. In the glowing dawn light, a heron was standing on one leg in the water. “He looks like me,” said Annie, hopping on one foot. 查尔斯摘下帽子，塞莉娜挥手回应。 然后杰克和安妮跟着弗洛伦斯走下码头。 在晨光中，一只鹭鸶站在水里，单腿站立。 “他看起来像我，”安妮单脚跳着说。

Florence smiled. “There is more activity here in the first hour of cool morning than at any other time of day,” she said. 弗洛伦斯微笑了。 “在凉爽早晨的第一个小时里，这里比一天中的其他任何时候都更热闹，”她说。

Men in robes and turbans were sitting under a palm tree, smoking a water pipe. Two women were bathing small children in the Nile. Some girls were spreading wet clothes over rocks to dry. 一些穿着长袍和头巾的男子坐在棕榈树下，抽着水烟壶。 两个妇女正在尼罗河里给小孩洗澡。 一些女孩正在把湿衣服铺在石头上晾干。

Jack heard someone shouting. He and Annie and Florence turned to look back at the sailboats anchored offshore. Lord and Lady Bickerson were on the deck of one of the boats. They were swatting and yelling at the flies. 杰克听到有人在喊叫。 他和安妮以及弗洛伦斯转过身，看向停泊在近海的帆船。 比克森男爵和女爵正在其中一艘船的甲板上。 他们正挥舞着驱赶苍蝇，大声喊叫。

“Poor souls,” breathed Florence. “可怜的人们，”弗洛伦斯轻声说道。

“The flies?” asked Annie. “苍蝇？”安妮问。

Florence laughed. “I like you both very much,” she said. “I think we could be good friends.” 弗洛伦斯笑了。 “我非常喜欢你们两个，”她说。 “我想我们能成为好朋友。”

“I think so, too,” said Annie. “我也这么想，”安妮说。

“Me too,” Jack added. His whole opinion of Florence had changed since she had tended to their injuries so kindly and he had heard her speak about her dreams. “我也是，”杰克补充道。 自从她那么温柔地照顾他们的伤，还听到她谈起自己的梦想后，杰克对弗洛伦斯的看法完全改变了。

“Ah, here is your carriage,” said Florence. “啊，你们的马车到了，”弗洛伦斯说。

Mustafa had arrived in his rowboat. He pulled it ashore. Then he and Florence helped Annie and Jack climb aboard and take their seats. 穆斯塔法乘着他的划艇到达了。 他把它拉到了岸上。 然后他和弗洛伦斯帮助安妮和杰克爬上船，坐好位置。

“Good-bye, Jack and Annie,” Florence said, standing on the riverbank. “You must visit me if you ever come to England.” “再见，杰克和安妮，”弗洛伦斯说，站在河岸边。 “如果你们以后去英国，一定要来看我。”

Jack was sad to leave her, and even sadder to think they would probably never see her again. “Good luck with everything,” he said. “Hold on to your dreams.” 杰克很难过要离开她，想到他们可能再也见不到她，更难过了。 “祝你们一切顺利，”他说。 “紧紧抓住你们的梦想。”

“We’ll miss you,” said Annie. “我们会想你的，”安妮说。

Florence smiled her most radiant smile and waved. Then she turned and walked away. 弗洛伦斯露出她最灿烂的微笑，挥手告别。 然后她转身走开了。

Mustafa used his oars to push off, then began rowing back across the River Nile. Geese flew overhead. A pelican was perched on the crocodile’s rock. The old Egyptian sang his song. The words were still impossible to understand, but Jack felt he knew what they meant now. They celebrated the Nile and all the life around it—ancient and new, beasts and birds. 穆斯塔法用桨划开水面，然后开始划着船往尼罗河对岸回去。 一群鹅从他们头顶飞过。 一只鹈鹕停在一隻鳄鱼的石头上。 那位年长的埃及人唱起了他的歌。 这些话还是听不懂，但杰克觉得现在他明白它们的意思了。 他们庆祝尼罗河，以及河岸上所有的生命——古老的和崭新的，动物和飞鸟。

When they came to the other side of the river, Mustafa pulled the rowboat up onto the bank, then helped Jack and Annie climb out. Nearby, Ali was talking to some travelers who wanted donkeys. He called to his grandfather. 当他们到达河的另一边时，穆斯塔法把小船拉上岸，然后帮助杰克和安妮爬出来。 附近，阿里正在和一些想要租驴子的旅客说话。 他叫了他的爷爷。

“Wait, please,” said Jack. He fumbled in his pouch for their Egyptian money. He pulled out their coins and offered them to Mustafa. “These are all for you, please. You helped us a lot. You saved us, actually.” “请等一下，”杰克说。 他摸着口袋，找他们的埃及钱。 他拿出硬币，递给穆斯塔法。 “这些都是给你的，请收下。 你帮了我们很多。 其实，你救了我们。”

The guide shook his head. “No,” he said. It was the first word Jack had heard him speak. “I helped you not for money, but because you needed help. It would be wrong for me to accept payment for a simple good deed.” 导游摇了摇头。 “不，”他说。 这是杰克第一次听到他说的话。 “我帮助你们不是为了钱，而是因为你们需要帮助。 如果我接受报酬，那就不对了。”

“Oh, yes. I understand,” said Jack. He put away his coins. “Thank you very much.” “哦，是的。” “我明白了。”杰克说。 他把硬币收了起来。 “非常感谢你。”

“Yes, thank you,” said Annie. “And please tell Ali good-bye from us.” “是的，谢谢你。”安妮说。 “请代我们向阿里道别。”

The old man nodded, silent again, and headed toward his grandson and the new travelers. Day after day, Mustafa and Ali help people, Jack thought, and always with gentleness and dignity. 老人点了点头，再次沉默，然后朝他的孙子和新来的旅人走去。 杰克想，日复一日，穆斯塔法和阿里帮助人们，总是温柔而有尊严。

“Come on, let’s go, quick,” Annie said, “while no one’s looking.” “快点，我们走吧，”安妮说，“趁没人注意。”

“Righto!” said Jack. “好啊！”杰克说。

Using her crutch, Annie hobbled to the sycamore tree. Jack followed her. When they slipped under the canopy of spreading branches, Annie grabbed the rope ladder. 安妮用拐杖一瘸一拐地走到那棵梧桐树旁。 杰克跟在她后面。 当他们从枝叶繁茂的树冠下钻过去时，安妮抓住了绳梯。

“Oh, man, can you climb up with your sprained ankle?” Jack asked her. “天啊，你的脚踝扭伤了，能爬上去吗？”杰克问她。

“No big deal,” she said. “But what about you, with your hurt shoulder and arm?” “没什么大不了的，”她说。 “那你的肩膀和手臂受伤了，怎么办？”

“No big deal,” said Jack. “没什么大不了的，”杰克说。

Annie propped her crutch against the tree trunk. Then she grabbed the sides of the ladder and stepped with her good foot onto the bottom rung. Moving slowly and carefully, she started up to the top. “Oww … oww … oww,” she said under her breath as she climbed. 安妮把拐杖靠在树干上。 然后她抓住梯子的两边，用那只没受伤的脚踩上最下面的横杠。 她慢慢地、小心地开始往上爬。 “哎哟……哎哟……哎哟，”她一边往上爬，一边小声地说。

Jack watched as Annie pulled herself inside the tree house. Then he grabbed a rung of the ladder with his left hand. At least this sycamore tree isn’t as tall as the oak tree in Frog Creek, he thought. Keeping his right arm still, he slowly pulled himself up, teetering this way and that on the rope ladder. Finally he painfully heaved himself into the tree house, too. He was exhausted. He had no idea how they were going to climb back down. 杰克看着安妮把自己拉进了树屋。 然后他用左手抓住了梯子的一根横杠。 他心想：至少这棵梧桐树没有蛙溪镇那棵橡树那么高。 他保持右臂不动，慢慢地往上爬，绳梯上左右摇晃。 最后，他痛苦地把自己拉进了树屋。他累极了。 他一点也不知道他们该怎么再爬下来。

“Ready to go?” Annie asked. She had already opened the Pennsylvania book to the picture of Frog Creek. “准备好了吗？”安妮问。 她已经把宾夕法尼亚那本书翻到蛙溪镇的那张图片那里了。

Jack sighed. “Yeah, but I don’t know how we’re going to explain your ankle and my shoulder to Mom and Dad.” 杰克叹了口气。 “是啊，但我不知道我们怎么向爸妈解释你的脚踝和我的肩膀。”

“We’ll solve that problem when we get home,” said Annie. She pointed at the picture. “I wish we could go there! Good-bye, Thebes!” “等我们回家再解决这个问题，”安妮说。 她指着那幅画。 “我希望我们能去那里！ 再见了，底比斯！

The wind started to blow. 风开始吹起来了。

The tree house started to spin. 树屋开始旋转起来。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 完全静止了。

Jack’s arm sling was gone. He moved his shoulder carefully. The pain was gone, too! He breathed a huge sigh of relief. He was happy to be wearing his own clothes again, and even happier that his arm and shoulder were perfectly fine. 杰克的护臂不见了。 他小心地动了动肩膀。 疼痛也消失了！ 他长长地松了一口气。 他很高兴又能穿上自己的衣服，更开心的是，他的手臂和肩膀都完好无损了。

“No more bandage!” said Annie. “No more sprained ankle!” “再也不用绷带了！”，安妮说。 “再也不用扭伤脚踝了！”，

Jack and Annie looked at each other in wonder. “Cool,” they said together. 杰克和安妮惊讶地互相看着。 “太棒了，”，他们异口同声地说。

“I guess what happens in Thebes stays in Thebes,” said Jack. “我想发生在底比斯的事就留在底比斯吧，”，杰克说。

Annie laughed. 安妮笑了。

“Okay! We have the third secret of greatness now,” said Jack. He took out his pencil and picked up the piece of paper from the floor. Under the words humility and hard work, he added, “好的！ “我们现在有了伟大第三个秘密，”杰克说。 他拿出铅笔，捡起地板上的那张纸。 在“谦逊”和“努力工作”下面，他写上了。

meaning and purpose 意义和目标

“Isn’t that two secrets?” said Annie. “这不是两个秘密吗？”安妮说。

“No, in this case, I think it takes two words to say one thing really well,” said Jack. “不，在这种情况下，我认为用两个词来表达一个意思会说得很好，”杰克说。

“I’m glad to know Florence’s life will have meaning and purpose,” said Annie. “I didn’t want to tell her this and freak her out, but she’ll go to nursing school in Germany. And she’ll be a great nurse in the Crimea, and eventually she’ll be the founder of modern nursing.” “我很高兴知道弗洛伦斯的生活会有意义和目标，”安妮说。 “我不想告诉她这件事，以免吓到她，但她会去德国的护理学校学习。” 而且她会在克里米亚成为一名出色的护士，最终她还会成为现代护理的创始人。

“Whew,” said Jack. “哇，”杰克说。

“Yeah, whew,” said Annie. “是啊，哇，”安妮说。

“And you know who else I think had meaning and purpose in his life?” said Jack. “你知道还有谁我觉得他的人生很有意义和目标吗？”杰克说。

“Who?” said Annie. “谁？”安妮说。

“Mustafa,” said Jack. “He worked so hard. But he wouldn’t take money for doing the right thing. And I could tell he really loved his grandson.” “穆斯塔法，”杰克说。 “他工作非常努力。 但他做正确的事时，不要钱。 我能看出来，他真的很爱他的孙子。”

“That’s true!” said Annie. “Mustafa’s life definitely had meaning and purpose.” “那是真的！”安妮说。 “穆斯塔法的一生肯定有它的意义和目标。”

“I’ll bet Ali grows up to be just like him,” said Jack. He took the tiny bottle of mist from his pack and put it on the paper. Then he looked at Annie. “Your ring, m’lady.” “我打赌阿里长大后会和他一样，”杰克说。 他从背包里拿出那瓶小小的雾，把它放在纸上。 然后他看着安妮。 “您的戒指，女士。”

“Oh, I forgot,” she said. She pulled the Ring of Truth off her finger and put it on the paper next to the bottle. “哦，我忘了，”她说。 她把真理之戒从手指上取下来，放在纸上的瓶子旁边。

“Okay! Let’s go home,” said Jack. “好了！ 我们回家吧，”杰克说。

“I’m ready,” said Annie. She led the way down the ladder. “我准备好了，”安妮说。 她带头顺着梯子往下走。

Jack followed. “Wow,” he said. “This ladder is a lot easier without a hurt shoulder and a sprained ankle, isn’t it?” 杰克跟在后面。 “哇，”他说。 “这梯子没有受伤的肩膀和扭伤的脚踝的话，确实容易多了，不是吗？”

“No kidding!” said Annie. “可不是嘛！”安妮说。

Jack and Annie started walking together through the Frog Creek woods. 杰克和安妮一起穿过蛙溪镇的树林。

“Hey, do you think our lives have meaning and purpose?” said Annie. “嘿，你觉得我们的生活有意义和目标吗？”安妮说。

“I guess so …,” said Jack. “We help people.” “我想是的……”，杰克说。 “我们帮助人们。”

“And animals,” said Annie. “Horses, pandas, penguins, polar bears, dogs, elephants, kangaroos, koalas, baby baboons—” “还有动物，”安妮说。 “马、熊猫、企鹅、北极熊、狗、大象、袋鼠、考拉、小狒狒—”

“I’ll tell you one thing,” Jack interrupted. “Right now, my life has only one purpose.” “我告诉你一件事，”杰克打断道。 “现在，我生活的唯一目标就是回家。”

“What’s that?” asked Annie. “是什么？”安妮问。

“To go home, sit on the front porch, read my book, take notes, and drink lemonade,” said Jack. “回家，坐在前廊上，读书，做笔记，喝柠檬水，”杰克说。

“Home, sweet home,” said Annie. “家，甜蜜的家，”安妮说。

“Home, sweet home,” repeated Jack. “家，甜蜜的家，”杰克重复道。

And he and Annie hurried home through the summery, good-smelling, bird-singing, shadowy Frog Creek woods. 他和安妮急忙穿过温暖、香气扑鼻、鸟儿歌唱、树影婆娑的蛙溪镇森林，赶回家。
