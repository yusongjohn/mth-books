WAHOO!

7. Panic 7. 恐慌

Koku shrieked and jumped up and down. 科库尖叫着，又跳又蹦。

Annie slapped Jack on the back. “You did it!” she cried. 安妮拍了拍杰克的后背。 “你做到了！”她喊道。

“We did it,” Jack said, breathing hard. “Thanks for saving me when I lost my grip!” He shook his finger at the baby baboon. “Bad time for an ear grab, Koku von Kensky!” “是我们一起做到了，”杰克喘着气说。 “谢谢你在我差点掉下去的时候救了我！”他指着小狒狒晃了晃手指。 “这不是抓耳朵的好时机，科库·冯·肯斯基！”

Koku pressed her lips against Jack’s cheek and gave him a kiss. “Eww, gross!” said Jack, laughing. 科库把嘴唇贴在杰克的脸颊上，给了他一个吻。 “哇，真恶心！”杰克笑着说。

“She’s so happy we saved her!” said Annie. “她这么开心，因为我们救了她！”安妮说。

Jack glanced down at the ground below the cliffs to make sure the jackals hadn’t returned. “Okay, let’s rest a minute,” he said. He sat back, closed his eyes, and took a long, deep breath. 杰克低头看了看悬崖下方，确认豺狼没有回来。 “好吧，我们休息一会儿，”他说。 他坐下来，闭上眼睛，深深吸了一口气。

“Koku, would you like a treat?” Annie said. “库库，你想吃点东西吗？”安妮说。

“Here,” said Jack. He opened his eyes and reached into his pouch. He took out a bit of bread and gave it to Koku. She chewed happily as the three of them sat together, looking out over the land of western Thebes. “给你，”杰克说。 他睁开眼睛，伸手到他的口袋里。 他拿出一块面包，给了库库。 她开心地嚼着，他们三个人坐在一起，望着西方底比斯的土地。

“What a great view,” said Jack. “这里的景色真棒，”杰克说。

The sun was setting behind the honey-colored mountains, and the River Nile glimmered with golden light. The cornfields were golden, too, as herds of sheep and goats headed home. Jack felt the same calm feeling he’d felt during his climb. He felt free of all his fears and worries. 太阳正落在蜂蜜色的山后面，尼罗河闪烁着金色的光芒。 玉米地也是金黄色的，羊群和山羊群正回家去。 杰克感到一种和他爬山时一样的平静感觉。 他感觉所有的恐惧和烦恼都消失了。

As the sky turned from gold to red to dark purple, the wind began to blow. The air grew cooler. Sand swirled around the two Colossi of Memnon. Jack imagined that the giants’ breath was stirring the sands. He shivered and pulled out the handbook and looked at the map of Thebes. 当天空从金色变成红色，再变成深紫色时，风开始吹起来了。 空气变得凉爽了一些。 沙子在两个门农巨像周围旋转着。 杰克想象着，巨人呼出的气正在搅动着沙子。 他打了个哆嗦，拿出手册，看了看底比斯的地图。

“Okay. On the other side of the Nile are the Temples of Karnak and Luxor,” Jack said. He looked across the Nile to the distant temples. Beyond the temples, a full moon was starting to rise. Jack looked back at the map. “And on this side of the Nile are the Valley of the Kings and the Valley of the Queens.” “好的。 尼罗河对岸是卡纳克神庙和卢克索神庙，”杰克说。 他望向尼罗河对岸，远处的神庙清晰可见。 在神庙后面，一个满月开始升起。 杰克回头看了看地图。 “在尼罗河这一边是国王谷和王后谷。”

“Look!” said Annie. She pointed to the fallen temple near the road to the river. “Do you see two people?” “看！”安妮说。 她指着靠近河边道路旁倒塌的神庙。 “你看到两个人了吗？”

Sure enough, in the distance two people were riding a horse and a donkey. “It’s them!” Jack said. “Florence and Mustafa! Maybe they can help us get back, since we don’t have donkeys anymore.” He stood up and waved his arms. “Hello!” 果然，在远处有两个人骑着一匹马和一头驴子。 “是他们！”杰克说。 “弗洛伦斯和穆斯塔法！ 也许他们能帮我们回去，因为我们现在没有驴子了。”他站起来，挥舞着双臂。 “你好！”

Annie joined him. “Hello!” she yelled. But Florence and Mustafa didn’t seem to hear them as they rode back toward the Nile. 安妮走到他身边。 “你好！”她喊道。 但弗洛伦斯和穆斯塔法似乎没听到他们，因为他们正骑着马往尼罗河方向回去。

“Hello!” Annie called once more, but it was no use. “你好！”安妮又喊了一遍，但没用。

“Well, we’ll just walk fast and catch up with them at the boat,” said Jack. “那我们就快点走，到船那里再赶上他们吧，”杰克说。

“We still have time to get down, don’t we?” said Annie. “I mean, our hour isn’t up yet, is it?” “我们还有时间下去，对吧？”安妮说。 “我的意思是，我们的一个小时还没到，对吧？”

“I … I don’t think so,” said Jack. He couldn’t believe he’d forgotten about the time! “How long did it take us to climb up? How long have we been sitting here?” “我……我想没有，”杰克说。 他简直不敢相信自己竟然忘了时间！ “我们花了多长时间才爬上来？“ 我们在这里坐了多久了？

“I don’t know,” said Annie. “我不知道，”安妮说。

The sun had sunk below the horizon, and the sky was quickly growing dark. 太阳已经沉到地平线以下，天空很快就变暗了。

“We have to hurry!” said Jack. “Before our hour runs out! Before we lose our rock-climbing skills! Before it gets darker! Before the hills become more dangerous!” “我们必须快点！”杰克说。 “在我们的时间用完之前！” 在我们失去攀岩技巧之前！ 在天黑之前！ 在山变得危险之前！

“Relax, Jack,” said Annie. “Remember, rock climbers go slowly. We look. We think. We move.” “放松点，杰克，”安妮说。 “记住，攀岩的人要慢慢来。我们得仔细看。” 我们思考。我们行动。”

“Right,” said Jack. “Right. Okay.” He took a deep breath and tried to slow his racing heart. He tried not to think about running out of time, or losing their rock-climbing skills, or the danger in the dark valley. “对，”杰克说。 “对。 好吧。”他深吸一口气，试图让狂跳的心平静下来。 他努力不去想时间不够了，或者他们失去了攀岩技巧，或者黑暗山谷里的危险。

“You okay?” said Annie. “你还好吗？”安妮说。

“Yes,” said Jack. “没事，”杰克说。

They looked over the edge of the cliff at the rock face. Jack realized he still knew exactly what to do. “No big deal,” he said. 他们俯视着悬崖边的岩壁。 杰克意识到他仍然清楚该怎么做。 “没什么大不了的，”他说。

“No big deal,” echoed Annie. “没什么大不了的，”安妮重复道。

Jack mapped out his route to the ground. He located bumps … ledges … holes … ridges … cracks … until he had a good plan. 杰克在地面上画出了他的路线。 他找到了凸起……台阶……洞穴……山脊……裂缝……直到他有了一个好计划。

“I’ll carry Koku on my back. She’s not heavy,” said Annie. “I’ll start down over there.” She moved about twenty feet away from Jack. “Don’t forget, go slowly,” she reminded Jack. “And don’t freak out! We look, we think, we move.” “我会背着库库走。 她不重，”安妮说。 “我会从那边开始下去。”她走到杰克大约二十英尺远的地方。 “别忘了，要慢慢来，”她提醒杰克。 “别惊慌！ 我们观察，我们思考，我们行动。”

“Yeah, right, I know,” said Jack. “Just concentrate on yourself.” “对，没错，我知道，”杰克说。 “专心注意你自己。”

Jack turned away from Annie and Koku. He took a full, deep breath. When he felt a perfect balance between calm and alertness, he started down the cliff wall. 杰克转身背对安妮和科库。 他深吸了一口气。 当他感到自己在平静和警觉之间达到了完美的平衡，他开始沿着悬崖壁往下走。

Jack inched smoothly along the face of the rock, shifting his weight and keeping his eyes on his feet. He reached down to lower ledges with his toes and lodged his fingertips in the cracks above. He kept his body straight and didn’t lean too closely into the rock. Jack knew exactly what to do—until he was halfway down. Then suddenly he didn’t know anything. 杰克平稳地沿着岩石表面移动，调整身体的重心，眼睛一直盯着自己的脚。 他弯下身子，用脚尖触碰下方的台阶，并把手指插进上方的裂缝里。 他保持身体笔直，没有靠得太近岩石。 杰克确切地知道该怎么做——直到他走了一半。 然后，突然之间，他什么也不知道了。

Jack froze. His body started to shake. His toes slipped from the cracks they were in. His hands slipped from the ridge he was gripping. He fell. 杰克僵住了。 他的身体开始发抖。 他的脚趾从裂缝里滑了出去。 他的手从他抓着的山脊上滑了下去。他掉了下来。

Jack hit the ground so hard he couldn’t breathe. His whole body screamed with pain, especially his right shoulder and arm. 杰克重重地摔在地上，喘不过气来。 他的全身都疼得叫了出来，尤其是他的右肩和右臂。

“Jaaack!” Annie cried out from above him. Then there was a terrible thud. “杰克！”安妮从他上面喊道。 然后传来一声可怕的巨响。

Jack heard Koku shriek. He wanted desperately to help Annie and the baby baboon, but he was still struggling to breathe. He couldn’t stand up. He couldn’t move at all! 杰克听到库库尖叫起来。 他非常想帮助安妮和小狒狒宝宝，但他还在努力喘气。 他站不起来。 他完全动不了！

Jackals started howling. It sounded like many more than just two. It sounded like a whole pack. The howling grew louder, then faded away. 豺狼开始嚎叫。 听起来好像不止两只。 听起来像是一整群。 嚎叫声越来越响，然后渐渐消失了。

“An-An …,” Jack whispered. He couldn’t even say her name. The wind made loud whistling sounds through the limestone cliffs. “安-安……，”杰克小声说。 他甚至说不出她的名字。 风穿过石灰岩悬崖，发出刺耳的呼啸声。

Jack was cold and scared. He worried that his bones were broken. He worried that Annie was hurt—or worse, maybe not alive. 杰克又冷又害怕。 他担心自己的骨头断了。 他担心安妮受伤了——或者更糟，可能已经不在人世了。

Koku made whining, weepy sounds. Then Jack heard a moan: “Jack!” 科库发出呜咽和抽泣的声音。 然后杰克听到一声呻吟：‘杰克！’

“Annie!” Jack said hoarsely. In spite of the pain, he forced himself to sit up. He saw Annie on the ground, lying not far from him. ‘安妮！’杰克沙哑地喊道。 尽管很疼，他还是强迫自己坐起来。 他看见安妮在地上，离他不远的地方躺着。

“I got the breath—knocked—out of me,” she said, gasping. “Couldn’t breathe. Or talk. Now my ankle … ohhh …,” she groaned. “我被击倒了——喘不过气来，”她喘着气说。 “我没法呼吸，也说不出话来。 现在我的脚踝……哦……”，她呻吟道。

“Hold on, don’t move!” said Jack. As he struggled to a standing position, he discovered that he couldn’t lift his right arm at all. It just hung limply from his shoulder at a strange angle. “抓紧，别动！”杰克说。 当他努力站起身时，他发现他完全无法抬起右臂。 它只是松松地垂在肩膀上，呈一个奇怪的角度。

Jack took a deep breath. He clutched his bad arm and walked barefoot to Annie. He had no idea where they’d left their boots. It was too dark to find them now. 杰克深吸了一口气。 他紧紧抓住受伤的胳膊，光着脚走到安妮身边。 他不知道他们把靴子放在了哪里。 现在太黑了，找不到它们。

“Ohhh,” said Annie. She was lying on her side, holding her right ankle and moaning. “哦——”，安妮说。 她侧躺着，抱着右脚踝，发出呜咽声。

“Can you stand up?” said Jack. “你能站起来吗？”杰克说。

“Maybe,” said Annie. “也许吧，”安妮说。

“I’ll help,” said Jack. He used one hand to help Annie roll onto her back and stand up. “我会帮忙，”杰克说。 他用一只手帮安妮翻过身，站起来。

“OWW!” she cried. “哎哟！”她喊道。

“Sorry, sorry,” said Jack. “对不起，对不起，”杰克说。

Annie tried to laugh. “No, I’ m sorry,” she said. “I didn’t mean to yell, but it really hurts if I put weight on it. What can we do? How can we get back?” 安妮试着笑了笑。 “不，对不起，”她说。 “我不是故意喊的，但如果我踩到它，真的会很疼。我们该怎么办？ 我们怎么回去呢？

“Don’t worry, I can walk,” said Jack. “You can lean on me and hop on one foot.” “别担心，我能走路，”杰克说。 “你可以靠在我身上，单脚跳。”

“Are you sure you’re not too badly hurt?” asked Annie. “你确定你没有受伤吗？”安妮问。

“No big deal,” Jack said. “Just lean on my left side. We’ll be fine.” “没什么大不了的，”杰克说。 “靠在我左边，我们会没事的。”

“Okay.” Annie placed her arm on Jack’s shoulder. “好的。”安妮把她的手臂搭在杰克的肩膀上。

“Here, Koku, hold my hand tight,” said Annie. She held out her left hand, and the baby baboon grabbed it. “来，库库，紧紧抓住我的手，”安妮说。 她伸出左手，小狒狒宝宝抓住了它。

“Ready, everyone?” Jack asked. “大家都准备好了吗？”杰克问。

“Ready,” said Annie. “准备好了，”安妮说。

“Good,” said Jack. “Let’s give it a try” “很好，”杰克说。 “我们试试看吧”
