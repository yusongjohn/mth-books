2. Welcome to Thebes! 2. 欢迎来到底比斯！

Warm, dry air filled the tree house. Jack wore a helmet-type hat, leather boots, a long-sleeved shirt, and a pair of heavy linen pants with a leather belt. A large pouch was attached to the belt. 温暖干燥的空气充满了树屋。 杰克戴着一顶头盔式的帽子，穿着皮靴、长袖衬衫，还穿着一条配有皮带的厚重亚麻裤子。 一个大口袋被缝在腰带上。

“I wish I was dressed like you,” said Annie, making a face. She was wearing a long white dress with frilly lace. “You look like a cool explorer. I look like I’m going to a tea party.” “我希望我穿得和你一样，”安妮说着，做了个鬼脸。 她穿着一条长长的白色连衣裙，裙边有蕾丝花边。 “你看起来像一个酷酷的探险家。 我看起来像是要去参加茶会。”

“Don’t feel bad,” said Jack. “My clothes are really scratchy and heavy.” “别难过了，”杰克说。 “我的衣服真的很扎人，还特别沉。”

HEE-HAW!

“Is that a donkey?” said Annie. She and Jack looked out the window of the tree house. Leaves and branches completely blocked their view. “那是一只驴吗？”安妮说。 她和杰克从树屋的窗户往外看。 树叶和树枝完全挡住了他们的视线。

“I think we landed in a sycamore tree,” said Jack, studying the leaves. “我觉得我们降落在了一棵悬铃木上，”杰克一边研究着树叶，一边说道。

Annie pushed some branches aside. All they could see below were more leaves. But straight ahead, in the distance, was a wide plain dotted with sand-colored ruins. Beyond the plain, mountains loomed against a cloudless blue sky. The Egyptian sun was blindingly bright. 安妮推开了一些树枝。 他们下面只能看到更多的树叶。 但正前方，在远处，是一片广阔的平原，上面散布着沙黄色的废墟。 平原的另一边，群山在无云的蓝天背景下若隐若现。 埃及的太阳亮得刺眼。

HEE-HAW!

“That’s definitely a donkey,” said Annie. “Let’s go look.” She gathered up her long white dress and started down the rope ladder. “那 definitely 是一头驴，”安妮说道。 “我们去看看吧。”她提起自己长长的白色裙子，开始顺着绳梯往下爬。

Jack stuck the small handbook into his leather pouch. He saw that his notebook, his pencil, and the bottle of magic mist were also inside the pouch, along with some coins that showed images of pharaohs. “Hey, we have some Egyptian money!” he called down to Annie. 杰克把那本小手册塞进了他的皮革小包里。 他看到自己的笔记本、铅笔和一瓶魔法雾也都在包里，还有一些印着法老图案的硬币。 “嘿，我们有一些埃及的钱！”他朝安妮喊道。

“Great, come on down!” said Annie. She was already on the ground. “太好了，快下来吧！”安妮说道。 她已经在地上了。

Jack buckled his pouch, then clumsily climbed down the rope ladder in his leather boots. As soon as he stepped onto the grass, flies landed on his face. He shook his head and waved his hands, trying to brush them away. 杰克系好他的袋子，然后笨手笨脚地沿着绳梯爬下来，穿着他的皮靴。 他刚一踏上草地，苍蝇就落在了他的脸上。 他摇摇头，挥挥手，试图把它们赶走。

The sycamore tree was surrounded by bushes and other plants on a lush green riverbank. Across the river, several dozen sailboats were anchored near a temple. 那棵梧桐树被灌木和其他植物包围着，位于一条郁郁葱葱的河岸上。 在河对岸，几十艘帆船停泊在一座神庙附近。

HEE-HAW! 嘿嘿哈！

The sound came from beyond the bushes. Jack and Annie stepped around them and peeked out. “Yep, donkeys. Two of them,” said Jack. “And there’s a little kid with them.” 声音来自灌木丛的另一边。 杰克和安妮绕过去，偷偷往外看。 “没错，是驴子。” “其中有两个人，”杰克说。 “而且他们还带着一个小孩。”

About fifty feet down the river, two small donkeys were standing under a cluster of palm trees. They were shaking their long, furry ears and swishing their tails to keep the flies away. A boy was napping in a rowboat on the riverbank. He wore a striped robe. 沿着河往下大约五十英尺的地方，两只小驴子正站在一片棕榈树下。 它们正在摇晃着长长的、毛茸茸的耳朵，甩着尾巴赶走苍蝇。 一个男孩正在河岸的划艇上打盹。 他穿着一件条纹长袍。

“Want to talk to him?” said Annie. “想和他说话吗？”安妮说。

“Sure,” said Jack. “当然，”杰克说。

They walked out of the bushes and headed toward the boy. “Hello!” Annie called. 他们从灌木丛里走出来，朝那个男孩走去。 “你好！”安妮喊道。

The small boy scrambled out of the rowboat. He looked to be only six or seven. “I did not see you coming,” he said. “Welcome to Thebes! My name is Ali. Do you need donkeys and a guide?” 那个小男孩从划艇里爬了出来。 他看起来只有六七岁。 “我没看到你过来，”他说。 “欢迎来到底比斯！我叫阿里。” 你们需要骆驼和向导吗？”，

“No thanks,” said Jack. “不用了，谢谢，”杰克说。

“My grandfather is the best guide in Thebes,” Ali said with pride. “He is returning now with two travelers from England. There he is! On the horse!” “我爷爷是底比斯最好的向导，”阿里骄傲地说。 “他现在正和两位来自英格兰的旅客一起回来。那就是他！” “在马上！”

In the distance, a white-bearded man wearing a turban was riding a packhorse. He was leading a man and woman on donkeys toward the river. 在远处，一个留着白胡子、戴着头巾的男人正骑着一匹驮马。 他正带领着一男一女骑着驴子朝河边走去。

“After my grandfather rows them across the Nile to their boat, he can guide you to the tombs in the cliffs!” said Ali. “Or the Temple of Luxor.” He pointed to the temple across the river. “等我爷爷把他们划过尼罗河到他们的船上后，他可以带你去悬崖上的墓穴！”阿里说。 “或者卢克索神庙。”他指着河对岸的神庙。

“Thanks. Maybe later,” said Jack. “谢谢。 也许以后吧，”杰克说。

“We are here every day. Come back!” said Ali, and he ran to meet his grandfather and the travelers from England. “我们每天都来这里。 改天再来吧！”阿里说，然后他跑过去迎接他的爷爷和来自英国的旅客。

“We just learned a lot,” Jack said to Annie. “It seems we landed in Thebes, Egypt, on the River Nile, across from the Temple of Luxor.” “我们刚刚学到了很多，”杰克对安妮说。 “看起来我们降落在埃及的底比斯，位于尼罗河畔，就在卢克索神庙的对面。”

“Sounds like a fairy tale,” said Annie. “听起来像童话故事，”安妮说。

Jack pulled out their Egypt handbook. He found Thebes and read aloud: 杰克拿出他们的埃及手册。 他找到了底比斯，大声读起来：

Travelers enjoy visiting the area of Thebes in Egypt. Four thousand years ago, the Egyptian city was the capital of the known world. At that time, it was the noisiest and liveliest place on the River Nile. 游客们喜欢参观埃及底比斯地区。 四千年前，这座城市是已知世界的首都。 那时，它是尼罗河上最热闹、最繁华的地方。

“Seriously?” said Jack. He looked around at the quiet riverbank, the donkeys, and the distant bare mountains. “真的吗？”杰克说。 他环顾四周，看着安静的河岸、驴子和远处光秃秃的山脉。

“I guess times have changed for Thebes,” said Annie. “我想底比斯的时光已经改变了，”安妮说。

“No kidding,” said Jack. “So I wonder what Florence Nightingale is doing here.” “别开玩笑了，”杰克说。 “那我就好奇了，弗洛伦斯·南丁格尔为什么会在这里。”

“Nursing,” said Annie. “She has to be! That’s what she’s famous for. Maybe those English travelers know something about her.” Annie pointed to the couple riding with Ali’s grandfather. “Remember, Florence was from England, too.” “她在护理，”安妮说。 “她肯定得在这里！ 这就是她出名的原因。 也许那些英国旅行者知道一些关于她的事。”安妮指着和阿里爷爷一起骑马的那对夫妇。 “记住，弗洛伦斯也是来自英国的。”

Jack and Annie watched the three riders arrive at the riverbank. Ali’s grandfather climbed off his packhorse and helped the couple off their donkeys. As Ali and his grandfather gave the donkeys water, the English woman noticed Jack and Annie. “Hello, children!” she called, waving. 杰克和安妮看着三个骑手到达河边。 阿里的爷爷从他的驮马上下来，帮那对夫妇从驴背上下来。 当阿里和他的爷爷给驴子喝水时，那个英国女人注意到了杰克和安妮。 “你好，孩子们！”她喊道，挥了挥手。

Jack and Annie waved back, and the couple headed toward them. “How delightful to see new faces in Thebes!” the woman said. 杰克和安妮也挥了挥手，那对夫妇朝他们走来。 “很高兴在底比斯见到新朋友！”那女人说道。

“Yes!” said the man, smiling. “Who are you? Where are you from?” The man and woman were both stout and middle-aged, but they had a young, exuberant air about them. “是啊！”男人笑着说。 “你们是谁？ 你们来自哪里？”男人和女人都身材魁梧，年过中年，但身上却带着年轻人般的活力。

“I’m Annie, and he’s my brother, Jack,” said Annie. “We’re from Frog Creek, Pennsylvania.” “我是安妮，他是我的哥哥，杰克，”安妮说道。 “我们来自宾夕法尼亚州的蛙溪镇。”

“Americans! Wonderful!” said the woman. “We are from England. My name is Selina Bracebridge. I’m traveling with my husband, Charles.” “美国人！ 太棒了！”女人说道。 “我们来自英格兰。” 我的名字是塞尔ينا·布拉西布里奇。 “我和我丈夫查尔斯一起旅行。”

“And I am that very Charles,” said Charles. “With whom are you two traveling?” “我就是那个查尔斯，”查尔斯说。 “你们俩是和谁一起旅行的？”

“Uh … our parents,” said Annie, “but they left us on our own in Thebes.” “呃……是我们的父母，”安妮说，“但他们把我们独自留在底比斯了。”

“To visit the ruins,” said Jack. “They said it would be … um … a great educational experience.” “去参观废墟，”杰克说。 “他们说这会……嗯……是一次很棒的教育经历。”

“What brave American children you are!” Selina said. “And what unusual parents.” “你们真是勇敢的美国孩子！”塞琳娜说。 “还有你们不寻常的父母。”

“Indeed. And where are you brave American children staying?” asked Charles. “确实。” “勇敢的美国孩子们，你们住在哪里？”查尔斯问道。

“Uh … up the Nile, that way,” said Annie. She waved her hand vaguely toward the tree house. “In a little house. It’s sort of like an inn.” “呃……在尼罗河那边，那个方向，”安妮说。 她挥了挥手，模模糊糊地指向树屋。 “在一间小房子里。 有点像旅馆。”

“I see. Good, good!” said Charles. “Well, can we do anything to help you? With your Thebes educational experience?” “我明白了。 很好，很好！”查尔斯说。 “那么，我们能做些什么来帮助你们吗？ 用你们在底比斯的教育经历？”

Jack could tell the man was joking, but Annie answered right away. “Actually you can help us,” she said. “Have either of you heard of a woman named Florence Nightingale? At the place we’re staying, someone said she was in Thebes.” 杰克能看出来那个人是在开玩笑，但安妮立刻回答了。 “其实，你可以帮我们，”她说。 “你们之中有人听说过一个叫弗洛伦斯·南丁格尔的女人吗？ 在我们住的地方，有人说她是在底比斯。”

Selina’s eyes widened in amazement. “Charles, did you hear that? They’re looking for Flo!” 塞琳娜惊讶地睁大了眼睛。 “查尔斯，你听到吗？ 他们在找弗洛！”

Jack looked at Annie, then back at Charles and Selina. “You know Florence Nightingale?” he said. 杰克看了看安妮，又看了看查尔斯和塞琳娜。 “你们认识弗洛伦斯·南丁格尔？”他问。

“Know her? She’s our best friend!” said Selina. “She’s traveling with us, for goodness’ sake!” She pointed at the sailboats anchored along the river. “认识她？ “她是我们最好的朋友！”塞莉娜说。 “她和我们一起去旅行，天哪！”她指着河岸边停泊的帆船。

“For many weeks now, she has been sailing up and down the Nile with us in our boat,” said Charles. “从几周前开始，她就一直和我们乘船在尼罗河上航行，”查尔斯说。

“That is amazing!” Annie said to Jack. “太棒了！”安妮对杰克说。

“Totally!” he said. “对极了！”他说。

“How do you two know Flo?” asked Selina. “你们俩怎么认识弗洛的？”塞莉娜问。

“Well, we don’t exactly know her,” said Annie. “We just know she’s a world-famous nurse.” “嗯，我们其实并不真正认识她，”安妮说。 “我们只知道她是一位世界著名的护士。”

“A what?” said Selina. “什么？”塞莉娜说。

“A world-famous … nurse?” said Annie. “Like, a nurse in a hospital?” “世界著名的……护士？”安妮说。 “比如，医院里的护士？”

Charles and Selina both laughed. “Oh, no!” said Selina. “Not Flo! She’s helped sick relatives and villagers in their homes. But she’s not the least bit famous for that!” 查尔斯和塞莉娜都笑了。 “哦，不！”塞莉娜说。 “不是弗洛！ 她曾在家里帮助生病的亲戚和村民。 但她因此一点也没出名！

“And she’s certainly never worked in a hospital!” said Charles. “I’m afraid you have found the wrong Florence Nightingale.” “而且她肯定从来没在医院工作过！”查尔斯说。 “恐怕你找错了弗洛伦斯·南丁格尔。”

“Oh,” said Jack. How many Florence Nightingales can there be? he wondered. “哦，”杰克说。 到底有多少个弗洛伦斯·南丁格尔呢？他想。

“But your Flo’s a great person, right?” Annie said. “但是你的弗洛是个很棒的人，对吧？”安妮说。

“We certainly think so!” said Selina. “我们当然这么认为！”塞莉娜说。

“Well, then we’d still like to meet her,” said Annie. “那我们还是想见见她，”安妮说。

“Righto!” said Selina. “Flo is visiting the Temple of Luxor this morning. Why don’t you come with us to our boat and wait for her to come back?” “好啊！”塞莉娜说。 “弗洛今天早上要去卢克索神庙。 你们为什么不和我们一起去船上，等她回来呢？”

“Oh, thank you!” said Annie. “哦，谢谢你！”安妮说。

“Excellent!” said Charles. “Mustafa will ferry us across in his rowboat.” He turned to the bearded guide and his grandson. “Mustafa, shall we be off?” he called. “Good-bye, Ali!” “太棒了！”查尔斯说。 “穆斯塔法会用他的划艇带我们过去。”他转向那位有胡子的导游和他的孙子。 “穆斯塔法，我们出发吧？”他喊道。 “再见，阿里！”

The boy waved good-bye, and his grandfather pulled the rowboat partway into the water. 男孩挥了挥手说再见，他的祖父把小船划到水里一部分。

“Children first,” said Charles. “孩子们先上，”查尔斯说。

Mustafa held the rowboat steady as Jack and Annie climbed aboard and sat down. Then he helped Charles and Selina aboard and climbed in after them. 穆斯塔法稳住小船，杰克和安妮上了船并坐下来。 然后他帮助查尔斯和塞莉娜上船，自己也跟着爬了上去。

Once everyone was settled, Mustafa pushed offshore with his oars and started rowing across the Nile. As the boat glided over the sun-sparkling water, the old Egyptian softly sang a song. Jack couldn’t understand the words, but the song was soothing, sung in rhythm with the movement of the oars. 一旦大家都坐好了，穆斯塔法用桨把船划离岸边，开始划过尼罗河。 当小船在阳光照耀的水面上滑行时，那位年迈的埃及人轻声唱起了一首歌。 杰克听不懂歌词，但这首歌很舒缓，节奏与桨的摆动一致。

“This is perfect,” Annie whispered. “Now all we have to do is spend time with Florence Nightingale and wait for the Ring of Truth to glow.” “这太完美了，”安妮小声说。 “现在我们只需要和南丁格尔一起度过一些时间，然后等待真理之环发光就行了。”

“Yeah, but she’s not a great nurse,” whispered Jack, fanning away the flies. “She’s not famous for being great at anything.” “是啊，但她不是个好护士，”杰克小声说，赶走飞来的苍蝇。 “她什么都没特别擅长。”

“I know. That’s a little confusing,” Annie whispered back. “我知道。” “那有点让人搞不清楚，”安妮小声说。

“Look, children!” said Charles. “Isn’t that a magnificent sight?” He pointed to a huge crocodile sunning itself on a river rock. “孩子们，快看！”查尔斯说。 “这不是太壮观了吗？”他指着河岸上一块大石头，上面有一条巨大的鳄鱼正在晒太阳。

“Whoa!” said Jack. “哇！”杰克说。

“Yikes!” said Annie at the same time. The crocodile had a scaly green hide with black spots. Its green eyes glimmered as the rowboat passed by. “天哪！”安妮同时说道。 这条鳄鱼的皮肤是带黑点的绿色鳞片。 当小船经过时，它绿色的眼睛闪闪发亮。

“Don’t be alarmed,” said Charles. “In our experience, Nile crocodiles are completely harmless.” “别担心，”查尔斯说。 “根据我们的经验，尼罗河鳄鱼完全无害。”

Harmless? Crocodiles? thought Jack. I don’t think so. 无害？鳄鱼？ 杰克想。我不这么认为。

Mustafa stopped singing as they reached the landing on the opposite riverbank. He climbed out of the rowboat and tied it up. Then he helped Annie and Jack onto the bank. Selina and Charles followed. 穆斯塔法在他们到达对岸河岸的台阶时停止了唱歌。 他从划艇里爬出来，把船系好了。 然后他帮安妮和杰克上了岸。 塞琳娜和查尔斯跟了上来。

Charles handed some money to the guide. “Thank you, Mustafa,” he said. “Please wait on this shore, as I believe Miss Nightingale plans to visit western Thebes later today, when it’s a bit cooler. This way, children.” 查尔斯给导游递了一些钱。 “谢谢您，穆斯塔法，”他说。 “请在这边等一下，我觉得南丁格尔小姐今天稍晚些时候会去西底比斯，那时候天气会凉快一点。” 这边来，孩子们。

Jack and Annie followed Charles and Selina as they walked briskly along the landing, passing the line of sailboats moored at the water’s edge. “Whose boats are those?” asked Annie. 杰克和安妮跟着查尔斯和塞琳娜，他们快步沿着码头走，经过停泊在水边的一排帆船。 “那些船是谁的？”安妮问。

“They are rented by travelers from all over Europe,” said Charles, “most of whom are hiding from the midday heat right now.” “这些船是来自欧洲各地的游客租的，”查尔斯说，“他们大多数人现在正在躲中午的热浪。”

I don’t blame them, thought Jack. The heat and the flies were almost more than he could bear. He felt sorry for the workers on the boat decks, scrubbing floors and mending sails. 杰克心想，我不能责怪他们。 这热浪和蚊子几乎让他受不了。 他为在船上甲板上打扫地板和修补帆布的工人感到难过。

“Here we are!” said Selina. She and Charles stopped at the largest boat anchored on the river. “Home, sweet home!” “我们到了！”塞琳娜说。 她和查尔斯停在了河里停泊的最大一艘船上。 “家，温暖的家！”
