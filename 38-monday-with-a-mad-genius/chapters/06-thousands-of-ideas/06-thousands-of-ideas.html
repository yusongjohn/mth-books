6. Thousands of Ideas 6. 成千上万的主意

A low fire burned in the hearth. Sunlight slanted across the warm room. Jack caught his breath as he looked around Leonardo’s studio. 壁炉里燃着一小团火。 阳光斜斜地照进温暖的房间里。 杰克环顾着莱昂纳多的工作室，屏住了呼吸。

There were mirrors, wooden trunks, globes, paint pots, and brushes. Sketches, paintings, and handmade maps were all over the walls. There were stacks of old books, half-built furniture, piled-up papers, theater masks, pieces of costumes, and musical instruments. 房间里有镜子、木箱、地球仪、颜料罐和画笔。 墙壁上到处都是草图、画作和手绘的地图。 还有堆成堆的旧书、半成品家具、堆积如山的纸张、戏剧面具、服装碎片和乐器。

“Oh, man,” murmured Jack, “I love this room.” “天啊，”杰克小声说，“我太喜欢这个房间了。”

“Me too,” said Annie. “我也是，”安妮说。

“Please, sit at my table. Let me get you something to eat,” said Leonardo. He pushed a bunch of things to the side of a long wooden table and pulled up two chairs. “请，坐到我的桌子旁。” “让我给你拿点吃的，”列奥纳多说。 他把一些东西推到一张长长的木桌旁边，然后拉出两把椅子。

“Thanks,” said Jack. He and Annie sat down. “谢谢，”杰克说。 他和安妮坐了下来。

Leonardo took the cheese and bread from the basket by the door and gave some to Jack and Annie. The cheese was dry but tasted good. And the bread was really delicious—hard and crackly on the outside, but soft and chewy on the inside. Hmm, I wonder how they make it like this, Jack thought. 列奥纳多从门边的篮子里拿出奶酪和面包，给了杰克和安妮一些。 奶酪是干的，但味道很好。 面包真的很好吃——外面又硬又脆，里面却柔软有嚼劲。 嗯，我真想知道他们是怎么做出这样的面包的，杰克心想。

“So why do you want to leave Florence, Leonardo?” Annie asked, her mouth full. “那为什么你想离开佛罗伦萨，列奥纳多？”安妮边吃边问。

“Because I will no longer be respected here,” said Leonardo. “Last week, the council told me I must finish my fresco soon. And now I will not finish it at all. Just recently Michelangelo accused me of never finishing anything!” “因为我在这里不再被尊重了，”列奥纳多说。 “上周，委员会告诉我，我必须尽快完成我的壁画。” 而现在，我根本不会完成了。 不久前，米开朗基罗指责我从来完不成任何东西！”

“Michelangelo? The great artist?” said Jack. “米开朗基罗？ 那位伟大的艺术家？”杰克说道。

Leonardo snorted. “You think Michelangelo is a great artist? Have you seen his statues? Those men with their big muscles? They look like sacks of walnuts!” 列奥纳多哼了一声。 “你觉得米开朗基罗是位伟大的艺术家？ 你见过他的雕像吗？ 那些肌肉发达的男人？ 他们看起来就像一袋袋核桃！”

Jack and Annie laughed. 杰克和安妮笑了。

Leonardo tried to hide a smile as he looked at them. “In truth, Michelangelo is a great artist,” he said. “Still, he should not accuse me of never finishing anything … even if it is true.” 列奥纳多试图藏起笑容，看着他们。 “说实话，米开朗基罗是个伟大的艺术家，”他说。 “不过，他不应该指责我从不完成任何事……即使这是真的。”

“Why don’t you finish things?” asked Annie. “你为什么不做完事情呢？”安妮问。

“Well, I shall not finish my battle scene now because I experimented with my paint,” said Leonardo. “I am experimenting all the time. And often my experiments lead nowhere.” “好吧，我现在不能完成我的战斗场景了，因为我试着用我的颜料画画，”列奥纳多说。 “我一直在做实验。 而且我的实验常常没有结果。”

“So is that your main problem?” asked Annie. “那么这就是你最大的问题吗？”安妮问。

“One of them,” said Leonardo, sighing. “The other is that there are too many things I want to do, and there is never enough time!” “其中之一，”列奥纳多叹了口气说。 “另一个问题是，我有太多想做的事情，但时间总是不够！”

“What else do you want to do?” asked Jack. “你还想做什么？”杰克问。

“Oh, I have thousands of ideas,” said Leonardo. He put down his bread and cheese and crossed to a wooden trunk in the corner of his studio. He raised the lid of the trunk and stared for a moment at its contents. “哦，我有成千上万的想法，”列奥纳多说。 他放下面包和奶酪，走到他工作室角落的一只木箱前。 他打开木箱的盖子，盯着里面的东西看了片刻。

Leonardo turned back to Jack and Annie. His eyes were bright again. Whatever was in the trunk had made him happier. “Come, look,” he said. 列奥纳多转身面对杰克和安妮。 他的眼睛又亮了起来。 箱子里的东西让他变得更开心了。 “来，看看，”他说。

Jack and Annie walked over to the trunk and peered inside. It held dozens and dozens of plain black books, large ones and small ones. 杰克和安妮走到木箱前，往里面张望。 里面摆着一大堆又一大堆的黑色平装书，有大本的，也有小本的。

“Notebooks,” said Leonardo. “I have filled over a hundred of them with my ideas.” “笔记本，”列奥纳多说。 “我已经有超过一百本笔记本，里面都写满了我的想法。”

“Oh,” said Jack, his eyes wide. “哦，”杰克说，眼睛睁得大大的。

“Jack keeps notebooks, too!” said Annie. “杰克也有笔记本！”安妮说。

“Do you mind if we look at them?” asked Jack. “你们介意我们看看它们吗？”杰克问。

“No, not at all,” said Leonardo. “不，一点都不介意，”列奥纳多说。

Jack and Annie started picking up the notebooks and turning the pages. The pages were crammed with doodles and writing. They showed sketches of people’s faces, animal heads, flowers, trees, rivers, mountains, the sun, and the moon. 杰克和安妮开始捡起笔记本，翻动书页。 书页上挤满了涂鸦和文字。 上面画了人们的脸、动物的头、花朵、树木、河流、山脉、太阳和月亮的草图。

One notebook was filled with drawings of horses. Another had sketches of bridges and buildings. Another had drawings of birds and machines. Many of the drawings in the notebooks had labels written in a strange language. 一本笔记本里画满了马的图画。 另一本里画了桥和建筑物的草图。 还有一本里画了鸟和机器。 笔记本里的很多画旁边都写着一种奇怪语言的标签。

“You cannot read my notes, can you?” said Leonardo. “你们看不懂我的笔记，对吗？”莱昂纳多说。

Jack and Annie shook their heads. 杰克和安妮摇了摇头。

“Hold them to a mirror,” said Leonardo. “把它们对着镜子看，”莱昂纳多说。

Jack and Annie stood in front of a wall mirror. 杰克和安妮站在一面墙镜前。

Each held up a notebook and looked at its reflection in the mirror. 每个人都举起一本笔记本，看着镜子里的倒影。

“Oh, I get it!” said Jack. He could read the words now! Leonardo had written everything backward—from right to left across the page. So the word bird was written as , and the word wind was written as . “哦，我明白了！”杰克说。 他现在能读这些字了！ 列奥纳多把所有内容都写反了，从右往左横着写在纸上。 所以“bird”这个词写成“drib”，“wind”这个词写成“dniw”。

图中文字：DNIW 图中文字：DNIW

“Why do you write this way?” asked Annie. “你为什么这样写？”安妮问。

“People think I am trying to keep my ideas a secret,” said Leonardo. “But, in truth, I am left-handed, and when I write normally from left to right, I smear ink across the page. One day I realized that if I wrote backward, I would not be so messy.” He laughed and sat down at the table. As he took a bite of bread, he seemed his happy self again. “人们以为我是在试图隐藏我的想法，”列奥纳多说。 “但事实上，我是个左撇子，当我正常从左往右写的时候，墨水会弄脏纸页。 有一天我意识到，如果我反着写，就不会那么乱了。”他笑了，然后坐在桌边。 当他咬了一口面包时，他又变得开心起来。

“What do you write in these notebooks?” said Jack. “你在这本笔记里写些什么？”杰克问。

“Oh, I’ve written down thousands of ideas,” said Leonardo. “For example—” He opened a notebook and read: “Fossils of tiny sea creatures have been found in the mountains of Italy. It is my belief that ocean water once covered the mountains millions of years ago.” “哦，我写下了成千上万的想法，”列奥纳多说。 “比如说——”他打开笔记本，读道：“人们在意大利的山里发现了微小海洋生物的化石。” “我认为，数百万年前，海水曾经覆盖过这些山脉。”

“Your belief is right,” said Jack. “你的想法是对的，”杰克说。

Leonardo looked at Jack with surprise. “You seem so certain,” he said. 列奥纳多惊讶地看着杰克。 “你看起来这么确定，”他说。

“Well, I know from science books that the oceans once covered many mountains of the earth, and that’s why you can find sea fossils there,” said Jack. “嗯，我知道科学书上说，以前海洋曾经覆盖过地球上的许多山脉，所以你在那里能找到海贝壳化石，”杰克说。

“We read lots of books,” said Annie. “我们读了很多书，”安妮说。

“Indeed?” said Leonardo. Then he picked up another notebook and read: “If a wolf stares at you, your voice will become hoarse.” “真的吗？”列奥纳多说。 然后他拿起另一本笔记本，读道：‘如果狼盯着你看，你的声音就会变得沙哑。’

“Um … that’s not true,” said Annie. “呃……那不是真的，”安妮说。

“It is not?” said Leonardo. “不是吗？”莱昂纳多说。

“Well, think about it,” said Annie. “How could an animal make a person hoarse? And why would they want to?” “那想想看，”安妮说。 “动物怎么能让人声音嘶哑呢？ 它们为什么要这么做呢？”

Leonardo nodded. “Yes, I think I agree with you,” he said. He cleared his throat. Then he read another idea: “A spider hatches its eggs by staring at them.” 莱昂纳多点了点头。 “是的，我想我同意你的看法，”他说。 他清了清嗓子。 然后他读了另一个想法：‘蜘蛛通过盯着卵来孵化它们的蛋。’

“Nooo,” said Jack and Annie together. “不——”，杰克和安妮异口同声地说。

“No?” said Leonardo. “不是吗？”莱昂纳多说。

“Trust us,” said Jack, smiling. This is really fun, he thought, knowing more than a great genius. Scientists have discovered a lot since Leonardo’s time. “相信我们，”杰克微笑着说。 这真的很有趣，他心想，自己知道的东西比一个天才还多。 自从列奥纳多的时代以来，科学家们已经发现了许多东西。

“All right. I don’t know why, but I shall trust you,” said Leonardo. He turned some pages and read: “The moon may be bright because it is made out of rippling water.” “好吧。 我不知道为什么，但我相信你们，”列奥纳多说。 他翻了几页，读到：“月亮可能很亮，因为它是由波浪般的水组成的。”

Jack shook his head. “Actually it’s made out of rocks,” he said. “It’s bright because it reflects the light of the sun.” Jack knew lots of facts about the moon. 杰克摇了摇头。 “其实它是由岩石组成的，”他说。 “它之所以亮，是因为它反射了太阳的光。”杰克对月亮知道很多事实。

“And did you know there’s no wind on the moon?” said Annie. “So someday when people walk there, their footprints will last forever!” “你知道吗，月亮上根本没有风？”安妮说。 “所以有一天，当人们走到那里时，他们的脚印会永远留下来！”

Leonardo grinned. “Wonderful,” he said. “I fear you are both speaking nonsense. But I like your original thinking!” 列奥纳多露出了微笑。 “太棒了，”他说。 “我担心你们两个在说胡话。 但我喜欢你们的创意！”

He turned the page and read another entry: 他翻过一页，读起了另一条记录：

“There must be a way to use a natural force, such as steam or wind, to help people do their tasks in less time, with less work—” “一定有办法利用自然的力量，比如蒸汽或风，来帮助人们用更少的时间、更少的力气完成任务——”

“That’s a great idea,” said Jack. “Maybe someday steam engines could run ships. Or maybe steam could help run a train.” “这是个好主意，”杰克说。 “也许有一天蒸汽机可以用来驱动轮船。 或者蒸汽可以用来驱动火车。”

“A train?” said Leonardo. “火车？”莱昂纳多说。

“Yes, a train!” said Annie. “A train is this thing we’ve imagined. It’s like—um—” “是的，火车！”安妮说。 “火车是我们想象出来的东西。它就像——嗯——”

“Like wagons connected to each other!” said Jack. “And they run over tracks that go across the land.” “就像连在一起的马车！”杰克说。 “而且它们会沿着穿过大地的轨道行驶。”

“Interesting,” said Leonardo. He closed his eyes as if trying to imagine it. “有趣，”莱昂纳多说。 他闭上眼睛，好像在努力想象。

“And then there are planes,” said Annie. “We’ve imagined these things called planes.” “然后还有飞机，”安妮说。 “我们想象过这些东西，叫飞机。”

“Yeah,” said Jack, “they have wings, and they fly you through the air.” “对，”杰克说，“它们有翅膀，能带你飞过天空。”

“Like birds!” said Annie. “像鸟一样！”安妮说。

Leonardo sat up very straight. “You imagine such a flying thing is possible?” he asked. 列奥纳多坐得笔直。 “你想象有这样一种能飞的东西是可能的吗？”他问。

“We’re positive,” said Jack. “我们确定可以，”杰克说。

Leonardo leapt to his feet. “You have been sent to me as a sign!” he said. 列奥纳多猛地跳了起来。 “你们是作为预兆被派来给我的！”他说。

“A sign of what?” said Annie. “什么预兆？”安妮问。

Leonardo’s eyes were gleaming. “I, too, believe humans can fly like birds. And today I shall prove it!” 列奥纳多的眼睛闪闪发亮。 “我也相信人类可以像鸟一样飞行。 今天我就要证明这一点！”

“You will?” said Jack. “你会吗？”杰克说。

“Yes! Until now, I have been fearful of testing my idea,” said Leonardo. “But the two of you have given me courage!” “是的！ 直到现在，我一直在害怕测试我的想法，”列奥纳多说。 “但你们两个人给了我勇气！”

What is Leonardo talking about? Jack wondered. 列奥纳多在说什么呢？杰克想知道。

“I am sure my plan will work now!” said Leonardo. “And it will bring me everlasting fame!” “我确定我的计划现在会成功！”莱昂纳多说。 “这将给我带来永恒的名声！”

“We don’t really know that much about flying,” Jack said. “我们其实对飞行了解得不多，”杰克说。

“Yeah, we were just imagining,” said Annie. “是啊，我们只是在想象，”安妮说。

But Leonardo had grabbed his cap and cloak. “Come with me, friends!” He headed out to the courtyard. 但莱昂纳多已经抓起了他的帽子和斗篷。 “跟我来，朋友们！”他朝庭院走去。

Jack grabbed his bag, and he and Annie followed. Leonardo jumped into the horse cart and picked up the reins. “Climb in! Climb in!” he said. 杰克拿了他的包，然后他和安妮跟了上去。 列奥纳多跳进马车，拿起缰绳。 “快上来！ 快上来！”他说。

Jack and Annie climbed into the cart and sat beside Leonardo. 杰克和安妮爬进马车，坐在莱昂纳多旁边。

“Today the Great Bird shall rise aloft high into the sky!” said Leonardo. “And the universe shall be filled with wonder!” “今天，伟大的鸟儿将飞上高高的天空！”莱昂纳多说道。 “宇宙将充满奇迹！”
