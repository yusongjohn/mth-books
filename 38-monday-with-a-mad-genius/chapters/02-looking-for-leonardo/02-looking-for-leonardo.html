2. Looking for Leonardo 2. 寻找列奥纳多

A different bell was bonging in the distance. Bright early sunlight poured through the tree house window. Teddy and Kathleen were gone. 远处传来一种不同的钟声。 明亮的阳光早早地照进树屋的窗户。 泰迪和凯瑟琳不见了。

Jack looked down at his clothes. He was wearing a knee-length tunic and dark tights. Annie wore a long dress with puffy sleeves. Jack’s backpack had changed into a cloth bag. 杰克低头看了看自己的衣服。 他穿着一条及膝的长袍和深色的紧身裤。 安妮穿着一件长裙，袖子蓬蓬的。 杰克的背包变成了一只布袋子。

Jack and Annie looked out the window. The tree house had landed in a tall tree in a garden surrounded by green hedges. Beyond the garden was a sea of red-tiled roofs. A huge eight-sided dome and a stone tower rose high above the red rooftops. 杰克和安妮看向窗外。 树屋降落在一棵高大的树上，周围是一个被绿色灌木环绕的花园。 花园外面是一片红色瓦片屋顶的海洋。 一个巨大的八边形圆顶和一座石塔高高耸立在红色屋顶之上。

“Welcome to Florence, Italy,” said Annie. “欢迎来到意大利的佛罗伦萨，”安妮说。

Jack opened their book and read aloud: 杰克打开他们的书，大声读起来：

In the early 1500s, many artists and craftspeople lived in the city of Florence. The city was filled with silk weavers, potters, and marble workers. Artists made sculptures, paintings, and tapestries. 1500年初，许多艺术家和工匠住在佛罗伦萨市。这座城市里有许多丝绸织工、陶工和大理石工匠。艺术家们制作雕塑、绘画和挂毯。

“Cool,” said Annie. “I love art.” “太酷了，”安妮说。 “我超爱艺术。”

Jack read more: 杰克继续读：

But the most amazing genius of that time did a bit of everything. Leonardo da Vinci was not only a great painter but also an inventor, architect, stage and costume designer, horseman, chef, geologist, and botanist. 但那个时代最了不起的天才什么都会一点。达·芬奇不仅是个伟大的画家，还是发明家、建筑师、舞台和服装设计师、骑手、厨师、地质学家和植物学家。

“What’s a geologist and a botanist?” said Annie. “什么是地质学家和植物学家？”安妮说。

“They’re scientists,” said Jack. “A geologist studies rocks, and a botanist studies plants.” He turned the page. “他们是科学家，”杰克说。 “地质学家研究石头，植物学家研究植物。”他翻了一页。

“Come on, we should go,” said Annie. “The tree house probably brought us right to Leonardo. We have to find him before he gets away!” “快点，我们应该走了，”安妮说。 “树屋可能把我们直接带到了莱昂纳多那里。 我们必须在他在逃跑之前找到他！”

“Oh, right,” said Jack. “哦，对了，”杰克说。

Annie started down the ladder. Jack packed up their research book and climbed down after her. 安妮开始往下爬梯子。 杰克收拾好他们的研究书，然后爬下去跟上她。

Jack and Annie walked around the tall hedge and came to a busy road that ran along a river. They stared at all the people going by. There were women in long silk dresses, priests in black robes riding donkeys, and soldiers in blue capes riding horses. 杰克和安妮绕过高高的树篱，来到一条沿着河边的繁忙道路。 他们看着路上走过的所有人。 有穿着长丝绸裙子的女士，穿着黑袍子骑着驴子的神父，还有穿着蓝色斗篷骑着马的士兵。

“I don’t see anyone who looks like the guy on the cover of our book,” said Jack. “我看不到任何看起来像我们书封上那个人的人，”杰克说。

“Let’s ask someone,” said Annie. She walked over to a girl selling flowers by the road. “Excuse me. Do you know a person named Leonardo da Vinci?” “我们问问别人吧，”安妮说。 她走到路边卖花的女孩那里。 “对不起。” 你知道一个叫达·芬奇的人吗？

“Of course! Everyone knows Leonardo!” said the girl. “He was just here! He bought some flowers from me. He said he was going to sketch them later.” Her eyes shone with excitement. “当然知道！ 每个人都认识达·芬奇！”女孩说。 “他刚才还在这里！ 他从我这儿买了一些花。 他说他之后要画它们。”她的眼睛因兴奋而闪闪发亮。

“Where did he go?” asked Jack. “他去哪儿了？”杰克问。

“He headed toward the Old Bridge,” the girl said. She pointed toward a covered bridge farther down the road. “他朝老桥那边去了，”女孩说。 她指着路上更远处的一座有顶棚的桥。

“Thanks!” said Annie. “谢谢！”安妮说。

Jack and Annie walked quickly along the bank of the river toward the bridge. “You were right,” said Jack. “The tree house brought us right to Leonardo. But while we were talking, he kept walking.” 杰克和安妮沿着河岸快步走向桥边。 “你刚才说得对，”杰克说。 “树屋把我们直接带到了列奥纳多面前。 但我们说话的时候，他一直在走。”

“Don’t worry,” said Annie. “We’ll catch up to him.” “别担心，”安妮说。 “我们会追上他的。”

The covered bridge was supported by three stone arches. It looked like a long house stretching over the river. 这座有顶的桥由三个石拱支撑着。 看起来像一座长长的房屋，横跨在河面上。

As they crossed the bridge, it was hard to look for Leonardo. The light was dim and the walkway inside the bridge was filled with people. 当他们走过桥时，很难找到列奥纳多。 光线很暗，桥里的走道上挤满了人。

Jack and Annie squeezed through the crowd to the other side of the bridge. The sunlight was so bright that Jack still couldn’t see clearly. He shaded his eyes with his hand. “I still don’t see him,” he said. 杰克和安妮挤过人群，走到桥的另一边。 阳光太亮了，杰克还是看不清楚。 他用手遮住眼睛。 “我还是看不见他，”他说。

“We can ask again,” said Annie. “That girl said everyone knows Leonardo!” She headed to a shop near the riverbank. Weavers were hanging colorful fabrics on a line. The red and purple silks waved in the breeze. “我们可以再问一遍，”安妮说。 “那个女孩说每个人都认识列奥纳多！”，她朝河边的一家店铺走去。 织布工们正把五颜六色的布料挂在绳子上。 红色和紫色的丝绸在微风中飘动。

“Excuse me!” Annie called. “Have you seen Leonardo da Vinci this morning?” “请问一下！”，安妮喊道。 “你今天早上见到过列奥纳多·达·芬奇吗？”

A toothless old woman smiled. “Oh, yes! Leonardo passed by only a moment ago,” she said. “On his way to the bakery, I think.” She pointed down a narrow lane. “He goes there every morning.” 一位没有牙齿的老妇人微笑着。 “哦，是的！”， “他刚才只是一会儿前经过的，”她说。 “我想他是去面包店的路上。”她指着一条狭窄的小巷。 “他每天早上都去那里。”

“Thanks!” said Annie. “谢谢！”安妮说。

Jack and Annie hurried to the bakery. The delicious smell of baking bread filled the air. 杰克和安妮赶紧跑到面包店。 烤面包的香味充满了空气。

“Excuse me, did Leonardo da Vinci come in here?” Jack asked. “请问，达·芬奇先生来过这里吗？”杰克问。

“Yes, he just bought his daily loaf of bread,” said the baker. “He always goes to the cheese shop next.” He pointed across the street. “是的，他刚买了每天的一条面包，”面包师说。 “他总是接着去奶酪店。”他指着街对面。

“Thanks!” said Jack. “谢谢！”杰克说。

Jack and Annie crossed the busy street to the cheese shop. “Is Leonardo da Vinci here?” asked Annie. 杰克和安妮穿过繁忙的街道，来到奶酪店。 “列奥纳多·达·芬奇在这里吗？”安妮问。

“He just left,” said the cheese man. He pointed up the street. “He was going to the blacksmith’s.” “他刚刚离开了，”奶酪店老板说。 他指着街的方向。 “他要去铁匠铺。”

“Oh, brother,” said Jack. “哦，天哪，”杰克说。

“Thanks!” said Annie, and they headed up the street. “谢谢！”安妮说，然后他们沿着街走了上去。

“I can’t wait to meet him!” said Annie. “我迫不及待想见他了！”安妮说。

“Me too,” said Jack. “If we ever catch up to him.” “我也是，”杰克说。 “如果我们能追上他的话。”

Loud hammering noises were coming from a shop. Jack and Annie looked inside and saw a blacksmith pounding a horseshoe with a huge iron hammer. A fire was roaring in a hearth nearby. 从一家店铺里传来很大的敲击声。 杰克和安妮往里看，看见一位铁匠正用一把巨大的铁锤敲打着马蹄铁。 附近壁炉里的火正烧得旺旺的。

“Excuse me!” Jack shouted. “请问一下！”杰克喊道。

The burly man stopped pounding. 那个身材魁梧的男子停下了敲击。

“Was Leonardo da Vinci just here?” asked Jack. “达·芬奇刚刚在这里吗？”杰克问。

“Yes, he paid me for his iron pots,” the blacksmith said gruffly. “Finally.” “是的，他刚刚付钱给我做了几个铁锅，”铁匠粗声粗气地说。 “终于。”

“Do you know where he was going next?” asked Jack. “你知道他接下来要去哪儿吗？”杰克问。

“Headed for the market, in a big hurry, as usual,” said the blacksmith, nodding toward the street. Then he went back to pounding. “他肯定是去市场了，像往常一样，急急忙忙的，”铁匠点头朝街道方向说道。 然后他又继续敲打起来。

Jack and Annie ran up the street. They rounded a corner and stepped into a huge square. Sunlight shone down on hundreds of tents and stalls. The air smelled of fish and cinnamon and other spices. 杰克和安妮跑上了街道。 他们转过一个拐角，走进了一个巨大的广场。 阳光照在成百上千的帐篷和摊位上。 空气里弥漫着鱼、肉桂和其他香料的味道。

“Oh, man,” said Jack. “It’s huge!” “哦，天哪，”杰克说。 “这也太大了！”

The market was jam-packed with shoppers. It was hard to see over the heads of all the grownups. “We could spend all day looking for Leonardo here,” said Jack. 市场里挤满了购物的人。 大人们人头攒动，很难看得见。 “我们在这里可以一整天找列奥纳多，”杰克说。

“This is not good,” said Annie. “We’re supposed to spend the day helping him, not looking for him! Remember the rhyme said, ‘Help the genius all day long, morning, noon, and afternoon, till the night bird sings its song.’” “这可不好，”安妮说。 “我们本来应该一整天帮助他，而不是找他！ 记得那首童谣说的是‘一整天帮助天才，早上、中午、下午，直到夜鸟唱起歌来。’”

“Yeah, whatever that means,” said Jack. “是啊，不管那是什么意思，”杰克说。

“Hey, maybe we should use the wand now,” said Annie. “This situation fits the rules. Finding Leonardo is not just for our good—it’s to help Merlin. And I think we’ve tried our hardest.” “嘿，也许我们现在应该用魔杖了，”安妮说。 “这种情况符合规则。 找到列奥纳多不仅对我们有好处，更是为了帮助梅林。 “我觉得我们已经尽力了。”

“Okay, let’s use it.” Jack slipped the Wand of Dianthus out of his bag and handed it to Annie. “Five words,” he said. “好吧，我们来试试。”杰克从包里拿出迪安瑟斯魔杖，递给安妮。 “五个词，”他说。

“I know, I know,” she said. She held up the wand and counted her words on her fingers: “Help. Us. Find. Leonardo. Now.” “我知道，我知道，”她说。 她举起魔杖，用手指数着词：“帮助。我们。 找到。列奥纳多。 现在。”

Jack and Annie held their breath and waited. But nothing changed. Everything around them looked exactly the same. 杰克和安妮屏住呼吸，等待着。 但什么也没变。 他们周围的一切看起来和之前一模一样。

“It’s not working,” said Jack. “What did we do wrong?” “不行，”杰克说。 “我们哪里做错了？”

“I don’t know,” said Annie. “I used five words. “我不知道，”安妮说。 “我用了五个词。

It’s definitely good for others. Maybe we haven’t really tried our hardest yet.” 这肯定对别人有好处。 也许我们还没真的努力过呢。”

Jack sighed. “Okay let’s keep trying.” He took back the wand and slipped it into his bag. 杰克叹了口气。 “好吧，我们再试一次。”他拿回魔杖，把它放回了袋子里。

“Oh, look at the birds over there!” said Annie. She pulled Jack over to a stall that sold caged birds. Only one bird was singing: a brown bird with a reddish tail. He was very plain, but he sang beautifully with whistles and trills. “哦，快看那边的鸟！”安妮说。 她拉着杰克走到一个卖笼中鸟的摊位前。 只有一只鸟在唱歌：一只尾巴带点红色的棕色鸟。 他看起来很普通，但他用口哨和颤音唱得非常动听。

“Hi, you,” said Annie. “你好，”安妮说。

The bird tilted his head and looked straight at Annie. He chirped softly. 那只鸟歪着头，直直地盯着安妮看。 他轻轻地叫了一声。

“Come on, Annie, we can’t waste time here,” said Jack. “We have to keep looking for Leonardo.” “快点，安妮，我们不能在这里浪费时间，”杰克说。 “我们必须继续寻找列奥纳多。”

“But didn’t you hear his song?” said Annie. “He wants to fly away. He wants to be free.” “但是你没听到他的歌声吗？”安妮说。 “他想飞走。 他想获得自由。”

Jack looked around for the bird seller. He was standing nearby talking to a customer. “Forget it, Annie. We don’t have money to pay for him,” said Jack. 杰克四处张望，想找卖鸟的人。 那个人正站在附近，和一位顾客说话。 “算了吧，安妮。 我们没有钱买下他，”杰克说。

“But he wants me to help him,” said Annie. “I can feel it.” She reached toward the cage door. “但他想让我帮忙，”安妮说。 “我能感觉到。”她伸出手，朝笼子的门靠近。

“Annie, don’t!” said Jack. “安妮，别动！”杰克说。

But Annie unlatched the door. The bird hopped onto the ground. 但安妮解开了门闩。 鸟跳到了地上。

“Oh, no!” said Jack. He grabbed for the bird, but he was too late. The brown bird was already flying away into the blue sky. “哦，天哪！”杰克说。 他伸手去抓那只鸟，但已经太晚了。 那只棕色的鸟已经飞向蓝天，飞走了。

“Yay!” said Annie. “太好了！”安妮说。

“Hey!” shouted the bird seller, rushing over to them. “Were you trying to steal my bird?” “嘿！”卖鸟的人喊道，赶紧朝他们跑过来。 “你是不是想偷我的鸟？”

“We weren’t stealing him!” said Annie. “We were setting him free!” “我们不是偷他！”安妮说道。 “我们是放他自由！”

The bird seller grabbed Jack by the arm. “You’ll have to pay for him, then!” he barked. 鸟贩子抓住了杰克的手臂。 “那你们就得付钱买下他！”他吼道。

“But … but …,” stammered Jack. “但……但……，”杰克结结巴巴地说。

“Marco, unhand that boy!” a man’s voice boomed. “马可，放开那个男孩！”一个男人的声音大声喊道。

Jack turned to see a tall man in a purple cloak and floppy blue cap. He had a long nose, bright, kind eyes with heavy eyebrows, and a flowing beard. He looked exactly like the man on the cover of their book. 杰克转过身，看到一个穿着紫色斗篷、戴着蓝色软帽的高个子男人。 他有着长长的鼻子、明亮友善的眼睛、浓密的眉毛，还有一把飘逸的胡须。 他看起来和他们书封上的那个人一模一样。

“Leonardo!” said Annie. “The wand worked!” “莱昂纳多！”安妮说道。 “魔杖起作用了！”
