5. Knock, Knock 5. 敲门，敲门

The panic of the battle scene seemed to spread through the big room. Leonardo’s apprentices looked around wildly, as if they didn’t know what to do. 战斗场面的紧张气氛似乎蔓延到了大房间里。 达·芬奇的学徒们惊慌失措地四处张望，好像不知道该怎么办。

“Water from the fountain!” Leonardo roared. “Hurry!” He ran out of the room. His apprentices rushed after him. “用喷泉里的水！”，达·芬奇大喊。 “快点！”，他跑出了房间。 他的学徒们赶紧跟了上去。

“We have to help, too!” Jack said to Annie. They took off after the others, following them down the stairs to the courtyard. “我们也得帮忙！”，杰克对安妮说。 他们赶紧追了上去，跟着其他人走下楼梯来到庭院里。

The apprentices were filling buckets with water from the fountain. “Hurry! Hurry! Hurry!” Leonardo shouted. 学徒们正用喷泉里的水往桶里装。 “快点！快点！ 快点！”，达·芬奇喊道。

Jack and Annie grabbed two of the full buckets and clumsily followed the others back up the stairs. “This is like—like Edo!” Jack said to Annie, remembering their recent trip to old Japan. 杰克和安妮抓了两个满桶，笨手笨脚地跟着其他人回到楼上。 “这简直就像——就像江户！”杰克对安妮说，想起了他们最近去日本的旅行。

“Yeah,” said Annie, “except that was a city on fire. This is just paint melting.” “是啊，”安妮说，“那次是城市着火了。 这次只是颜料在融化。”

True, thought Jack. But Leonardo was acting like it was a matter of life and death. 确实如此，杰克心想。 但达芬奇却表现得好像这是生死攸关的大事。

Inside the hall, Leonardo and the apprentices carried the buckets up the ladder. They splashed water over the flames in the two iron pots. But it was too late. The helmets and faces and swords of the fighting men had become a messy blur of streaks and blotches. The painting was ruined. 在大厅里，达芬奇和学徒们把桶里的水沿着梯子往上送。 他们把水泼在两个铁锅里的火焰上。 但已经太晚了。 战斗士兵们的头盔、脸庞和剑刃已经变成了一片混乱的条纹和污渍。 这幅画被毁了。

Leonardo stared for a long moment at the wall. Then he climbed down the ladder and walked away. When he got to the door, Zorro shouted, “Master, wait!” But Leonardo kept walking. 达·芬奇久久地盯着墙看了好一会儿。 然后他爬下梯子，走开了。 当他走到门口时，佐罗喊道：“大师，等等！”但达·芬奇继续往前走。

“We have to follow him,” Annie said to Jack. “我们必须跟上他，”安妮对杰克说。

“He seems really upset,” said Jack. “他看起来真的很伤心，”杰克说。

“I know,” said Annie. “But we have to do what the rhyme says—‘Help the genius all day long.’” “我知道，”安妮说。 “但我们必须按照那首童谣说的做——‘整天帮助这位天才。’”

“But what if he doesn’t want our help anymore?” said Jack. “但如果他不再想要我们的帮助了呢？”杰克说。

“Look! He forgot his basket, with all his stuff in it,” said Annie. “We can take it to him.” “看！ 他忘记带他的篮子，里面装着他所有的东西，”安妮说。 “我们可以带去给他。”

“Okay. Good,” said Jack. “好，太好了，”杰克说。

Annie picked up Leonardo’s basket filled with feathers, flowers, cheese, and a loaf of bread. Jack grabbed his own bag and they hurried out of the council hall. When they got to the entrance of the palace, they saw Leonardo striding across the square. 安妮捡起了列奥纳多的篮子，里面装满了羽毛、花朵、奶酪和一条面包。 杰克拿起了自己的包，他们赶紧离开了会议厅。 当他们到达宫殿入口时，他们看到列奥纳多正穿过广场走来。

“Leonardo!” Annie yelled. “列奥纳多！”安妮喊道。

Leonardo didn’t look back. He disappeared down a narrow lane. 列奥纳多没有回头看。 他消失在一条狭窄的小巷里。

“Quick!” said Jack. “快！”杰克说。

Jack and Annie took off across the square. When they got to the lane, they saw Leonardo at the far end. 杰克和安妮跑过广场。 当他们跑到小巷时，看见列奥纳多在小巷的尽头。

“Leonardo, wait!” Annie shouted. “列奥纳多，等一下！”，安妮喊道。

But Leonardo didn’t wait. He kept going and rounded a corner. 但列奥纳多没有停下来。 他继续往前走，绕过了一个拐角。

Annie and Jack ran faster. When they turned the corner, they looked right and left. Kids were playing in the street. Two women were leaning out of windows talking to each other. But there was no sign of Leonardo. 安妮和杰克跑得更快了。 当他们转过拐角时，他们左右看了看。 孩子们正在街上玩耍。 两个女人正从窗户里探出头来，互相交谈。 但没有看到列奥纳多的踪影。

“Excuse me,” Annie called to the women. “Have you seen Leonardo da Vinci?” “对不起，”安妮对那些女人喊道。 “你们见过列奥纳多·达·芬奇吗？”

“Oh, yes, he just got home!” one woman said. “哦，是的，他刚到家！”一个女人说。

“He lives just over there!” said her neighbor. She pointed to a narrow building across the street. “他住在那边！”她的邻居说。 她指着街对面的一栋窄房子。

“Thank you!” said Annie. She and Jack walked quickly to the building. A stone arch opened onto a wide pathway. They walked under the arch and down the pathway to a sunny cobblestone courtyard. A big white horse was tied to a cart. Chickens pecked the dirt between the warm stones. “谢谢！”安妮说。 她和杰克快步走向那栋建筑。 一道石拱门通向一条宽阔的小路。 他们穿过拱门，沿着小路走到一个阳光明媚的鹅卵石庭院。 一匹大白马拴在一辆马车上。 母鸡们在温暖的石头之间啄食泥土。

“Hi, guys,” Annie said to the horse and chickens. “嗨，大家，”安妮对马和母鸡说道。

Jack pointed to an open doorway across the yard. “He’s in there. I hear him,” he said. 杰克指着院子里的敞开的门。 “他在里面。 我听到了，”他说。

Annie and Jack moved quietly across the courtyard. They stopped outside a window. 安妮和杰克悄悄地穿过庭院。 他们在一扇窗户外面停了下来。

Leonardo was pacing up and down inside. His cap and cloak were on the floor. His hair was wild. 莱昂纳多在里面来回踱步。 他的帽子和斗篷在地上。 他的头发乱糟糟的。

“I’ll leave Florence—that’s what I’ll do,” Leonardo said to himself. “I shall go to Rome! Or back to Milan!” “我要离开佛罗伦萨——我就是这样打算的，”列奥纳多对自己说道。 “我要去罗马！ 或者回到米兰！”

Jack turned to Annie. “We shouldn’t bother him,” he whispered. “If I felt that bad, I wouldn’t want people to bother me.” 杰克转向安妮。 “我们不应该打扰他，”他小声说。 “如果我感觉那么难受，我就不想让别人打扰我。”

“Not bother, ” said Annie. “Help. If I felt that bad, I’d want people to help me. Come on, at least we can give him his stuff.” Before Jack could stop her, Annie stepped into Leonardo’s room. “Knock, knock,” she said loudly. “不是打扰，”安妮说。 “是帮助。 如果我感觉那么难受，我就想让别人帮助我。 来吧，至少我们可以把他的东西给他。”杰克还没来得及阻止她，安妮就走进了列奥纳多的房间。 “敲门，敲门，”她大声地说。

Leonardo whirled around. His face was red. He was scowling. “What are you doing here?” he said. 列奥纳多猛地转过身。他的脸红了。 他皱着眉头。 “你们在这里干什么？”他说。

“We brought your things,” said Annie. “You forgot them.” She held up the basket. “我们带来了你的东西，”安妮说。 “你把它们忘在家里了。”她举起篮子。

“Oh.” Leonardo’s face softened. “Thank you. Leave it all by the door, please,” he said. “哦。”列奥纳多的脸柔和了下来。 “谢谢。 请把东西都放在门口，”他说。

Annie put the basket down. Then she looked up at Leonardo. 安妮把篮子放下了。 然后她抬头看向列奥纳多。

“We’d better go,” Jack said softly to her. “我们最好走了，”杰克轻声对她说。

“Wait.” Annie stepped farther into Leonardo’s room. “We’d like to help you,” she said. “等等。”安妮走进了列奥纳多的房间。 “我们想帮助你，”她说。

Leonardo scowled again. “You cannot help me,” he said. “Do as your brother says, little girl. Go now.” 列奥纳多再次皱起眉头。 “你们不能帮助我，”他说。 “听你哥哥的话，小女孩。现在离开吧。”

But Annie didn’t move. “Excuse me, but we’re supposed to help you all day,” she said. “You made us your apprentices for the day, remember?” 但安妮没有动。 “对不起，但我们今天本来就是来帮助你的，”她说。 “你今天让我们当你的学徒了，还记得吗？”

“Can you not see that I am miserable?” said Leonardo. “你难道看不见我很痛苦吗？”莱昂纳多说。

“But why are you miserable?” said Annie. “You said that fame was the secret of happiness. And you’re still famous.” “那你为什么这么痛苦呢？”安妮说。 “你说过名声是幸福的秘密。 而你仍然很有名。”

“But what good is fame in the face of failure?” shouted Leonardo. “This fresco was to be my masterpiece! What good is fame when everyone will now laugh at me and mock my failure? Go! Please!” “可是在失败面前，名声又有什么用呢？”莱昂纳多喊道。 “这幅壁画本应是我的杰作！ 当所有人都嘲笑我、讥讽我的失败时，名声又有什么用呢？走开！ 求求你了！”

“Oh, okay. I’m sorry,” said Annie in a small voice. “We just wanted to help.” She and Jack turned to go. “哦，好吧。 对不起，”安妮用很小的声音说。 “我们只是想帮忙。”她和杰克转身要走。

“Wait, wait, wait,” said Leonardo. “Forgive me.” “等等，等等，等等，”列奥纳多说。 “请原谅我。”

Jack and Annie looked back at Leonardo. The great genius rubbed his face and sighed. Then he waved his hand. “Please, forgive me. Come in, come in,” he said. 杰克和安妮回头看向列奥纳多。 这位伟大的天才揉了揉脸，叹了口气。 然后他挥了挥手。 “请，原谅我。 进来吧，进来吧，”他说。

“Thanks,” said Annie. And she and Jack stepped inside Leonardo da Vinci’s studio. “谢谢，”安妮说。 安妮和杰克走进了达·芬奇的工作室。
