3. Ten Types of Noses 3. 十种鼻子

“Let the boy go, Marco,” Leonardo said again. “让那个男孩走开，马可，”列奥纳多又说了一遍。

“But I caught him trying to steal my bird,” said Marco. “但是我想他偷了我的鸟，”马可说。

“No, Marco. The girl said they were setting him free,” said Leonardo. “And I believe her.” “不，马可。 女孩说他们要放他走，”列奥纳多说。 “我相信她，”

“Then let them pay me!” the bird seller said. “那让他们付钱给我！”，卖鸟人说。

“We don’t have any money,” Annie said in a small voice. “我们没有钱，”安妮小声地说。

“I will take care of it,” said Leonardo. He put down the basket he was carrying. It was filled with flowers, cheese, and a loaf of bread. He pulled out a gold coin. The bird seller let go of Jack and took the coin. “我会处理的，”列奥纳多说。 他放下了他拿着的篮子。 篮子里装满了花、奶酪和一条面包。 他拿出了一枚金币。 卖鸟人松开了杰克，拿走了金币。

“Marco, when I lay in my cradle as a child, a bird flew down and struck me with its tail,” said Leonardo. “Ever since then, I have wished—” “马可，当我还是个孩子躺在摇篮里的时候，一只鸟飞下来，用尾巴打了我一下，”列奥纳多说。 “从那以后，我一直希望——”

“I know, I know,” interrupted Marco, “to be a bird yourself. You have told me this many times, Leonardo.” The bird seller turned away to help a customer. “我知道，我知道，”马可打断了他，“希望自己变成一只鸟。 你已经跟我说过很多次了，列奥纳多。”卖鸟人转身去帮一位顾客。

Leonardo turned to Jack and Annie. “Yes,” he said, “to be a bird myself—which is why I often buy birds from Marco and set them free. So you see, my friends, you and I are kindred spirits.” 列奥纳多转向杰克和安妮。 “是的，”他说，“希望自己变成一只鸟——这就是我经常从马可那里买鸟然后放生的原因。 所以，我的朋友们，你和我其实是志同道合的人。”

“Yes, we are!” said Annie, grinning. “是的，我们是！”安妮笑着说。

“Thanks for helping us!” said Jack. He gave Leonardo a big smile. He wanted the great genius to like them so they could spend the whole day with him. “I’m Jack and this is my sister, Annie. Actually it was Annie who freed—” “谢谢你们帮我们！”杰克说。 他给了列奥纳多一个大大的微笑。 他希望这位伟大的天才喜欢他们，这样他们就能和他一起度过一整天。 “我是杰克，这是我姐姐安妮。 其实，是安妮解救了——

But Leonardo didn’t give Jack a chance to finish. He kept talking. “In truth, I love all creatures! Every bird and animal known to man—and even the ones not known!” He laughed heartily. 但列奥纳多没有给杰克机会说完。他一直在说话。 “说实话，我爱所有生物！ 每一只鸟和动物，只要是人类知道的——甚至那些不知道的！”他开心地笑了。

“Me too!” said Annie. “我也是！”安妮说。

“Me too!” said Jack. “我也是！”杰克说。

Leonardo picked up some bird feathers from the ground. “Ah, beautiful,” he said, holding them up to the sun. “I will sketch these later.” He tucked the feathers into his basket with the bread and cheese and flowers. “Well, I must be on my way now, friends,” he said. “Good day!” Leonardo turned and began walking briskly away from the bird stand. 列奥纳多从地上捡起一些鸟羽毛。 “啊，真漂亮，”他说到太阳底下举起来看。 “我待会儿再画这些。”他把羽毛和面包、奶酪和花一起放进篮子里。 “好吧，朋友们，我现在得走了，”他说。 “祝你今天愉快！”列奥纳多转身，快步离开了鸟摊。

Oh, no! thought Jack. 哦，不！杰克心想。

Before he could think of anything to say, Annie shouted, “Mr. da Vinci! Leonardo!” 还没来得及想出什么话要说，安妮就喊道，“达·芬奇先生！ “莱昂纳多！”

Leonardo looked back at her. “Yes?” 莱昂纳多回头看着她。 “是吗？”

“Do you … um … do you need any help today?” Annie asked. “Jack and I would really, really like to help you … all day … somehow.” “你……嗯……今天需要帮忙吗？”安妮问。 “杰克和我真的很、真的很想帮你……一整天……以某种方式。”

Jack was embarrassed. He was sure Leonardo would say no. But to his surprise, the great genius was looking at them closely and tapping his chin. “Well … actually I am facing a great task this morning,” he said with a smile. He nodded. “Yes. Perhaps you could be my apprentices—just for today.” 杰克感到很不好意思。 他确定达·芬奇一定会说不。 但让他惊讶的是，这位伟大的天才正仔细看着他们，还用手敲着下巴思考。 “嗯……其实我今天早上正面临着一项艰巨的任务，”他微笑着说道。他点了点头。 “是的。 也许你们可以当我的学徒——就今天一天。”

“Great!” said Annie. “太棒了！”安妮说道。

“What’s an apprentice? ” asked Jack. “学徒是什么？”杰克问道。

“Apprentices help a master artist or skilled worker,” said Leonardo. “They work hard and study hard in hopes they’ll become masters themselves someday.” “学徒是帮助大师艺术家或熟练工匠的人，”达·芬奇说道。 “他们努力工作，努力学习，希望有一天自己也能成为大师。”

“Cool,” said Jack. “真酷，”杰克说道。

“Come along, then!” said Leonardo. He started walking again. Jack and Annie hurried alongside him. They left the crowded market and started up a cobblestone street. “快点，走吧！”莱昂纳多说。 他又开始走路了。 杰克和安妮赶紧跟在他身边。 他们离开了拥挤的市场，沿着鹅卵石街道走了上去。

“Do you children live in Florence?” asked Leonardo. “你们这两个孩子住在佛罗伦萨吗？”莱昂纳多问。

“No, we’re from … um … far away,” said Jack. “不，我们来自……嗯……很远的地方，”杰克说。

“We’re here on a mission,” said Annie. “We’re looking for the secret of happiness.” “我们是来执行任务的，”安妮说。 “我们在寻找幸福的秘密。”

Leonardo smiled. “Ah, yes, I discovered that secret some time ago,” he said. 莱昂纳多笑了。 “啊，是的，我很久以前就发现了那个秘密，”他说。

“You did?” asked Jack. “你做到了？”杰克问。

“Yes, it’s something I sought and now I have it,” said Leonardo. “It’s really quite simple.” “是的，这是我一直在寻找的东西，现在我得到了它，”列奥纳多说。 “这其实很简单。”

“What is it?” said Jack. “是什么？”杰克问。

“The secret of happiness is fame,” said Leonardo. “幸福的秘密就是名声，”列奥纳多说。

“Really? Fame?” said Annie. “真的吗？ “名声？”安妮说道。

“Yes!” said Leonardo. “When I look into the eyes of complete strangers and see their awe and admiration—that makes me very happy!” “是！”莱昂纳多说。 “当我看着完全陌生的人的眼睛，看到他们的惊叹和钦佩——这让我非常开心！”

As Leonardo strode a few feet ahead of them, Annie looked at Jack. “Fame,” she said. “I guess that’s our answer.” 当莱昂纳多走在他们前面几步时，安妮看着杰克。 “名声，”她说。 “我想这就是我们的答案了。”

“I don’t know,” said Jack in a soft voice. “Remember what the rhyme said: ‘Though the question is quite simple, simple answers might be wrong.’” “我不知道，”杰克用轻柔的声音说。 “还记得那首童谣说的是：‘虽然问题很简单，但简单的答案可能不对。’”

“Oh, yeah!” said Annie. “And the rhyme says that to learn the answer, we have to stay with him all day.” “哦，对啊！”安妮说。 “而且童谣还说，要找到答案，我们必须陪他一整天。”

“Yep,” said Jack. He didn’t mind that part. Spending the day with one of the most amazing geniuses who ever lived seemed like a great idea. “是的，”杰克说。 他并不介意这一点。 和历史上最了不起的天才之一度过一整天，听起来是个很棒的主意。

Jack and Annie followed Leonardo into a square with a huge cathedral. On the top of the building was the enormous eight-sided dome that they’d seen from the tree house. How did anyone ever build that? Jack wondered. 杰克和安妮跟着列奥纳多走进了一个有巨大教堂的广场。 建筑物的顶部有一个巨大的八边形圆顶，他们从树屋里就看到过这个。 这东西是怎么建起来的？杰克心想。

As hundreds of people moved about the square, Leonardo stopped. He stared into the crowd. “Oh! Oh!” he said. 当数百人在这个广场上走动时，列奥纳多停下了脚步。 他盯着人群看。 “哦！哦！”他说。

“What? What?” asked Annie. 什么？“安妮问。

“I see an angel!” said Leonardo. “我看到一个天使！“列奥纳多说。

“An angel?” said Jack. He looked at the crowd. He didn’t see any angels. “一个天使？“杰克说。 他看了看人群。 他什么天使也没看到。

“Over there!” Leonardo pointed to a short, dark-haired girl standing by herself. The girl didn’t look at all like an angel to Jack. She looked like an ordinary kid. “那边！”列奥纳多指着一个站在那儿的、个子矮小、头发乌黑的女孩。 这个女孩看起来一点都不像天使，杰克觉得。 她看起来就是一个普通的孩子。

Leonardo put down his basket, untied a small book from his belt, and pulled out a piece of chalk. He started to draw. “I have been seeking an angel for one of my paintings,” he murmured as he sketched the girl. “I think I may have found her.” 列奥纳多放下他的篮子，从腰带上解开一本小书，拿出一块粉笔。 他开始画画。 “我一直在为我的一幅画寻找一个天使，”他一边画一边小声说，画的是那个女孩。 “我想我可能找到了。”

In a moment, Leonardo was done. “There.” He showed his sketch to Jack and Annie. With just a few quick lines, he had created an angel. The drawing looked just like the real girl, yet somehow she really did look like an angel now. 一会儿，列奥纳多就画完了。 “看这儿。”他把画好的素描展示给杰克和安妮看。 只用了几笔，他就画出了一位天使。 这幅画看起来就像那个真正的女孩，但不知为什么，她现在看起来真的像天使。

“That’s the nicest angel I’ve ever seen,” said Annie. “这是我见过最漂亮的天使，”安妮说。

“Hmm, I don’t know,” said Leonardo. “I fear the nose is not quite right. I’m afraid I must keep looking.” He tore the page out of his sketchbook. “Perhaps you and Jack would like to have this?” “嗯，我不确定，”列奥纳多说。 “我担心鼻子不太对劲。 恐怕我得继续找找看。”他从速写本里撕下了那一页。 “也许你和杰克会想要这个？”

“Oh … yes!” said Annie. “Thank you.” “哦……是的！”安妮说。 “谢谢。”

“I’ll carry it,” said Jack. He took the drawing from Leonardo and carefully slid it into his bag, between the pages of their research book. “我来拿着，”杰克说。 他从列奥纳多那里接过画，小心地把它放进自己的包里，夹在他们的研究书的书页之间。

Leonardo put away his chalk and sketchbook and picked up his basket. “Come along,” he said. 列奥纳多收起了他的粉笔和素描本，拿起他的篮子。 “快点走吧，”他说。

Jack and Annie half walked and half ran, trying to keep up with Leonardo’s long strides. 杰克和安妮一边走一边跑，努力跟上列奥纳多大步的步伐。

“When I travel through the streets, I am always gathering information,” Leonardo said. “I observe like a scientist. For instance, after years of observation, I now know there are ten different types of noses.” “当我走在街上时，我总是收集信息，”列奥纳多说。 “我像科学家一样观察。 比如，经过多年观察，我现在知道有十种不同类型的鼻子。

“Really?” said Annie. She felt her nose. “真的吗？”安妮说。 她摸了摸自己的鼻子。

“Yes,” said Leonardo, “straight, round, pointed, flat, narrow…. Of course that is from the side. If you look people straight in the face, you will find eleven types of noses.” “是的，”列奥纳多说，“直的、圆的、尖的、扁的、窄的…… 当然，这是从侧面看的。 如果你直视别人的眼睛，你会发现有十一种不同形状的鼻子。”

“No kidding,” said Jack. “真的吗？”杰克说。

Jack tried to get a good look at the noses they passed. He saw flat ones, round ones, straight ones … but many were hard to describe. 杰克努力看清他们路过的每个人的鼻子。 他看到了扁平的、圆圆的、笔直的……但很多很难描述。

“My observations have also led me to conclude that there are many more types of mouths than noses,” said Leonardo. “But the location of every mouth is almost always the same. It is halfway between the base of the nose and the chin.” “我的观察也让我得出结论，嘴巴的种类比鼻子还要多，”列奥纳多说。 “但每个嘴巴的位置几乎都是一样的。 它在鼻子底部和下巴中间的位置。”

“Really?” said Annie. She held up two fingers, trying to measure the distance between her nose, mouth, and chin. “I think you’re right, Leonardo.” “真的吗？”安妮说。 她举起两根手指，试着测量自己鼻子、嘴巴和下巴之间的距离。 “我觉得你说得对，列奥纳多。”

“I study people’s expressions and gestures,” said Leonardo. “I study their hands, their eyes, their hair. But to be a truly great artist, you must learn to combine your observations with your imagination.” Suddenly he stopped. “Look up, look up!” “我研究人们的表情和手势，”列奥纳多说。 “我研究他们的手、眼睛和头发。 但要成为一个真正伟大的艺术家，你必须学会将你的观察与想象力结合起来。”突然，他停下了。 “抬头看，抬头看！”

Jack and Annie stopped and looked up. 杰克和安妮停下了，抬头看。

“See the clouds?” said Leonardo. “看到云了吗？”列奥纳多说。

A few billowy clouds dotted the sky. 天空中散落着几朵蓬松的云。

“What do they look like to you?” asked Leonardo. “What sorts of things?” “它们在你眼里像什么？”列奥纳多问。 “像什么种类的东西？”

Big white blobs, thought Jack. 大大的白色团块，杰克心想。

“The biggest one looks sort of like a castle,” said Annie. “最大的那个看起来有点像城堡，”安妮说。

“Good, good!” said Leonardo. “好，好！”列奥纳多说。

“And that little one looks like a dog’s head,” said Annie, “like a Scottie puppy.” “那个小一点的看起来像狗的头，”安妮说，“像一只斯科特犬小狗。”

A Scottie puppy? thought Jack. He squinted, trying to see a puppy. 斯科特犬小狗？杰克心想。 他眯起眼睛，努力想看到一只小狗。

“Excellent!” said Leonardo. “And you, Jack? What about that one?” He pointed at a long cloud. “What do you see?” “太棒了！”列奥纳多说。 “杰克，你呢？ 你觉得那个怎么样？”他指着一朵长长的云。 “你看到什么了？”

Jack studied it. “Uh … well, I guess it sort of looks like a boat,” he said. 杰克仔细看着它。 “呃……嗯，我想它看起来有点像一艘船，”他说。

“Wonderful!” said Leonardo. “I get ideas for my paintings from everything! I look at a watermark on a wall and see an old woman’s face. I look at a food stain on my tablecloth and see a horse! I study rain puddles and rocks and see oceans and mountains!” “太棒了！”，列奥纳多说。 “我从所有东西中得到绘画的灵感！ 我看着墙上的水印，看到一位老妇人的脸。 我看着桌布上的食物污渍，看到一匹马！ 我研究雨水坑和石头，看到海洋和山脉！”，

“Oh, I do that kind of thing, too!” said Annie. “哦，我也做过类似的事情！”，安妮说。

“I imagine that the very first drawing might have been a simple line drawn around the shadow of a man on the wall of a cave,” said Leonardo. “我想，最早的画可能是一条简单的线，围绕着洞穴墙壁上一个人的影子，”列奥纳多说。

“Wow,” breathed Annie. “哇，”安妮轻声说道。

Pretty cool, thought Jack. He liked Leonardo’s way of thinking. “挺酷的，”杰克心想。 他喜欢达·芬奇的思维方式。

“Listen now to the cathedral bells,” said Leonardo. “现在听一听教堂钟声，”达·芬奇说。

Jack listened. The bells played notes that went up and down: 杰克听了。 钟声发出的音符上下起伏：

Bong-bing-bong-bing. 当——叮——当——叮——

Bong-bing-bong-bing. 邦——邦——邦——邦。

“I hear the bells’ voices as if they were singing to me,” said Leonardo. “Can you hear what they are saying?” “我听到钟声，好像它们在对我说唱呢，”列奥纳多说。 “你能听到它们在说什么吗？”，

Well … no, thought Jack. He just heard bongs and bings. 嗯……不能，杰克心想。 他只听到钟声和叮当声。

“They’re saying: You have much to do this Monday, Leonardo da Vinci! Get to work!” Leonardo laughed. “So let us be on our way, my friends!” And the great genius took off, walking quickly through the streets of Florence. “他们在说：你这周一有很多事情要做，列奥纳多·达·芬奇！” “快去干活！”列奥纳多笑了。 “那么，让我们出发吧，我的朋友们！”这位伟大的天才出发了，快速穿过佛罗伦萨的街道。
