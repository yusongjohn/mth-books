7. The Great Bird 7. 伟大的鸟儿

Leonardo shook the reins. The white horse clopped out of the courtyard and into the street. 莱昂纳多摇动了缰绳。 白马从院子走出来，走进了街道。

“So where are we going?” Annie asked. “那我们去哪儿呢？”安妮问。

“To a steep hill just outside the city walls,” said Leonardo. “One day you will tell people that you were with me on this historic Monday. You saw the mad genius, Leonardo da Vinci, and his Great Bird!” “去城外的一座陡峭的小山，”列奥纳多说。 “有一天你会告诉人们，你曾和我一起度过这个历史性的一天。 你看到了疯狂的天才，列奥纳多·达·芬奇，和他的大鸟！”，

“Cool, but can you tell us exactly what you’re planning to do?” said Jack. “太酷了，但你能告诉我们你具体打算做什么吗？”杰克说。

“For twenty-five years, I have sketched birds and bats,” said Leonardo. “I have studied all their movements, their gliding, their flapping, their landing, and their rising into the air. I have asked myself over and over, Why can a person not fly as birds do? So, years ago I began building my Great Bird.” “二十五年来，我一直画鸟和蝙蝠，”列奥纳多说。 “我研究了它们的所有动作，滑翔、拍翅、降落和起飞。 我一遍又一遍地问自己，为什么人不能像鸟一样飞呢？ 所以，多年前我就开始建造我的大鸟。”

“Your Great Bird?” asked Annie. “你的大鸟吗？”安妮问。

“Ha-ha!” laughed Leonardo. “Wait and see! Wait and see!” “哈哈哈！”列奥纳多笑了。 “等着瞧吧！等着瞧吧！”

The horse pulled the cart through the city gates, heading into the countryside. The chilly air was warmed by bright sunlight. 马拉着车穿过城门，驶向乡间。 寒冷的空气被明亮的阳光温暖了。

Leonardo tugged on the reins and his horse turned off the main road and started up a narrow, rocky path. The cart bumped past pale green olive trees and yellow fields of wildflowers. Soon it came to the bottom of a steep hill. 列奥纳多拉住缰绳，他的马偏离了主路，开始走上一条狭窄的、崎岖的小路。 马车颠簸着经过淡绿色的橄榄树和金黄色的野花田。 很快，它来到了一座陡峭山丘的底部。

Leonardo pulled the reins and his horse halted. “There! Can you see it?” he said. “My Great Bird.” He pointed to a strange-looking structure on the top of the hill. 列奥纳多拉住缰绳，他的马停了下来。 “看那儿！ 你能看到它吗？”他说。 “我的大鸟。”他指着山顶上一个看起来很奇怪的结构。

“What is it?” asked Jack. “这是什么？”杰克问。

“The wings are like those of a bat, only much, much larger—large enough for a man!” said Leonardo. “About a month ago on a moonlit night, my apprentices and I brought it to the top of this hill. I did not have the confidence to try it then, but now I do.” “它的翅膀就像蝙蝠的翅膀，不过要大得多——大得足以装下一个人！”列奥纳多说。 “大约一个月前，在一个月光皎洁的夜晚，我和我的学徒们把它带到了这座山的山顶。 我当时没有信心去试飞它，但现在我有啦。”

Jack was confused. He knew people didn’t fly airplanes until the beginning of the 1900s. “Um—maybe you should work on this one a little longer,” he said. “I mean, maybe—” 杰克感到很困惑。 他知道人们直到20世纪初才开始驾驶飞机。 “呃——也许你应该再花点时间完善一下这个设计，”他说。 “我是说，也许——”

“No, no, today is the day! I feel it!” said Leonardo. “Stay here and watch.” “不，不，今天就是这一天！” “我感受到了！”列奥纳多说道。 “待在这里看着。”

Leonardo leapt down from the cart and took long strides up the steep slope. 列奥纳多从马车上跳下来，沿着陡峭的斜坡大步向上走。

“Quick, look up Great Bird in the Leonardo book,” Annie said to Jack. “快看，莱昂纳多书里有大鸟，”安妮对杰克说。

Jack pulled out their research book and looked up Great Bird in the index. “It’s here!” he said. He found the right page and read aloud: 杰克拿出他们的研究书，在目录里查找“大鸟”. “找到了！”他说。 他找到了正确的那一页，大声读起来：

Leonardo da Vinci spent years making a flying machine that he called the Great Bird. But not until the invention of lightweight motors, nearly 400 years after Leonardo’s time, would human flight be possible. It is not known whether Leonardo ever tried to fly the Great Bird. If he did, he surely crashed. 达芬奇花了好多年制作了一架飞行器，他称之为“大鸟”。但直到轻便发动机的发明，也就是达芬奇时代之后近400年，人类飞行才成为可能。没人知道达芬奇是否曾经尝试驾驶“大鸟”飞行。如果他试过的话，肯定摔得很惨。

“Oh, no!” said Annie. “His machine won’t work! If Leonardo tries to fly off that hilltop, he’ll crash. We have to stop him before he hurts himself!” “哦，不！”安妮说。 “他的机器坏了！ 如果列奥纳多从那个山顶飞下去，他就会摔跤。 我们必须在他受伤之前阻止他！

Annie jumped out of the cart. Jack put the book away. He left his bag in the cart and ran after her. They started up the steep hill. 安妮从马车里跳了出来。 杰克把书收了起来。 他把包留在了马车里，然后跑去找她。 他们开始爬那条陡峭的山丘。

“Leonardo, stop!” shouted Annie. “莱昂纳多，停下！”安妮喊道。

But Leonardo kept climbing. 但莱昂纳多继续往上爬。

“Human flight isn’t possible yet!” Jack cried. “人类飞行现在还做不到！”杰克喊道。

“Don’t try it, Leonardo!” yelled Annie. “别试了，莱昂纳多！”安妮大喊。

Jack and Annie were only halfway up the hill when Leonardo reached the top. He began strapping himself into a harness on the Great Bird. 杰克和安妮才爬到小山一半，莱昂纳多就已经到达山顶了。 他开始把自己绑在巨鸟的背带上。

Large handles were attached to the harness. On each side were huge cloth wings stretched over a wooden frame. 背带上有很大的把手。 两侧是巨大的布翅膀，覆盖在木制的框架上。

“Don’t!” shouted Jack. “别动！”杰克喊道。

But Leonardo was already staggering toward the edge of the steep hill with the flying machine on his back. It was so heavy that he could hardly stand up. 但莱昂纳多已经背着飞行器，摇摇晃晃地朝陡峭小山的边缘走去。 飞行器太重了，他几乎站不稳。

“Leonardo, stop!” cried Annie. “You need a motor!” “莱昂纳多，停下！”安妮喊道。 “你需要一个发动机！”

But Leonardo bent his legs and lowered his body close to the ground. He grabbed the two large handles and pulled them toward his chest. The huge wings rose into the air. 但莱昂纳多弯下腿，把身体贴近地面。 他抓住了两个大把手，把它们拉向自己的胸口。 巨大的翅膀升到了空中。

“The Great Bird raises its wings and is pushed by the wind!” shouted Leonardo. “那只大鸟展开翅膀，被风吹起来了！”列奥纳多喊道。

“Noooo!” yelled Jack and Annie. “不——！”杰克和安妮喊道。

Leonardo leapt off the side of the hill into the air. A gust of wind lifted him. As the wind held his wings aloft, he pushed and pulled on the handles. The wings moved up and down. 列奥纳多从山丘的一侧跳了下去，飞向空中。 一阵风吹起了他。 当风托起他的翅膀时，他拉扯着把手。 翅膀上下移动。

But Leonardo couldn’t make the wings flap fast enough. Though he pushed and pulled wildly on the handles, he soon began falling through the air—until wings and wood and Leonardo all crashed to the ground. 但列奥纳多无法让翅膀快速扇动。 尽管他疯狂地拉扯把手，但很快他开始从空中坠落——直到翅膀、木头和列奥纳多一起摔到了地上。

“Leonardo!” yelled Annie. “莱昂纳多！”安妮喊道。

Jack and Annie charged down the hill. At the bottom, Leonardo da Vinci lay in a silent heap. His twisted wings spread over the grass. Jack and Annie rushed to him. 杰克和安妮冲下山去。 在山脚下，列奥纳多·达·芬奇静静地躺在地上。 他扭曲的翅膀铺满了草地。 杰克和安妮赶紧跑过去。

“Are you all right?” cried Annie. “你还好吗？”安妮喊道。

There was no answer. 没有回应。

Oh, no! We’ve killed him! thought Jack. 哦，天哪！我们把他弄死了！ 杰克心想。

But then Leonardo stirred. He moved his hand. 但接着，莱昂纳多动了动。 他动了动手。

“Are you all right?” Annie asked again. “你没事吧？”安妮又问了一遍。

Leonardo moved his other hand. He rolled over on his side and unbuckled the straps of the harness. He crawled away from the flying machine and hauled himself to a sitting position. His face was scraped and red. 列奥纳多动了动另一只手。 他翻了个身，解开背带上的带子。 他爬开飞行器，把自己拉到坐姿。 他的脸被刮伤了，红红的。

“Are you all right?” Annie asked once more. “你没事吧？”安妮又一次问。

Leonardo looked at her. The light had gone out of his eyes. “No,” he said in a quiet voice. “I am not all right.” 列奥纳多看着她。 他的眼睛失去了光彩。 “没有，”他用轻声说。 “我没事。”

“Did you break something?” asked Annie. “你把什么东西弄坏了？”安妮问。

Leonardo stood up. He stared at the twisted and torn wings of the Great Bird. He sighed deeply. “Only my heart,” he said. “Only my heart.” 列奥纳多站了起来。 他盯着那只大鸟扭曲而撕裂的翅膀。他深深地叹了口气。 “只有我的心，”他说。 “只有我的心。”

Leonardo turned and limped across the grass back toward his horse and cart. Jack and Annie followed. When Leonardo got to the cart, his white horse snorted, as if trying to comfort him. Leonardo pressed his head against the horse’s neck. 列奥纳多转身，一瘸一拐地穿过草地，朝他的马车走去。 杰克和安妮跟了上去。 当列奥纳多走到马车旁时，他那匹白色的马喷了口气，好像在安慰他。 列奥纳多把头靠在马的脖子上。

Annie stepped toward him. “Why is your heart broken, Leonardo?” she asked quietly. 安妮朝他走了过去。 “你的内心为什么这么难过，列奥纳多？”她轻声问道。

Leonardo looked back at the hill. “All my life, I have started projects that have come to nothing,” he said. “My towers and bridges have never been built. My scientific ideas have never been proven.” 列奥纳多回头看向那座小山。 “我这辈子开始了很多项目，但都一事无成，”他说。 “我的塔和桥从来没有被建成过。 我的科学想法从未被证明过。”

“But—” said Annie. “但是——”安妮说。

Leonardo went on: “For years, I made drawings of an enormous horse I planned to sculpt for the Duke of Milan. But in the end, that work came to nothing, too. I have finished only a few paintings. I cannot even finish my favorite one, a portrait of a lovely lady of Florence. Today my fresco in the hall of the great council was ruined. But always, in spite of all my failures, one thing brought me comfort.” 列奥纳多继续说：“多年来，我画了很多张图，画的是我计划为米兰公爵雕刻的一匹巨大的马。” 但最后，那项工作也失败了。 我只完成了几幅画。 我连我最喜欢的那幅画都没法看完，那是佛罗伦萨一位美丽女士的肖像。 今天，我画在大议会厅里的壁画被毁了。 但不管我有多少失败，有一件事总让我感到安慰。”

“What?” asked Jack. “什么？”杰克问。

“I knew someday I would be the first person in the world to fly,” said Leonardo. His voice quavered. “Talking with the two of you, I knew the time had finally come to test my machine.” “我知道总有一天我会成为世界上第一个会飞的人，”列奥纳多说。 他的声音颤抖了。 “和你们两个人谈话后，我知道终于到了测试我的机器的时候了。”

“We’re sorry,” said Annie. “我们很抱歉，”安妮说。

“No, no, I had to test it sooner or later,” said Leonardo. “But now that dream, too, has come to nothing. I will never achieve fame by flying. I will never fly.” He hung his head and stared at the ground. “I shall go home now. I shall burn all my notebooks and my unfinished paintings and inventions. I shall leave Florence and never return.” “不，不，我迟早得测试它，”列奥纳多说。 “但现在，那个梦想也化为泡影了。” 我飞不起来，永远也飞不起来。 我永远都不会飞。” 他垂下头，盯着地面看。 “我现在要回家了。 我要把所有的笔记本、未完成的画作和发明都烧掉。 我将离开佛罗伦萨，再也不回来了。”

“Oh, no!” said Jack. “哦，不！” 杰克说。

“Wait a minute,” said Annie. “You will fly.” “等一下，” 安妮说。 “你会飞的。”

“Annie,” Jack warned. Since the machine would never work, he didn’t want her to give Leonardo false hope. “安妮，” 杰克警告道。 因为这台机器根本不可能运作，他不希望她给列奥纳多带来虚假的希望。

“You are going to fly, Leonardo,” said Annie. “And you’re going to love it.” “你要飞起来啦，列奥纳多，”安妮说。 “你会爱上飞行的。”

“Annie, human flight isn’t possible at this time in history!” Jack whispered to Annie. “A person needs a motor. We don’t have a motor.” “安妮，人类飞行在历史上这个时期是不可能的！”杰克低声对安妮说。 “人需要一个发动机。 我们没有发动机。”

But Annie paid no attention. “Hold on, everyone,” she said. “I have to get something.” She climbed into the cart and reached into Jack’s bag. 但安妮根本没有注意。 “大家抓紧了，”她说。 “我得去拿点东西。”她爬进马车，伸手到杰克的包里。

When Annie turned back around, Jack gasped. He had forgotten all about the Wand of Dianthus. 当安妮转身回来时，杰克惊呼起来。 他完全忘记了那根迪安图斯魔杖。
