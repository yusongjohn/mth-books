9. The Thing 9. 那件东西

Jack and Annie climbed into the tree house. 杰克和安妮爬进了树屋。

Peanut was sitting on a stack of books. 花生正坐在一摞书上。

Annie patted Peanut’s little head. “Thanks,” she said softly. 安妮轻轻拍了拍花生的小脑袋。 “谢谢。”她轻声说。

“I have to write some notes about the rain forest,” said Jack. “You find the Pennsylvania book.” “我得写一些关于雨林的笔记。”杰克说。 “你去找宾夕法尼亚的那本书。”

Annie began searching for the Pennsylvania book—the book that always took them home. 安妮开始找那本宾夕法尼亚的书——那本总能带他们回家的书。

Jack pulled out his notebook. 杰克拿出他的笔记本。

He had wanted to take lots of notes here. But all he’d written so far was: 他本来想在这儿记下好多笔记。 可到目前为止，他只写了：

图中文字：The Amazon rain forest is 图中文字：亚马逊雨林是

“It’s not here!” said Annie. “不在这儿！”安妮说。

“What?” Jack looked up. He glanced around the tree house. “什么？”杰克抬起头。 他朝树屋四周扫了一眼。

Annie was right. The Pennsylvania book was nowhere in sight. 安妮说得对。 宾夕法尼亚那本书哪儿都看不见。

“Was it here before we left home?” said Jack. “我们离开家之前它在这儿吗？”杰克说。

“I don’t remember,” said Annie. “我不记得了。”安妮说。

“Oh, man,” said Jack. “Now we can’t get back to Frog Creek.” “天哪。”杰克说。 “现在我们回不了蛙溪镇了。”

“That means we’ll be here when the vampire bats come out,” said Annie. “那意味着吸血蝙蝠出来的时候我们还会在这儿。”安妮说。

Something came flying through the tree house window. 有什么东西从树屋的窗户飞了进来。

“Ahhh!” Jack and Annie hid their heads. “啊！”杰克和安妮把头藏了起来。

Thud.

Something hit the floor. A red fruit.

Jack looked up. The monkey was sitting in the window. His head was cocked to one side. He seemed to be grinning at them. 杰克抬起头。 猴子正坐在窗边。 它的头歪向一边。 它好像正朝他们咧着嘴笑。

“You’re safe!” said Annie. “你没事了！”安妮说。

“Thanks for saving us,” said Jack. “谢谢你救了我们。”杰克说。

The monkey just grinned. 猴子只是咧嘴一笑。

“I have just one question,” said Annie. She pointed at the fruit. “Why do you keep throwing those at us?” “我只有一个问题。”安妮说。 她指着那颗果子。 “你为什么老是把那些东西扔给我们？”

The monkey grabbed the fruit. 猴子抓住果子。

“No! Don’t throw it!” said Jack. He ducked. 没什么可怕的。”杰克喊道。 别扔！”杰克说。他赶紧低头躲开。

But the monkey didn’t throw the fruit. 可猴子没有扔果子。

He held it out to Annie. He moved his lips as if he were trying to say something. 它把果子递给安妮。 它动了动嘴唇，好像想说什么。

Annie stared into the monkey’s eyes. He moved his lips again. 安妮盯着猴子的眼睛。 它又动了动嘴唇。

“Wow,” Annie said softly. “I understand now.” “哇。”安妮轻声说。 “我现在明白了。”

“Understand what?” said Jack. “明白什么？”杰克说。

Annie took the fruit from the monkey. “This is it,” she said. “The thing we need.” 安妮从猴子手里接过果子。 “就是这个。”她说。 “我们需要的那件东西。”

“What thing?” said Jack. “什么东西？”杰克说。

“One of the special things we’re supposed to find for Morgan,” Annie said. “To free her from the spell.” “我们该给摩根找的那些特别的东西之一。”安妮说。 “好把她从魔咒里解救出来。”

“Are you sure?” said Jack. “你确定吗？”杰克说。

Before Annie could answer, Jack saw the Pennsylvania book. “Look! Our book!” he said, pointing. 还没等安妮回答，杰克就看见了宾夕法尼亚的那本书。 “快看！ 我们的书！”他指着说。

“We found the thing. And now we can see the book,” said Annie. “That’s the way it works, remember?” “我们找到了那件东西。 现在我们又能看见那本书了。”安妮说。 “就是这样的，记得吗？”

Jack nodded. Now he remembered. The ninja master said they wouldn’t be able to find the Pennsylvania book until they had found what they were looking for. 现在他想起来了。 忍者大师说过，他们要先找到要找的东西，才能找到宾夕法尼亚那本书。

The monkey screeched with laughter. 猴子尖声大笑起来。

Jack and Annie looked at him. He was clapping his hands together. 杰克和安妮看着它。 它正在拍手。

Annie laughed with him. “How did you know to give this to us?” she said. “Who told you to do that?” 安妮也跟着它笑。 “你怎么知道要把这个给我们？”她说。

The monkey just waved at Jack and Annie. Then he turned and swung away out of the tree house. 猴子只是向杰克和安妮挥了挥手。 然后它转过身，荡出了树屋。

“Wait!” said Jack, looking out the window. “等等！”杰克说着，朝窗外望去。

Too late. 太晚了。

The monkey was gone. He had vanished below the treetops. 猴子已经不见了。 它消失在树顶下面。

“Good-bye!” called Annie. “再见！”安妮喊道。

A happy screech came from the mysterious world below. 一声快乐的尖叫从下面那个神秘的世界传来。

Jack sighed. He picked up his notebook again. He looked at his writing: 杰克叹了口气。 他又拿起笔记本。 他看着自己写的字：

图中文字：The Amazon rain forest is 图中文字：亚马逊雨林是

He had to write something before they left. He quickly added— 他赶紧又加了一笔——

图中文字：amazing 图中文字：令人惊叹的

Jack put away his notebook. Annie picked up the Pennsylvania book. 杰克收起了笔记本。 安妮拿起宾夕法尼亚的那本书。

“Now it’s really time to leave,” she said. “现在真的该走了。”她说。

She turned to the picture of the Frog Creek woods. “I wish we could go there,” she said, pointing at the picture. 她翻到蛙溪镇树林的那幅画。 “我希望我们能去那儿。”她指着图画说。

The wind started to blow. 风开始刮起来。

The leaves began to tremble. 树叶开始颤动。

The tree house began to spin. 树屋开始旋转。

It spun faster and faster. 它转得越来越快。

Then everything was still. 然后，一切都静止了。

Absolutely still. 绝对静止。
