4. Millions of Them! 4. 好几百万只！

Jack didn’t move. He kept staring down at the forest floor. 杰克一动不动。 他一直盯着下面的森林地面。

“What’s wrong?” Annie called from above. “怎么了？”安妮从上面叫道。

Jack didn’t answer. 杰克没有回答。

“You don’t see any giant spiders, do you?” Annie said. “你没看见什么巨型蜘蛛吧？”安妮说。

“Well … no.” Jack took a deep breath. “嗯……没有。”杰克深吸了一口气。

We have to keep going, he thought. We have to find the special thing for Morgan. 我们必须继续往下，他想。 我们得找到给摩根的那件特别的东西。

“No spiders. Nothing scary,” Jack called. And he started down the ladder again. “没有蜘蛛。 没什么可怕的。”杰克喊道。 他又开始顺着梯子往下爬。

Jack and Annie climbed down through the understory. Finally they stepped onto the forest floor. 杰克和安妮爬过林下层。 他们终于踩到了森林地面。

Only a few rays of light slanted through the gloom. 只有几缕光线斜斜地穿过昏暗。

The trees were very, very tall and very wide. Vines and moss were hanging everywhere. The ground was covered with dead leaves. 那些树又高又粗。 藤蔓和苔藓到处垂着。 地面上铺满了枯叶。

“Before we do anything, I’d better check the book,” said Jack. “在做任何事之前，我最好先查查书。”杰克说。

He pulled out the rain forest book. He found a picture of the dark world under the treetops. 他抽出那本雨林的书。 他找到一幅树顶下面那个黑暗世界的图画。

He read: 在雨林里，许多生物都会融入周围的环境。

In the rain forest, many living creatures blend in with their surroundings. This is called camouflage. 在雨林中，许多生物会和周围环境融为一体，这叫做伪装。

“Oh, man,” said Jack. He closed the book and looked around. “There’re tons of creatures down here. We just can’t see them.” “天哪。”杰克说。 他合上书，四处看了看。 “下面有好多好多生物。 只是我们看不见它们罢了。”

“Really?” whispered Annie. “真的吗？”安妮小声说。

She and Jack peered around at the quiet forest. Jack felt unseen eyes watching them. 她和杰克朝静悄悄的森林四周张望。 杰克觉得有看不见的眼睛在盯着他们。

“Let’s hurry and find the special thing,” whispered Annie. “咱们快点找到那件特别的东西吧。”安妮小声说。

“How will we know when we find it?” Jack said. “我们怎么知道找到了呢？”杰克说。

“I think we’ll just know,” said Annie. She headed off through the gloom. “我觉得我们到时候就会知道。”安妮说。 她穿过昏暗出发了。

Jack followed. They crept between the huge trees and past hanging vines. 杰克跟了上去。 他们悄悄走过巨大的树木，绕过垂下来的藤蔓。

Annie stopped. “Wait—what’s that?” 杰克和安妮停下来，喘了口气。 “等等——那是什么？”

“What’s what?” “什么什么？”

“Listen—that weird sound.” “听——那个奇怪的声音。”

Jack listened. He heard a crackling sound. It sounded like a person walking over leaves. 杰克侧耳听着。 他听见一阵劈里啪啦的声音。 听起来像是有人在树叶上走路。

Jack looked around. He didn’t see anyone. 杰克四处看了看。 他谁也没看见。

But the sound got louder.

Was it an animal? A giant bug? One that had never been named? 是动物吗？还是一只巨型虫子？ 一只从来没有名字的虫子？

Just then the silent forest came alive. 就在这时，寂静的森林突然活了过来。

Birds took off into the air. Frogs hopped over the leaves. Lizards ran up the tree trunks. 鸟儿扑棱棱飞上了天空。 青蛙从落叶上跳过去。 蜥蜴顺着树干往上跑。

The weird noise grew louder and louder. 那奇怪的声音越来越大，越来越大。

“Maybe the book explains it,” said Jack. He opened the book. He found a picture of different animals running together. He read: “也许书上有解释。”杰克说。 他打开书。 他找到一幅不同动物一起奔跑的图画。他读道：

When animals hear a crackling sound, they flee in panic. The sound means that 30 million flesh-eating army ants are marching through the dead leaves. 当动物们听到噼里啪啦的声音时，它们惊慌地逃跑。这个声音意味着有3000万只食肉蚂蚁正穿过枯叶行军。

“It’s army ants!” cried Jack. “Millions of them!” “是行军蚁！”杰克喊道。 “有好几百万只！”

“Where?” cried Annie. “在哪儿？”安妮喊道。

Jack and Annie looked around wildly. 杰克和安妮慌忙四处张望。

“There!” Annie pointed. “那儿！”安妮指着说。

Army ants—millions and millions of them—were marching over the leaves! 行军蚁——好几百万、好几百万只——正从落叶上浩浩荡荡地爬过来！

“Run to the tree house!” cried Annie. “快跑回树屋！”安妮喊道。

“Where is it?” said Jack, whirling around. All the trees looked the same. Where was the rope ladder? “树屋在哪儿？”杰克转着圈说。 所有的树看起来都一样。 绳梯在哪儿？

“Just run!” cried Annie. “快跑就行了！”安妮喊道。

Jack and Annie took off. 杰克和安妮拔腿就跑。

They ran over the dead leaves. 他们踩着枯叶奔跑。

They ran between wide tree trunks. 他们在粗大的树干之间跑着。

They ran past the hanging vines and mosses. 他们跑过垂下来的藤蔓和苔藓。

They climbed over thick roots. 他们爬过粗壮的树根。

Jack saw a clearing ahead. It was filled with sunlight. 杰克看见前面有一块空地。 那里充满了阳光。

“That way!” he cried. “那边！”他喊道。

Jack and Annie hurried toward the light. They pushed their way through the bushes. 杰克和安妮朝亮光奔去。 他们拨开灌木丛往前挤。

They burst onto the bank of a river. 他们猛地冲到了河岸上。

They stared at the slow-moving brown water. 他们盯着缓缓流动的棕色河水。

“Do you think the ants will come this way?” Annie said, panting.

“I don’t know,” said Jack. “But if we wade a few feet into the river, we’re safe. The ants won’t go into the water. Come on.” “我不知道。”杰克说。 “不过要是我们蹚进河里几英尺，就安全了。 蚂蚁不会下水的。来吧。”

“Look!” said Annie. “快看！”安妮说。

She pointed to a big log rocking at the edge of the river. The inside of the log was dug out. 她指着河边一根摇晃着的大木头。 原木的内部被挖空了。

“It looks like a canoe,” said Jack. He listened to the crackling sound in the distance. “Let’s get in it. Quick!” “看起来像一只独木舟。”杰克说。 他听着远处噼啪噼啪的声音。 “咱们上去。快！”

Jack shoved the book into his backpack. Then he and Annie carefully climbed into the dugout log. 杰克把书塞进背包。 然后他和安妮小心翼翼地爬进了那截挖空的木头。

Annie leaned out of it. She pushed away from the bank with her hands. 安妮探出身子。 她用手把船从岸边推开。

“Wait!” said Jack. “We don’t have paddles!” “等等——”杰克喘着气说。 “我们没有桨！”

“Oops,” said Annie. “哎呀。”安妮说。

The canoe started moving slowly down the muddy river. 独木舟开始沿着浑浊的河水慢慢往下漂。
