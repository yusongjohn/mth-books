10. HaIfway There 10. 成功一半了

Jack opened his eyes. Peanut was on the tree house windowsill. 杰克睁开眼睛。 花生在树屋的窗台上。

“We’re home,” said Annie. “我们到家了。”安妮说。

Jack breathed a sigh of relief. 杰克松了一口气。

Annie held the fruit up to the afternoon light. 安妮把果子举到午后的阳光下。

“What exactly is this?” she asked. “这到底是什么？”她问。

“Maybe it’s in the book,” Jack answered. “也许书里有。”杰克回答。

He pulled out the rain forest book. He flipped through the pages. He came to a picture of the red fruit. 他抽出那本雨林的书。 他一页页翻着。 他翻到一幅红果子的图画。

“Here it is!” he said. He read out loud: “在这儿！”他说。他大声读道：

“The mango has a sweet taste like that of a peach.” “芒果的味道甜甜的，像桃子一样。”

“Mango? Hmmm,” said Annie. She brought the fruit close to her lips. “芒果？ 嗯。”安妮说。 她把果子凑近嘴唇。

“Hey!” said Jack, grabbing the mango from her. “We have to put it with the moonstone.” “嘿！”杰克说着，从她手里把芒果抢了过来。 “我们得把它和月光石放在一起。”

Jack placed the mango on the M carved into the floor. Next to the clear moonstone. 杰克把芒果放在地板上刻着的M上面。 紧挨着那块透明的月光石。

“Moonstone … mango,” whispered Annie. It sounded like a spell. “月光石……芒果。”安妮小声说。 听起来像一句咒语。

“We’re halfway there,” said Jack. “ Two more to go.” “我们完成一半了。”杰克说。

“Then we can free you, Morgan!” Annie called, as if Morgan were nearby. “这样我们就能解救你了，摩根！”安妮喊道，仿佛摩根就在附近。

“How do you know she can hear you?” said Jack. “你怎么知道她听得见？”杰克说。

“I just feel it,” Annie said. “我就是感觉得到。”安妮说。

“Oh, brother,” said Jack. He needed more proof than that. “哎呀，真是的。”杰克说。 他需要比这更多的证据。

Squeak. Peanut was looking at Jack and Annie. 花生正看着杰克和安妮。

“We have to leave you now,” Jack said to the mouse. “我们现在得离开你了。”杰克对小老鼠说。

Squeak.

“Can’t we take her with us?” said Annie. “我们不能带她走吗？”安妮说。

“No,” said Jack. “Mom won’t let us keep a mouse in the house. She doesn’t like mice, remember?” “不行。”杰克说。 “妈妈不会让我们在家里养老鼠的。 她不喜欢老鼠，记得吗？”

“How could anyone not like a mouse?” said Annie. “怎么会有人不喜欢老鼠呢？”安妮说。

Jack smiled. “How could anyone not like a spider?” he said. 杰克笑了。 “怎么会有人不喜欢蜘蛛呢？”他说。

“That’s different.” Annie patted Peanut’s head. “Bye,” she said. “Wait for us here. We’ll be back tomorrow.” “那可不一样。”安妮拍了拍花生的头。 “再见。”她说。 “在这儿等我们。 我们明天就回来。”

Jack patted the mouse, too. “Bye, Peanut. Thanks for your help,” he said. 杰克也轻轻拍了拍小老鼠。 “再见，花生。 谢谢你的帮助。”他说。

Squeak.

Jack put the rain forest book on top of the book about ninjas. 杰克把雨林的书放在忍者那本书上面。

Then he pulled on his backpack. And he and Annie left the tree house. 然后他背上背包。 他和安妮离开了树屋。

They climbed down the rope ladder. They stepped onto the ground. 他们顺着绳梯往下爬。 他们踩到了地面上。

They started walking through the Frog Creek woods. 他们开始穿过蛙溪镇的树林。

Leaf shadows danced in the light. 树叶的影子在光里跳动。

A bird called out. 一只鸟叫了一声。

These woods are very different from the rain forest, Jack thought. 这片树林和雨林很不一样，杰克想。

“There’re no jaguars or army ants here,” he said. “No little monkeys.” “这儿没有美洲豹，也没有行军蚁。”他说。 “也没有小猴子。”

“You know, that monkey was never being mean,” said Annie. “He was just trying to give us the mango.” “你知道吗，那只猴子从来没有存心使坏。”安妮说。 “它只是想把芒果给我们。”

“I know. Actually, nothing was being mean,” said Jack. “The army ants were just marching. That’s what army ants do.” “我知道。”安妮皱着眉说。 其实，谁也没有存心使坏。”杰克说。 “行军蚁只是在行军。 行军蚁就是这样的。”

“The piranhas were just being piranhas,” said Annie. “食人鱼只是在做食人鱼该做的事。”安妮说。

“The snake was just being a snake,” said Jack. “蛇只是在做蛇该做的事。”杰克说。

“The crocodile was just being a crocodile,” said Annie. “鳄鱼只是在做鳄鱼该做的事。”安妮说。

“The jaguar was just taking care of her baby,” said Jack. “美洲豹只是在照顾她的宝宝。”杰克说。

Annie shuddered. “I still don’t love bugs,” she said. 安妮打了个哆嗦。 “我还是不喜欢虫子。”她说。

“You don’t have to love them,” said Jack. “Just leave them alone. And they won’t bother you.”

In fact, that’s true about the whole rain forest, Jack thought. Everyone should just leave it all alone. 其实，整个雨林都是这样，杰克想。 大家应该让这一切都保持原样。

“Who cares if the bugs don’t have names?” he said softly. “ They know who they are.” “虫子有没有名字又有什么关系呢？”他轻声说。 “它们知道自己是谁。”

Jack and Annie stepped out of the Frog Creek woods. 杰克和安妮走出了蛙溪镇的树林。

They started walking up their street. It was lit with a golden light. 他们开始沿着自家的街道往上走。 街道沐浴在金色的光里。

“Race you!” said Annie.

They took off running. 他们拔腿就跑。

They ran across their yard. 他们跑过自家院子。

They raced up their steps. 他们冲上台阶。

“Safe!” they shouted together, tagging their front door. “安全！”他们一起喊着，拍到了自家前门。
